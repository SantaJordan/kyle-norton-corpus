# AI can do most everything a salesperson does in a day. | Kyle Norton · LinkedIn · 2026-03-31

**Kyle Norton**: CRO at Owner.com for 2 years 7 months with 18 years 1 month of total professional experience. Previous roles include SVP Sales & Partnerships at Owner.com, Founding Member - Toronto Chapter at Pavilion, and Director of Revenue & Merchant Success, Canada at Shopify. Based in San Francisco, California, United States [US].

---

AI can do most everything a salesperson does in a day.

Research the prospect. Write the email. Take the call notes. Fill the CRM. Send the follow-up.

So why do I keep hiring so many salespeople?

I got to sit down with Eric Janssen for the last episode of Sales Reframed this season. He put the same question to me, Daniel Pink (author of To Sell is Human, which is one of the first books I tell new reps to read), and Asad Zaman (CEO of Sales Talent Agency) in separate interviews.

None of us heard each other's answers.

We all said the same thing.

Eric framed it better than any of us could: AI does the "homework." Humans do the "hard work." Research, data entry, note-taking? Automated. Judgment, trust, reading the room on a deal that could go either way? That's worth more now than it was five years ago.

One thing from Asad's interview I can't stop thinking about: researchers gave AI tools to material scientists. Doubled productivity for the top third. Did nothing for the bottom third. The difference was expertise. The best scientists knew which AI suggestions were good and which were garbage. The weaker ones couldn't tell.

Same thing happens in every sales org I've seen. AI is a multiplier. Multiplying zero still gives you zero.

My take for revenue leaders: stop asking "where do I start with AI?" Start asking "how do I make my company AI native?" One is a project. The other is a permanent shift in how you operate. And it starts with your data foundations, not your tool stack.
