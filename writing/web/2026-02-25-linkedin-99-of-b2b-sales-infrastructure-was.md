# 99% of B2B sales infrastructure was built for buyers who sit at desks and respond to emails. · 2026-02-25T00:00:00+00:00

99% of B2B sales infrastructure was built for buyers who sit at desks and respond to emails.

Restaurant owners do neither.

That disconnect is why most restaurant GTM motions are quietly burning seven figures running in place.

David, Ganesh and the DataLane team just published The Canonical Guide to Selling to Restaurants, and I was fortunate to contribute some of what we’ve learned building Owner.com’s go-to-market.

The short version of our approach: we don’t do field sales. No in-person meetings. Even if you ask us to show up, we’re probably not gonna do it.

That’s not laziness. It’s congruency.

Every piece of the system has to fit together: ACV, sales motion, data infrastructure, tech stack, channel mix, etc. When we invested in the architecture underneath instead of headcount on top, our connect rate went from 3% to 16% (thanks, Datalane), BDR productivity tripled then doubled again and we scaled to unicorn status.

The guide covers the frameworks behind this: GTM congruency, the disqualification cascade, why your “5,000 account territory” is really 1,200 workable accounts, and why most teams are wasting hours on manual research and gatekeeper calls.

If you sell to restaurants or any local business where the buyer isn’t on LinkedIn, it’s worth the read.

p.s. They did something super smart and provided a markdown file that you can throw into your favourite LLM and talk to it.
