# A lot of revenue leaders are waiting on the sidelines to see how "a lot of this AI stuff" plays out. I think that's the wrong approach. · 2025-04-01T00:00:00+00:00

A lot of revenue leaders are waiting on the sidelines to see how "a lot of this AI stuff" plays out. I think that's the wrong approach.

I understand this perspective because what they're saying is that there might be a second-mover advantage.

The concern is that if you partner with an early-stage startup, they might not build in the right direction, it's not really going to work, and you've done all this work to implement it and train your team; only to find out you might be wrong.

That scenario is guaranteed to happen sometimes.

We have tried a bunch of things at Owner. We've implemented about six major AI use cases. One of them didn't really work, and we spent a bunch of time on it and it was definitely an inconvenience, but the cost of not pursuing these initiatives and not having the other five successful cases (or even if it was two failures and three successes) is much too great in my opinion.

If you decide to wait it out and jump onto the winning vendors later, that's fine but then your whole organization is 12, 18, or 24 months behind in terms of their understanding and adoption.

Half the reason to try is to learn about this new platform imo.

You just better hope that your direct competitor isn't farther along.

For us, the advantage in rep productivity, revenue growth, and the LTV-CAC profile is profound. It represents a very durable business advantage, and it will likely remain so for a while because we're potentially expanding our lead versus competitors.

I think that's the danger for those taking a "wait and see" approach.
