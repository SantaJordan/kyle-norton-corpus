# Most companies are implementing AI backwards. · 2025-10-21T00:00:00+00:00

Most companies are implementing AI backwards.

They want the AI SDR. The flashy tools. The quick wins.

But after three years of aggressive AI implementation at Owner.com, I can tell you: that approach fails fast.

I sat down with James Geyer on the Boardroom RevOps podcast to break down what actually works:

1. Start with data, not tools
AI is a pattern recognition machine. Feed it bad patterns and you get bad outputs. We spent our first year building data foundations: first-party (what's happening in our customer journey) and third-party (deep market understanding and contact enrichment).

Unsexy? Yes. Critical? Absolutely.

2. The RevOps resource "problem" isn't a problem
We have 7 RevOps people for 30 sales reps. On paper, this looks insane.
But when you look at per-rep efficiency, growth rate, and project ROI, it makes complete sense. Most companies under-resource the team that builds the infrastructure for scale then wonder why their systems are on fire at $50M ARR.

3. Trust is the foundation of your tech stack
Here's something soft that matters: people want to do the right thing.
When your systems imply you don't trust your team and that you need to box them in, they feel it. And they'll act accordingly. This is one of Steve Dinner's greatest super powers as a RevOps leader.

We design systems that remove friction for good work. This philosophy shows up in everything from our Salesforce architecture to which AI tools we implement.

The full conversation covers the AI implementation pyramid, how to resource RevOps properly, what to look for when hiring, and why most AI projects fail after the initial wins fade.
