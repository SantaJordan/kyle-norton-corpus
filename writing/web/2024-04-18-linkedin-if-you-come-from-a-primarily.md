# ❓ If you come from a primarily inbound sales model, can you learn outbound as a first time Head of Sales? ❓ · 2024-04-18T00:00:00+00:00

❓ If you come from a primarily inbound sales model, can you learn outbound as a first time Head of Sales? ❓

💡 My answer 💡
No, and with very few exceptions. I'll take it one step further and say that Founders should be very weary of hiring a VPS that doesn't have a well established outbound muscle.

Very few companies are so good at marketing that they can survive on inbound. Outbound is still an essential channel for distribution. If you're VPS can't grok outbound, you're heading down a treacherous path.

Would love to hear thoughts and opinions!

**Martin Roth** · 2024-04-18T00:00:00+00:00 · 13 reactions
> You can go outbound to inbound, but you can't go the other way.
>
> This is true for AEs, sales managers, and senior sales leadership.
>
> There are rare exceptions to this rule.
>
> It's the same reason you hire people who have sold a similar ACV and sales cycle. We have muscle memory that stays with us over the course of a career. 
>
> There are nuances that make outbound different and more difficult than inbound.
>
> Consider "5 stages of awareness" from Eugene Schwartz.
>
> You have to take a prospect from unaware > problem aware > solution aware > product aware > most aware.
>
> When you are working inbound leads, you have skipped at least the first step, maybe more.
>
> That's a BIG deal!
>
> Outbound requires the salesperson to move the prospect "business as usual" to a state of understanding the problems in their business.
>
> It's almost impossible to do this on-the-job if you don't have experience with how to do outbound, how to coach outboun, and how to scale outbound.
>
> My credentials?
>
> We built a $30MM revenue business with about 60% inbound and 40% outbound.
>
> The first $2MM was almost 100% outbound. After a few years the inbound machine started to work and the demand balance shifted.
