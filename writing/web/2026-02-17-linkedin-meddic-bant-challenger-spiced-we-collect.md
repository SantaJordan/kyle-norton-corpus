# MEDDIC, BANT, Challenger, SPICED... we collect these frameworks like trading cards. And they work. Until the pattern changes. · 2026-02-17T00:00:00+00:00

MEDDIC, BANT, Challenger, SPICED... we collect these frameworks like trading cards. And they work. Until the pattern changes.

Then you're solving this year's problem with last year's playbook.

Usha Iyer and I went deep on this on the podcast.

She's a former C++ developer who ran a $350M product portfolio and now owns GTM and CS at Hivebrite. She's one of the few people who can speak credibly about both product and revenue.

One thing she said that I keep coming back to: product leaders think in hypotheses. Revenue leaders think in decisions. A hypothesis can be wrong and that's fine. A decision carries ego, and we'll run at a bad one for three quarters before admitting it didn't work.

This resonates with me because one of the most formative experiences in my career was being at Shopify and watching how a world-class product org attacked problems. They didn't jump to solutions. They sat with the problem longer than any sales leader would be comfortable with. And the quality of what came out the other side was on a different level.

I've been trying to bring pieces of that into how I run revenue ever since.

Here's a stat from HBS that should scare you: even well-reasoned experiments fail two-thirds of the time. So every org-wide rollout you do without piloting first is worse than a coin flip.

Five things revenue leaders should steal from the product playbook:

1. Think first principles before reaching for a framework
2. Trust your inputs as much as your outputs (outcomes and outputs aren't the same thing)
3. Find the friction your dashboards can't see — it's the silent retention killer
4. Pilot with 3-4 people before deploying to 50
5. Lead every cross-functional ask with the problem, not your solution

Full breakdown with research from Kahneman, Stanford, HBS, and more on the Substack.
