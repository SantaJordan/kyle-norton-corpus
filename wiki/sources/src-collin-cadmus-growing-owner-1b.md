# Collin Cadmus Podcast: Growing Owner.com to $1 Billion Valuation w/ Kyle Norton, CRO at Owner.com
**Show / host:** Collin Cadmus Podcast, hosted by Collin Cadmus (ex-SinglePlatform, Doctor.com, Aircall) · **Published:** 2025-06-11 (YouTube upload date; metadata.json has no date) · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/collin-cadmus-growing-owner-1b/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xY0IK9zCr6I

## TL;DR
- Outbound isn't dead; the bar is higher. "The phone is better than ever." Owner's BDR emails are fully automated, and a hyper-personalized, Snowflake/Clay-powered email program warms accounts before BDRs call with prompts tied to those emails. Owner is also working with Jordan Crawford on cold email at scale.
- Strategy must be congruent. Owner is all-in on digital: pure round-robin, no territories, no site visits. It sells with radical transparency and makes cancelling easy, a long game in a distrustful restaurant market.
- Revenue leaders must understand venture capital (power law, fund math, fundraising cycles). VP Sales tenure is short partly because leaders chase titles over company quality and skip diligence. "optimize for company quality."

## What Kyle argued
- Avoid binary takes like "outbound is dead". Declining reply and delivery rates reflect more noise, not broken channels. 2016-style lightly personalized blasts no longer work; "you just have to step up your game." @04:11–05:15 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- "The phone is better than ever, in my opinion, because people stopped using it as a channel." Social selling is "as effective as it's ever been." @05:15 ([[outbound-at-scale]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Advantages from new channels decay faster now (Outreach/SalesLoft gave about five years of advantage). On deliverability you have to "figure it out" with Instantly, Smartlead, domain warming and alternative domains. @07:20–08:23 ([[ai-sales-stack]])
- Hand-personalized email by BDRs is a poor use of their time when selling to restaurants. Cadence emails auto-send, and the goal is to move email fully into the background, "serve it on a platter for the BDR team". @08:23–10:26 ([[outbound-at-scale]], [[gtm-engineering]])
- BDRs drifted too far toward harvesting existing demand, which hit leads too often and lowered win rates. Owner is "tilting them back towards cold" because craft and data preparation have improved. @10:26–11:32 ([[outbound-at-scale]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Radical transparency suits a market burned by Yelp, POS vendors and competitors with "horrendous Better Business Bureau pages": lead with the good and the bad, no long contracts, easy cancellation. "I don't believe in doing business that way." @13:42–16:47 ([[churn-and-retention]], [[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Treating churning customers well is a long-term advantage. Owner is "the single highest rated software in all of restaurant technology" on G2/Trustpilot. @16:47 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Congruence beats any single choice. Toast's in-market regional model and Owner's all-digital model both work; the "no man's land" in between fails. @18:53–23:05 ([[org-design]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Know your game: venture money means spend fast and build a platform, not a point solution. Raising venture money and then playing efficiency (or bootstrapping while spending like a venture-backed company) are "both dead ends". @23:05–24:13 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- Revenue leaders need venture fluency (the power law, fund math, carry). VCs only care about outliers, which is why boards push for big swings. @29:22–37:22 ([[fundraising-and-investability]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
- Avoid the "okay growth, meaningful burn" middle zone. It leads to bad terms and liquidation preferences. Slowing down to fix things has to be reconciled with cash runway. Owner raises with about 24 months of cash and has "been preempted most times". @38:43–41:30 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- Short VP Sales tenure is as much the VPs' fault as founders': they push for titles beyond their capability and do no diligence. Take a director title at a great company, and ask the CFO to screen-share the growth model, recent financials and last board deck. @45:24–54:22 ([[career-and-leadership]], [[sales-hiring]])
- AI will both replace many roles and augment teams. Owner is "massively focused on augmenting our team with AI". @55:27 ([[ai-in-gtm]])

## Numbers & facts he cited

- Meeting show rates: "about 50%" aimed for overall, but "up to like 75 80% for inbound and 70 ish percent for outbound", backed by a tight show playbook (pre-call research, call/text on demo day, a call as the demo starts). @19:54

- Owner raises with "almost 24 months of cash left in the bank" and has "been preempted most times". @40:10

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Avoid binaries:** channels decline because of noise, so the bar to stand out rises.
- **Email-warms, phone-closes:** auto-sent, hyper-personalized email built on a "monster clay table" plus an MCP server connected to Snowflake that surfaces insights restaurants "don't know". BDR call prompts are tied to those emails ("problems based hypothesis of need"). @09:23–10:26 ([[clay]])
- **XDR segmentation:** inbound SDRs, PLG BDRs, outbound BDRs → inbound AEs, outbound single-location AEs, multi-location AEs. @10:26–12:36
- **Radical transparency selling** plus easy cancellation as a trust moat.
- **Strategic congruence:** pick digital *or* in-market (Toast) and commit; avoid the no-man's land.
- **Know the game you're playing:** venture vs bootstrap must match spend.
- **Power law and fund math:** recommends *The Power Law* (Sebastian Mallaby), 20VC, BG2, and Jack Altman's *Uncapped* episode with Josh Kopelman.
- **Raise from strength:** about 24 months of runway when raising.
- **Optimize for company quality over title:** title and company quality are "inversely correlated".
- **Pre-offer diligence:** an hour-long CFO screen-share of the growth model, financials and last board deck; stress-test conversion, unit economics, burn and retention. He mentions a planned newsletter post on this.
