# E09: Building Repeatable Revenue Engines at Outreach and Catalyst with Mark Kosoglow
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Mark Kosoglow (transcribed "Coziglou"), first employee & sales leader @ Outreach, then CRO @ Catalyst · **Published:** 2024-11-20 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E09-building-repeatable-revenue-engines-at-outreach-and-catalyst/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Repeatability means no single choke point, plus being able to predict outputs from inputs. It decays: tactics get copied ("sales is like a copy me sport") and the responsive TAM gets exhausted. So it rests on documented process plus measurement that tells you when it's breaking.
- Instrument the funnel by channel (inbound vs outbound) and by both volume and value. Use L1 (volume and conversion) and L2 (the four or five drivers behind each) metrics, and plot ACV, win rate and cycle time against cohorts to find breaks. Pressure-test finance-plan assumptions.
- People: hire for traits (coachability first) over skills. The enablement leader is a "principal", not a "teacher". Managers run a weekly every-deal review that the leader coaches from recordings. Kyle adds his "manufacturing line" definition, the could-you-do-this-job-from-our-Notion test, Owner's segment-by-stage heat map, and Shopify's Apex-code routing scar.

## Guest's core ideas
- Coming from school sales, he had to teach tech sellers to multithread with the "rule of 52": five active contacts per account, at least two decision makers. @07:06–09:10 ([[sales-methodology]])
- Repeatability is no choke point plus predictable outputs from inputs. It ends through copycats (the "bet email") and exhaustion: "everything has a time has a shelf life". @11:10–17:17 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- Self-efficacy: you can only change what is standardised. Founders show traction, then an experienced leader builds the first process. @19:20–21:23 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Plans: find finance's assumptions. A 20% to 28% win-rate jump is "a 40% increase", which is unrealistic, while about 10% is doable. Then ask what process change produces the lift (citing Sam Jacobs and Jay Nathan on financial fluency). @21:23–23:27 ([[capacity-planning]], [[person-sam-jacobs]])
- Instrumentation: split inbound and outbound (one blended funnel hid a hold-rate problem), and track volume and value. Plot ACV, win rate and cycle time against cohorts; at Outreach, "at 35 employees, cycle time goes up 50%, deal size goes up 50%". The outbound funnel: active accounts (manual touch in 14 days), engaged, booked, held, qualified pipeline. @25:35–32:48 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- L1 metrics (lagging volume and conversion) vs four or five L2 drivers each. Going more than three dashboards deep means "you're done". Example: a renamed Salesforce field had sequences "firing at 30%". @33:54–41:09 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[revops]])
- Hiring: "Skills are nice to have. Traits are required". Coachability is the trait that unlocks skills (a former seventh-grade teacher closed a $1M CenturyLink deal). @42:09–45:16 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Enablement: hire a curriculum builder ("the principal"), not a trainer. His own 107-page first Outreach playbook was "too much information". @45:16–48:25 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Operating rhythm: managers review every deal with every rep weekly. He had them send "two of their recordings every quarter" and coached the deal review, "the most important meeting in the entire sales organization". @49:31–50:35 ([[operating-rhythm]])
- Quickfire: value support orgs (enablement, RevOps); "build your squad"; a leader's job is to "affect change"; some politics is required. @50:35–52:44 ([[career-and-leadership]])
