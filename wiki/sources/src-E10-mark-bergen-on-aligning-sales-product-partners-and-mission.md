# E10: Mark Bergen on Aligning Sales Product Partners and Mission
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Mark Bergen, fractional revenue leader (ex-head of sales for Shopify Plus, 6.5 yrs; ex-Vision Critical, 10 yrs), Kyle's former boss · **Published:** 2024-11-27 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E10-mark-bergen-on-aligning-sales-product-partners-and-mission/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Selling inside a product/engineering-first company (Shopify): leadership kept "a list of large brands we'd said no to" as a point of pride. Toby thought long-term, and sales leaders carried no variable comp so they wouldn't optimise short-term. Influence the roadmap through relationships, customer-rooted asks in product's language and themes, and by bringing engineers to customers (checkout "code red").
- Partnerships fail when you can't say what's in it for the partner. Wait for PMF, a clear ICP and real overlap. Decide whether you're a platform or an application, invest deeply in a few partners, and beware incenting partner reps.
- Kyle discloses his own Owner comp: "80 base 20 variable". He chose it deliberately after Shopify ("you don't want me on a 50 50 plan"), and advises founders not to put first heads of sales on big variable. He credits Bergen with giving him his start in tech and with the "default to trust" principle in his "blueprint" operating manual.

## What Kyle said
- Origin story: Bergen "gave me my start in tech". Kyle "cold called you out of nowhere", with nothing on his resume but "Hawking crappy, crappy conferences", and later "tagged along to Shopify with you". They have known each other since about 2012. @05:08–06:09 ([[career-and-leadership]], [[shopify]])
- His head of RevOps, "who's worked with me for eight years now across a few different companies". @12:23 ([[talent-density]], [[revops]])
- Shopify's hierarchy (joking): "engineering, then product, then design… janitorial services, and then sales is like just right one under there". He "loved that part of my Shopify experience", did a Reforge course, and calls it "one of the biggest lessons I took away", though "I don't think comfortable for everyone". @14:24–15:25 ([[shopify]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- Saying no to custom enterprise asks "was, I think unequivocally the right way to do things". In his three years there, Shopify went from "quite competitive in enterprise to being like pretty de facto". @18:32 ([[icp-and-saying-no]], [[shopify]])
- Comp: at Shopify he had no variable either ("it broke my brain when I first got the offer"). Now: "my current comp plan is 80, 80 base 20 variable. I said to the owner, you know, Hey, look, you don't want me on a 50 50 plan. I cannot resist my own urges to make money". "our founder was really open and excited about that view." @21:40 ([[comp-plans]], [[owner-com]])
- Advice he gives founders hiring a first head of sales: "you're going to give them the wrong quota". Tying a leader to a wrong number drives "short-term decisions", "poor customer quality and weird culture stuff". Several heads of sales and CROs have adopted this; he cites Roberge on full base salary for first reps. @21:40–22:43 ([[comp-plans]])
- Unlabelled, most likely Kyle: "My sales team had no variable comp when I got there, then we introduced variable comp and then COVID hit and we pulled them off of variable comp. No discernible change in, in active behavior." Then: "Find the intrinsic motivators" for junior reps, "less and less true as you move into mid market and enterprise". @23:44 ([[comp-plans]], [[recognition-and-motivation]])
- Cross-functional learning: he listens to Lenny's podcast "every single week" and cites *Range* (David Epstein) and Toby's "T-shaped" idea. @31:02–32:07 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Roadmap asks must be "rooted in the customer first". Revenue leaders say "it's 12 customers at 75,000 ARR a piece", and "that's not how a PM or an engineering leader thinks". Sales leaders who say product "is not listening" are "using totally the wrong language without doing the work to seek to understand first". @36:13, @41:23 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Shopify checkout "code red": an all-hands AMA question surfaced customers "hijacking our checkout" and triggered a swarm, and "now checkout is this like insane competitive advantage for the company." @38:17–39:18 ([[shopify]])
- Partnership scepticism: "I also see partnerships go wrong so frequently". He asks why a partner would care if "you're 7 million ARR, you've got 75 customers". Vendor claims that ecosystem-led growth "is going to like fix all your woes" are "sort of disingenuous". If you're an application, ask "whose platform do you plug in and make better? Legitimately." His heuristic: wait until you can commit "multiple resources and significant time exec down", and you "can't get a partner motion stood up if you're still iterating on your messaging every 60 days and your ICP is still shifting." @44:29–58:55 ([[icp-and-saying-no]], [[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Leadership origin: he came "from a boiler room… turn and burn". Bergen's "default to trust" is "in my operating manual… I call it my blueprint… here's the blueprint of working with Kyle". On spin: "your quota going up is actually a great thing" is the kind of line that destroys trust. @67:18–68:20 ([[culture-and-high-standards]], [[career-and-leadership]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Fractional work gives time to understand problems deeply. As an operator, block multi-day offsites with a notebook, not two-hour calendar holds. Loren Padelford (transcribed "Lauren Paddleford") handed off responsibilities on vacation and never took them back. @07:13–14:24 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Shopify said no to big brands needing custom work, and executives kept the list of "no"s as "a point of pride". Toby thought long-term and tolerated missed quarters. @16:30–20:37 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- No variable comp for sales leaders "if we put you on variable, you're going to think short term". Ask founders "what are you optimizing for", because "the moment you introduce a compensation program, you very quickly create your culture". @20:37–23:44 ([[comp-plans]])
- A strong "why" (Sinek's golden circle) aligns the org. Operationalise long-term thinking with no variable for senior leaders, existential offsites ("How would you go to market and compete with Shopify? You weren't allowed to hire a single salesperson") and guest sessions from engineering. @24:47–31:02 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Partnering with product: weekly lunches with engineering leaders, asks tied to the mission and "80% problem", product themes, and engineers meeting customers. @34:11–42:25 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Getting sales accepted: walk the halls and retell the story every quarter. @42:25–44:29
- Partnerships: "I didn't actually step back to take the time to really understand why the partner cared about it". At Vision Critical, the agency program contradicted the core value proposition. Prerequisites are PMF and a clear ICP; then decide platform vs application. Shopify invested heavily (MDF, partner managers). Go deep with a few partners; incenting partner reps draws CEO pushback. @45:30–63:03 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Followership: vulnerability ("I don't know"), default to trust, no spin. Most common advice: "do you really understand the problem". Hardest lesson: understanding himself, with a coach. @64:04–71:22 ([[career-and-leadership]])
