# E14: A systematic approach to GTM infrastructure and talent with CRO, Martin Roth
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Martin Roth, former CRO @ Levelset (acquired by Procore), now advisor · **Published:** 2025-01-01 (RSS pubDate) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E14-a-systematic-approach-to-gtm-infrastructure-and-talent-with/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- SMB and enterprise need different revenue engines. SMB lives on centrally manufactured demand and operational rigor. Enterprise lives on deal excellence and account-based plays.
- Talent in SMB is "minted": write down the playbook to raise the floor, hire raw talent, micro-coach, celebrate "bright spots," and build career paths and a leadership curriculum because frontline managers are the most important role.
- Infrastructure means a fixed operating rhythm (weekly 1:1s, team meeting, forecast meeting, call camps), company-supplied leads, and a hand-built monthly one-page funnel with productivity per rep as the top metric.

## What Kyle said
- Framing: most GTM discourse is enterprise-focused and doesn't separate the two motions. SMB is a great place to build careers, but investors avoid it because "the economics are tough": LTV/CAC, high churn, high rep turnover, people-heavy. @05:11–06:14 ([[smb-sales-motion]])
- Owner hiring: "I think we've only had one AE mishire out of maybe 16." He credits doing it a second time, his LinkedIn brand, company buzz, and hiring Levelset alumni and President's Club reps from restaurant tech. @10:23 ([[sales-hiring]])
- "You can't grade yourself based on your top people"; grade on the middle cohort, which shows the system. @11:27 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Talent density: restaurant-tech consolidation lets Owner "go pick off like the best people because... there's not seven companies that are all crushing it." @14:33 ([[talent-density]])
- Owner demand mix: "Paid is still a huge part of our overall pipeline mix", driven by an "awesome head of growth". Organic has run "for like a year" with a CEO who is great on camera ("we de facto own that position"). Plus healthy outbound and a new partner motion with US Foods ("the second biggest food distributor in the U S"), run by a hire who has built it twice. "We don't need that pipeline now"; it's redundancy for 2025. @26:49–28:53 ([[smb-sales-motion]], [[owner-com]])
- Planning: he plans with confidence intervals (e.g., 70% confident paid scales another 50%) and takes the expected value to build redundancy into the plan. @29:56 ([[capacity-planning]])
- In SMB the gate to scale is meetings, not talent: "the really hard puzzle to solve is how do you generate the demand economically." Manage an enterprise rep with SMB spiffs and weekly coaching and they'll "flip you the bird." @34:06–36:10 ([[smb-sales-motion]], [[outbound-at-scale]])
- Owner is "a fully distributed team and it's working great." @36:10 ([[office-vs-remote]])
- Recognition: after a Q1 Cancun awards trip he made glass plaques with chips for each milestone, "a physical manifestation of people's success". He wanted a Slack "trophy case" but "It's not possible in slack." @47:33–48:34 ([[recognition-and-motivation]])
- Leadership development: "I hate the team lead role" (conflict of interest). Let people experience managing first. Give top ICs as much access and voice as managers so not everyone wants to manage. "CS is a much better craft to come from to be a manager." He tells managers "do not spin this" about quota increases or territory cuts. @58:57–62:04 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[org-design]])
- His six-pillar leadership framework: "can you sell, can you hire, can you coach, can you build, can you operate, can you engage". It is being built into a leadership academy with 7–15 lessons per pillar. With two layers now between him and managers, "consistency of system is, is critical." @63:06–64:08 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Forecasting: "we don't run a forecast meeting at all." A bottoms-up model got him within 2.5% after being ~30% to number mid-month. He watches it daily and paces daily opportunity targets. @77:33–78:35 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- A former Levelset manager on his team (Davenport, "B-bot") brings the "how lucky are we" energy. @85:41

## Guest's core ideas
- The #1 founder mistake: hiring good reps and leaving them to figure it out. "If you know what success looks like in sales, then you have a responsibility to write it down." Leaders must "raise the floor of performance." @07:18–10:23 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- More productivity per rep is now possible; he regrets throwing too much headcount at the number. @12:29–14:33 ([[capacity-planning]])
- "Mint" sales talent from Uber drivers, bartenders and door-to-door sellers. SMB can put people on a path to "three or $400,000 a year within five years." @16:34–18:37 ([[sales-hiring]], [[talent-density]])
- Levelset moved from lumpy enterprise to SMB. Messaging, value prop, demo and stakeholders are "night and day" between segments, and running both at once was "trying to fly two planes at once." @19:38–23:45 ([[smb-sales-motion]])
- SMB demand comes from organic or paid inbound plus outbound; paid has diminishing returns. Unit-economics math: a $75K-OTE rep needs ~$350–400K bookings, about seven $5K deals a month. Channel partners never exceeded 10% of bookings. @24:47–33:02 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- SMB should be in-office ("call me a dinosaur"). "Micro coach" to the playbook: can't do it → teach; won't do it → "go be successful somewhere else." A successful SMB rep's tenure in seat is ~1.5–2.5 years. @36:10–41:21 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[office-vs-remote]])
- Celebrate "bright spots" (from Chip Heath): bat ceremony at $100K bookings, record wall every $500K, monthly org-wide email with a published stack rank, and handwritten cards to everyone who hit quota. @43:24–53:46 ([[recognition-and-motivation]])
- Frontline managers are "the single most important role." Build them with career pathing, HBR's *For New Managers*, and by finding "the helpers." Career pathing raised retention and shortened ramp. @55:49–65:12 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- Operating rhythm: weekly rep-owned 30-min 1:1, 15–20-min weekly team meeting, weekly forecast meeting, call camps (required for underperformers). Leads must come from the company, not reps Googling. Use a hand-updated monthly one-page funnel; productivity per rep is "the number one metric." @67:18–77:33 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
