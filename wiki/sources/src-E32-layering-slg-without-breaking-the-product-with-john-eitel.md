# E32: Layering SLG Without Breaking the Product with John Eitel
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** John Eitel (transcript: "Itel"), ex-CRO @ Demandbase (previously Canva, WP Engine, Rackspace) · **Published:** 2025-05-08 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E32-layering-slg-without-breaking-the-product-with-john-eitel/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Add sales on top of PLG only once you see *pull*, meaning enterprise logos already self-adopting (Patagonia at WP Engine, about 200 big-company teams at Canva). Interview them, then package the table stakes (security, SSO, SLAs, scale, a "red phone" support line, brand controls).
- Build sequence: AEs, then a manager, then CSMs, then enablement at about 15–20 reps. Start in mid-market and hire for stage fit. Firewall self-serve from sales-led with company-size lines (e.g., no enterprise sales under 300 employees at Canva) to avoid cannibalization and cost blowout.
- On careers, both agree the CRO market is a "flight to quality". Kyle's advice is to optimize for company quality over title.

## Guest's core ideas
- Trigger: watch for organic enterprise adoption, then interview those buyers with the CPO and CTO. "you gota you got to get the early adoption first". Too-early SLG (copying others) fails. @06:07–11:12 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Enterprise packaging: compliance, SSO, scale and fault tolerance, DDoS protection, logs, and a dedicated support line that later became a differentiator. Keep terms simple for low-ASP land-and-expand. @13:12–19:20
- Pricing "maybe a month in the lab", anchored bottoms-up (cost) with an aspirational stretch. Don't start too low, because you get stuck with it. Doing it internally is faster, consultants future-proof. @20:22–23:24
- Hiring: bring in experienced bag-carriers (internal transfers struggled) and screen hard for stage fit. A big-company rep "freaks out in like the first week" without a manual. @27:28–30:29 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Sequence: 4–6 AEs, a leader, CSMs at roughly 1:1 with AMs, and an enablement leader at 15–20 reps. Start lower mid-market, then upper mid-market, then enterprise. @37:35–42:41 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[org-design]])
- Investment signals are thin early, so build a culture of experimentation (a rep on nights and weekends testing a package for 90 days), then model payback and LTV:CAC once behavior repeats. @42:41–47:46 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Canva's buyer was the CMO, not the doers. That led to brand controls ("freedom within a framework") and an org structure. "Canva coaches" (a blend of support rep and SDR) broke team-growth stall points; a team reaching 20 designs in the first month was the turning point. @32:31–35:34, @53:53–55:55
- CRO pressures: post-pandemic do-more-with-less, unrelenting board expectations, AI noise, bloated stacks and team burnout. The upside is CFO-level metric fluency and acting as bridge-builder with product and marketing. Company-picking: stage fit (his is about $80M), financials and burn, exec-team health, an AI point of view, and passion for the product. Be wary of "if we just had a great sales team". @64:05–79:17 ([[cro-role-and-board]])

## Numbers & facts
- WP Engine before SLG: about 150 employees and about $30M revenue. About 25–30 organically landed big customers. WordPress went from 7–8% of the internet to "upwards of 25 to 30%". @06:07, @08:10, @23:24
- Pricing jump: from "$29 plans" to "5,000 a month". @20:22
- About 10–15 sales hires in the first year. The sales team doubled every year for almost four years. @26:26–28:28
- Canva: grew from under 400 to over 3,000 people (Kyle's intro). About 200 big-company customers self-adopted. No enterprise sales under 300 employees. @01:00, @31:30, @50:51
- Kyle cites Sierra raising $275M. @69:08
