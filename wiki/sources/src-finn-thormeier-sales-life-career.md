# Finn Thormeier Podcast: Sales, Life, and Career Lessons from Owner.com CRO Kyle Norton

**Show / host:** Finn Thormeier Podcast / Finn Thormeier · **Published:** 2025-10-09 (YouTube upload date; metadata had none) · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/finn-thormeier-sales-life-career/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5bHtUnxW6lo

## TL;DR
- A personal, wide-ranging conversation: martial arts as the root of Kyle's practice-based sales training, health discipline (1,000 consecutive workout days), and a blunt account of how kids cut working hours.
- Lessons from CEO Adam Guild: founder intensity, closeness to the customer, and a contrarian early bet on organic brand (~$1M spent at ~$10M ARR) that became a moat.
- What separates elite CROs (think beyond sales, be bar raisers, own the board/founder relationship), why Kyle builds a personal brand, and Owner's "7+ production" AI use cases built on data foundations.

## What Kyle argued
- Owner really started in 2020: Adam got the earlier business over $1M ARR (2018–2020), COVID took it to zero, he rebuilt from March 2020 and was back over $1M ARR within a year @01:01–02:03. [[owner-com]]
- Martial arts taught acceptance of failure and disciplined practice; "a lot of our sales training is built around the principles of practice in sport" @02:03–03:04. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]] [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Physical fitness is part of being "a corporate athlete"; daily workouts and 8 hours of sleep are why he can sustain a high tempo without burnout @06:06–07:09. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Building a "generational company" requires founder-level intensity; work-life-balance founders won't get there @09:10. [[culture-and-high-standards]]
- Adam's long-term orientation and customer intuition justified a contrarian early investment in organic brand (YouTube/Instagram/Facebook, where restaurant owners live, not LinkedIn); organic is now a compounding moat @12:19–15:26. [[marketing-alignment]]
- Elite CROs think holistically beyond sales: help product get closer to the customer, partner with CS, even trade new ARR for retention @25:45–27:47. [[cro-role-and-board]]
- Elite CROs are "bar raisers" who create positive friction that pulls everyone up, versus zero-sum CROs who deliver the number but are "a pain in the ass" @27:47–28:49. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[culture-and-high-standards]]
- CROs must stop blaming founders/VCs, learn to manage the board and collaborate with the founder; reframe "the number is too high" as an enterprise-value trade-off @29:50–31:53. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[fundraising-and-investability]]
- The revenue leader is arguably the most influential person on culture after the CEO because they run the biggest team @32:54. [[culture-and-high-standards]] [[org-design]]
- Personal brand began as talent attraction for reps, now aimed at leadership topics; brand makes "everything easier" (university hiring, partners, fundraising, deal flow) @34:55–37:59. [[sales-hiring]] [[career-and-leadership]]
- AI: start with third-party data foundations, then instrument first-party data (customer-journey transcripts → structured fields); only then layer AI use cases, because "AI is a pattern recognition machine" @46:14–47:15. [[ai-in-gtm]] [[ai-sales-stack]] [[gtm-engineering]]
- Pick early-stage vendors like a VC: back the founders with the most aligned worldview, not today's best tech @50:21. [[build-vs-buy-software]] [[ai-sales-stack]]
- Lemkin lessons: "run towards bad news" with your board, and "clarity is kindness" @51:24–52:28. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[person-jason-lemkin]]

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Career checkpoints** (video-game metaphor): reach a career level you can always get back to (e.g., "functional CRO for a $20 million ARR company") before having kids @22:41.
- **Seasons of life**: embrace the kids-under-10 season @20:37.
- **Bar raiser CRO** vs. zero-sum CRO @27:47–28:49.
- **Podcast guest intake** borrowed from Harry Stebbings: ask for three close colleagues and email them for superpowers/stories @25:45–26:46.
- **AI data foundations**: third-party data (market → accounts → people → contact data) then first-party data (instrumented customer journey) @46:14–47:15.
- **VC-style vendor picking**: worldview alignment + founders' ability to win the category @50:21.
- Revenue Leadership Podcast as "Lenny's podcast for revenue people" @39:01.

## Stories / anecdotes
- Ran a beginner-friendly MMA gym in downtown Toronto (dentists, lawyers, tech people); two knee surgeries ended it @02:03–05:05.
- Adam out-working Kyle was a self-esteem shock; a Thursday idea came back Sunday night as a 17-page document @08:10–10:15.
- Joined Owner after investing via GTM Fund and advising Adam; feared watching "some crazy update on LinkedIn every year" from the great company he missed @10:15.
- Adam built Owner's origin for his mom's failing dog-grooming business @11:17.
- As a young VP of sales at a company without PMF, he blamed others; "2025 Kyle" would have handled it differently @30:51–31:53.

- AI tools named (as transcribed): Datalane (third-party data), Momentum (replaced Gong; first customer on its CI platform), an AI sales simulator ("Avara"/"Amara" in transcript), OneMind (AI Adam education avatar + website grader), Pavlov (post-sale incentive mgmt); VP of BizOps Jonathan Shankman built early waterfall enrichment before Clay @46:14–50:21.
