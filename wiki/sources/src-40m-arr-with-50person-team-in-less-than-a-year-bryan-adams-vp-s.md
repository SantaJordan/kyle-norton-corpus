# E54: $40M ARR with 50‑Person Team (in less than a year) - Bryan Adams, VP Sales at bolt.new
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Bryan Adams, VP Sales @ bolt.new (ex-TaxFyle, JFrog, Sedai) · **Published:** 2025-11-19 (RSS; metadata has no date) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/40m-arr-with-50person-team-in-less-than-a-year-bryan-adams-vp-s/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Bolt went from $0 to $4M ARR in its first 30 days and passed $40M about a year later with a 50-person company. GTM ran "off of a Google sheet" when Adams joined, then moved to HubSpot, Apollo/Clay, Freckle (unmasking), an AI support tool and Sybill.
- For Adams, build vs buy "comes down to speed". Buy when it's much faster or lets others run faster. Build the nice-to-haves.
- Vibe-coding is a "knife fight": low switching costs, multi-tool buyers and churn push Bolt toward white-glove onboarding and CS. His leadership model is to clear the runway, "only hire adults", and fire fast.

## Guest's core ideas
- Vibe-coding craft: use Plan mode, iterate in conversation, then layer features one at a time (ChatGPT can help sequence them). Adams built Bolt's live pricing page himself, and a new rep built a feature-upvote tool in his first two weeks. @04:06–09:10 ([[personal-ai-stack]])
- Build vs buy is about speed and scale: how fast it can be implemented and whether it empowers others. @11:11–12:13 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Stack: HubSpot (kept despite his Salesforce background), Apollo and [[clay]] for enrichment, heavy LinkedIn, and Freckle to unmask personal-email signups (about 15% more identified). It showed that only 20% of users were truly PMs, not the self-reported 50%. An AI support tool handles about 80% of inbound support. @13:14–16:17, @27:28 ([[ai-sales-stack]], [[ai-in-gtm]])
- Motion: PLG inbound with enrichment-based routing. Fortune 500 hand-raisers get a call immediately, and 25% of meetings are still phone calls. Outbound to ICP started 2–3 months earlier, plus LinkedIn DMs (surprisingly effective). The best early fit is B2C-facing enterprises that must keep pace with consumers. @15:16–24:23 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- Category churn: buyers run multiple tools and avoid annual commitments. The answer is a diligent POC, white-glove onboarding ("get your team of 100 PMs in the same room") and CS focus. @30:32–35:36 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Lean team: what if a rep's $2M number could be $5M? Automate call-recording to CRM to follow-up email (Sybill). @40:40–43:44 ([[ai-sales-stack]], [[talent-density]])
- Leadership: "clearing the runway". Weekly team meeting as a "locker room" where "adults policing themselves". "I only hire adults." Fire on fit and behavior, not only revenue. @44:44–51:51 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])

## Numbers & facts
- Bolt went from $0 to $4M ARR in its first 30 days and is "north of 40" about a year later. The whole company is 50 people. @00:00, @28:28–29:31
- Adams had been full-time for 9–10 months, after advising first. @29:31
- Signups run to tens of thousands a month, and 98–99% use personal emails. Freckle uncovers about 15% more. @13:14–14:15
- 25% of meetings are phone calls. The AI support tool handles about 80% of inbound support. @15:16–16:17
- Of self-reported PMs (50% of users), only 20% verified as PMs. @27:28
- Deals were initially bought in tranches ("we'll take 250 licenses"). Now buyers mostly sign 12-month agreements starting at 25–50 users, and some six-month deals are paid as full commission. @37:37–38:37
- Rep capacity framing: a $1–2M traditional quota vs a "what if they can do five million" aspiration. @41:42
