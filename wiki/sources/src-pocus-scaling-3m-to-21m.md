# Pocus Unlocking Revenue: Secrets for scaling $3M to $21M

**Show / host:** Pocus "Unlocking Revenue" (introduced on air as the "Go-To-Market 10x podcast") / Pocus host (not named) · **Published:** 2025-07-03 (podcast feed; content suggests recorded spring 2025: "almost three years" at Owner, ARR "well into the 30s") · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/pocus-scaling-3m-to-21m/transcript.md` · **Audio:** podcast feed (anchor.fm), no YouTube

## TL;DR
- First question for any founder or sales leader: "what game are you playing?" Owner plays the venture-backed, winner-take-most game in a big consolidating market, which is why he hired RevOps (contract → full-time) and enablement and made big data bets at ~$4M ARR.
- Regrets are about speed: with hindsight he'd have been more aggressive (especially demand gen) and decided faster on two-way doors—but would also have built change-management communication (a change-management model transcribed as "graphic light model", likely traffic light) earlier, because "smooth is fast".
- Prevent churn upstream: a far narrower ICP plus "point of view positioning" (radically transparent selling) so wrong-fit buyers opt out in 15 minutes; behave like a "buying Sherpa" to avoid reactance.

## What Kyle argued
- "What game are you playing?" (venture growth vs. "boot scaling" vs. one-and-done) decides answers to when to hire a VP Sales, add channels, or a second product @02:07–04:09. [[fundraising-and-investability]]
- Owner plays a winner-takes-all game in a big, competitive market, so early RevOps/enablement/data investments made sense—while unit economics improved monthly @04:09–05:11. [[revops]] [[fundraising-and-investability]]
- In hindsight he'd have been "even more aggressive" (2023 felt frozen—SVB), especially in demand gen @06:14–07:17. [[marketing-alignment]]
- Gut–data–gut (from an episode guest): pattern recognition sandwiches limited early data; most month-long delays could have been faster calls @08:23–09:28. [[career-and-leadership]]
- Reversible vs. irreversible: on two-way doors, "ship and iterate"; a little whiplash beats going too slow @09:28–10:31. [[operating-rhythm]]
- Twice-yearly enablement surveys told him reps couldn't keep up with change; he built a change-management model and required documentation before shipping changes—slower to go faster @10:31–11:32. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]] [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- Knowing there was an early-churn problem, he spent the first 60 days on deal quality (fit, expectations, handoff), accepting two down months @12:36–14:45. [[churn-and-retention]] [[person-adam-guild]]
- Advice: think about customer quality and expectations from the start rather than discovering churn at $5M ARR (caveat: month-to-month SMB) @15:52–16:54. [[churn-and-retention]]
- Get far narrower on ICP than feels comfortable; don't chase interesting out-of-ICP deals—they close poorly, churn, and pull product off course @16:54–17:59. [[icp-and-saying-no]]
- Point-of-view positioning / "radically transparent selling": Owner's belief is that owners are better off giving up choice to opt into a proven system; refer wrong-fit buyers to BentoBox or cheaper tools @17:59–21:03. [[point-of-view-positioning]]
- Lead with your point of view and one 10x feature (Lemkin), not product superiority—"I'm not saying my product is better because of this"; "Nobody believes that" @21:03–23:11. [[point-of-view-positioning]]
- Disqualify honestly with the math ($500/month vs. projected order volume) and name the better-fit tool; prospects then want you more @24:12–26:18. [[icp-and-saying-no]] [[smb-sales-motion]]
- Pushing triggers reactance; act like a "buying Sherpa" and let customers opt in—"When you say it, it's sold. When they say it, it's gold" @26:18–27:24. [[sales-methodology]]
