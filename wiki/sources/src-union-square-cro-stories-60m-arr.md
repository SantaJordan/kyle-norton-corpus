# Union Square Consulting (CRO Stories / Go-To-Market Science): The Investments and Priorities That Took Owner.com to $60M ARR

**Show / host:** Go-To-Market Science, "CRO Stories" segment (Union Square Consulting) / host Rachael Buechert · **Published:** 2026-04-20 · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/union-square-cro-stories-60m-arr/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=naK3Rb2H5v8

*Dating caveat: Kyle says Owner is "over 60" ($M ARR) here, while the Mar 24, 2026 Crew episode has him *almost at 100* This episode was probably recorded well before its April 2026 publish date. Treat the $60M as a point-in-time figure, not April 2026.*

## TL;DR
- AI call scoring (Replays engagement, ~150 skills analyzed) found that 10 of 12 win-correlated "critical skills" were **end-of-call**: pricing, value stack, setup/tie-down, next steps, asking for the business. It was not discovery. A focused 10-week, one-skill-at-a-time rollout moved win rate **38% → 52%**.
- Adoption is a learning-science problem. Use the **4i method** (introduce, isolate, integrate, inspect; adapted from Matt Thornton's 3i), spaced repetition against the forgetting curve, Bloom's taxonomy, social learning, and a **traffic-light** model of change management.
- The growth "secret sauce": early, oversized investments in RevOps, enablement, and tooling as compounding assets; extreme talent density; and mostly out-of-the-box Salesforce. Prioritize with the **5P / expected-value** model.

## What Kyle argued
- The call-scoring project grew from curiosity, not a crisis: "I consider myself to be a data-driven CRO." The funnel is deconstructed monthly @03:07. Other AI coaching tools lacked quality @03:07–04:08. [[metrics-and-forecasting]] [[ai-in-gtm]]
- The data contradicted the "it's discovery" orthodoxy. In a transactional SMB sale, "excitement is everything" and the purchase is emotion-driven @04:08–07:13. [[smb-sales-motion]]
- Change one thing at a time, with depth over breadth. Three modules over 10 weeks (value stack/pricing, then setup and tie-down, then next steps), with daily scoring and a SPIFF. The scripting is "almost identical today," about two years later @08:15–09:17. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Why adoption usually fails: one-and-done training with no coaching, deep practice, repetition, "progressive resistance," social learning, or reinforcement @10:21. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Forgetting curve plus spaced repetition. The enablement design is pre-work → small-group live session (social accountability) → live lab/workshop (create something, per Bloom's taxonomy) → manager reinforcement in a weekly coaching meeting separate from 1:1s → expert feedback/inspection @11:26–17:35. Current example: a ~12-week trust and crucial-conversations course built on Brené Brown's BRAVING @14:28–15:28. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]] [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- Traffic-light change management. Green-light changes go out by Slack message. Red-light (mission-critical) changes need many channels: Slack, video, Guru card, WorkRamp, team meetings, town hall @18:35–19:36. [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- 4i came from martial arts. Kyle co-owned a martial arts academy, and Matt Thornton of Straight Blast Gym had a "three I methodology"; Kyle added inspect. Reading list: *The Talent Code* (Coyle) and Malcolm Knowles on adult learning @20:36–24:48. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- "At the end of the day, our entire job is behavior change." The alternative is "paying tippy top market for tenured reps" @24:48–25:52. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- The job changes with scale. At $2.5M ARR, the SVP/CRO job "was actually sales manager." Now most of his work has "a multi-year payoff": the move to hire every net-new BDR and inside rep in Toronto, and a 12-month pricing project with a consultant @26:52–29:59. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[career-and-leadership]]
- Prioritization is the "zillion dollar question" ("way more good ideas than we have bandwidth," per Dan Ross). He uses the 5P approach from Annie Duke: priorities and possibilities, payoff, probability (expected value), and perspiration, including *whose* perspiration. Above $25–50M ARR, the cross-team roadmap-alignment "tax is very high" @29:59–37:13. [[org-design]]
- RevOps early: bring in a trusted full-time head as soon as you can (Steve, ~10 years across three companies, started on contract for 4–6 months). Otherwise vet consultants hard through personal recommendations. In advisory work he connects companies to RevOps consultants at "one, two million ARR" @37:13–40:27. [[revops]]
- Keep the CRM mostly out-of-the-box until ~$10M ARR. Custom objects and Apex code create cost and break integrations @41:30–43:32. [[revops]]
- Endorses Jason Lemkin's article on AI in SaaStr's GTM stack: "you can't scale something that's broken." The cold-start work falls to the founder or revenue leader, because "If Jason freaking Lemkin can be tuning AI BDRs... you... could be doing that as well" @44:33–45:35. [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]] [[person-jason-lemkin]]
- Growth drivers: playing for a "grand slam" or "generational company" outcome, not a PE exit. Enablement was "twice or three times bigger" than peers'. Headcount was diverted from reps into RevOps and enablement @47:39–48:42. "crazy talent density" @50:46. [[talent-density]] [[culture-and-high-standards]]
- Martial arts lessons: learn to lose, take feedback, build mental toughness. He likes hiring marathon runners, and says do something hard outside work, "maybe not pickleball" @51:47–54:51. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]] [[sales-hiring]]
