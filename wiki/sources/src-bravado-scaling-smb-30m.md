# Bravado: Scaling SMB to $30M, Investing in RevOps Early, and Stage-appropriate Sales Hiring

**Show / host:** Bravado podcast / Bravado's founder-CEO (not named in transcript; Sahil Mansuri per Kyle's own episode with him, [[src-E12-bravados-ceo-sahil-mansuri-on-the-future-of-sales-reps-and]]; ex-Meltwater and Glassdoor rep) · **Published:** 2025-02-05 (recorded January 2025: host says "we're now in january of 25") · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/bravado-scaling-smb-30m/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pNnUxp8Nufk

Note: the host tells long first-person stories (Meltwater, Glassdoor equity, Bob Hohman) @08:25–25:21; those are not Kyle's.

## TL;DR
- Career is company selection: "number two person at a number one company" beats the VP title at a weak one; diligence a startup harder than a VC does (team ~70% of the weight; his four-trait founder model).
- Hiring an early sales leader: jobs-to-be-done → competencies → values/DNA → history of excellence (from *Who*); at $1–3M ARR you're hiring a 24-month "bridge", and getting layered later is a success. SMB leaders must be systems builders who love coaching.
- Owner (~$30M ARR, ~60 in sales) put RevOps and enablement on contract by ~$3M ARR and full-time by $4–5M, and just opened a Toronto hub where all new SDRs/BDRs work five days in office—a "bold bet" against complacency.

## What Kyle argued
- Martial arts (BJJ, 3 hrs/day for ~8 years) taught him resilience and "how to learn"; much of how he teaches sales comes from that feedback loop @01:02–03:07. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Early career: optimize for leadership and a learning environment; later, "hit your wagon to a winner"—no sales heroics fix out-of-control churn @06:17–07:20. [[career-and-leadership]]
- Be the number two at a number one company; a VP at a crappy company is "a glorified manager" @07:20–08:25. [[career-and-leadership]]
- Look beneath superficial résumé signals (big brand or not) for DNA: persuasion, grit, initiative @11:37–13:42. [[sales-hiring]]
- Joining a company = VC-style diligence on team/product/market, ~70% team (really founders); his founder model: force of nature, learning machine, founder-market fit, humility @16:55–20:07. [[fundraising-and-investability]] [[person-adam-guild]]
- Don't reason by analogy from investor logos; do your own product diligence (churn, NRR, loss reasons) and walk the growth model line by line @25:21–28:36. [[career-and-leadership]]
- First sales-leader framework: jobs to be done, competencies/skills, values/DNA, validated history of excellence (from *Who*) @29:37–31:40. [[hiring-scorecards]]
- At $1–3M ARR hire a stage-appropriate VP Sales (a bridge for ~24 months), not a true CRO; being layered should count as success @33:43–36:01. [[sales-hiring]] [[org-design]]
- SMB leaders need systems/data orientation plus a love of coaching, or growth stalls once the leader is "more than an arm's length away from every rep" @38:06–40:17. [[smb-sales-motion]] [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- RevOps/enablement are a chicken-and-egg spend: commit once you have evidence of go-to-market fit (Roberge's "science of scaling"); start on contract and vet contractors hard @42:22–47:46. [[revops]] [[capacity-planning]]
- Early reps: "pirates and romantics" (Lemkin) and "renaissance reps"; at Owner he first paid top-of-market for proven performers, now hires earlier-career people and promotes from within @49:50–51:57. [[sales-hiring]] [[talent-density]]
- Interview by digging into "the how" of past numbers; at early stage make candidates demo *your* product and unpack their prep—resourcefulness is the point @53:01–57:13. [[hiring-scorecards]]
- Toronto hub: all new SDR/BDRs five days in office; existing remote reps stay remote; reps eventually graduate to hybrid/remote. Rationale: ramp, productivity, retention/community, backed by peers' A/B data @58:13–62:22. [[office-vs-remote]]
- "If it ain't broke": complacency is "the number one enemy of success"; every great company has "a brush with death" @63:22–66:34. [[culture-and-high-standards]]
- Set your own number above finance's (the "Kyle number"); hard work means 50–70-hour weeks @69:50–75:09. [[recognition-and-motivation]] [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
