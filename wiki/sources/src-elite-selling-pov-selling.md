# Elite Selling Podcast: PoV selling with Kyle Norton

**Show / host:** Elite Selling Podcast / Griffin (co-host Frankie absent this episode) · **Published:** 2025-01-24 · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/elite-selling-pov-selling/transcript.md` · **Audio:** https://anchor.fm/s/a2f07060/podcast/play/97491509/

Transcript note: Whisper renders the host's employer Sprinklr as "sprinkler". The host previously worked at People.ai; the People.ai-vs-SalesLoft/Outreach example @30:22–33:30 is the host's description, which Kyle then frames.

## TL;DR
- Most positioning fails because it's "me, me, me" feature claims buyers can't verify, so they default to the status quo (*JOLT Effect*). Point-of-view positioning instead sells a contrarian belief about how the market should work that leads naturally to your product.
- Origin: League (benefits/healthcare OS) late-funnel losses with no "philosophical alignment"; refined at Shopify POS (2019) where the product was deliberately feature-poor on reporting/inventory. Result: bad-fit buyers opt out fast, fits "leap across the table", and you escape price comparison ("Don't give your customers a comparison, give them a choice").
- Elite seller (his evolved definition): serves self, customer and company — smokes the number with high-quality, right-fit deals; the input is being "a learning machine".

## What Kyle argued
- Vendor positioning is all about "us"; buyers can't untangle competing "we're the best" claims, lack trust in sellers, and revert to the status quo out of fear of messing up (*The JOLT Effect*) @01:01–03:09. [[point-of-view-positioning]]
- He's mostly sold challenger products; PoV positioning was born as VP sales at League, where close-lost interviews showed many deep-funnel deals "were never truly on board with this vision" @03:09–06:19. [[point-of-view-positioning]] [[icp-and-saying-no]]
- "there's no better or worse product necessarily. It's really all about fit" — replace "we're the best" with a core belief about how the market should run @07:23–08:25. [[point-of-view-positioning]]
- League PoV: employees don't understand/use benefits so don't value them; fixing the experience lowers cost and raises perceived value — HR buyers who didn't believe healthcare experience was "broken" never closed @08:25–10:29.
- Shopify POS (first 18 months): if POS is an operational/reporting tool, Shopify isn't the answer; if POS should be a revenue driver tied to e-com and clienteling, it is @10:29–11:33. [[shopify]]
- Outcomes: shorter "time to lose" for bad fits; fits jump in; radical transparency on strengths and weaknesses builds trust and speeds deals @12:34–13:38. [[sales-methodology]]
- Building a PoV is founder-down (the founder's founding belief) or product-out (product team's trade-offs — Shopify sacrificed inventory, permissions, reporting in 2019); align with product marketing so top-of-funnel carries it @14:41–15:43. [[marketing-alignment]]
- Practical: build one or two slides into the first deck, use a customer-voice Mad Libs ("customers that choose to work with us believe…"), ask "how do you react to that?"; a "lead balloon" means the PoV isn't contrarian enough — iterate until people react strongly in 5–10 tries @16:46–17:48. [[point-of-view-positioning]] [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- Core message stays, flexed by persona — over-specializing per stakeholder kills common ground (*The Challenger Customer*) @18:50–20:55. [[sales-methodology]]
- Always speak through the customer's voice — "no one gives a shit what we think" as salespeople @23:00. [[point-of-view-positioning]]
- It's not passive: actively sell the worldview — "do not pass go" until alignment; nurture with third-party (not vendor) content @24:03–25:06. [[point-of-view-positioning]]
- Once the worldview is set, you shape the decision criteria (MEDDIC); feature/checkbox battles get you "ground way down" @27:15–29:21. [[sales-methodology]]
- "Don't give your customers a comparison, give them a choice" (Mike Maples, *Pattern Breakers*): architect a choice between ways of doing business so price comparisons become irrelevant; commitment bias and endowment effect then protect ACV and discounting — but it "has to be legit" @29:21–36:38. [[point-of-view-positioning]]
- Elite seller now = serves themselves, the customer and the business; reps who close deals that churn are "not that beneficial"; craft = radical transparency, professionalism, business acumen, curiosity, drive, grit, creativity; input is being a learning machine @39:43–42:49. [[churn-and-retention]] [[career-and-leadership]] [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Revenue Leadership Podcast is "a selfish endeavor" to pick a top operator's brain ~90 minutes/week, strictly for revenue leaders @38:40–39:43. [[revenue-leadership-podcast]]
