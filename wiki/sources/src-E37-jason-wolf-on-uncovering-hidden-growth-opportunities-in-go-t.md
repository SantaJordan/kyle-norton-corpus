# E37: Jason Wolf on Uncovering Hidden Growth Opportunities in Go-To-Market
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Jason Wolf, President @ FullStory (transcript: "Fulltory"); ex-CRO Ping Identity; ex-global CRO SAP Ariba · **Published:** 2025-06-18 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E37-jason-wolf-on-uncovering-hidden-growth-opportunities-in-go-t/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Step-change growth comes from new markets (geographies, personas) or new capabilities. Find it by following business processes upstream and downstream of your core (Ariba: procurement → sourcing → spend intelligence → payments/treasury), not by copying competitors.
- New geos: start simple and partner-led, focus on one industry, land 3–5 local reference customers and go hyper-local. Look international once you have fit and scale (roughly 20–50M ARR). Send a GM-style pioneer plus pre-sales, implementation and support.
- The "new algorithm": celebrate the renewal, not the deal. Sell a "clear solution based on clear value organized around a clear plan" with named customer owners, and validate new products through a specialist "say, show, prove" team and large early-access cohorts.

## What Kyle said

- He quotes Lemkin (transcript: "J Lin") on going multi-product: "go too early and it's a distraction. Go too late and growth stalls." He applies it to "multi-reion, multi- anything". @30:36 ([[person-jason-lemkin]])

Not every GTM leader should weigh in on the roadmap, because it "takes a lot of humility" to admit you're not "a full stack thinker". @35:41, @56:05–57:07 ([[marketing-alignment]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Growth = new markets or new capabilities. Map the office (CIO/CHRO/CPO and their lieutenants) to find personas. Weigh "stated truth versus our observed truths" (customer pull vs hunch). @04:03–07:08
- Ariba's path: follow the process upstream (strategic sourcing, via acquisition) and downstream (payments in treasury). "we let the business process and our customer feedback lead us as much as our worry about our competitors." @10:13–13:15
- International: it's "not the United States of Europe". Go industry-specific, win 3–5 local references, go hyper-local, use communities (a FullStory gaming event with Google in London), choose a few partners over many, and have execs meet partners monthly. Regional teams can out-innovate home base ("lead east to west"). LATAM grew from almost nothing to Ariba's second-largest region. @14:16–29:33
- First crew in a new region: a GM-style "pioneer" who sees the whole lifecycle, plus pre-sales, implementation and local support. You enter better with a couple of solutions than with one. @25:28–33:38 ([[org-design]])
- Explore vs exploit (*Lead and Disrupt*, "ambidexterity"): a separate specialist SE team spends 50% of its time in the field battle-testing new products through "say, show, prove". Early access runs with 20–60 customers before launch. Each specialist must "name an enemy" to replace. @36:42–48:57
- The revenue algorithm moved from "can you sell it" to "can you renew it". Value must be 3–10x the cost, "If it's not, you're selling churn." Put sales, pre-sales, implementation and support on one leadership team. @57:07–61:13 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- A clear plan "is not a Gant chart": it names customer owners with pre-agreed time, in the customer's own language. It speeds the close and cuts time to implementation. @65:16–68:21
- Alignment is "chiefly a personal decision". Run cross-functional forums without formal lines and use neutral shared language ("level up", "assess and adapt") to defuse tension. @71:26–75:34 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Hire AEs who want to be experts in the space. Past club trips matter less than curiosity. @51:00–53:04 ([[sales-hiring]])
