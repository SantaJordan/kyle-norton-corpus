# E63: A Product-Informed Approach for Go-To-Market (Usha Iyer: Chief Customer & Growth Officer @ Hivebright)
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Usha Iyer, Chief Customer & Growth Officer @ Hivebright (ex-C++ developer, product leader @ Honeywell) · **Published:** 2026-02-11 (RSS; metadata has no date) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/a-product-informed-approach-for-gtm-usha-iyer-chief-customer-growt/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Bring a product mindset to GTM: first principles, deeper discovery (Hivebright uses SPICED instead of BANT/MEDDPICC), hypotheses instead of fixed decisions, and root causes instead of symptoms. In her words, "product is input driven and GTM is output driven".
- Experiment small, then scale. Pilot with a SWAT team, even if it runs manually in spreadsheets, before asking RevOps to build it. That requires a data layer that mirrors the customer journey.
- Kyle's Owner example: a 4-person BDR pilot with hourly AI iteration loops lifted per-rep output 85% before rollout. Leaders who launch AI tools org-wide and see them flop "lose credibility".

## Guest's core ideas
- What product sees is value. What it misses is friction, "like tax". Friction can reveal roadmap opportunities, as with Hivebright's QR-code event check-in. @08:07–12:10
- Bring product leaders problems, not solutions: the pain, its impact, and the opportunity cost. @12:10 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Product mindset in sales: first principles, deeper discovery, product expertise, and the confidence to say no instead of "yes your way through a deal". Hivebright moved from BANT/MEDDPICC to SPICED. @13:10–21:22 ([[sales-methodology]])
- Churn diagnosis: pool usage, CI, NPS, G2, support and subscription data. Segment repeatedly, then compare winners with look-alike failures (a "control set"). @21:22–29:30 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Discount pressure is often positioning or discovery, not price. Diagnose forecast changes systematically with data. @30:31–34:33 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Experiment loop: hypothesis, then a pilot with a few reps (manual is fine), then ask RevOps to operationalize. "bring your business model into a data structure" and mirror the customer journey in systems. @34:33–39:39 ([[revops]])
- Examples. Back-office checklist widget (GMO) to get onboarding tasks done. A contractor-built integration offering that now sells to over 30% of customers. A 3-person SWAT team for the Orbit acquisition (first $1M over 6 months). An L2 support team set up and dismantled within 3–4 months. @48:52–58:03
- Pricing and packaging is the place to experiment as SaaS moves toward credits and consumption. A great CRO is "data driven". Don't import outside frameworks wholesale. @42:43–44:46, @61:07
