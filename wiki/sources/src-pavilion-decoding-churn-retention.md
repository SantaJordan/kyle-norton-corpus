# Pavilion: Kyle Norton on Decoding Churn - Enhancing Retention in High-Growth Businesses

**Show / host:** Revenue Blindspots (Pavilion, powered by Otter.ai) / Sam Jacobs · **Published:** 2025-07-31 (YouTube upload date) · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/pavilion-decoding-churn-retention/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i65RC1YbW5c

Note: the opening ~1 minute is a teaser montage of later clips (@00:00). Kyle references Gemini 2.5, Claude 4, Codex and Google I/O (@24:02), so recording likely predates upload by some weeks.

## TL;DR
- Kyle's "blind spot": in 2024 churn was the only non-world-class metric at Owner, and the team lacked instrumentation, data and a business rhythm to find the "atomic units of churn."
- Fixes spanned the journey: a second ICP tightening (raising the GPV threshold using E-Win/E-Churn ML models), a "growth potential score" for onboarding configuration quality, and post-sale service metrics — and the data showed configuration quality mattered more than fast time-to-value.
- Everyone owns retention; a real revenue leader's job is enterprise value, not the closed-won number.

## What Kyle argued
- The core blind spot was not knowing what caused churn — no instrumentation, data, or rhythm to work the inputs (@04:08–@05:09). See [[churn-and-retention]].
- Fix churn "as far upstream as we could": separate what's causal from merely correlated (@05:09).
- ICP was tightened twice: in his first 30 days (2022) and again in 2024, by raising the GPV floor where churn break-points appeared — easier to enforce on outbound (@06:10–@08:19). See [[icp-and-saying-no]].
- A regression across product/configuration metrics produced a "growth potential score" so the launch team knows how well a customer is configured before go-live (@08:19–@10:24). See [[gtm-engineering]].
- Beware best practices: slowing onboarding to improve configuration quality raised retention; time-to-value was less correlated with retention than configuration quality (@10:24–@11:30).
- A legit revenue leader's job is enterprise value, which justifies short-term discomfort like closing fewer deals (@13:32–@14:40). See [[cro-role-and-board]].
- Everybody owns retention (CS technically owns the metric); sales/onboarding process churn is "my churn" (@15:43–@17:47).
- Analyze churn both by cohort (down to lead source — some channels retain much better) and case by case (@18:51–@19:53).
- Some value churn was customers not seeing value Owner did generate → a workstream to show value more clearly (@19:53).
- Non-dual thinking: urgency day-to-day plus a year-out level of abstraction; top-down/bottom-up triangulation (@19:53–@20:54). See [[executive-performance-and-mindset]].
- Keeping pace with AI is "an existential thing" for GTM leaders, and the live conversation happens on X (@22:58–@24:02). See [[ai-in-gtm]].

## Key quotes
- "churn was the only metric in the business that wasn't really world-class." (@04:08)
- "we spent the better part of 2024, and it's still a big focus for us today, getting down to the atomic unit of churn." (@05:09)
- "time to value was actually less correlated with retention than was the quality of the configuration." (@11:30)
- "if you're a legit revenue leader and not just like a dressed up sales manager with a VP of sales title, your job is enterprise value." (@14:40)

## Numbers & facts he cited
- Owner's customers: mom-and-pop restaurants, "one, two, three locations, owner operated" (@04:08).
- Churn was the 2024 focus; still a big focus at recording (@04:08–@05:09).
- ICP narrowed within his first 30 days in 2022; fewer deals closed in month 1 and fewer again in month 2 (@06:10–@07:13).
- ML models: expected payment volume (GPV), E-Win score, E-Churn ("estimated churn rate") score (@07:13).
- Customer trajectory usually readable "within a week or two of launch" (@10:24).
- Product-related churn "is like sub five percent" (@16:45).
- Monthly churn review, down to individual cases (@16:45).
- Awards (as he listed them): 2024 CRO to Watch (Pavilion), CRO of the Year nominee, GTM 10 award (Cello and Lightspeed), Winning by Design award for GTM AI innovation (@02:04–@03:07).
- Host bio: Kyle owns sales, partnerships, onboarding, demand gen, RevOps and enablement; LP in GTM Fund and Stage 2 Capital; Toronto Pavilion co-chapter head (@01:02–@02:04).

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Atomic units of churn** (@04:08–@05:09).
- **E-Win / E-Churn / GPV models** for ICP thresholds (@07:13).
- **Growth Potential (GP) score** — "like golf, the higher the score, the worse it is" (@08:19).
- **Preventable vs unpreventable churn** (process, customer-quality, product vs business closed) (@16:45).
- **Non-dual thinking** and **top-down/bottom-up triangulation** (@19:53–@20:54).
