# Bridge the Gap (Revenue Reimagined): Real World Secrets to Scaling Startups from Owner.com CRO
**Show / host:** Revenue Reimagined Podcast (published on the GTM Uncensored YouTube channel; folder name "Bridge the Gap"), co-hosted by Adam (ex-Toast) and a second host · **Published:** 2024-10-30 (YouTube upload date; metadata.json has no date) · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/bridge-the-gap-scaling-startups/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GtMkCWc9Npc

## TL;DR
- A senior leader is *additive*: they think about their team, the sales number and short-term results. An executive is a *multiplier*: they think about the company, enterprise value, long-term revenue quality and the P&L. Kyle learned this from Lemkin/SaaStr and an executive coach.
- To be taken seriously as a C-suite revenue leader, master three things beyond the sales funnel: churn (especially the share sales causes), efficiency and unit economics by channel, and holistic P&L fluency.
- His Revenue OS has three parts: a measurement cadence, a lightweight monthly planning rhythm, and team operating rhythms. These include "can't miss metrics" on the SLT agenda and weekly MAPLE written updates.

## What Kyle argued
- At Shopify he had pre-committed budget (about 80 headcount earmarked in year one), so he could hire ahead of stage (e.g. a former VP Sales as a first-line manager) and bring in enablement and RevOps early. A startup has to stage and gate those investments to avoid over-hiring relative to pipeline or product maturity. @05:06–07:07 ([[capacity-planning]], [[org-design]], [[shopify]])
- Owner now has "extreme product market fit" and consistent, predictable unit economics, so it can "hit the gas". @07:07 ([[capacity-planning]])
- You can only confidently add headcount once systems, structure and a track record exist (e.g. a 12-month history of BDR cohorts). Without them, "one person is ripping it" and "two people are doing nothing". @08:08–09:10 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[capacity-planning]])
- Fixing onboarding and activation was one of the most important early fixes because Owner sells month-to-month. "half or more of churn is probably customer quality." @09:10–10:13 ([[churn-and-retention]], [[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Senior leader versus executive: "An executive is a multiplier." The board and CEO tell him they value that he doesn't think only about the closed-won number. @11:14–13:21 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- Turn off the competitor when dealing with teammates. Prosecutor/preacher debating (Adam Grant) creates win-lose arguments you can't win against a CEO. Build win-win conversations instead. @15:22–16:25 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- The three metrics areas people miss: (1) deep churn understanding, including sales-driven churn, which you should own; (2) efficiency and unit economics (payback, CAC/LTV) by channel; (3) holistic P&L fluency. A deal that churns in 12 months is worse than no deal. @17:26–21:34 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[churn-and-retention]])
- Look at gross and net retention together ("triangulation"). NRR can hide 20% logo bleed. @22:37–23:42 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Thin the data cloud into action. A monthly business review (monthly because "S&P high velocity", i.e. SMB) picks the "biggest needle mover", and the SLT agenda keeps a standing "can't miss metrics" section. @23:42–24:42 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[revops]])
- When building a GTM motion for the first time, start with measurement, then add a light planning rhythm, then team operating rhythms. "If you can get that stuff in place, like the engine will work pretty well." @26:48–28:51 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[revops]])
- Name your rituals (MAPLE, can't miss metrics, "Kyle's top 10") to create a common language. @29:52 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Senior leader (additive) vs executive (multiplier):** team vs company, comp plan vs enterprise value, quarter vs long-term revenue quality, plus P&L understanding. He says he was writing a newsletter on this. @25:44
- **Three C-suite metric competencies:** churn (sales-driven share), efficiency and unit economics by channel, P&L fluency.
- **Gross + net retention triangulation** (NRR / NDR / GRR, logo churn).
- **Biggest needle mover** (from VP Sales Brett) and **can't miss metrics** (a standing SLT Notion section).
- **Revenue OS** (name credited to "Brandon Gailey" as transcribed): measurement cadence → planning rhythm → team rhythms. Templates are on his Substack. @24:42–28:51
- **MAPLE weekly update:** Metrics, Advanced, Planned, Learnings, Emergencies. Senior leaders submit it; Kyle reviews them Sunday nights and uses them to drive one-on-ones. @27:48
- **Hundred-year company / market as weighing machine** (Shopify philosophy he admired). @04:04–05:06
- **Prosecutor/preacher vs win-win** (Adam Grant).
