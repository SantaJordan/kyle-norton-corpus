# E30: Building AI-Powered Sales Teams with Finally's CRO Kevin "KD" Dorsey
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Kevin "KD" Dorsey, CRO @ Finally · **Published:** 2025-04-23 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E30-building-ai-powered-sales-teams-with-finallys-cro-kevin-kd/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- KD skips "soft" AI use cases (account research, enrichment) and starts with the most valuable surface: AI call scoring against existing manual scorecards. That feeds issue diagnosis, then AI coaching, then role play.
- Prerequisite: "capture the wiggle". Document what good looks like, ideally by talking it out with voice-to-AI, before you automate. Don't put AI on anything not already done manually.
- Kyle lays out Owner's AI program at the time: a Head of Applied AI hire, a "management intelligence layer" vision, build-vs-buy instincts, and an AI-built podcast-prep and newsletter workflow.

## Guest's core ideas
- Mental checkpoint: before any recurring task, ask "could AI do this? Or how could AI help me with this?" Meta-prompt AI for how it can help (e.g., a digest of 14 weekly 1:1s with sentiment). @04:02–06:05 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- Prioritize the highest-value surface, the conversations: "what I say on the call still matters." @13:14–15:15
- AI call scoring is built from manual scorecards. It scores 17–18 discovery behaviors 1–5 with reasoning and heat maps. Give it examples of what good is *and isn't*. It is rolling out from AEs to SDRs, onboarding and CS. @15:15–18:19 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Next step is AI issue diagnosis: drill from a metric (e.g., 17% close rate) through call scores, then segment and lead source, to the root cause ("What's the ID?"). @20:23–22:24 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Build vs buy: build when it's within your understanding, needs to connect to other steps, and is low risk (call scoring). Buy when it's out of scope (AI voice) or high risk (data interpretation). Re-scan vendors monthly. @35:46–39:52 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Fears for SaaS-to-SaaS: buyers like Kyle are building tools in-house. He predicts fewer $50–100M ARR companies and "thousands of three to 10s". @10:11–11:11, @41:56–42:57
- Dark days/weeks to document "the wiggle". If you can't step away for a day, you're the bottleneck. @27:28–29:32 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Parting exercise: audit next week's calendar with two questions, "Could AI do this?" and "Could AI help with this?" (echoing Tobi's memo). @74:40–75:42
