# E13: Scott Leese on Bridging the Gap Between Founders and VPs
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Scott Leese, fractional CRO / founder of a GTM consulting firm (Surf and Sales; transcript spells "Lease") · **Published:** 2024-12-25 (RSS pubDate) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E13-scott-leese-on-bridging-the-gap-between-founders-and-vps/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Founders should hire a stage-appropriate VP of sales who has done the same stage and deal cycle, use a scorecard shaped by someone who has held the role, and stop over-indexing on industry experience.
- VP candidates should vet the founder, the product (talk to customers), the financial model, and the market. They should also ask up front whether they'll be "topped off," and push for extended exercise windows and milestone-based vesting.
- Once in the seat, most founder–VP friction comes from the plan, measurement and communication. Proactive daily updates and yes-and answers (here is what I need) to aggressive targets defuse it.

## What Kyle said
- Framing: there are two camps on LinkedIn. Founders and VCs see VPs of sales "quite transactionally" (improve in 60 days or move on), and there's "a lot of truth and fairness to that, like this is capitalism after all." The VP camp is "woe is me... nothing is ever my fault." @10:11 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- He added deal-cycle fit to stage-appropriateness: "You can't take an enterprise seller and ask them to do high velocity." @18:20 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Scorecards counter the charisma "halo effect": "people just gravitate to communicators." @19:21–20:24 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- On taking the Owner job: "I personally talk to customers when I accepted the owner job, because I was like, this is too good to be true." He had "such a great setup at Shopify" and was "pulled out kicking and screaming." @27:33 ([[owner-com]], [[shopify]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- Equity: a 90-day exercise window can trap a successful early VP into staying for liquidity. Extended windows and milestone-based vesting are "not a giveaway... it is actually good for the company" because departing seniors stay ambassadors. @36:44–37:46, @43:56 ([[comp-plans]])
- Vet the financials: sit with the CFO, go through next year's model's key assumptions (pipeline, conversion, "butts in seats"), compare them to the trailing three months, and ask the CFO's honest probability. @44:57 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Market vetting flexes. In GTM Fund's portfolio, Owner is in the "unsexy mom and pop restaurant space" while Writer is in a hot space at "almost a $2 billion valuation." @50:06 ([[owner-com]], [[fundraising-and-investability]])
- First-time VPs ignore red flags (Upton Sinclair). "Dude, be patient"; at a crappy company "Don't go be a head of sales yet... Just go be the number two." Directors from Brex, Vanta or Rippling come out well; he cites Sam Blond's CRO alumni. @51:09–54:16 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- He sees the annual plan and "the thing you're inheriting" as the biggest breaking point between the camps. @56:20 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- His founder messages any time but has "never did the triple tap." "I personally don't mind the sense of urgency... it's my job not to get stressed out about it." As "a very, like, extreme ownership person," his job is to have the conversation. @65:31 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Revenue leaders should communicate with "low stakes, low anxiety cortisol levels" and have "big girl and big boy conversations"; this is the theme of his Substack piece on revenue leader vs executive. @66:32–67:33 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Citing Lemkin: once you see fear in a VP of sales "they're cooked." His fix is "yes and": "yeah, we can do this and here's what I need" (e.g., $10M marketing budget, +10% NRR from CS). @70:36, @73:42 ([[person-jason-lemkin]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Have someone who has done the role help hire the role; recruiters charge "a 40 or $50,000" fee. Don't hire a VP too soon. @12:14–13:14 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Avoid the ex-Oracle "VP of spreadsheets and strategy"; early stage needs a "ditch digger" whose "motor revs." @14:14–15:16 ([[sales-hiring]])
- "Industry experience to me is the easiest thing to learn." Example: Qualia, title-insurance software, now "worth a couple billion dollars." @16:17–17:18 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Vet the founder ("There's a difference between intensity and tyranny") and the goals: VPs often inherit a target set because "that's what I told the board that we're gonna do." @21:29–24:32 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Vet the product by talking to customers (he estimates fewer than 10% of candidates do), and prefer unsexy, underserved vertical markets with antiquated incumbents. @25:33–29:36, @45:58–49:04 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Ask openly whether you'll be "topped off"; he agreed to a 0→$25M, ~3-year tenure and hired his own replacement. Advocates milestone-based vesting and longer exercise periods; calls 30/90-day windows "predatory." @30:37–42:56 ([[comp-plans]])
- Proactive daily two-to-three-sentence recap to the founder; founders must not issue "900 different level one priorities." @58:26–62:28 ([[operating-rhythm]])
- A great CRO builds a coaching tree; he counts "almost two dozen" former reps now in VP/CRO roles. @55:18, @75:43 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
