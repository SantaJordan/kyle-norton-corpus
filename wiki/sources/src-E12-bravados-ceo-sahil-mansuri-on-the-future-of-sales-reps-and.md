# E12: Bravado's CEO Sahil Mansuri on the Future of Sales Reps and Sales Leadership
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Sahil Mansuri, founder & CEO @ Bravado (transcript spells "Sohail") · **Published:** 2024-12-18 (RSS pubDate) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E12-bravados-ceo-sahil-mansuri-on-the-future-of-sales-reps-and/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Bravado's quota-attainment data says "we are so back": reps hitting quota fell from 56% (Q1 2022) to 17% (Q3 2023) and recovered to 41% (Q3 2024), which the guest calls the normal 40–45% range.
- Venture-backed companies signed up for hypergrowth, not "profitable, efficient growth"; reps at stalled ex-unicorns should optimize for company quality and leadership, even if it means stepping down a segment.
- Kyle's counter-weight: the rep shortage is partly a leadership failure. He describes Owner's heavy enablement/RevOps ratios, ~50 internal promotions, and skepticism of AI SDRs as a non-durable "party trick."

## What Kyle said
- Intro: host, "CRO at Owner.com"; the show is "no fluff, no sales pitches and no platitudes." @02:01 ([[revenue-leadership-podcast]])
- On the Michelin-star vs quick-serve analogy, he pushed on scale: "There's not enough Michelin star reps out there at all of the different price points that people need to hire at." His own version: graduate people through the QSR experience, then give "more latitude to maybe color outside the lines." @09:23–12:34 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Market read: "I'm seeing so many more exciting deals today and companies that are growing really, really fast." @13:41 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- Long-term thinking, citing Shopify colleague Lynette Prestorius ("kindness is just about having a short time horizon"): managing through stress and micromanagement gets action today, engagement has a longer payoff; the same short-termism drives poor planning and neglect of CS and product. @48:45–50:49 ([[culture-and-high-standards]], [[shopify]])
- Comp: "Why are you putting a VP of sales on a 50-50 plan? They need to think six to 18 months in the future." @50:49 ([[comp-plans]])
- Enablement and RevOps are under-invested; a 40-rep competitor with one enablement person has "no consistency in execution." @56:07–57:13 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[revops]])
- Owner's change-management system (from his SaaStr talk): green/yellow/red light changes; a red-light comp change needs a Notion doc, an in-person training, then manager 1:1 Q&A. "It seems heavy handed... but I'm also getting 16 reps that are doing the work of 45 reps other places because our system is good." @59:19 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[saastr]])
- Talent development: around 50 promotions in 18 months (SDR → senior → AE, inbound → outbound → multi). @63:29–64:32 ([[sales-hiring]], [[talent-density]])
- AI SDRs: short-term; "There's no way you can spam your entire market sustainably through $50 million ARR"; there is a 6–12 month window before email providers close the door. Borrowing Vanta CRO Stevie Case: "it's a party trick. It's not durable," also used as a hiring screen. @64:32–65:33 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]], [[outbound-at-scale]], [[hiring-scorecards]])
- Remote: as a dad of a two-and-a-half and four-and-a-half year old, a daily office commute "would be hard." @70:56 ([[office-vs-remote]])
- Recognition: Owner uses plaques with insertable award pieces ("like in a video game, you earn badges"); remote wins need a "moment or a physical object." Reading *The Power of Moments*. @75:08–76:13 ([[recognition-and-motivation]])
- He likes the work: "I genuinely enjoy the puzzle and the people development." @78:20 ([[career-and-leadership]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Michelin-star vs QSR selling: top reps know the playbook and then improvise. Under ~$50k ASP (SMB/mid-market) you must build QSR; reserve Michelin treatment for the top 20–100 enterprise accounts (ABM). @07:16–12:34 ([[smb-sales-motion]])
- Bravado's "Portfolio" quota tracker gives a live read on attainment: average is 40–45% of reps and 45–50% of teams. @14:46–19:00 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- "Profitable, efficient growth" is misleading for early-stage VC-backed companies; you signed up to compress 20–30 years into 5–7, so slow growth means bootstrapping. @20:00–25:09 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- Everyone was complicit in ZIRP-era comp (VPs offered $400–500k OTE, mid-market reps $350–380k OTE). Use "extreme ownership and intellectual honesty." @28:17–33:40 ([[comp-plans]])
- "It's not your fault. Your company is just fucked." Optimize for company quality, product-market fit and leader; take the step down (enterprise → mid-market at Vanta). @41:08–45:28 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- The definition of a good rep now includes "technical capability" (fluency with AI, industry, ICP), plus customer empathy (e.g., ex-kitchen operators at Owner). Kyle's JDs are praised as "explicitly exclusionary." @53:00–56:07 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Outbound is fraying: the options are the phone, or in-person and personal brand, not "an army of 22, 23 year old spamming people." @56:07 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- Automating entry-level roles threatens the future talent pipeline; "your best talent is the talent that you develop internally." @62:26–63:29 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]], [[talent-density]])
- Bravado data: no correlation between remote/in-person and quota attainment overall, but in-person correlates better for SMB and remote for enterprise. Reps with kids under five perform better. @68:48–73:00 ([[office-vs-remote]])
