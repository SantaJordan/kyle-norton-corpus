# E53: Sales Capacity Planning That Doesn't Suck
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Jenny Dingus, SVP Global Sales @ Clio · **Published:** 2025-11-12 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/sales-capacity-planning-that-doesnt-suck-jenny-dingus-svp-global-sa/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- "People times quota" is a broken growth model. Plan in this order: product attach assumptions, monetization (ASP/ARPA), a full pipeline plan with marketing, then emerging bets (new segments and geographies) baked in conservatively as upside. Headcount and quota follow from that strategy.
- Clio planned only Q1 of FY26 so it can gather data on new AI products and the vLex acquisition before setting the full-year plan. The integration ran as five dedicated workstreams on a three-month clock.
- Change management means people have to "feel it": hands-on demo sandboxes, customer-led demos, preview leaders before the field, an FAQ Gemini gem, a weekly newsletter, meeting-in-a-box, and AI roleplay.

## What Kyle said
- Framing: "the death of linear capacity planning. A topic that I feel very strongly about." @01:01 ([[capacity-planning]])
- **Vertical SaaS:** "I'm in the same world vertical SAS". After penetration, the game is how you "continue to take wallet share". "all of restaurant tech tends to converge on on like a similar product portfolio" (Toast, DoorDash going first party, Owner). @04:03–05:04 ([[smb-sales-motion]])
- **Why reps×quota persists:** bottoms-up channel planning is "uncomfortably murky which is why if you're a finance person and you know you you view the world through a spreadsheet it's much easier to say like all right It's reps times quota." He called handing that plan to a new VP "one of the the least kind things we do to firsttime VPs of sales". @11:11–12:12 ([[capacity-planning]])
- **Quota philosophy for new products:** "give somebody a number they can go beat and build a winning like build winning momentum and then and then grow quotas on the back of it". He calls it "an underrated contributor to like momentum and a culture of winning and honestly like employee retention and attraction". @14:14–15:16 ([[comp-plans]], [[recognition-and-motivation]])
- **Speculative bets:** he warned against booking a new product as a "$10 million revenue line next year, 50 million" and then "trying to clean up a a $35 million gap in your plan" (hypothetical). @20:19–21:20 ([[capacity-planning]])
- **Owner replans quarterly:** "we're you know a few stages behind as sort of late series C and we'll replan almost every quarter at this point". Emerging winners get more resources but "it's got to carry more of the number then". @23:23 ([[owner-com]], [[operating-rhythm]])
- **Owner BDR capacity:** through 2025 Owner built new marketing channels and had to "pivot especially our BDR uh capacity from like cold outbound to this new thing". He said "we're hiring like 25 BDRs in 90 days". If the demand doesn't show, the backup plan is "we really want to pursue cold outbound in a in a more significant way than we have historically". @25:28–26:30 ([[outbound-at-scale]], [[sales-hiring]])
- **Why:** an "incredible new CMO" is "60 days in and the the lead number is already flying". Kyle described him as "a mathupen math grad like very ibanker like you know pragmatist" and "extremely bullish". Kyle's view: "I am not going to get caught in a position where I don't have enough capacity to meet to meet the AQL demand. That's like a cardinal sin of a sales leader." Either "Wyatt's going to do what I think he's going to do" or "I'm going to take a much bigger swing at at cold outbound". "we're playing to win the market, not to be like a you know, successful little startup." @27:31–28:32 ([[marketing-alignment]], [[capacity-planning]])
- **Strategic congruence:** in his GTM AI talk at Pavilion's DC event, his caveat was "you got to know what game you're playing". Owner's game is to "raise a bunch of money" and play "go big or go home", which is "what venture capital wants". Other paths now exist (lean, raise once, bootstrap). @29:32–30:32 ([[fundraising-and-investability]], [[pavilion]])

## Guest's core ideas
- "People times quota" ignores pipeline and market opportunity. Headcount and quota "should be a byproduct of that strategic work". @08:06–09:08 ([[capacity-planning]])
- Integrated planning: (1) assumptions on how new products attach to new and existing customers; (2) monetization strategy ("you can't back into quotas until you understand what your possible ASP or ARPA is going to be"); (3) a full left-to-right pipeline plan with marketing across inbound, BDR and channel; (4) incremental swings into emerging segments and geographies (enterprise, EMEA, APAC, Canada). @16:18–20:19 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Bake new bets in conservatively and treat them as upside that can de-risk other parts of the plan. Get aggressive only after a year of data. Clio rarely reforecasts. @21:20–23:23 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Clio is planning only Q1 for FY26 so it doesn't overhire or set unreachable quotas. When quotas rise, explain the "path to quota". @13:14–16:18 ([[comp-plans]])
- Lean into headcount where demand is expected. Overhiring is survivable when other parts of the business can absorb people. Post-launch data is reviewed "multiple times a week". @24:25–25:28, @35:38–37:40 ([[capacity-planning]])
- vLex integration: "draw the owl"; five workstreams with dedicated leaders; teams spending 70% of their time on it, some workstreams with 30–40 people; Bain consultants policing deal boundaries. @38:42–42:47 ([[org-design]])
- Comms: weekly all-hands, "five times five different ways", and "people have to feel it". Use demo sandboxes, let the customer guide the demo, preview sales leaders before the field, and run a restricted FAQ Gemini gem fed from Slack questions. @43:48–53:01 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Enablement "does not stop when you launch": a weekly sales newsletter, "meeting in a box" for managers, and Hyperbound AI roleplay bots for sales and part of CS. @55:03–59:05
- Great CRO: humility and curiosity. Advice: don't assume anyone has all the answers. Hardest lesson: "you can't say yes to everything". Book: *The Happiness Advantage* (Shawn Achor). @61:08–64:14
