# E51: How Lightspeed Commerce Built a $1B+ Sales Engine
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** JD Saint-Martin, President @ Lightspeed Commerce · **Published:** 2025-10-30 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/how-lightspeed-commerce-built-a-1b-sales-engine-jd-saint-martin-p/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Saint-Martin came into Lightspeed through the 2019 Chronogolf acquisition and rose from senior director to president. He runs all of GTM (about 2,000 people, 700 quota-carrying reps) as the company grows from 75M toward 1.2B in recurring revenue.
- His high-performance system: candid hiring ("we're not for everyone"), activity / success / outcome metrics on public leaderboards, and a points-based "elite" program that promotes reps automatically, paired with a formula-driven PIP. Kyle compares it to Owner's values-gated "three door" promotion framework and automatic "get on track plan".
- Strong opinions: enablement is underinvested, five days in the office (losing the office's "osmosis" after COVID hurt ramp), no work-life balance at a hypergrowth company, and owning outcomes rather than tasks.

## What Kyle said
- Owner's pre-screen video: for senior roles he sends a "22ish minute video" before the first hiring-manager screen, covering the problem, structure, the role and the culture ("very intense very high expectations high pace not a work life balance culture"). Good candidates have pulled out after watching it and thanked him for the transparency. @20:21–21:22 ([[sales-hiring]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Pushed on input vs output metrics: managing to dials "oftent times just means people making ghost dials" and "vanity metrics", but too much focus on outputs can starve the inputs. @23:22 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Owner baked its company values into the same pyramid over the last ~18 months. A "three door promo framework" gates promotion: metrics (top ~10%), then skills, then values ("built the right foundations as a corporate athlete"). He and Adam "from day one saw the world in like a cohesive way." @26:25–27:27 ([[recognition-and-motivation]], [[culture-and-high-standards]], [[person-adam-guild]])
- Owner's automatic trigger: "two months in a row below 75%" (or another threshold) starts a "get on track plan". It is not a PIP and carries heavy manager commitment. Making it algorithmic keeps it from fraying the manager–rep relationship and "makes it so much less emotional." @33:37–34:38 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Enablement: "Why don't people invest more in enablement? It's a mystery to me." He also pushed back that the early-COVID success was because "the market was just ripping." @40:43, @43:47 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- AI readiness: "the winners of AI are people with like structured businesses and good data and process and documentation." @50:56 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Comp: at Shopify, merchant services was "65 70%" of revenue, and comping on it is still "a massive work in progress." Owner is mostly subscription plus some take rate. A predictive model sets the expected deal value and reps are paid up front on it. The variable piece "could be off by 25% up or down" on a deal, but it nets out across hundreds of deals, and "sales reps don't want to wait to get paid." @52:59–55:00 ([[comp-plans]], [[shopify]], [[owner-com]])
- Work-life balance: "you can't expect extraordinary career outcomes without extraordinary effort." It isn't a moral judgement. A company can set whatever expectation it wants "as long as you treat people well." @58:04–59:06 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- He did a skip-level that morning, a customer meeting days earlier, and talent meetings that week. He does the podcast and LinkedIn for talent brand, and the podcast is "my mentorship at scale" because he never had close mentors. @68:15–69:16 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- He was "slightly negative" on Radical Candor at first because people used it as an excuse to be harsh. The care has to be real, and the packaging matters because people in "ego defense" mode can't hear the message. @71:19–72:21 ([[career-and-leadership]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Founder-to-exec: doing every GTM role at Chronogolf built empathy. The hardest change was letting go as a "control freak"; he uses "blue collar" leadership and "roll in the mud". @08:09–13:13 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- Advice to his day-one self: understand how decisions get operationalized. Avoid "spreadsheet warrior" plans that just add reps, and invest in RevOps, enablement and systems. @14:14–16:17 ([[revops]], [[capacity-planning]])
- Hiring: be prescriptive and candid up front (dial expectations, a 60% quota cliff, chaos) for both ICs and leaders. @17:19–20:21 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Metrics: three buckets (activity, success, outcome) with ranges and three leading metrics per role, shown on leaderboards everywhere. Manager "Maslow" pyramid: coverage, then activity, then skill, then engagement. @22:22–26:25 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- The elite program: points earned only from quota attainment translate automatically into title, responsibility and equity. It is rigid by design, paired with a numbers-driven PIP ("snake and ladder"), and built to raise tenure, since tenure correlates most with performance. Comp is 80% individual and 20% team, plus peer-voted "changemaker" awards for the six company values. @28:29–39:43 ([[recognition-and-motivation]], [[comp-plans]])
- Enablement: when COVID ended office "osmosis", ramp collapsed. They cut SEs ("terrible idea"), then rebuilt with enablement leaders inside each business unit so GTM leaders own enablement and its outcomes. @41:45–49:55 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[org-design]])
- Office: everyone in five days a week. Remote is worst for early-career staff. @44:48–46:51 ([[office-vs-remote]])
- Wild horses vs consistency: business units customize, but tooling, systems and comp converge on one central structure (owned by RevOps), which matters even more for AI. @49:55–52:59 ([[revops]])
- Leadership habits: a to-do list plus an "outsource" list (a name that keeps recurring is a problem); always be hiring, talking to customers and doing skips; "own the outcome" not the task; give new motions dedicated teams, not 10–20% of someone's job. @61:09–78:31 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]], [[operating-rhythm]])
- A great CRO understands every instrument in the GTM "orchestra", not just sales. @72:21–74:24 ([[cro-role-and-board]])

## Numbers & facts
- Lightspeed serves "326,000 plus SMBs". @00:00–01:00
