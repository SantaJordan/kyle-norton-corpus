# E35: Building a Multi-Product Platform with Seismic's CRO, Hayden Stafford
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Hayden Stafford, President & CRO @ Seismic (ex-Microsoft Dynamics, Salesforce, IBM, EY) · **Published:** 2025-06-04 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E35-building-a-multi-product-platform-with-seismics-cro-hayden/transcript.md` (the transcript ends mid-sentence at about @59:28, so the rest of the episode is missing)

## TL;DR
- Point solutions eventually run out of TAM. Big enterprises now want "best of platform" over "best of breed". Seismic expanded from content automation for asset managers into content management, learning (the Lessonly acquisition) and meetings.
- Going platform broke the sales motion. Perennial winners who sold one product to one buyer stopped hitting plan. Stafford killed the double-comped overlay specialists, kept specialization only in technical roles, and upskilled reps on value-led, multi-persona selling.
- Platform prerequisites: a real partner ecosystem (run with the same rhythm of business as sales), sound pricing and packaging, and an arm's-length value-consulting team reporting to the CRO that owns "value promised, value delivered, value realized".

## Guest's core ideas
- Platform definition: multiple integrated capabilities on one platform, so each new piece isn't "another herculean task" to implement. It is a necessity at scale because big buyers (e.g., Bank of America's vendor summit) want best of platform, which "may mean good enough for some capabilities". @04:15–06:18
- Why he joined (2022): he wanted authority, not just accountability (at Microsoft he had "all of the accountability and little of the authority"), believes in enablement, liked the culture and CEO, and saw the IPO upside. @14:42–19:55 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- The platform transition: after Lessonly, overlays were double-comped ("we both got paid at 100%") for about 8 months. His model: "specialization needs to occur with the... technical roles" (SEs, architects), while sellers are wide enough to be good enough. Hire "athletes" and upskill with your own product. @20:56–29:13 ([[comp-plans]], [[org-design]])
- Messaging shifts from product "speeds and feeds" for one knowledgeable buyer to business impact per stakeholder (COO efficiency, CRO top line, marketing top of funnel). Marketing and product must adopt a platform mindset too. @50:02–53:14 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Ecosystem: most "partnerships" are just marketplace listings. Pick partners for "least amount of friction and best lift". Judge them on win rate, velocity and renewal with vs without the partner. Run pipeline reviews and targets with partners. "Anchor on the comp structures and the incentives first." Partner teams should meet product weekly or bi-weekly. The Salesforce AgentForce partnership is an example. @30:15–39:40 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Buy, build or partner: for non-core capabilities like RFP management, partner tightly (near-OEM, reciprocal revenue). @37:33–39:40
- Value consulting reports to the CRO, sits outside the quota team (so it isn't "the commission killer"), and is measured on retention and CSAT/NPS. The "value continuum" (value promised, value delivered, value realized) is needed to defend an eight-figure line item at renewal. He wants MSA language allowing anonymized before/after measurement. @41:44–50:02 ([[churn-and-retention]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Verticalize go-to-market only once you have industry-specific product (citing Keith Block at Salesforce). Seismic is verticalized only in financial services, which has its own industry data models. @52:13–55:21
- Moving up from enablement buyers to CROs: be relevant to their metrics (productivity, revenue per head, not "75% of your team has taken training"), then win friendly CROs first for social proof. @56:21–59:28
