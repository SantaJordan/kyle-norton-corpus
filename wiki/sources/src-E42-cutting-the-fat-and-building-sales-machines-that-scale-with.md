# E42: Cutting the Fat and Building Sales Machines That Scale with Tom Hanrahan
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Tom Hanrahan, EVP & Global Head of Sales @ Square (about 10 years); ex-Amazon, LivingSocial, Aramark · **Published:** 2025-08-07 · **Kyle's role:** host (heavy co-discussant) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E42-cutting-the-fat-and-building-sales-machines-that-scale-with/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Efficiency first: large sales teams cover inefficiency with headcount. Before adding heads, ask about quota modeling and capacity, lead efficiency and distribution, then specialization. Square holds the team to a 2–4x ROI.
- Specialize only when it gives a material, at-scale lift. It adds complexity and single points of failure, so test it first in incubation pods or with SPIFFs or routing changes. Specialization by inbound vs outbound (around 80–95% pure) and a comp change to net-new were Square's biggest efficiency step-changes.
- Talent: build from the bottom up with entry-level hires, screen for coachable, competitive, motivated and hardworking, use a brutally honest closing talk track, and retro interview scores against in-seat performance.

## What Kyle said
- Efficiency vs VC-style growth is "a little bit of a false binary". You can "grow at venture capital speeds with a winner take all mentality without burning cash". @03:02–04:05 ([[capacity-planning]])
- The tension: under pressure to grow, leaders add bodies, "But what you find is actually adding those bodies makes it slower and more difficult to get efficient." Too few reps and win rates slump. Too many and "Nobody nobody likes to be to have like one demo a day." @10:13, @14:18 ([[capacity-planning]])
- Opportunity volume, not rep count, is the constraint: "the bottleneck 90% of the time is opportunity volume". Model AEs as opportunities divided by rep capacity, not revenue as reps × quota, because "The reps don't produce revenue". @18:22 ([[capacity-planning]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Owner's channel mix swung from predominantly inbound to predominantly outbound, then back to "predominantly inbound again" after nailing growth marketing. That raises whether to segment by channel, by geography, or by size (single vs multi-location). @19:24–20:25 ([[owner-com]], [[org-design]], [[outbound-at-scale]])
- Specialization makes the engine "more complex and sometimes brittle". Split a BDR team of four and one promotion wipes out half a segment. At Shopify, his BDR + AE + launch pod (about 70 people) worked, but each disruption put the whole pod "under so much pressure". @24:31–25:35 ([[shopify]], [[org-design]])
- His GTM 2024 (Pavilion) talk modeled efficient sales teams. Once churn is factored in (e.g. 20% of roles restarting ramp), brittle structures "destroy" the spreadsheet. @26:36–27:38 ([[pavilion]], [[capacity-planning]])
- Incubation programs "need their own oxygen": a dedicated leader and resources, not "off the side of somebody's desk". @30:40 ([[org-design]])
- Heuristic: slice win rate, deal velocity and ASP by channel, market and size, carried through to retention for LTV, and "let the data dictate". 5% might justify a change, but you'd want more for a big reorg. @33:44–35:46 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Quoting Ryan Milligan (QuotaPath): "don't change your comp plan until you've run like an aggressive spiff on something". @36:47 ([[comp-plans]])
- Owner has "ripped off best months ever in sales like I don't know basically back toback for years now", yet the MBR spends about "six of 90 minutes" on what's working and hammers the weak metrics. This is cultural, "pragmatic and and objective". @42:57–44:00 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Outbound should go upmarket. Owner sets outbound "floors" and has raised them via the lead rotator to orient reps to "the highest expected value actions". Larger targets have lower book and win rates but higher ASP, so find "the top of that hill". It limits BDRs choosing whom to call. @51:07–53:11 ([[outbound-at-scale]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Do LTV:CAC by channel with retention baked in, or "you're signing up for a problem in a couple years". Cost to serve creeps up on big deals. @55:13–56:14 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- His hypothesis: efficient teams are built "from the bottoms up" with entry-level talent. Lateral senior reps are expensive, and A++ talent four or five years in is hard to pry loose. @57:15–58:16, @60:19 ([[talent-density]], [[sales-hiring]])
- Owner "this year made a big shift to hiring uh mostly new grads into SDR", business-school grads from top Ontario schools, "100% 5 days a week in office". "the results have been bonkers", especially speed to ramp. @61:19 ([[office-vs-remote]], [[sales-hiring]])
- He is the last interview for all GTM frontline hires, with a brutally honest talk track: "We're a performance management culture" and "we're not really like a work life balance place". It lets the wrong people opt out, and the right ones show "that glimmer". @66:25–68:27 ([[sales-hiring]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Hiring science: ATSs are a black box. His vibe-coded Lever API app reached "like 80%", then kept failing at the LLM step. He started this at Shopify before a reorg. "I want to get more scientific about hiring." @69:27–71:30 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Owner's first hiring retro: every new hire from the last two years, interview scores out of 16 vs in-seat results. A 13 became a top producer, a 15 was mediocre, and the highest scorer was one of the fastest exits ("they were just lying"). @71:30–73:31 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- His early-career mistake was bringing a competitor's mentality to internal stakeholders, which "create[s] friction". @78:38 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- Personal: he's "almost 100% audio books now". Both he and Hanrahan recently lost their fathers. His dad, a Vancouver business lawyer, insisted on taking "the scenic route", and "I'm trying to take the scenic route more now". @79:40–80:40 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])

## Guest's core ideas
- "every dollar" is measured: give us $1 and get $3–5 back. Large teams cover gaps "through manpower". Lean teams have more leads per rep and happier reps. @04:05–07:06 ([[capacity-planning]])
- Run the factory like *The Goal*, learned from Eric Sager and Alyssa Henry: "scalable, repeatable, predictable". @08:07–09:09
- The quarterly question order: quota modeling, lead efficiency and distribution, specialization (e.g. take onboarding off AEs for about 20% at scale), then headcount. Capacity modeling by channel (marketing, partners, BDR, AE-sourced with a cap). @11:14–17:20
- Specialization tests: organic win patterns (food and beverage), a large TAM, a differentiated buying experience and training, and a material productivity lift. Square's other verticals were "probably prematurely" specialized and rolled back. @31:42–33:44 ([[org-design]])
- Hidden inefficiencies: inconsistent manager playbooks, unweighted leads, and performative instead of productive KPI reviews. Weekly 45-minute WBR (from Amazon), with deep dives deferred to the monthly. @37:48–42:57 ([[operating-rhythm]])
- Square structure (US): restaurant vs other. Inside (60–80% inbound), outside (BDR-fed, $1–10M), field (zip codes, 100% in field), enterprise. Global SDR team on a national router. @46:01–49:04
- Biggest step-changes: verticalization, comp focused on net-new profit (up to "3x" output in some markets), and inbound/outbound specialization at roughly 80–95%. @49:04–51:07 ([[comp-plans]])
- Hire for coachable, competitive, motivated and hardworking ("have you done hard things?"). The St. Louis first class of six (about nine years ago) mostly rose to senior roles. Test motivation with an honest pre-offer call ("this role might be 100% outbound"). @62:20–66:25 ([[sales-hiring]])
- A great revenue leader is an operator who can also inspire. New leaders should "get curious and shut up". Cross-functional work is like enterprise sales. @74:32–78:38 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
