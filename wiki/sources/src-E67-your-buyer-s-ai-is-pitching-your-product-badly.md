# E67: Your Buyer's AI Is Pitching YOUR Product (Badly)

**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Adrian Rosenkranz, CRO @ Webflow (ex-global sales at Tableau; transcript spells "Audrein") · **Published:** 2026-04-29 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E67-your-buyer-s-ai-is-pitching-your-product-badly/transcript.md`

**Attribution note.** No speaker labels. Below, a line counts as Kyle's only when it is his intro or question, it's first-person about Owner, or the guest's reply makes the switch clear. Webflow data, the efficiency trap, the narrative funnel, the self-updating ICP file, and the build-vs-buy/Duolingo framing are Adrian's. The "mini retro" passage @44:02–46:08 is marked *likely Kyle*: the same turn ends with "All right. I cut you off".

## TL;DR
- Nearly 20% of traffic to Webflow customer sites is AI bots, and ~43% of that is live user queries. Buyers' LLMs now answer on your behalf, so CROs must decide: "are we going to step up and shape it or are we going to be shaped by it?". Answer: one site serving both humans and agents (schema markup, markdown for agents), not a separate agent site.
- **Efficiency trap**: customer-facing staff building their own AI tools is "hyper-realistic work-like activities". Both leaders centralize AI. Adrian merged marketing/sales/post-sales/rev ops into one GTM engineering team, and Kyle points to Owner's applied AI + RevOps + BizOps + data model and his "3X" Substack.
- Evolving context files (weekly ICP drift, Owner's "Gardener", Kyle's personal context file) beat static docs. Don't buy tools that hold your intelligence hostage (Kyle: why he cut Gong for Momentum).

## Guest's core ideas
- AI share of traffic to Webflow customer sites went from under 1% (~16 months ago) to "nearly 20%". About 43% of that is live retrieval to answer a user. Model UI changes (inline links to a "sources" panel) cut Webflow referrals 30–40%, and those visitors showed up as direct type-ins instead @03:07–07:24. [[buyer-ai-aeo-geo]]
- Citation ≠ referral. Referred visitors convert about 6x better because they're further down the funnel @05:15. [[buyer-ai-aeo-geo]] [[metrics-and-forecasting]]
- **Don't build two websites.** SEO gives you AEO "for free", and authority has to be built either way. Add schema.org markup and markdown versions of pages (Webflow ships a "markdown for agents" toggle) @08:28–11:32. [[buyer-ai-aeo-geo]] [[marketing-alignment]]
- The LLM long tail means you must answer far more questions. Buyers will ask "is this pricing fair?". Revenue leaders must choose to "help shape the information that goes to LLMs" @12:39–14:43. [[buyer-ai-aeo-geo]] [[cro-role-and-board]]
- **Narrative over features:** the "call blitz every Monday" analogy, where predictable rhythms hide change. A strong narrative lets new features slot in. Good docs give real-time answers in Slack. "product should just release when it's ready" @15:49–19:51. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]] [[marketing-alignment]]
- Built an agent that watches "OK to go to market" Slack channels, flags features that fit the ICP narrative, and matches them to open deals using call transcripts @20:56. [[ai-sales-stack]]
- **Efficiency trap:** being productive at the wrong thing. Over 100 people don't each need their own call-data tool. He collapsed marketing ops, sales ops, post-sales ops and RevOps into one team with GTM engineers (led by Tara) because workflows cross departments. Stewart Butterfield's "hyper-realistic work-like activities". "are you on calls more?" @23:01–31:28. [[ai-in-gtm]] [[revops]] [[gtm-engineering]] [[org-design]]
- The two most useful LLM context sources are **usage data** and **customer calls**. Future ops skills are eval builders, knowledge-base managers, and builders who translate the business's needs into agents @30:24–34:33. [[gtm-engineering]]
- Use cases: (1) an email agent (Slack, 60 days of calls, calendar, Gmail, voice skill, built in Claude Code/Cowork via MCP, cron scheduled) that grades itself and needs no edits close to 90% of the time; (2) a weekly self-updating **ICP markdown file** with drift tracking, anti-ICP patterns, quotes and JTBD; (3) just-in-time SDR call scripts in Slack; plus QBRs built as password-protected Webflow pages in 15 minutes instead of 5–8 hours @35:36–56:23. [[ai-sales-stack]] [[icp-and-saying-no]] [[churn-and-retention]]
- Build vs. buy: buy systems you can "vibe on top of" (governance, roles, permissions). Build throwaway apps for short-lived needs, and judge by longevity and price-to-value (the Duolingo example). Avoid black-box "next best action" tools, preferring ones like Trig where you own the logic. Walled gardens aren't a durable moat @57:25–64:42. [[build-vs-buy-software]]
