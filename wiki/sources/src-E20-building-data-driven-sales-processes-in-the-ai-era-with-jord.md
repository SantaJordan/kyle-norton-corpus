# E20: Building Data-Driven Sales Processes in the AI Era with Jordan Crawford
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Jordan Crawford, CEO/founder @ Blueprint GTM · **Published:** 2025-02-12 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E20-building-data-driven-sales-processes-in-the-ai-era-with-jord/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Jordan's thesis: you can't know where to start with AI "without, uh, inventing the perfect message". The perfect message implies the list, the list implies the data, the data implies the systems. Then ask where AI fits. "The list is the message."
- Horizontal SaaS must pick one narrow segment (CRM win-rate/ACV/time-to-close analysis, SDR and CSM interviews), run it manually first (offshore researchers, "zero AI"), then automate piece by piece. "don't try to solve a pipeline problem. Try to solve a data problem."
- Kyle confirms Owner already works this way: "It's all about the data." He describes the narrowing (500,000 independent restaurants → top 30,000 via ML models) and argues CROs must pick the segment and build AI literacy but hand the technical work to a dedicated full-time data/biz-ops owner.

## Guest's core ideas
- **Attention ≠ intention.** Shallow personalization ("you wear a shirt. I wear a shirt") is irrelevant to propensity to buy. "people think that attention is intention and that's totally wrong." @17:02 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- **Perfect message → list → data → systems.** Ask what you'd write with unlimited time to the platonically perfect prospect, then derive the data and systems. Only then add AI. "the list is the message." @18:06–24:27 ([[icp-and-saying-no]], [[gtm-engineering]])
- **Horizontal SaaS must pick a segment.** It is "best for the organization, but worse for his job" for a CRO to drop nine of 10 segments for nine months. Otherwise you go "inch deep mile long". Use CRM data (via ChatGPT code execution), SDR and CSM input, and closed-won customer interviews. @21:18–29:42 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- **Metaprompt Process:** give full context, then "do not answer this question" and have the model write the prompt that will. Warm up chats with website, reviews and transcripts. @33:51–35:59, @31:45 ([[personal-ai-stack]], [[context-harness-and-memory]])
- **Shovels/GAF example:** a permit-API company targets GAF roofing distributors with permit-derived insight on the top roofers in their territory and their GAF share. "That's an email that you would pay to receive". @35:59–39:12 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- **Off-the-shelf signal vs off-the-shelf insight.** Signal platforms sell "I saw that you" data that degrades as more people use it ("for them to grow means for you to shrink"). A generic signal plus a counterintuitive proprietary insight can work, but unique datasets are the moat. @42:26–47:36 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- **Start manual, then automate.** Shadow SDRs' research routes, have an outsourcer do it, then move it to AI (Clay, n8n) step by step. It is a 6–9-month commitment, so start with a pod. "don't try to solve a pipeline problem. Try to solve a data problem." @48:41–53:03 ([[gtm-engineering]], [[clay]])
- **Five C's of Truth** (customer and market context → CRM → on-the-ground validation → back to closed-won customers). Mantras: "process over prompts", "Tooling is for tools", "you are the guide, not the river". Bolting AI SDRs onto the old system is the wrong move. GTM needs its own GitHub/PM-style transformation. @57:14–58:14, @66:48, @83:33 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]])
- **Quickfire:** great CROs are "system thinkers" (and "the most like Kyle as possible"). Advice to founders: "narrow your focus". His own lesson: saying no to off-focus work doubled his take-home pay. @84:36–88:51 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
