# 13 Jobs — The Chief Revenue Podcast: 063 — Kyle Norton's Revenue Leadership Framework

**Show / host:** 13 Jobs — The Chief Revenue Podcast / host not named in transcript (says he's known Kyle "about a year") · **Published:** 2024-10-08 · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/chief-revenue-podcast-063/transcript.md` · **Audio:** https://api.spreaker.com/download/episode/62278336/kyle_norton_trimmed.mp3

## TL;DR
- Kyle's six jobs of a sales manager: **sell, coach, hire, build, operate, inspire**; written systems/artifacts let Owner onboard managers and scale "from five to a hundred people in 26 months" without things breaking.
- Coaching is the pillar to over-invest in: focus on one thing, questions-based, deep practice via isolation drills, care, and reinforcement against the forgetting curve. Never coach a whole call; never coach inside a 1:1.
- Hiring fails for lack of a system: a DNA/craft/knowledge scorecard with mapped questions per interview stage beats "vibes" and halo/horns bias — the "Ironman suit" he described at SaaStr.

- Six manager jobs, applying to directors too (bigger "build"): sell (top-20% seller, no "dashboard jockeys"), coach, hire, build, operate (rhythms, reporting, "math of sales"), inspire (Daniel Pink's autonomy/mastery/purpose, not "rah-rah") @06:23–10:35. - Salespeople are weak operators; he promotes reps who are naturally operational and who light up when coaching others — tested via mentoring/buddy assignments @10:35–14:41. [[career-and-leadership]] [[org-design]]
- "sales naturally breeds pretty bad managers" because the job is inwardly focused; CS is a more natural manager source @11:36–14:41. [[career-and-leadership]]
- Elevate the senior IC track: top sellers recognized, well paid, included in product strategy; gave a director title to a top rep at a prior company @16:46–18:53. [[recognition-and-motivation]] [[org-design]]
- Coaching is "the single most important" pillar: (1) focus on one thing (*The Talent Code*), (2) questions-based (*The Coaching Habit*), (3) deep practice / isolation drills of 10–30 reps, (4) caring, (5) reinforcement vs the forgetting curve @18:53–24:09. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Coaching is separate from managing ("operate"): managers may not coach in 1:1s @22:06–23:06. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]] [[operating-rhythm]]
- His coaching roots: fine-arts student, taught preschool art and sports, coached jiu-jitsu; Matt Thornton's Straight Blast Gym drills; studied adult learning principles; goal is to "become a talent factory" @25:10–28:24. [[career-and-leadership]] [[talent-density]]
- Never coach an entire call — e.g. pull 10 minutes of discovery from seven calls and drill only second-level questions to compress trigger → recognition → application @29:28–32:36. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Sequence coaching by the "reverse salient" (monkey juggling chainsaws before building the pedestal); Owner measured skill scores against quota attainment to pick which skills matter @32:36–34:42. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]] [[metrics-and-forecasting]]
- Hiring mistake is no system: scorecard (DNA = mindset, habits, intelligence, character; craft; specialized knowledge) with questions mapped across recruiter screen, hiring manager, case study, and finalist meeting with Brett and Kyle ("rugged flexibility", Brad Stulberg) @34:42–38:58.
