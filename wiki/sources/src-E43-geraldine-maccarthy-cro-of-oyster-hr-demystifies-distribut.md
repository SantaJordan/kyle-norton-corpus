# E43: Geraldine MacCarthy, CRO of Oyster HR, Demystifies Distributed Sales Teams
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Geraldine MacCarthy, CRO @ Oyster; ex-CRO Personio (Series C–E), Dropbox, Google, Accenture · **Published:** 2025-08-13 · **Kyle's role:** host (heavy co-discussant) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E43-geraldine-maccarthy-cro-of-oyster-hr-demystifies-distribut/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Remote vs office is a false binary. Most companies are a blend, decided by role (engineering remote, junior BDRs together), access to talent (distributed hiring skews senior and specialist), and infrastructure.
- Distributed-team building blocks: tools, explicit working norms ("follow the sun", availability, escalation paths), home-office ergonomics and breaks, trust (work-with-me docs, radical candor, closing feedback loops), and open-by-default communication.
- They disagree on remote enablement. MacCarthy says well-documented onboarding (e.g. 100 Gong calls) can beat in-office. Kyle's Owner pilot found in-person SDR ramp "literally 3x" remote on the same programming. Both agree managers need dedicated enablement.

## What Kyle said
- In his first tech role he managed BDRs in Paris, Cologne and London. Canada is basically the same market as the US ("We're a little more riskaverse"). @06:11 ([[shopify]])
- Remote vs office is "such a silly false binary". LinkedIn turns it into a "moralistic argument" for engagement farming. @09:16–10:19 ([[office-vs-remote]])
- Owner's blend: engineering is fully remote ("we'll hire developers anywhere"), and "sales is now consolidating in our Toronto sales hub", with people graduating out of full in-person as they level up. "I'm not going to make an enterprise rep come in five days a week". SDRs benefit "tremendously" from co-location "in the data". @10:19–11:20 ([[office-vs-remote]], [[owner-com]])
- Owner's footprint: SDR/BDR new hires are "five days a week fully in person", "half the company's fully remote", there are "big teams in Latin America", developers are everywhere, and there's "an executive HQ in in San Francisco". He wants a name for this hybrid-of-hybrids. @20:31 ([[org-design]])
- Trade-offs: in person brought faster ramp and high productivity, but some candidates declined AE offers for remote ones. @21:33
- The default should be remote principles: "you can work as as a remote company and support people in office. You can't really work as an in-off company and and support remote people well". Remote norms set "a higher floor" for communication and enablement. @22:33–23:35 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Async: write "fully formed thoughts" for a "one passback decision-making process", not Slack ping-pong. @16:26 ([[operating-rhythm]])
- Until recently "90% of my team was not coll-located with me". He takes about two walk-and-talk meetings a day with the agenda in the Notion app. @29:39 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- At Shopify, when it went permanently remote he was "not excited about it" after a career in bullpens. He studied the best remote companies (Matt Mullenweg) and wrote a "digital by design" working guide covering Slack vs phone vs Notion and response times. For deal issues: "Pick up the phone". @30:41–31:43 ([[shopify]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- His "blueprint" doc says "opinionated but open". He'll grill ideas, and the classic failure he flags is an idea that is "great for sales and this sucks for everything downstream of sales". @34:47–36:49 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- "I'm really like anti-meating." Owner's operating-methodology doc says that if a meeting is "90% you talking at people... That is not a meeting", it should be a recording or a doc. Don't brainstorm in groups (citing Annie Duke's *How to Decide*). Decisions go through Notion templates (problem statement, desired outcomes, a draft, comments) before a synchronous discussion. "writing is thinking". The MBR pre-reads everything and discusses only the debates. @38:51–42:56 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[person-annie-duke]])
- His own weakness: after about 10 meetings a day, small commitments fall through the cracks. AI note-takers mangle action items. Owner is testing Notion's recorder. Every internal meeting of his is transcribed, and he misses being able to query in-person meetings ("this is why the pendant is going to is going to be great"). @47:01–48:02, @53:08–54:08 ([[personal-ai-stack]])
- Channels over DMs: "I probably say this twice a day". @50:06 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Communication systems: a weekly MAPLE update from every leader in one shared place (Metrics, Advanced, Planned, Learnings, Emergencies). Traffic-light change comms: green means a Slack post, red means "three points of contact", a video, manager discussion in team meetings, and updates in Notion and Guru. @55:10–56:13 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Limit surfaces: IC job knowledge (approval matrix, DQ criteria) lives in Guru, reachable via Slack. Notion holds internal docs and projects. Shopify "was all Googled". @56:13–57:14 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- In-person enablement data: Owner hired a senior enablement leader "way earlier" than most and thinks its onboarding is "probably better than 99% of companies at at our stage". Yet in-person SDRs on the same program (Avara sim, buddy, mocks) ramp far faster. In a side-by-side pilot, "it only took four months" to see "ramp productivity uh is literally 3x in person". He thinks this is unique to early-career roles, and the cause is the pace of Q&A ("they just go to Sariah"). He had been "really resistant" because remote let him "pick off President's Club performers from wherever". @63:21–66:24 ([[office-vs-remote]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Owner "significantly underenable[s]" managers, so it's adding train-the-trainer: the redeployed "coaching mastery methodology" went to directors first, a week or two ahead, and directors helped deliver it. @72:32 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- He checks manager call participation ad hoc in Momentum. @74:34 ([[momentum]])
- Personal: after reading *Breath* he has taped his mouth shut while sleeping "for years" and does a weekly nasal-breathing run. He "boxed and wrestled for years". @78:39 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])

## Guest's core ideas
- The in-market leader bringing a US product abroad must learn local use cases and competitors, then synthesize the needle-movers for HQ in a heterogeneous region. @04:05–06:11
- Early international teams effectively operate remote whatever the policy. @07:12
- The case for distributed is access to talent (e.g. a native German seller). Distributed hires skew senior. An employer of record is faster than setting up entities. @12:22–20:31 ([[office-vs-remote]])
- Infrastructure: tools (Oyster's open-source "the reef"), norms ("follow the sun", with a 10% exception for emergencies), home-office setup and intentional micro-breaks, since remote workers take fewer and burn out more. @23:35–29:39
- Trust: work-with-me docs, radical candor, naming what you see ("you're pretty stressed"), "say what you're going to do and then you should do what you're going to say", and closing feedback loops. @33:46–37:49, @44:59–47:01 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Communication: open calendars, docs and channels by default. Weekly pulse updates as both video and bullets. Push hallway decisions out to regional teams. Avoid duplicate posts. Friday "must-have" wrap-ups from first-line managers. @49:03–59:17
- Remote onboarding can be better (100 historical calls). The real gap is morale when a remote rep has a bad week, so managers must check in proactively. @60:17–63:21
- Enablement: partner with PMM as release gatekeepers, measure outcomes, be a partner rather than a "service desk", and managers own "the last mile of enablement". Specific first-line-leader training. @67:27–74:34 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- A great CRO works the whole value chain (talent, marketing, product). New leaders should "go a little bit slow to go fast". Her hardest lesson was delivering bad news straight. Recommends *The Qualified Sales Leader*. @74:34–77:37 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
