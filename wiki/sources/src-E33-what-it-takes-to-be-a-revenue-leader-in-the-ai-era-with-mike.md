# E33: What It Takes to Be a Revenue Leader in the AI Era with Mike Donohue, CRO of 11x
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Mike Donohue (transcript: "Donahue"), CRO @ 11x · **Published:** 2025-05-21 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E33-what-it-takes-to-be-a-revenue-leader-in-the-ai-era-with-mike/transcript.md` (the transcript ends mid-sentence at about @59:10, so the rest of the episode is missing)

## TL;DR
- Donohue puts average GTM AI adoption at "around like 20" out of 100. Early startups adopt fastest and large enterprises slowest. Copilots are a 2–3-year stepping stone to fully agentic workflows.
- 11x runs with no BDRs. Over 90% of the funnel comes from its own digital workers (Alice for email, Julian for voice), centrally orchestrated by 3–5 people like growth-marketing campaigns. Speed-to-lead voice agents work 24/7.
- Kyle frames AI fluency as existential for CROs. He disqualifies manager candidates who can't speak to AI, sets a high bar for calling something an "agent", and argues first-principles thinking starts with human psychology (the overjustification effect in spiffs).

- "I think this is one of the most important things for a revenue leader to be able to figure out. I'm in the tools every day myself. I'm like vibe coding apps and Windsurf just for fun". He calls AI "the single biggest platform change any of us have ever seen in our careers and potentially the biggest one in like 100 years." @08:14 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[personal-ai-stack]])
- "Yeah, I think it's existential." Having just come off stage with Jason Lemkin at SaaStr, he says an all-in AI-native CRO will "outcompete everybody in your market", an advantage "even more profound" than the Salesloft-era sales-acceleration edge. @11:19 ([[saastr]], [[person-jason-lemkin]], [[ai-in-gtm]])
- The guest quoted Kyle from an earlier episode: it's riskier *not* to experiment with multiple AI tools than to have one fail. @03:06 ([[ai-sales-stack]])
- AI outbound is easier to believe in when you sell AI. Owner's "SMB mom and pop restaurants are like probably a trickier category". @25:54 ([[smb-sales-motion]], [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]])
- Hiring filter: "I've mandated this for management and sort of leveraged IC roles." He told senior leaders that when hiring managers they must ask about AI, "and if somebody has no answer to it, then they are disqualified." @30:01–31:01 ([[sales-hiring]], [[hiring-scorecards]])
- "you just got to take the demos". About two hours a week, and he tells founders "I don't really want the product demo. I want you to show me, like, live use cases" to see the "art of the possible". @34:09 ([[ai-sales-stack]])
- His learning baskets for CROs: tools and foundation models, prompt engineering, architecture (how pieces plug together, but not "you gotta go learn Python"), and the art of the possible. @39:16 ([[ai-sophistication-ladder]])
- Agent definition, "the like old school definition": it must "reason, take action and then, and choose from different paths and access other tools". Input-then-action is "an AI workflow". He cites Sam Altman's five levels. @42:29 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]])
- Courses "get out of date so fast", and no book stays "topical for more than 60 days". He learns via peers (texting with Kevin Dorsey and trading demos, articles and X posts) and community (Pavilion "pushing big into this"). @43:32, @46:43 ([[person-kevin-dorsey]], [[pavilion]])
- First principles means understanding humans: "learn about behavioral economics and psychology". Teaching his team better spiffs, he shared papers on the overjustification effect (internal vs external locus of control). Don't pre-announce "dial late and expense dinner". Reward the people who chose to stay afterwards. @56:02–59:10 ([[comp-plans]], [[recognition-and-motivation]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Adoption tiers: AI-first startups (clean slate), mid-stage experimenters, and enterprises that want AI but sit near zero. Winners trial about 5 AI products a quarter and keep what works. @03:06–07:11 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- AI will set new growth-efficiency standards (rule of 40 or 60). Large SDR teams become a single GTM engineer or 2–3 marketers managing agents. @09:16–11:19 ([[gtm-engineering]])
- The internet is becoming conversational, "from human speaking computer language to computers speaking human language". @14:25
- Digital workers prospect, personalize, update the CRM, log calls and book meetings. Digital-worker leads are treated as MQLs with predictable ACV and close rate, and new campaigns (closed-lost, events) launch without retraining 20 SDRs. @15:27–22:49 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]], [[marketing-alignment]])
- Speed-to-lead and after-hours voice agents capture richer info than forms. @22:49–24:51
- AI customer success depends on teaching clients AI GTM best practices (prompting, templates mixing fixed and AI text). Using AI should never be seen as cheating. CROs must lead from the front. Each leader should bring 3–5 AI solutions a quarter. @27:58–38:15 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- First principles: a sales stage matrix (5–6 steps with activities, gives/gets and exit criteria). Be rigid on exit criteria, experimental on execution. No standard AE:SDR ratio. Rowing analogy for optimizing each step. @49:52–56:02 ([[sales-methodology]])
