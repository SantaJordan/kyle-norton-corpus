# E50: The Secret to High-Trust, High-Performance GTM Teams
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Tara DiCristo-Schmitt, CRO @ Houzz · **Published:** 2025-10-23 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/the-secret-to-high-trust-high-performance-gtm-teams-with-cro-of-houzz/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Tara's culture stack: trust as the foundation, then vision → expectations (including "the messy in the middle") → consistent recognition of progress. She moved from "activity will win" to "clarity over activity will create productivity", and credits ADP's machine-like playbook and recognition culture.
- Practical tools: a company-wide mission with a single number ("Mission X", "build it, sell it, start it, keep it"), a recognition cadence from daily to quarterly, a seven-step execution model for hard times, and bottoms-up quota setting ("the forecasting is about more is about trust more than numbers").
- Kyle adds his boiler-room roots, ZIRP-era expectation inflation (his Shopify paper wealth), the spiff "wrinkle", his polarizing-reference email, and his quota philosophy: hittable quotas, with Owner AEs' OTE attainment at 148% last quarter.

## What Kyle said
- **Boiler-room roots:** "I grew up in a boiler room too. Like I I grew up in a cold call environment reading like word for word from a script". He added: "my foundations in a highly structured rigorous like sales playbook environment is is given me such an advantage I find But people people poo poo it now." @04:05–05:07 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[sales-methodology]])
- **ZIRP hangover:** "a lot of it is is like easy money and zerp driven". He blamed "hadonic adaption" and post-COVID comp expectations: "those years are make believe". At Shopify, "my shares had like 7xed in the course of like 13 months" and then "I lost 80% of it or 75% of it". @07:10–09:12 ([[shopify]], [[comp-plans]])
- **His hiring edge:** "I can hire people with that drive and mentality ... my team operates at like the old old school effort levels". He mentioned Brad Stulberg's *Master of Change* (Stulberg was an upcoming guest). @09:12 ([[sales-hiring]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- **Clarity:** "slow is smooth and smooth is fast" (which he says "every fourth podcast"). Younger managers go "go go go" and reps end up "spinning in circles". @18:18–19:19 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- **Intrinsic motivation:** a compelling vision lets reps do things "for their own reasons" and keeps their "internal locus of control". He credits "freedom through discipline" to Jocko Willink. @19:19–20:19 ([[recognition-and-motivation]])
- **Dopamine and progress:** "dopamine is released when we anticipate the like achievement", so build more milestones. He cited "cognitive closure or the closure effect" and admitted: "you announce a big thing and then we don't we don't like beat the drum". @28:29–30:31 ([[recognition-and-motivation]])
- **SPIFF design:** spiffs often launch and then go quiet. "Do you have a scoreboard? Are you beating the drum?" His running joke: "there's always a wrinkle in the spiff", added "twothirds or threequarters of the way through" to pull people back into contention. @36:36–37:36 ([[comp-plans]], [[recognition-and-motivation]])
- **Decisions for credibility:** "you're the second guest to mention this exact thing", after Walnut's CRO a year earlier. New leaders feel pressure to "earn all of this credibility in the first 60 days or first 90 days". @39:40–40:41 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- **In-person:** "I learn so much when I'm sitting down with like four or five reps at a dinner". Bringing the whole team together costs "like 150 grand easy" and takes everyone off the phones. "a lot of sales is now back in in the office in Toronto". Owner is SMB and hires "earlier career folks" for whom "5 days a week in office is not just like workable but actually they're excited about it". He knows it is harder for mid-market reps with families. @44:50–46:51 ([[office-vs-remote]], [[owner-com]])
- **Hyper-transparent references:** before an offer, he sends senior candidates "every single person who's worked for me directly over the last like I think it goes back seven or almost nine years now", plus key partners in marketing and CS. "I'm polarizing to work with ... I have very high expectations and I'm not afraid to push", and he won't "allow for poor effort or like lack of competence". @49:54–50:56 ([[sales-hiring]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- **"Exothermic":** borrowed from Tobi Lütke ("Toby"): people "that bring heat and energy to the place that they are". He is "such a big Toby fan". @51:57–52:58 ([[shopify]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- **Quota philosophy:** "we generally try to set a number that we feel like people can really hit and the top reps can go smoke". Owner AE "OT attainment so not like the quote attainment but actually like the the take-home in people's pockets ... was 148% last quarter". Top-of-funnel candidates are sometimes underwhelmed by Owner's OTE, so recruiters have to explain the "faux te" problem elsewhere. "If you have an environment where 40 or 50% of your reps aren't hitting quota, then people don't care about quota. It stops meaning anything." Finance leaders have understood that the OTE/quota pairing can "net it out to roughly the same place". @60:05–62:06 ([[comp-plans]], [[recognition-and-motivation]])
- **High-leverage bets:** "you really just need to make like two to five high leverage bets a year and they have to work". @62:06–63:08 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- **Can't-miss team:** "we are a can'tmiss team. Like we we bring a never miss mentality to it". That message "trickled up to the board". "my job is to bail everybody out. If marketing if marketing struggles, if CS is off track ... find a freaking way." @64:10–65:11 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])

## Guest's core ideas
- ADP culture: a fixed six-step sale, objection handling, weekly in-person recognition with names on the big screen, "stand and deliver" forecasts, and Presidents Club tiers (Presidents Club → Pinnacle → Board of Directors). The sticky-note exercise: "We can always do more." @11:14–15:15 ([[recognition-and-motivation]])
- The foundations are trust plus "high standards and uh high devotion" (Frances Frei). Vision, then expectations "include the messy in the middle", then consistent recognition of progress. @16:15–17:17 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Vision beats velocity: clarity eliminates chaos. Setting it: what's possible, then base it in reality, then stages, then communicate progress. The autonomy of the high-demand era no longer fits a tighter market. @17:17–22:22
- Mission X: one customer-count number, branded company-wide, which broke silos (the designer story). Recognition is quarterly company-wide ("build it, sell it, start it, keep it"), monthly for revenue, daily shout-outs, weekly progress notes, and CEO/CRO milestone notes. Movie-credits thinking recognizes the people behind sales. @22:22–28:29 ([[recognition-and-motivation]])
- In hard times: communicate (or people create "their own stories"), ideate up and down, then run an execution model. Plan and prepare, create hype, train (the "Miyagi move"), set goals and expectations, practice constantly, share results and recognition, and make it cultural. @32:32–36:36
- Trust for new leaders: don't make decisions to gain credibility. Run listening tours with consistent questions ("what do you believe is possible?"). Remote trust takes deliberate effort. @38:38–44:50 ([[office-vs-remote]])
- Her interview trust test: "9 to 10 months from now ... I wish we knew that about Kyle in the interview process. ... What would that be?" @47:52–49:54 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Quota should be bottoms-up from a rep's own conversion inputs. "HPDP" = happy people drive productivity. Top-down quotas breed sandbagging, burnout and misalignment. Forecasting works only when reps aren't afraid to give the real number. @52:58–59:04 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[comp-plans]])
- A great CRO has conviction in decisions. The advice: "the good ones always find a way". Hardest lesson: patience. Reading: Mike Weinberg's *Sales Management. Simplified.*, and the Bible. @62:06–66:11
