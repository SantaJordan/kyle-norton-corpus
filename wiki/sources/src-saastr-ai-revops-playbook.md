# SaaStr AI: The RevOps Playbook: Owner.com CRO's Secrets to Scaling

**Show / host:** SaaStr AI (YouTube channel; solo SaaStr stage talk with slides, no Q&A in the recording) · **Published:** 2025-01-29 · **Kyle's role:** guest (presenter) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/saastr-ai-revops-playbook/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IcWaOtvDImo

Note: ~38-minute monologue; the first minute is a cold-open teaser repeated from @02:01–03:02. Kyle describes Owner as "a series B business" with a team of "about a hundred" (company ~200), so the talk was likely recorded in late 2024 (the stoplight change process is "the last few months" @27:36, matching [[src-getaccept-change-management]]). Despite the "RevOps" title, the talk is about Kyle's **revenue operating system**.

## TL;DR
- A **revenue operating system** is the interface between the revenue leader and the org's people/resources — his "Iron Man suit" — with four pillars: **measuring** (heads-up display), **managing** (arc reactor), **planning** (Jarvis) and **communicating** (thrusters).
- Three build principles: **minimum effective dosage**, **measure what you want to manage** (inputs and behaviors, not just outputs), and **match the system to the state of your business**.
- Concrete tools: control towers vs. rep scoreboards, MBR, target setting; the weekly written **MAPLE** update; structured 1:1s; a monthly game plan graded A–F; a growth model built on pipeline × conversion (not reps × quota); a stoplight change process. Start with the basics, standardize, document, and only then automate (e.g., Momentum alerts).

## What Kyle argued
- A revenue operating system turns startup chaos into order — the alternative is being "in the vortex" (e.g., inbound win rates slip 9% over six weeks unnoticed; the model needs 12 ramped reps and you have six) @05:05–07:07. [[operating-rhythm]] [[metrics-and-forecasting]]
- Company-building stack: mission → values → vision → strategy → principles → org design → operating system → tools/playbooks/training @04:04. [[playbooks-and-documentation]] [[org-design]]
- Minimum effective dosage: every rhythm has a time and "cognitive load cost"; don't spend more time talking about the business than building it; build rhythms that help ICs, not just leaders @07:07–09:09. [[operating-rhythm]]
- Measure inputs/behaviors you can manage, not only outputs; don't measure too many things @09:09–10:10. [[metrics-and-forecasting]]
- Match the system to your stage: Owner is SMB with "a four-day sales cycle"; how the founder/leader engages with the system also changes as layers are added @10:10–11:10. [[operating-rhythm]]
- Measuring: laddered control towers (exec → functional → manager) plus rep-facing scoreboards (stack ranks, pacing), a monthly business review, and a target-setting process @12:13–15:16. [[metrics-and-forecasting]] [[revops]]
- Managing: remote and asynchronous by default; weekly written MAPLE (Metrics, Advanced, Plans, Learnings, Emergencies) due Sunday 8pm; a coaching rhythm separate from 1:1s; far fewer meetings than most orgs @15:16–18:25. [[operating-rhythm]] [[office-vs-remote]] [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- 1:1s start with "scale one to 10, how you doing, why?" (borrowed from Lynette Pistoris) and are for problem-solving, never updates; team meetings track "Kyle's Metrics" (top five), critical projects, FYIs, discussion, actions @18:25–20:26. [[operating-rhythm]]
- Planning: write it down ("unless you can write well, you can't really think well"); a growth-plan spreadsheet, planning and project cadence, and a prioritization framework (credits Annie Duke: potentials, payoffs, perspiration, probability) @20:26–23:32. [[prioritization-5p]] [[person-annie-duke]] [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- Monthly game plan shared with the whole org: outcomes, shout-outs, strategic priorities graded A–F, next month's priorities, FYIs @23:32–24:34. [[operating-rhythm]]
- Communicating: a single knowledge hub; default to transparency and "treat my team like adults"; communicate in three ways ("three points of contact," from rock climbing) via all-hands, AMAs and the monthly plan @24:34–27:36. [[culture-and-high-standards]] [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- Deploying change is how startups win on speed; Owner wasn't good at it until recently; stoplight framework — green (Slack + Notion), red (e.g., comp overhaul: written update, video, LMS training, team meetings, 1:1s) plus a change-log template @27:36–30:42. [[playbooks-and-documentation]] [[comp-plans]]
- Keys to victory: build up slowly (Tony Stark's first suit was crappy), start with a measurement rhythm, a growth model and team rituals; standardize formats and tools (don't make sales re-enter a Salesforce forecast in Notion); block 60 minutes a week to build the system @30:42–35:52. [[operating-rhythm]] [[revops]]
- Growth model mistake: assuming reps × quota = revenue; revenue comes from pipeline generated and conversion, with rep count a non-linear function of that @31:44–32:45. [[capacity-planning]]
- Only automate once rhythms work; Momentum pushes Salesforce events (e.g., a case stuck in back-to-sales) to leaders in Slack @35:52–36:53. [[momentum]] [[revops]]
