# TechTO: Why Sales Teams Need Better Coaching — Winning Through Adversity with Kyle Norton

**Show / host:** TechTO (live event talk, shared stage with author David Priemer; transcript renders the name "David Premer") · **Published:** 2024-05-15 (YouTube upload date) · **Kyle's role:** guest (short talk + audience Q&A) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/techto-sales-coaching-adversity/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=grspTN6Gt7w

## TL;DR
- A ~15-minute talk arguing most managers are bad at coaching, so they avoid it; the fix is frameworks for managers, not more rep training.
- Kyle's coaching model: separate coaching time from 1:1s, coach through questions (let the rep struggle), and focus on one skill per session.
- The core mechanic is "isolation drills" — repeated role-play reps on one moment of a real call, with progressive resistance and an expert-feedback loop, run weekly.

## What Kyle argued
- Managers deprioritize coaching largely because they are not good at it and it doesn't feel rewarding; with frameworks they'd do more of it @01:02. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Enablement is ~90% rep-focused / 10% manager-focused; he'd flip toward 60% manager / 40% rep @04:10. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Telling, deal inspection, forecasting, and calendar review are not coaching; coaching must be a separate 30–45 min meeting, never mixed with the 15–20 min 1:1/pipe review @04:10–05:11. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Great coaching is questions-based: play the clip, ask the rep what went well/badly, give only small breadcrumbs, and let them struggle — "the struggle is where the learning happens" @06:14–09:17 (cites Bloom's taxonomy, *The Coaching Habit*, *The Talent Code*).
- Managers should talk only 30–40% of a coaching conversation; he records coaching sessions in Gong and then coaches his managers on them @09:17–10:21. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Great coaching is focused: one skill per session, never a full-call review; coaching on 10 things is "the worst thing you could possibly do" @10:21–12:25.
- Isolation drills: replay the buyer moment, rep responds, manager plays buyer, reset and repeat 10+ times with progressive resistance @12:25–13:26.
- Isolation drills run every week and are "the core learning experience in my sales teams"; followed by an expert-feedback step on one live call before moving on @15:27–16:28.
- New-rep success: in a ~1-week sales cycle, expects ~half quota in month one and full quota in month two, but in the first 30 days weights learning, effort/intensity, and attitude over results; if two of three are missing he performance-manages fast @16:28–18:33. ## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Separate coaching from 1:1s** (1:1 = pipe/blockers, 15–20 min; coaching = 30–45 min, no deal inspection).
- **Questions-based coaching**: start wide, go narrow, use breadcrumbs, don't bail them out (Bloom's taxonomy; error-feedback loop; learning relevance).
- **Focused coaching**: one skill per session (golf analogy — 10 putts/10 chips/10 drives = no improvement).
- **Isolation drills** with **progressive resistance**, followed by an **expert feedback loop** on a live call.
- **First-30-days rubric**: learning, intensity/enthusiasm, attitude — two of three missing triggers performance management.
