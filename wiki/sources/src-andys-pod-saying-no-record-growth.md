# Andy's Pod: How Saying NO to Customers Led to Record Growth - Kyle Norton, Owner.com CRO
**Show / host:** Andy's Pod, hosted by Andy (founder of Distribute, ex-Outreach) · **Published:** 2025-05-06 (YouTube upload date; metadata.json has no date) · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/andys-pod-saying-no-record-growth/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UBcgvyeyMYM

## TL;DR
- The biggest change from VP Sales to CRO is thinking about enterprise value, not just the closed-won number. At Owner he tightened deal qualification against roughly 30% early churn. Bookings fell for two straight months, an awkward first board meeting followed, and then growth went "up into the right".
- Mindset: put the company ahead of self-preservation, run toward bad news, be "impatient day-to-day with your action" but patient with results, and keep an abundance or "can't miss" mentality. That mentality produced Owner's biggest single day ever at the end of April (2025).
- Early-stage go-to-market: sell through concentric circles of trust before cold outbound, go to market with a point of view (not "my product is better"), and over-serve your first customers. On sales calls, practice radical transparency: pitch up front, say what you're *not* good at, and off-ramp bad-fit buyers.

## What Kyle argued
- CROs struggle to think beyond the closed-won number. Seeing the role as "generating enterprise value" is "one of the three biggest changes" from VP Sales to CRO. @10:19–11:22 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- Enterprise value means closing customers and compounding revenue over a long time. In an AI era where software is easy to build, word of mouth, customer satisfaction and market momentum matter "more than ever". @10:19–11:22 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Owner was churning about 30% of signups in the first 60–90 days. That is "wasted calories" and "sand in the gears" for sales, launch, CS and product. He tightened deal-qualification rules even though it meant fewer deals. @12:25–13:25, @15:28–16:28 ([[icp-and-saying-no]], [[churn-and-retention]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- You get trust for a short-term dip by communicating the plan, the vision and the reason in advance (he gave Adam a heads-up). @15:28–16:28 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Decisions are bets. Even 80% confidence is good enough (Annie Duke, *Thinking in Bets*). @19:33
- Put yourself second. Self-preservation mode "is actually what ends up getting you fired" because people stop believing you have the company's interest at heart. @20:34–21:35 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- Telling the board about a missed number or lost rep is short-term pain but builds long-term trust; "if you start to lose trust with your executive with your board, like long-term you're, you're absolutely cooked." @26:42 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Abundance versus scarcity: "The thing going wrong is not why you miss. Your reaction to that thing is why things get bad." He frames this as neuroscience-backed optimism, not *The Secret*. @28:45–29:46 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Early customers come through concentric circles of trust: your network, then friends of friends, advisors and investors. Avoid cold-outbounding your first ~20 customers. @39:55–44:09 ([[smb-sales-motion]])
- Go to market with a point of view, not with "my product is better". Early products are at their worst, so you win buyers who share your view of the category. @41:01–45:10
- Make your first customers wildly successful through unscalable "mechanical Turk" work. They will "give you a lot of grace". @45:10–46:12 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Radical transparency on sales calls: give the elevator pitch up front, "feature dump" Owner's five things, state the tradeoff (less choice and flexibility in exchange for more money and less work), and "off-ramp" buyers who care about brand control. @48:15–53:29 ([[icp-and-saying-no]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Giving away the playbook is safe because execution is the moat; startup competitors "couldn't execute the way out of a wet paper bag". @56:30–57:32

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Enterprise value over the closed-won number:** the VP Sales → CRO shift (one of "three biggest changes"; the other two aren't named here).
- **Thinking in bets** (Annie Duke): probabilistic, expected-value decisions.
- **Impatient with action, patient with results.**
- **Run toward bad news** (Kyle credits the phrase to Jason Lemkin). @25:42–26:42
- **Abundance mindset / "can't miss mentality".**
- **"Well, what did you expect?"** reframe: problems are expected on the way to building a generational company. @27:44
- **Three keys to the first customers:** concentric circles of trust → point-of-view positioning → swarm early customers to make them successful.
- **Point-of-view positioning / radical transparency selling:** pitch up front, feature dump, state the tradeoffs, **off-ramp** bad fits. (Andy relays Gong's Udi as "better to be different than better"; Kyle says it's "different language for the same thing that I say".)
- **Owner's tradeoff pitch:** "you get less choice and flexibility on owner" in exchange for centralized optimization by "an army of... the best software engineers in restaurant tech".
