# E07: Building a Learning and Revenue Machine with Stevie Case, CRO of Vanta
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Stevie Case, CRO @ Vanta (ex-VP mid-market sales @ Twilio) · **Published:** 2024-11-06 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E07-building-a-learning-and-revenue-machine-with-stevie-case-cr/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- "Execution is the only moat." Case's system has three parts: a clear north-star metric (a growth %), a shared "how we work" (value-based selling plus MEDDPICC), and a weekly forecast call run as a "learning machine" (deep deal inspection, win rate by competitor, stage conversion, act and come back next week), plus quarterly QBRs.
- Scaling it across managers: the right leaders at every layer, documented manager playbooks co-owned with RevOps, exposing each layer to the problems one level up, and hard boundaries on change. A published "balanced scorecard" leaderboard rewards durable behaviours (deal size, win rate, outbound) beyond quota.
- Kyle's own mirror: "high probability practices" over best practices; Owner runs monthly business reviews, stack-ranks reps monthly on durability ("party trick" vs sustainable), promotes on metrics/skills/habits/character/business need, and custom-builds Clay-style enrichment in-house because restaurant owners aren't on LinkedIn.

## Guest's core ideas
- First-time CRO: there is "no CRO playboy" (playbook). Benchmarks are guides, but have "a point of view about why your business is unique". Leaders who arrive with a bought playbook are "a huge red flag". @10:41–16:48 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Execution is the moat because buyers can't tell good products from copies. Expose the truth and create a better buyer experience. @23:15–26:22 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Forecast as a range: commit (worst case), best case and most likely (the "accuracy number"). @26:22–27:25 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- The learning machine: a north star, how we work, and a weekly cadence. "you improve one percent every week". Ask what it would take to 2x or 10x a metric. @28:27–36:40 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[sales-methodology]])
- Spot-check managers through deal inspection. The head of RevOps is "the single most important org for me", and the rhythm is re-documented quarterly. @36:40–39:45 ([[revops]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- A model "down to the penny": weekly ACV by segment, win rate by segment and competitor, and time to close. @40:47–45:02 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Scaling leadership: "don't settle". Bring frontline leaders into the next-level forecast so they see one layer up. @51:19–54:26 ([[org-design]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Behaviour change is harder when things go well. Over-communicate the why and what's in it for them, reinforce with systems, and set firm boundaries. A balanced-scorecard leaderboard covers attainment plus deal size, win rate and outbound opps. @57:35–66:05 ([[recognition-and-motivation]], [[outbound-at-scale]])
- AI: "if you give everybody a job, it's nobody's job"; a dedicated AI workflow team; Clay persona enrichment as an "account map in a box". @70:10–75:15 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[clay]])
- Quickfire: she should have refused to decide without good data in year one. Great CROs "can actually move the metrics" against board-level goals. "You gotta slow down to speed up". @76:21–80:32 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
