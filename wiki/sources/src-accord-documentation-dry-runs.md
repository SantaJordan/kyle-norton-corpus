# Accord: The Critical Role of Documentation, Dry Runs, and Structure with Kyle Norton
**Show / host:** Accord podcast, hosted by Ross Rich (Accord founder, ex-Stripe) · **Published:** 2024-02-07 (YouTube upload date; metadata.json has no date) · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/accord-documentation-dry-runs/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uL_0qpB2oN8

## TL;DR
- Deal excellence comes from three disciplines: document everything (down to a word-for-word "golden script"), dry-run your key meetings, and impose structure so the team can iterate on one shared system instead of each rep improvising their own.
- He teaches sales top-down (a script reps can follow) and bottom-up (the psychology behind it, e.g. psychological reactance and negativity bias) so reps understand *why* the script works.
- Strategic advice: invest in RevOps earlier than feels necessary, point it at prospect data first, and don't over-hire AEs ahead of pipeline. Fund BDRs instead.

## What Kyle argued
- The hardest part of the CRO job is balancing the short and long term. In transactional SMB you deliver a number every month while also planning 6–12 months ahead. For example, a second-line leader you need in nine months has to be recruited now, because the search can take 60–120 days. @03:10–04:10 ([[capacity-planning]], [[org-design]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Everything must be documented, especially in remote or hybrid teams where people pick up less by osmosis. That includes the philosophy behind the demo, the training on each component, and the full script written out word for word. @07:17–08:22 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Documentation only works if it is measured and audited. Owner uses the tool Replays to score rep calls against the script and give live feedback; managers can do the same in Gong. @08:22–09:23 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- His tactic for scaling documentation with AI: record a coaching session, export the transcript, have ChatGPT turn it into a guide (it gets "70% of the way there"), then touch it up. @09:23–10:26 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Dry-run your important meetings as real mock conversations, not just strategy talks, with an open, critical culture. He did this for the biggest deals at League. Owner is too transactional to need it for deals, but the exec team dry-runs board meetings. @11:30–13:35 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Structure is what makes iteration possible. Early on you hire "Renaissance reps", but at scale you need one baseline system, and every rep should be "innovating our system" rather than each innovating their own. @14:35–16:38 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[sales-hiring]])
- Scripting frees up the seller's thinking. Automate the repetitive basics so reps can spend their attention on listening intently, picking up cues, and asking second- and third-level questions. @17:40–18:44 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Adults need to know the "why". He teaches psychological principles (reactance, negativity bias) at the top of each script, which explains why his closing framework is built on questions rather than pressure. @20:50–23:57 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Organization and daily discipline can be taught; growth mindset, resilience and drive cannot. Owner documents operating rhythms from IC to leadership level, including "math of sales" at the start of each month. @28:09–30:15 ([[culture-and-high-standards]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[hiring-scorecards]])
- Invest in RevOps early, even if that means trading AEs out of the headcount plan, and point it at prospect data first. Aim for roughly 90% effectiveness/accuracy. @33:20–34:20 ([[revops]], [[outbound-at-scale]])
- Don't over-hire AEs because the spreadsheet says AEs × ARR-per-AE equals revenue. With three to five AEs, hiring three or four BDRs to feed them pipeline gives a better revenue outcome and better efficiency. @34:20–35:26 ([[capacity-planning]])
- Teams mostly miss ARR targets because of bad target setting. Executives and finance won't face "the reality of the ground floor". @36:28 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
- Venture capital still rewards growth over efficiency: "growth is still the thing that's going to get you funded." @36:28–37:28 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- Don't unplug, lean in: "If you want to be great, you gotta be like all in all the time." @38:31–39:37 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Three tips for deal excellence:** (1) document everything, (2) dry runs for key meetings, (3) provide structure.
- **Golden script:** word-for-word scripts paired with the underlying psychological principles and skills. Top-down (script) plus bottom-up (psychology).
- **Psychological reactance and negativity bias:** the foundations behind his question-based closing framework.
- **Math of sales:** a monthly sit-down where each rep works backwards from their goal through their conversion rates to daily inputs.
- **"Discipline is freedom"** (Jocko): structure is the baseline for creativity.
- **Standards of performance** (Bill Walsh, *The Score Takes Care of Itself*): process and inputs over outcomes.
- **Ray Dalio's *Principles*:** he is using it to rebuild Owner's leadership system, from values to day-to-day operating procedures. @36:28
- **Record → transcript → ChatGPT guide:** his documentation-scaling hack.
- **BDRs before AEs:** fund pipeline before adding closers (credits Sam Blond).
