# E57: The New Rules of Executive Recruiting in the AI Era
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Asad Zaman, CEO @ Sales Talent Agency (STA) · **Published:** 2025-12-10 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/the-new-rules-of-executive-recruiting-in-the-ai-era-asad-zaman-ceo/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- AI has made take-home case studies low-signal. STA now uses live whiteboarding and a new "teach me something" step. Leaner, AI-leveraged teams make each senior miss more expensive.
- Asad's process runs in this order: business problem → jobs to be done → what good looks like in years 1/2/3 → must-have scorecard → constraints (usually geography and comp) → operationalize. That means mapping 100% of companies and candidates, weighting the scorecard, and assigning a test to each attribute. The job description comes last ("a marketing document").
- Kyle's contribution: he argues the "era of the pure people manager is is ending" in favor of systems-engineer leaders, and describes his senior-hire case study. Candidates review calls, then live-coach him as the rep on one thing, in depth.

## Guest's core ideas
- AI has removed the friction from homework, so case studies narrowed "the performance dispersion". STA switched to live whiteboarding about "a couple of quarters" ago and is now adding "teach me". @05:08–06:09, @53:10, @56:16–57:18 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Leaner teams mean "you have to miss a lot less". Example: one company has "about 35 salespeople for about 150 million in ARR", with quota doubled and 80% hitting. @07:10–08:11 ([[talent-density]])
- The best VPs are teachers, "one-on-one and then teaching through systems". Leaders who are strong on people alone have "less room to run". @09:15–10:16 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Talent market data: tech layoffs in 2025 were down overall, but average layoffs per company were up "more than 80%", concentrated where AI works (support, design). The best search firms see about 45% offer acceptance. Above 75% "it's as good as you can be right now". @13:23–17:29
- Clients now call out AI capability "at the front of the conversation". Advantages come from a combined effort, not one RevOps hire. @22:35–24:37 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- Scorecard building: start with the problem and jobs to be done, not HR's job description ("Job description is a marketing document"). Must-haves include stage experience, GTM/deal-motion alignment, culture, your specific org problems, hiring, forecasting, and teaching. @25:38–32:45 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Constraints (comp and geography) quietly shrink the pool. Founders often fight over "0.25" equity at the end. "if they're not good they're not going to make it to the 12-month cliff anyways". @33:45–37:46
- Operationalize: map 100% of target companies and candidates and count the viable ones. One CRO search found 11 viable, with two in New York, so the client gave on location. Engage everyone who is open. Weight the scorecard, send a sheet of everyone the candidate has hired, and use intelligence and personality tests. @41:54–49:02 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Whiteboarding tests for systems/first-principles thinking, temperament and collaboration. End-of-process questions: is this a future CRO, and are they before, at, or past their peak (Ronaldo analogy)? @58:18–63:24 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- CROs' biggest blind spot is a lack of "taste for what great is in that role". And "no one does the process. Everybody shoots from the hip." @63:24–65:28
