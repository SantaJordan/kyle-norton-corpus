# E06: Untold Strategies of High-Impact Sales Recruitment with Asad Zaman
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Asad Zaman, CEO @ STA (Sales Talent Agency; transcribed "SDA"), Topline co-host · **Published:** 2024-10-30 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E06-untold-strategies-of-high-impact-sales-recruitment-with-asad/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- This was the first episode recorded for the show. Kyle says hiring is the topic he gets asked about most. Zaman says hiring fails because the talent supply/demand gap is structural and companies treat hiring "as art, not science"; under seat-filling pressure, "nobody is better than somebody bad".
- STA's system has three stages: launch, search and last mile. Investing 3–4 hours in launch (jobs to be done, scorecard, JD, interview plan, hiring-committee decision rights, a recruiting podcast per role, a communication framework) more than doubled clients' win rates. Scorecards need a hiring memo. Talent = Drive, Nature, Acumen.
- Kyle shared his own process at Owner: a 20–25 min pre-interview video, candid job posts, a case study, mutual references (every direct report from 8 years), multi-hour resume calibration with recruiters, and LinkedIn posting as a recruiting engine.

## What Kyle said
- "this is the first recording of the podcast". Hiring is the topic he says "I get asked about the most of anything, which is how to hire incredible people." @01:05–02:05 ([[sales-hiring]], [[revenue-leadership-podcast]])
- Research says "between a third and half of sales reps don't make it through their first year"; at Owner, "we do a little better than that." @04:06 ([[sales-hiring]], [[churn-and-retention]])
- What makes a great revenue leader is thinking on a longer time horizon ("a quarter, two quarters, three quarters ahead"). Landing the plan in January with an eight-week hiring cycle, three-month ramp and six-month deal cycle means "you're already cooked". He also names a "P&L fluency gap": "you can torture a financial model and make it tell you anything". Start bottoms-up from historical ramp, deal cycle and pipeline. @09:15–10:17 ([[capacity-planning]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
- He has had managers do Pavilion's Revenue Architecture school and gives them a book list to prepare for executive jobs. @10:17–11:18 ([[career-and-leadership]], [[pavilion]])
- Home-grow talent: it is "much easier to sway an early career first, second, third year rep into your system versus somebody paying a premium at year five and six." @13:22 ([[talent-density]])
- Owner's communication framework is "organic". He has expectations for "the quality of notes that people leave in our ATS". "I don't want you to tell me about a candidate before I meet them because I want to be unbiased". @21:46 ([[hiring-scorecards]], [[owner-com]])
- Owner candidates get "a 20, 25 minute video of me talking about the role, our culture, the tradeoffs". His job posts say "what's good and what's not good about the company", and there is a case study. He hears that candidates are impressed by "how like operationally rigorous we are", and believes it lifts close rates. @26:55–27:57 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Mutual references: when he is ready to make an offer, he sends a list of every direct report "since my first head of sales role" (eight years), plus CS and marketing partners, with phone numbers and LinkedIn URLs, about "twenty five names". He also asks permission to find his own back-channel references. @30:00 ([[sales-hiring]])
- LinkedIn as recruiting ROI: "hundreds of thousands of dollars" in saved fees "just for me posting twice a week", which "took a bunch of years". @37:09 ([[sales-hiring]], [[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Recruiter calibration: his VP of Sales "just spent like a multiple hours on a screen share with our sales recruiter" reviewing resumes. At Shopify he sat with two recruiters "in a boardroom for like multiple hours" against "a fairly detailed written scorecard". Without that, "you just end up with seven first rounds to get one case study." @39:12–40:13 ([[hiring-scorecards]], [[shopify]])
- Takeaways: "give me another one" was "a light bulb for me". He agrees on servant leadership. @55:41, @57:48 ([[hiring-scorecards]], [[career-and-leadership]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Supply/demand: tech companies grow "at 7% per quarter" while tech talent grows "at 2% per year". Everyone fights over the top 10%. @05:08, @12:21 ([[talent-density]])
- Plan-driven hiring in Q1 puts you behind. Hire in Q4 so people are ramped for the year (citing John McMahon). @07:11–09:15 ([[capacity-planning]])
- Launch phase: jobs to be done, then scorecard, JD, interview process (who qualifies what, and are they equipped), and hiring-committee decision rights ("doesn't mean it's a decision by committee"). Add a private 30-min recruiting podcast per role ("No one elite joins a company for a job. They join for a journey") and a communication framework for debriefs. @16:30–22:49 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Only about 5% of clients have a "legit" scorecard. Clarify constraints (comp, location radius) and options before going to market. @23:51–26:55 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Search: employer brand and network, job boards ("luck isn't strategy"), and headhunting, which someone must own full-time. Generalist HR or player-coach managers won't do it. A fintech's recruiters carried 25–40 reqs vs STA's 5–9. @31:02–39:12 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Hiring memo: five questions. Why will this succeed? How do I amplify it? Why might it fail? How do I hedge? Would I enjoy working with them? @42:20–45:26 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Scorecard = talent + experience. Talent is Drive (resiliency + work ethic, unteachable; ask for three hardship examples), Nature (EQ balanced to stage; "strategic empathy") and Acumen (focused curiosity + intelligence; "what's something really important that you change your mind about recently"). @45:26–55:41 ([[hiring-scorecards]], [[sales-hiring]])
- Leaders hold an "unwritten contract" to make each report the best professional they can be. @56:44–57:48 ([[career-and-leadership]])
