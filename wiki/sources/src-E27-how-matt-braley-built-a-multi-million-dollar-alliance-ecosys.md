# E27: How Matt Braley Built a Multi-Million Dollar Alliance Ecosystem at InvoiceCloud
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Matt Braley, former CRO @ InvoiceCloud (AE #3 at $0.5M ARR → first CRO at $28M → $170M and a $4B Vista acquisition); now advising and investing · **Published:** 2025-04-02 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E27-how-matt-braley-built-a-multi-million-dollar-alliance-ecosys/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- InvoiceCloud's technical alliances with vertical core-system and ERP vendors (grown from 15 to 50 partners) produced over half of bookings. Partner reps opened the door so deals "start at step three". Partners also warned of at-risk accounts, which helped NRR.
- Most partnerships fail as "paper partnerships" because they aren't qualified with enterprise-sale rigor. Braley's new **VECTOR** framework: Value (for me, for them, for the market), Economics & incentives (down to the partner's AEs/CSMs: "why is this going to help you hit quota?"), Co-selling motion, Technical integration, Organizational commitment, Results & metrics (KPI-tiered rev share).
- Kyle's takeaway: the key test is incremental value over going direct, given long payoffs. Owner's big food-distributor referral partnership (3,000 field sellers) took about six months to activate and another six before closed-won deals were material, even with every advantage.

## Guest's core ideas
- **The alliance type that works:** technical integration plus joint GTM ("one plus one equals three"). Resellers produced only "a trickle" because it's hard to teach a third party a complex, long-cycle sale. Partner AEs and CSMs join the first meeting, then help with account mapping and stuck deals. Rev share flows to the partner. @03:07–14:24 ([[org-design]])
- **Partner motives:** market leaders want new logos, while laggards want retention, a new revenue stream and a moat. "understanding the partner's motivation is critical". @14:24–15:26
- **Paper partnerships:** caused by "a lack of upfront alignment and qualification". Run a full enterprise cycle (multithreading, mutual action plans, champions), then a joint product launch across two companies. "Medic... was built for enterprise sales". @05:12–19:32 ([[sales-methodology]])
- **Fit:** best for ACV around $40–50K+, vertical SaaS or vertically oriented GTM (overlapping ICP via an **ideal partner profile**, IPP), and a differentiated product that isn't easily replicated. InvoiceCloud overlapped 10–25% with partners' light versions, and partners often stopped selling their own. Target systems of record and ERPs, which have the most "pull" with customers. It needs exec-level, cross-functional commitment, "can't be a side project". @20:33–27:42 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- **VECTOR details:** set a value "floor" (e.g., warm intros to 25% of base, pipeline in 12 months) and compare with "what would it cost... to just go it alone?" Partner reps retired quota on InvoiceCloud only about 10% of the time, so use stickiness and spiffs instead. Co-sell via 80/20 account mapping, biweekly rep pairings, and "A lot of spreadsheets" and Teams channels. Technical integration ranges from API data exchange to an embedded joint product. Commitment means a champion/owner, QBRs and certification. Results are KPI-tiered rev share. @27:42–64:48 ([[comp-plans]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- **Field data and plays:** SDRs cold-called US utilities for about 1.5 years to map their ERPs (75–80% coverage of about 15,000 utilities across 200+ ERPs). Partner user groups were a top pipeline driver (buy lunch, 30-minute slot, customer co-present), and partner marketing was under-indexed. @52:27–53:29, @60:43–62:45 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- **Building alliance pipeline:** start with the top 3–5 IPP targets and go two-pronged, with SDRs and AEs hitting partners' customers while the CRO or alliance lead approaches partner executives. Build the hypothesis from end users. @64:48–68:52
- **Quickfire:** great CROs are "effective at all altitudes". "Great leaders make everyone around them better, up, down, and sideways." "You have to let people fail." Best sellers are "200% better at disqualifying opportunities". @73:59–77:03 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[career-and-leadership]])
