# Outbound Kitchen: Why AI SDRs Are Dead Wrong - Inside Owner.com's $21M ARR Growth Strategy

**Show / host:** Outbound Kitchen / Elric Legloire · **Published:** 2025-01-31 (YouTube upload date) · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/outbound-kitchen-ai-sdrs-dead-wrong/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O0s7vdn2-MM

Part 2 of a two-part conversation; part 1 is [[src-outbound-kitchen-scale-outbound-sales]]. The episode title's "$21M ARR" and the intro's "3 million to 21 million RR in 22 months" are the host's framing (@00:00), echoing the figure Kyle gave on SaaStr ([[src-saastr-gtm-efficiency-smb]]).

## TL;DR
- Kyle says most AI SDR usage (~95%) is a "race to the bottom" carpet-bomb; the higher-leverage AI is internal: Momentum for CRM fill/alerts/SPIFF tracking, ML lead scoring, and Avara AI role-play sims that are driving record ramp times.
- Annual planning = triangulating a top-down company target against a bottoms-up funnel model, then stack-ranking projects with Annie Duke's "four P's"; current outbound focus is BDR book-to-show rate, attacked through habit/environment design (James Clear, nudge theory) rather than exhortation.
- Owner is moving SDR/BDR hiring to a five-day-a-week Toronto office based on peer pilots; in hindsight he'd have done it, and senior hires (second-line BDR leader, VP of Sales), earlier.

## What Kyle argued
- AI SDRs aren't impossible but ~95% of current use is "pretty crap" and a race to the bottom: a short-term pop from latent demand, then fall-off (@01:02–@02:07). See [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]].
- Done well (he cites [[person-jordan-crawford]] and Florin Tatulea), high-intent, tailored personalization at scale works, but it's "the exception, not the norm" (@02:07, @05:21). Generic AI email ("bot breath") is a brand/reputation risk and risks inbox providers clamping down (@03:11).
- AI outbound is a return of old-school growth marketing (list + dynamic fields + volume) (@04:16).
- Internal AI use cases are more powerful than external ones because LLMs excel at pattern-matching / fill-in-the-blank work (@06:26). See [[ai-in-gtm]], [[ai-sales-stack]].
- Momentum runs custom prompts over cold call, demo and post-sale transcripts to fill Salesforce fields, alert managers, and automatically score SPIFF behaviour (e.g. did the rep use the new research doc on the call) (@07:28–@08:30); keyword alerts flag unqualified meetings booked anyway, "like guardrails, just like… bumpers down the bowling lane" (@16:56–@17:59).
- Avara AI sims (trained on Owner's methodology, scripts, Hall-of-Fame demos and transcripts) beat managers as mock-call partners because ~80% of calls fall into a few outcome buckets LLMs replicate well, and reps practice more without judgment (@08:30–@13:42). See [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]].
- He picked Avara over Hyperbound for customizability and speed of iteration; with early-stage vendors he asks "how fast can you move with me?" (@14:45). He resists generic AI co-pilots built on MEDDIC; he wants models tuned to his own SMB framework (@15:51). See [[build-vs-buy-software]].
- Annual planning: top-down target vs bottoms-up model of inputs and conversion rates ("basically it's a manufacturing line"), triangulated; the gap forces problem-solving, including new channels like partnerships/referrals (@18:59–@23:08). See [[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[capacity-planning]].
- Prioritize funnel projects with four P's from Annie Duke: potentials, payoff, probability, perspiration (@21:04–@22:04).
- Don't just tell reps to "do better"; build systems (reminders, Salesloft prompts, a data layer, a dashboard metric) so every BDR runs the sell-between/confirmation playbook every time (@25:13–@26:14). See [[playbooks-and-documentation]].
- Hiring pace is bounded by ramp lag/CAC payback (payments monetization) and above all management capacity — "usually the place that things fall apart" — plus documentation quality (@31:31–@33:42). See [[capacity-planning]], [[org-design]].
- In-person beats remote for early-career SMB sales; peers' pilots all favored in-person; field sales is too inefficient vs 3–4 held demos/day (@34:44–@43:02). See [[smb-sales-motion]].

## Numbers & facts he cited
- BDR team is ~90% phone (host suggested 95%) (@06:26).
- Jordan Crawford's prompt chain: 86 prompts from account to email sent (@05:21).
- New AE cohort (BDR promotes) did ~5–7 hours of sims each; both on pace to beat quota, one fastest-ever BDR-promote to quota (@09:33–@10:38). New SDRs run ~100 sims (@10:38).
- Company goal: "more than double in 2025" (@18:59). Bottoms-up example: scale ~70% by adding headcount before things break (@20:01).
- Illustrative funnel lever: AQL-to-book rate "only 40%" (@23:08).
- Prior company (two companies ago): 25% of BDR activity on tier-C accounts, 30% on tier A; after a dashboard module made it visible, it "snapped to like 80%" into A-tier within weeks (@29:22–@30:28).
- A ramped manager can run ~10 reps; won't give a green manager nine (@32:37). Planning a cohort of ~10 new hires in March 2025 (@33:42–@34:44).
- New hires: CPO and VP of Engineering; Q1 he'd rather fund engineering capacity (@33:42).
- Peer in-person pilots: ramp 20–40% faster; productivity +30% up to 2x; one 100+ BDR company saw >60% higher activity per person in-person (@36:50). One dinner contact ran 15 in-person vs 15 remote BDRs (@35:46).
- SDR/BDR now hired only in Toronto, five days in office (@39:54).
- Owner AEs run 3–4 held demos a day (@42:01). At Shopify, field sales would have needed an 80% win rate to work, so it was killed (@43:02).
- BDRs produce ~6–7k MRR every month; +20% across 30 BDRs would be "real revenue" (@45:08).
- VP of Sales has been in seat "close to a year… Maybe only nine months" (@47:12). "We're growing faster today than we were at any point in the first half of last year." (@47:12)

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Internal vs external AI use cases** (@06:26).
- **Top-down / bottoms-up triangulation** ("W framework") for annual planning (@21:04).
- **Four P's** (Annie Duke): potentials, payoff, probability, perspiration (@21:04–@23:08).
- **Secret sauce** — each rep's one monthly skill focus; managers coach one thing at a time (@27:15).
- **Habit/environment design** — James Clear's *Atomic Habits* ("make it easy, make it visible, make it rewarding") and Thaler's *Nudge* (@27:15–@29:22).
- **Sell-between / meeting-confirmation playbook** for book-to-show (@24:10–@25:13).
- **Non-dual thinking** (@01:02).

## Stories / anecdotes
- Jordan Crawford ran Owner through his 86-prompt chain; Kyle said "I would totally send that" (@05:21).
- UK organ-donor opt-out as the nudge example (@28:20).
- Steve (head of RevOps, two companies ago) silently added an "activities by account tier" module to the BDR dashboard; behaviour shifted with no announcement (@29:22–@30:28).
- Early return-to-office advocates were unconvincing because they'd done remote enablement badly ("one person in enablement and they've got 40 reps") (@35:46); "KD" was a major influence on the office decision (@38:52).
- Two people in his wedding party came from his first job's in-person team (@37:51).
- Hindsight: would have run the in-person experiment ~12 months earlier, hired a second-line BDR leader and VP of Sales earlier; head of RevOps and head of enablement hires visibly lifted results (@44:07–@47:12).
