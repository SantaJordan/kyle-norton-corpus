# E49: All about AI and GTM at Clay - Round Two with Everett Berry
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Everett Berry, Head of GTM Engineering @ Clay · **Published:** 2025-10-15 (RSS; metadata has no date) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/all-about-ai-and-gtm-at-clay-round-two-with-everett-berry/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- A listener-sourced AI Q&A and a follow-up to [[src-E45-the-inside-scoop-on-gtm-engineering-with-clays-everett-berr]]. About "60%" of listener questions asked how [[clay]] runs internally. Clay has an external forward-deployed GTM engineering team and an internal team of about 6 GTM engineers that runs like an engineering org (tickets, sprints, twice-monthly releases).
- Kyle's thesis: the rep-facing surface is up for grabs for the first time in a decade. Sales-engagement incumbents are email-centric, and Owner sells by phone to mom-and-pops, so Owner may custom-build its stack.
- Both expect rep bifurcation, not fewer reps: creative, solution-oriented sellers become more valuable. To win AI budget, build something and show results. Hire a GTM engineer as early as possible.

## Guest's core ideas
- Clay's internal GTM engineering team (about 6, led by Osman, reporting to co-founder Varun) serves CX, marketing, growth, partnerships and the forward-deployed team. It triages tickets, ships twice-monthly releases with notes, and version-controls work. The stack is Clay, Snowflake, Salesforce, Gong and a Slack app. Handoff and QBR decks are auto-generated from credit consumption, Gong and Salesforce data. @03:02–07:07 ([[gtm-engineering]], [[revops]])
- RevOps sits half in that team and half in finance. Clay is about 50/50 self-serve and sales-led, at "somewhere around 100 million in revenue", with no CRO yet. Unifying self-serve into sales-led is a major initiative. @07:07–12:14 ([[revops]], [[org-design]])
- Other AI tools: Dust (Q&A agent over internal data and Slack), Crosby (first-pass legal redlines, cutting response "from like a few days to like hours"), and Granola/Attention. Gong is always on as "the piping". @13:16–16:20 ([[ai-sales-stack]])
- The top internal use case is content creation (follow-ups, decks). Clay's native sequencer isn't for 50,000+ emails a month. He praises Gong Engage's per-contact sequence API. He sees a future single-pane rep interface orchestrated by agents and Clay, plus DM-channel selling (WhatsApp, iMessage). @18:21–30:35 ([[ai-sales-stack]])
- AI won't mean far fewer reps. Creative, expert, solution-oriented selling grows. A strong SMB seller could manage a thousand-account book by instructing agents. @33:38–40:44 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]])
- To win AI budget, "showing by doing". If leadership still won't believe, "maybe that's not the right company for you". @44:48–45:48 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- Hire a GTM engineer first, alongside a founding AE, an SE/expert and an agency. Their first task is the "golden list" of accounts. @46:49–48:52 ([[gtm-engineering]], [[icp-and-saying-no]])
