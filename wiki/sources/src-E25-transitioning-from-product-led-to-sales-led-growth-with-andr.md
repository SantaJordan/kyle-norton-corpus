# E25: Transitioning from Product-led to Sales-led Growth with Andrew Johnston of Superhuman
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Andrew Johnston, VP Sales @ Superhuman (ex-SendGrid/Twilio head of SendGrid sales, Scale AI) · **Published:** 2025-03-19 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host (and a self-described Superhuman "super user"; Superhuman was also the show's sponsor in this period) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E25-transitioning-from-product-led-to-sales-led-growth-with-andr/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Johnston's PLG → SLG pillars: re-derive the ICP for a *buyer* (not just a user), data and instrumentation (product signals surfaced in Salesforce and Pocus), product for the buyer (SAML/SCIM, team features), team (hunters plus retrained PLG reps, SEs, GTM ops) and positioning that reframes Superhuman as a team product. The common failure is not adapting the product to the new buyer.
- Superhuman's enterprise motion goes top-down from executive power users (a COO champion) and expands down. Email "inherently sucks" for anyone with 30–50+ emails a day. Culture shift comes from exec buy-in, bringing engineers into deals and MEDDIC.
- Kyle's most detailed personal-AI walkthrough of this era: ChatGPT Deep Research and o1 Pro for problem-solving, Claude projects trained on his writing for the newsletter (a 4-hour piece in about 30 minutes), meta-prompting, and projects over custom GPTs. Owner "just yanked out GONG" for Momentum.

## What Kyle said
- **Category typecasting:** once you're a senior revenue person in a category, "your unique value to those companies is so different than your value to any other B2B company that it's hard to unstick yourself". @04:31 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- **Superhuman super user:** a customer for "a couple of years". He has "maybe got two or three official referrals out of 30. Because I'm always berating people about email best practice." His pitch is "shortcuts and templates everywhere". He gets through email in "maybe a quarter of the time" using snippets (podcast scheduling, interviews) and reminders, "because I like zero inbox." @04:31–09:46 ([[personal-ai-stack]])
- **Shopify's enterprise perception lag:** at Shopify (2019–2022) "people still really felt like Shopify was a mom-and-pop product" while it was winning "the Mattels of the world" and had enterprise customers "for like five, six years". "those preconceptions, they don't go away very easily." Shopify "was only product-led for years and years" and early Plus was basically "we'll give you a CSM". @19:10–20:15 ([[shopify]], [[marketing-alignment]])
- **Give others the sales bug:** "even if you're a PM or an eng... if those people are involved tangentially and are involved in a win... that feels fun. We've had some product folks involved in deals and saves and stuff here." @35:04–36:06 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- **Gong out, Momentum in:** "I just yanked out GONG a couple months ago, because it was just so expensive. And we're doing it through Momentum". It is "not feature parity to GONG today. But for a fraction of the cost and to consolidate just in one vendor, we took the plunge pretty early." He recommends CRM enrichment as an AI starting point: "It's pretty low stakes, really high value". @39:17–40:23 ([[momentum]], [[ai-sales-stack]], [[build-vs-buy-software]])
- **Board tie:** Synch's seed round was "led by Jack Altman, who's on our board, who I know well." @42:31 ([[owner-com]])
- **Rep opportunity:** "I would kill to be a rep right now in a company that has some AI in go-to-market but not a lot because I would be in there building clay tables and running my own automations and going completely rogue." At Owner "We're doing most of it top down... We're SMB". @43:36–44:36 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[clay]], [[gtm-engineering]])
- **His personal AI stack:** "Operator is not that functional." "I use Deep Research all the time, constantly." He dictates via Super Whisper into o1 Pro and Deep Research for problem-solving and synthesis ("tell me what work streams I'm missing"). "Clod, I use for writing". Claude learned his writing style, and a podcast transcript fed into his "newsletter writer" yields a draft that needs "a 20 to 30-minute edit". "this is like a four-hour piece of writing. And it took me 30 minutes." @44:36–46:47 ([[personal-ai-stack]], [[context-harness-and-memory]])
- **Workflows make the output possible:** "I really don't think I could do the podcast and write the newsletter and keep doing LinkedIn content without some of these workflows." For any repeated task he spends "20 minutes, do this meta prompting process". "I've stopped using custom GPTs. I just use projects now in OpenAI". @46:47–47:50 ([[personal-ai-stack]])
- **Health and gambling projects:** a ChatGPT health project holds "my aura ring data, and like a health dossier". He told it to "ask me as many questions as you need to understand my health profile", then ran Deep Research on hair loss ("basically none of the crap that people are like trying to shove to you through Instagram ads"). A Deep Research/o1 Pro gambling model was "hitting it like 80% correct for a while". "I'm obsessed." @47:50–49:55 ([[personal-ai-stack]], [[context-harness-and-memory]])
- **"Bot breath":** "so much of the output is like really generic, and it has bot breath". A clearly AI-generated email leaves him "sort of offended". He uses Superhuman AI replies for low-stakes mail but hasn't "graduated to like responding to... higher stakes" with AI. @49:55–50:59 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]], [[outbound-at-scale]])
- **Product feedback:** he wants tunable AI replies (e.g., CC Viviana and defer scheduling, less formal, trained on his writing). For now he uses vendor, partner, interview and personal snippets. @53:09, @55:14

## Guest's core ideas
- **Why Superhuman:** "imagine if you took Gmail, and you redesigned it in 2025". AI labels by prompt, splits, auto-reminders, auto-drafts. @05:33–09:46
- **Motion history:** after the 2021 Series C, it started consolidating 3–10-seat SMB pockets onto contracts, then moved upmarket. The ICP was derived from PLG buyers and refined for someone who brings the product to an org. @10:48–12:54 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- **Top-down inversion:** execs are the users (a Fortune 500 COO champion texts him), so the hard part is going from 100 users to 75% of an org. Cold outbound goes to lookalikes of wins (a Big Four firm's ~4,000-MD cohort). @12:54–16:03 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- **Five pillars:** ICP, data and signals, product for buyer and org, team, and marketing/positioning ("pre-nurture them with the messaging" that it's a team product). PLG "ultimately is instrumentation". @17:05–19:10, @30:50–31:52 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- **PLG vs SLG sellers:** PLG sellers unblock and make it easy to buy. SLG needs hunters running a tighter MEDDIC process. PLG reps transition best in dev tools. Most of the original 3 AEs stayed (some became account managers). He hired enterprise reps, a lead SE and GTM ops, and screens for prior product advocates. @20:15–26:41 ([[sales-hiring]])
- **Data:** a strong B2C analytics team means the job is translation. Pocus scores "plays" (e.g., a company with 3–4 users at 50%+ monthly engagement) and pushes them to sequencers. The most personal touches are VP-sent from Superhuman. @26:41–30:50 ([[ai-sales-stack]])
- **Culture:** M-team buy-in to "going B2B", bringing CTO and engineers into deals, celebrating the big consulting win company-wide, and giving other executives pipeline "quotas" for intros. @32:54–37:09 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- **AI in GTM:** Gong/Wiser CRM enrichment, an RFP tool (one SE turns 250-question RFPs in under 24h), Synch for forecasting and natural-language reports, Claude and ChatGPT licenses for all, and an "Andrew interview GPT" to check his own bias. Reps need sharper soft skills and to be "a builder". @37:09–44:36, @50:59–53:09 ([[ai-sales-stack]])
- **Quickfire:** great leaders "get your hands dirty". Advice: "raise your altitude". Lesson: "Go slow to go fast", because he has made one-way-door decisions by accident. @56:20–59:31
