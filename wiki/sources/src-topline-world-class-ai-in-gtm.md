# Topline (Pavilion): What World-Class AI in GTM Looks Like

**Show / host:** Topline (Pavilion). Co-hosts are Sam Jacobs (CEO, Pavilion; left early for a board call), AJ (Quotapath; "Ryan, our CRO"), and Asad Zaman (CEO, Sales Talent Agency) · **Published:** 2026-04-05 · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/topline-world-class-ai-in-gtm/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-1w3tRUX0JI

*Attribution note: this is a four-voice panel with no speaker labels. The Quotapath stats (71% without quotas, "250% higher chance" of quarterly comp changes), the "existential crisis," the product-vs-services centralization split, and the founder-mode skepticism belong to the co-hosts, not Kyle.*

## TL;DR
- Start with **data foundations**. Third-party data means a mapped, enriched TAM/SOM with mobile numbers. First-party data means every call parsed into fields (Momentum). With those in place, Owner's BDRs went from a 3–4% connect rate to 20 decision makers a day, booking at 14–16%, and closed-won ARR per BDR comp went "well over 10x."
- **Centralized beats decentralized AI.** Kyle pushed broad AI adoption ~12 months earlier, but the impactful implementations were all central. An applied AI lead's output is "an order of magnitude" better than a savvy AE's. Own the intelligence layer; buy the workflow/UX (e.g., sales engagement).
- Spend **80% of early AI effort on pipeline**. Choose bets with the 5P framework (possibilities, payoff, probability, perspiration). There are "no excuses": fund an applied AI hire by cutting bottom performers.

- Improve the scaffolding daily. A new senior data scientist rebuilt the customer-size model. Owner built a pickup-likelihood score, and high-connect leads pick up at ~2.3x @08:27. [[metrics-and-forecasting]]
- BDR economics flipped once the data foundation was in place @08:27–09:31. [[smb-sales-motion]] [[outbound-at-scale]]

His Substack piece on this was "by far the most popular" he has written @10:36. [[ai-in-gtm]] [[org-design]]
- Deploy into existing surfaces (Salesforce, Salesloft). They did not vibe-code a pre-call prep app: "I don't want additional surfaces" @12:40. [[ai-sales-stack]]
- Build versus buy: own the intelligence (deal health score, estimated win rate, lead prioritization) and buy workflows/UX. He hasn't seen a sales-engagement vendor build viable intelligence. Don't vibe-code what reps use daily, because you need "rock solid stability." He discussed this at the Clay CRO summit @16:51–19:00. [[build-vs-buy-software]] [[clay]]
- Keep GTM intelligence central and separate from product AI. Everything market-facing, from marketing to support, shares skills, context files, and prompts @31:36. [[gtm-engineering]]
- Engineers: "if 20% or more of your code is handwritten still, you are very behind the market" @30:33.
- Prioritize with the 5P framework (from Annie Duke). Map possibilities, then weigh payoff × probability = expected value, then perspiration @33:40–34:45. [[revops]]
- 80% of early AI effort should go to pipeline. The AI website grader is "the world's best lead magnet" @35:50. 80% of the AI lead's work is pipe-gen. Weekly tests cover call-time-of-day, meetings scheduled into low-connect windows, time to first touch, and AI pre-call research of "Just the three things" @36:57–37:58.
