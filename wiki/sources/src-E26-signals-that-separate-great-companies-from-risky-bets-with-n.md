# E26: Signals That Separate Great Companies from Risky Bets with Neal Patel from Crunchbase
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Neal Patel, CRO @ Crunchbase (ex-Google Maps, search partnerships, Google Fiber; former corporate attorney) · **Published:** 2025-03-26 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E26-signals-that-separate-great-companies-from-risky-bets-with-n/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Patel's AI thesis: agents will disintermediate app UIs the way Google disintermediated browsing, and the GTM stack may shrink from 10–15 tools to "two or three AI-driven platforms". Crunchbase answered its own disruption risk (scrapable historical data) by pivoting, after a customer tour with its CPO, to predictions built on proprietary engagement data.
- For CROs choosing a company, his advice: verify PMF claims by back-channeling customers and end users, test executive peer fit over meals ("my job is to make you look like a hero"), probe how leadership would answer a hypothetical $1B offer, avoid businesses that rely only on scrapable data or thin workflow, and "go someplace that wants you to adapt".
- Kyle reports he is "probably the most deeply entrenched in AI right now of anybody in my go-to-market team". He vibe-coded a calendar-and-Notion weekly-update app himself. Owner has tried "six major AI use cases" (one failed), and he calls the resulting productivity and LTV:CAC edge "a very durable business advantage for us."

## What Kyle said
- **Apps as UX on a database:** "I think Chamath said it really well... you look at a bunch of these enterprise apps and they're just a workflow. It's just UX on top of a CRUD database." Value may accrue unexpectedly: "does Salesforce become the most important part of your stack? Like, does this make Snowflake like way more important than it's ever been?" @26:54–27:54 ([[build-vs-buy-software]], [[ai-sales-stack]])
- **Leading AI from the front:** "unfortunately, I am probably the most deeply entrenched in AI right now of anybody in my go-to-market team... I would prefer that not to be the case". "I just have such a strong conviction that this will touch every single part of our business." @31:59 ([[ai-operating-model]], [[ai-in-gtm]])
- **Weekend vibe-coding:** in San Francisco after his family went home, he and an engineer built "an app to try to pull out data from everybody's Google calendar and synthesize that with what's in Notion and then like pre-build their weekly update" for his sales leaders. It won't move the needle much, but it gave him "exposure to vibe coding" (including OAuth) and "a mental model of how to think about this stack." @31:59–33:02 ([[personal-ai-stack]], [[operating-rhythm]])
- **Two levels of abstraction:** "one of the important things about being a great CRO or revenue leader is you have to live at two levels of abstraction". Think years ahead about agents, and also learn the discrete inputs ("How is an agent different than just an AI workflow?"). "I'm really pushing my organization to just like get exposure and wrap their heads around the foundations of these tools". @33:02–34:02 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[ai-sophistication-ladder]])
- **Against waiting for winners:** "we've got like six major AI use cases... One of them didn't really work". Even at two failures and three successes, the cost of not pursuing them "is much too great in my opinion". Waiters end up "12, 18, 24 months behind", and "you just better hope that your direct competitor is not farther along". "for us, the advantage in rep productivity and like revenue growth and the LTV CAC profile is like pretty profound. Like it is a very durable business advantage for us." @36:08–38:12 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[owner-com]])
- **Horse race:** "you'd rather bet on the wrong horse than not be in the race because at least you're learning to ride a horse." @40:14
- **Back-channel without a network:** "You can just cold reach out to people on LinkedIn." He tells candidates for his team, "I don't give references. I just tell you to go find your own". Find people who have been there "more than six months. So they're out of the honeymoon period". His teams are free to talk to candidates who reach out, and ex-employees are "another great back channel". "There's no real alternative than sitting down with somebody for three hours over a meal." @48:20–50:23 ([[sales-hiring]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- **Objections:** at Chris Orlob's pclub course, "one of the biggest things I tried to emphasize is we want objections. You want to pull out objections so that you can address them head on". @65:40–66:41 ([[sales-methodology]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- **Knowing when to fold:** "The best poker player is no one to fold." He cites Annie Duke's book on quitting. @67:43 ([[person-annie-duke]], [[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- **Newsletters:** "I often joke I have knowledge FOMO." @68:45

## Guest's core ideas
- **Macro:** more optimism (IPO and M&A thawing on AI). "a little bit of something is better than nothing". Balance macro awareness with the quarterly number, because reps "are paying their rent by that monthly number". @05:06–12:25 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- **Agents disintermediate apps:** like travel sites losing users to booking agents, enterprise apps risk becoming "invisible infrastructure sitting behind the UI of an agent". LLM data harvesting threatens data businesses. @07:13–10:22, @23:51–26:54 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]], [[build-vs-buy-software]])
- **Customer tour → predictions:** customers asked for features competitors already had (contact data). The real insight was "Every piece of data that anybody has about a company is historical", while buyers care about the future. Crunchbase used 10+ years of profile-change history and engagement from 80M users to predict fundraising and M&A (e.g., Coda→Grammarly, Hippocratic AI). @12:25–19:40, @21:45–22:46 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- **Future GTM roles:** humans weight toward relationships, creative strategy, negotiation and customized solution selling. Start with assistant tools now to condition people. "you're not gonna have a choice not to use AI". Spend at least 10% of your time learning, or your plan "only works if you plan on retiring in two years". @28:55–31:59, @35:07–36:08 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- **Company selection:** verify revenue, retention and PMF via customer and end-user back-channels, where you may find upside ("predict the future of that company"). Test exec peer fit in person. Ask how leadership would weigh a 10x acquisition offer and judge *how* they answer. Peel "big market" labels down to problems and propensity to pay. @42:15–57:32 ([[career-and-leadership]], [[fundraising-and-investability]])
- **AI-era red flags:** companies built solely on scrapable data or simple workflow features. Proprietary, dynamic data (Crunchbase's engagement and edit history) and human-relationship or creative-strategy sales are harder to disrupt. Beware roles that just want you to rerun your old playbook. @57:32–63:37 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- **Quickfire:** a great CRO is defined by "What you do with what you have". "create the context for your success" ("if you've objection handling, you're already lost"), setting up "heads we win, tails we still kind of win". Lesson: "I can't solve every problem and I shouldn't." @63:37–68:45 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
