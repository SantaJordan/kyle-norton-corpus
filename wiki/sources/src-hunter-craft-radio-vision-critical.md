# Hunter & Craft Radio: Radio Episode #12: Kyle Norton of Vision Critical

**Show / host:** Hunter & Craft Radio / Evan Lewis · **Published:** 2015-09-28 · **Kyle's role:** guest (then Director of Inside Sales, Vision Critical, Toronto) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/hunter-craft-radio-vision-critical/transcript.md` · **Audio:** https://feeds.soundcloud.com/stream/225891967-hunterandcraft-kyle-norton-vision-critical.m4a

**Speaker check — confirmed same Kyle Norton.** Biographical markers match the rest of the wiki: Vancouver-born, Queen's business school, boiler-room start (World Trade Group, 150 dials/day), mixed martial arts / jiu-jitsu, cold-calling Vision Critical's EVP of sales to break into tech (same story he tells in [[src-dmz-chat-shopify-2020]]), and the SalesLoft Cadence rollout at Vision Critical he cites a decade later in [[src-20vc-20sales-2m-to-40m-arr]] @62:02–63:03. Earliest source in the wiki — ~7 years before Owner.

## TL;DR
- Career origin story: boiler-room event sales at World Trade Group (undifferentiated product → pure persuasion), co-founded the Open Mat MMA gym (self-taught AdWords/SEO/copywriting funnel), then cold-called his way into Vision Critical to run inside sales during its consulting→SaaS transition.
- Early versions of lifelong themes: test everything ("it's all about testing and iteration"), learn from people better than you / read constantly, build sales tech + enablement systems, daily coaching rituals, "servant leadership" and positivity over 1980s scared-straight management.
- Cold-call method "checkpoint selling": gravitas → credibility → relevance → intrigue, then stop talking and ask questions.

## What Kyle argued (2015)
- World Trade Group taught one-to-one persuasion with no product differentiation: "150 calls every single day", hard urgency and closing; skills "translate anywhere" @02:02–04:04. [[outbound-at-scale]] [[career-and-leadership]]
- Biggest early lesson: learn from people better than you — read, sit beside top reps, then test messaging; there's no universal rule (he says "how are you doing today?" on every call) @04:04–05:04. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- **Checkpoint selling** (racing-game metaphor): tonality/"gravitas", instant credibility (name-drop), relevance (specific pain, not generic), intrigue (mystery, no feature dump) — then switch to genuinely curious questions @05:04–09:11. [[sales-methodology]]
- Over-prepare: research so you can "teach the person something that they don't already know" @09:11–10:12. [[sales-methodology]]
- Martial arts built his sales mentality — getting beaten for months while staying driven; "every kid should do two years of martial arts" @10:12–12:14. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Open Mat MMA gym: co-founded with head instructor Elliot (5,000 sq ft, "kicked the ladder out"); marketing is "very scientific" (A/B tests, conversion rates) "but at the same time, it's just about people"; brought rigorous A/B testing back into sales @12:14–18:25. [[marketing-alignment]] [[metrics-and-forecasting]]
- Advice to any founder or new rep: learn from experts and don't reinvent the wheel; young reps given good systems still "reinvent the wheel"; haircut your plan by 25% and "Sell your ass off" @23:33–26:37. [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- Broke into tech with a methodical target list of 5–10 companies vetted by a VC friend, then cold-called the EVP of sales (Aaron Ross's "lost lamb" approach); job was created for him @26:37–30:45. [[career-and-leadership]]
- Sales stack: evaluated 20+ tools in weeks; chose SalesLoft (over Tout/Outreach/Sendbloom), DiscoverOrg for direct dials ("I'm still a believer in the phone"), Ambition for a weighted single daily-productivity score; tools need a training/rollout plan with enablement @33:54–37:59. [[ai-sales-stack]] [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Multichannel: there's no universal best-practice cadence — "it changes business by business and you don't know unless you test"; use phone, email, LinkedIn, Twitter, challenger-style messaging @40:00–43:05. [[outbound-at-scale]]
- Leader mood matters: put on a "brave face" for your people; positivity, encouragement and recognition beat "high pressure, maximum accountability" management; empathy — "people are people", not everyone is money-hungry @45:09–48:12. [[recognition-and-motivation]] [[culture-and-high-standards]]
- Credibility comes from being able to do the job, but caring beats "chest thumping"; "servant leadership is the best way to say it" (he attributes the book to "Daniel Coleman", likely a mix-up) @48:12–51:16. [[culture-and-high-standards]]
- Daily 8:30 "fire drill": shout-outs, yesterday's numbers, a learning; 2-week themes (questioning, then "prospecting with posture"/assertiveness); weekly training; one-on-one coaching is "a really big differentiator" @51:16–54:21. [[operating-rhythm]] [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Self-described as not naturally organized (ClearFit profile: lowest organization score) — compensates by calendaring everything; loves designing structure but dislikes bureaucracy @57:25–59:28. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Learning habit: audiobooks in the shower, on walks and while lifting @59:28–61:31. [[career-and-leadership]]
