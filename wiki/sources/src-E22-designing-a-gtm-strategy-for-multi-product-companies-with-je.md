# E22: Designing a GTM Strategy for Multi-Product Companies with Jeff Perry, CRO of Carta
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Jeff Perry, CRO @ Carta (ex-Oracle 13 yrs, DocuSign VP commercial SMB) · **Published:** 2025-02-26 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E22-designing-a-gtm-strategy-for-multi-product-companies-with-je/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Carta runs as "three different companies" at different maturities: Corporations (cap tables, steady-state SaaS), Venture Capital fund admin (software plus services) and Private Equity (educating a market). Each has its own sales leader, segmentation, marketing motion and delivery team.
- Org-design choices are experimental and data-informed: specialized AEs with separate "growth" reps for cross-sell (except enterprise VC), a shared cross-trained SE "bank", "mountain jumping" between units, and international GMs in-continent. Perry won't force "trifecta" deals and still gets more than 50% attach.
- Kyle brings his own operating mechanics: a customer > company > team > rep > manager decision hierarchy, publishing rules-of-engagement (ROE) dispute rationales to the team, and PIPs reframed as coaching plans that managers "fight like hell" to help reps beat.

## What Kyle said
- **MMA background:** "I come from a mixed martial arts background. That'll definitely teach you a lot of the same lessons. You got to be real humble and getting beaten up every day for years and years." @05:18 ([[kyle-norton]])
- **Plans break early:** "We were three weeks into January, then I was like, god damn, I'm basically throwing out big parts of my 2025 plan. Because one of our longer range bets started to just absolutely rip months earlier than we thought." @33:44 ([[owner-com]], [[operating-rhythm]])
- **Post-sale lesson:** in his first VP sales role he learned the importance of orienting to post-sale early. "it's not until you own it that I think you viscerally feel the pain of sales reps being sloppy with their handoff or expectation setting or onboarding being under-resourced". He also recorded a webinar on the topic with the CRO of ZoomInfo and the president of SEON. @35:50 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- **Multi-product selling risk:** "sometimes trying to sell those extra products up front can actually slow the deal down, lead to just like overload, information overload and get your deal stuck." @46:28
- **Decision hierarchy:** "One of the things that I stress to my teams is this sort of decision-making hierarchy. It's customer, company, team, rep, manager, last." Apply it to org design: pick what serves the customer best, because otherwise "you disappear into a spreadsheet." @55:59–57:00 ([[org-design]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- **ROE transparency at Owner:** the enablement survey showed reps frustrated that ROE wasn't enforced, even though managers spent heavy time on disputes. "We close like hundreds of deals a month, like 400 or 500 deals a month." The gap was "we weren't sharing the rationale behind the decision making publicly enough". Owner now publishes a synthesized version of each manager-channel debate, including one where a manager admitted the mistake and gave up credit. @58:04–59:07 ([[revops]], [[comp-plans]], [[owner-com]])
- **Codifying ROE:** "I find the reps who are friends bicker the most". The doc is "too big now", but when it was looser "it was too opaque" and drew favoritism accusations, since "people are always going to see the world through the lens that is the most kind to themselves". Now "we're trying to codify basically everything... There's no room for interpretation, black or white." @63:28–64:32 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- **Non-dual thinking:** after Brad Stulberg's *Master of Change* he sees the pattern everywhere: "having an extreme sense of urgency in everything that you do and also an extreme patience with things", or being relatable yet able to make objective decisions. @68:54–69:54 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- **Luck:** executing like a professional "doesn't guarantee success, because luck plays a big role in the world. But not executing that way almost... solely guarantees failure." He cites Jim Collins' "return on luck". @71:57–72:58 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- **PIPs as coaching plans:** "We don't call them PIPs. We call them coaching plans here." "a PIP is not paperwork to terminate somebody". "your job is to fight like hell to help this rep beat the PIP." "I've had multiple top performers who were on PIPs". He offers listeners his coaching-plan document by email. @75:05–77:12 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- **Books:** he recommends *Chip War* and *Master of Change*. A vendor's thoughtful gift of *Relentless* (Tim Grover) might make him "actually buy their product". @68:54, @78:15–79:16

## Guest's core ideas
- **Three companies in one:** Corporations (since eShares, 10+ years), VC fund admin (since late 2018, plus the Tactyc tax/modeling acquisition) and PE/LLC. The PE unit emerged from inbound LLC portcos (about 10% of inbound), was specialized inside Corp, then spun out on TAM: "Carter wasn't just going to stop at the corporations business and become like a really nice couple hundred million dollar business." @06:21–25:17 ([[org-design]])
- **No silver-bullet org model:** his Oracle instinct was core rep plus overlay, but "that wouldn't have worked for us". Instead every AE pitches the whole stack, backed by a solutions-engineering team built in the last 18–24 months. Carta has swung between functional and BU models, now a matrix. @15:46–22:06 ([[org-design]])
- **Different motions:** Corp is an SMB-velocity motion on a monthly cadence (about 14-day cycles, referral-driven). VC/PE firms behave like enterprise accounts on a quarterly forecast, with cycles of 90 days to 18 months. PE runs a top-down (firm AE plus BD) and bottom-up (portco AE) territory pairing. Marketing differs too: in-market PE coffee-shop takeovers and CFO community dinners vs Corp add-on education. @25:17–28:24, @39:01–42:12 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- **Growth reps vs hunters:** separate growth teams handle renewals and cross-sell in Corp and VC. Enterprise VC AEs keep accounts "in perpetuity" because the universe of firms is finite. Decisions come from lead→opp→close conversion and attach data. "I fear that trying to force a trifecta deal... just creates a worse customer or prospect experience". @44:21–48:36 ([[churn-and-retention]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- **Unit economics:** VC fund admin includes services, so implementation quality (banking integrations, automated money movement) drives margin. "five years ago... It was like customer acquisition at all costs". In PE, a bad portco implementation becomes Carta's reputation across the whole firm. @33:44–36:54
- **Talent:** frontline managers grown from AEs suit Corp. VC/PE need external industry hires (fund accounting, competitors). "Mountain jumping" lets internal people move units and ramp faster, and SEs are being cross-certified to reduce key-person risk. @37:56–52:44 ([[sales-hiring]])
- **Decisions:** seek consensus. Ask (as CEO Henry Ward does) "what are you trying to achieve?" Lay out 3–4 options (often a fifth hybrid wins), and focus on inputs: "the number will be what the number is." @52:44–55:59 ([[operating-rhythm]])
- **Leadership:** balance being close to people with making hard calls (he still sends prospecting emails for SMB AEs). "what do you do when no one's watching?" Career patience. Early performance management. @67:51–75:05 ([[career-and-leadership]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
