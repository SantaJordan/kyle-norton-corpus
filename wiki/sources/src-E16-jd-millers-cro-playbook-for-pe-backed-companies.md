# E16: JD Miller's CRO Playbook for PE-Backed Companies
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** JD Miller, operating advisor @ Five Arrows Capital Partners (Rothschild); ex-CRO @ Motus and Contata; author of *The CRO's Guide to Winning in Private Equity* · **Published:** 2025-01-15 (RSS pubDate) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E16-jd-millers-cro-playbook-for-pe-backed-companies/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- PE CRO work is "house flipping with tech companies": a 3–5 year horizon, an explicit value-creation thesis, and centralized rigor (annual plan, weekly flash report) that Kyle says is rarer in venture.
- Miller's annual plan is "built on fact, not hope": a row-by-row, month-by-month model of new logo, attrition and ramp, renewals and price increases, cross-sell, and new products. Any higher target must come with agreed, measurable bets. That transparency is how CROs outlive the ~18-month average tenure.
- Inspection comes from a weekly flash report, a forecast that rolls commit and best case up, and a quarterly "performance plan" for every seller. Kyle matches it with Owner's monthly MBR grid and rep "secret sauce" report cards.

## What Kyle said
- "I've only done venture"; he had assumed PE means less money, stripping cost to "make it more fit." @12:27 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- The rigor of the PE annual plan and weekly flash "is definitely more rare in, I would say, venture-backed businesses. Oftentimes they're growing so fast, you're just sort of like catching demand and flying by the seat of your pants." @20:45 ([[operating-rhythm]])
- When a board or CEO "make[s] up a number," he uses an objection-handling "yes and": e.g., 70% instead of 50% growth means doubling the BDR team, marketing +40%, and enablement headcount. "At least now we're having a trade offs conversation... we're on the same side of the table." @40:24–41:26 ([[capacity-planning]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
- Citing Kyle Lacy (E15): the CMO "should never celebrate hitting their pipeline number if the sales team is missing their bookings number." Trust enables productive conflict (Lencioni), and he adds a missing layer: "common language, common data." @47:32–48:34 ([[marketing-alignment]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Hypergrowth: per Reid Hoffman (family → tribe → village → city) and Dunbar's number, "your systems and structure have to drive alignment," which is part of why CRO tenure is short when "that business is so different every 12 to 24 months." @52:43–53:43 ([[org-design]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
- Owner JDs and interviews: postings state "We sell to blue collar, it's 90% phone, it's highly transactional" so candidates self-select out, and others have borrowed the template. He is the final step in rep hiring and stresses the downside: "Every other person was a President's Club performer... We expect a high work rate." @56:50–58:55 ([[sales-hiring]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Ryan Holiday on accepting hard truths: "We got bought. We're a $500 million company now... we need to move on to the next thing." @59:59–61:01 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- Owner instrumentation: "we run a monthly MBR" with a grid of every segment × every stage, from top of funnel "through to handoff and... successful customer launch," with targets and red/yellow/green. Rep instructions live in Guru and Notion. "Don't even spend time trying to problem solve until you can have your baseline of confidence in the inputs." @67:13–68:15 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[revops]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Owner coaching: "a monthly report card that the managers and the reps work on collectively". Reps pick a "secret sauce," "the number one skill that will move the needle on your results"; it is rep-driven, and the manager corrects if way off. @71:18 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])

## Guest's core ideas
- PE appeal: an exit focus, pace, a known investment thesis and end date. PE firms specialize as growth or efficiency investors; the rule of 40 frames trade-offs. @08:14–14:33 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- Motus (Thoma Bravo, merged with Runzheimer): from ~30 sellers to "just under 200" in sales and marketing, three segments (enterprise, mid-market, digital SMB with ~6–7 day cycles), new industries, a COVID pivot to home-office stipends, and acquisitions. Thoma Bravo calls it its "seventh best investment." @15:34–26:04 ([[org-design]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Annual plan built "on fact, not hope": past performance, attrition and backfill, ramp, renewals, price increases, white space, new products and M&A. Higher targets require agreed bets (training lift, hiring pace, pricing). @32:11–40:24 ([[capacity-planning]])
- A "win creation waterfall" back-solves quota → deals → win rate → opps → demos → SDR calls and marketing spend, so misses are diagnosable rather than "a black box." @42:28–44:28 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[marketing-alignment]])
- Diagnostic story: a supposed talent-problem team actually had a 36% win rate but only 1.2× pipeline coverage because of poor MQL→SQL conversion. @45:31–46:31 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Hiring lesson: a 200%-of-quota big-company seller failed because he had never self-sourced. Screen for context and skills, not results. @53:43–56:50 ([[sales-hiring]], [[hiring-scorecards]])
- Weekly flash (about four slides, every Wednesday): cumulative weekly bookings vs plan, last quarter, and last year, plus renewals and price increases, shared with managers, the C-team and the board. Seller 1:1s → manager commit/best case → CRO. @61:01–67:13 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[revops]])
- Every seller gets a quarterly "performance improvement plan" reframed as development. "Recency and firsthand experience is not data." @69:17–74:24 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
