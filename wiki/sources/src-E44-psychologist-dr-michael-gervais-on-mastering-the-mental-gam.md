# E44: Psychologist Dr. Michael Gervais on Mastering the Mental Game of Leadership
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Dr. Michael Gervais, high performance psychologist @ Finding Mastery (his consulting firm; ex-Seattle Seahawks; transcript renders his name "Jery") · **Published:** 2025-08-27 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E44-psychologist-dr-michael-gervais-on-mastering-the-mental-gam/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Healthy competition means competing to be your very best and a great teammate, not beating colleagues. Leaders run it through "support and challenge" relationships. Sales floors that become shark tanks are a culture failure.
- Business teaches hard work but not recovery. Chronic stress makes people revert to self-protection. Mental skills (calm, confidence, optimism) can be trained in a few minutes a day, starting with a four-step morning mindset routine.
- FOPO (fear of people's opinions) is the "greatest constrictor". The cure is moving from a performance-based to a purpose-based identity, taking feedback only from people who know you, and staying curious. Kyle ties this to revenue leaders whose identity is "our number".

## Guest's core ideas
- Elite sport trains three things: craft, body and mind. Business mostly trains craft. Mental skills are the "wide open playing field". @04:05 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Optimism sits at "the center of mental toughness". He tells the Marshawn Lynch "Who wants to come to the front with me?" story. @07:08–09:12
- Build culture on inputs, not outputs. The Seahawks talked about winning about once a year and spent a three-week "install" on principles and practices, because of about 60% annual turnover. @10:13–11:14 ([[culture-and-high-standards]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Compete to create: compete to be your very best, then to be a great teammate. Coaches learn each person's "support and challenge" calibration (public vs private, triggers). From Bill Russell: wake up asking how to be great today for each teammate. @17:21–22:29 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Under chronic stress, people revert to survival and abandon teammates. "in the business world we do not teach recovery". The equation is high stress plus high recovery. @24:30–27:36 and @63:27 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Morning mindset routine: one breath, one gratitude, one intention, one moment of being present. The edges people hit are those of their belief system, not their potential. @28:37–32:42
- Performance-based vs purpose-based identity: "be more and then let the doing flow". Purpose has three legs: it matters to you, it's bigger than you, and it's out in front of you. Find it by journaling, in conversations with people of wisdom, or through meditation. @46:02–52:12 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- FOPO: an evolutionary fear of being rejected by the tribe. The problem is the worry, not caring. Take feedback only from people who know your scar tissue and ambitions. Curiosity is an off-ramp. If you do the work yourself, trust follows (character across conditions, ability, benevolence). "we don't rise to the occasion. We actually fall to the level of our training." @53:14–72:36, @77:44
