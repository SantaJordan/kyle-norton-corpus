# E21: From Building Startups to Betting on Them with Jason Gelman of Primary Ventures
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Jason Gelman, Operating Partner @ Primary Ventures (co-founder, Jump; ex-head of revenue strategy & ops @ Compass) · **Published:** 2025-02-19 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E21-from-building-startups-to-betting-on-them-with-jason-gelman/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Gelman chose an operating-partner seat at seed fund Primary for learning over direct impact: information asymmetry, boardroom and fundraising exposure, "try before I buy" with founders. He is now co-founding Jump (a "revenue calendar") deliberately without VC.
- Operators should borrow VC "picking" discipline. Gelman's tool is Primary's "God of Beliefs" (i.e., got-to-believes) list of explicit bets. Kyle adds his own CRO diligence move: ask for the board deck and walk the growth model with finance.
- AI is retrofitting two decades of SaaS. Both expect a possible mega-GTM tool, and Kyle says Salesforce is unseatable for the first time. Kyle's stance on tools: be early and accept some will be obsolete. "I'm not a waiting person."

## What Kyle said
- **Founder-led sales:** "the most common LinkedIn message I get coming inbound is, I got three or four customers. What do I do?" He calls it "a really difficult chasm to cross." @04:12 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- **Pushback on the idea that advisors lack impact:** "I would definitely disagree with the assumption that you can't have a lot of direct impact." Bad secondhand advice is common, and "the right framework for an early stage company can be really inflection changing." @20:44 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- **Actionable frameworks beat generic advice.** For founders hiring a first VP of sales he hands over "my framework... and the questions and the case. I'll give them my case study that I've written." @22:48 ([[sales-hiring]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- **No best practices:** advising GTM Fund and Stage 2 portcos suffers from "such a lack of context". "there's no such thing as a best practice. There's high probability practices", so he became "much less certain of the advice that I wanted to give" (Dunning-Kruger). @32:16 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- **Scaling without PMF:** "Super talented team, but we were brute forcing growth... We never had customers that were willing to do customer reference calls for us, but we still jammed that business to 25 million ARR." @37:34 ([[fundraising-and-investability]], [[churn-and-retention]])
- **VC talent networks:** he routes job-seekers to GTM Fund portcos "because my money's in the fund". People are "intimidated by venture capital funds" but "the incentives are so aligned." @41:45 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- **CRO job diligence:** "primary might make 12 bets a year and you make 0.3 bets a year as an operator." "If you're interviewing for a CRO role, ask for the last board deck. When I was talking to owner about this role, the founder flipped me through a board deck and then I asked for time with the COO who ran finance". They spent an hour on a screen share going through the growth model segment by segment (growth, retention, unit economics, cash burn). "And if it's a no, in my opinion, it's a pretty big red flag." @45:58–48:04 ([[owner-com]], [[cro-role-and-board]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- **"God of Beliefs":** "I really like this God of Belief concept." @50:07 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- **Owner's founder and the investor update:** "Our founder talks about this a lot. He used to do it before midnight, the day the month closed. Then we moved it to the first of the month." He "Never, ever missed that deadline", including New Year's Eve and his birthday. It "shows a level of professionalism and seriousness". @55:22 ([[person-adam-guild]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
- **Pace of change:** "I'm certain that this is just a bonkers time" (DeepSeek, Operator, reasoning models, agents, deep research). @55:22–56:30 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- **Salesforce is unseatable for the first time:** "This is the first time I'm like, oh man, somebody could definitely get Salesforce now. Because that's basically just a series of workflows on top of a big database." @62:53–63:57 ([[build-vs-buy-software]], [[ai-sales-stack]])
- **Early-adopter doctrine:** Owner was Momentum's "first, second, or third customer", the first customer of Ping (and "Cullivara" as transcribed), and a first design partner. Earlier he was among the earliest SalesLoft, Chili Piper and Chorus customers. "Being early on Sales Loft was wild." "I've just come to the realization, I'm not a waiting person. And I prefer to make some mistakes along the way, but be early and carve an advantage there", knowing some tools "might be obsolete". @67:06–68:09 ([[ai-sales-stack]], [[momentum]])
- **Picking AI tools:** most AI prompt "thought leader" content is "sort of crap". An early-stage tool is "really like a founder bet". He runs a pilot, gives feedback and watches "how fast they implement it". Most buyers end up with "three or four tools, and it's just mass confusion". His advice to someone else: "you got to go learn to be a venture capital investor, basically... or maybe just wait." @70:15–71:20 ([[ai-sales-stack]], [[build-vs-buy-software]])
- **Leaders must learn AI:** "If I wasn't so locked in and excited about [Owner]... that might be something I'd go start". "you just have to make this a priority. It's not really a choice." He told his leadership channel to watch Karpathy's 3.5-hour LLM video, comparing it to "I don't really need to know how a CRM works... no, that's part of the job requirement." @73:24–74:25 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])

## Guest's core ideas
- **The operating-partner role:** no checks or board seats, functional help after investment. At seed that means pre-PMF scrappy sales. He works deeply with 6–10 companies and lightly with 10–15 more, out of 100+ in the portfolio. @03:09–06:14
- **Raise VC only for big ambitions:** with AI lowering startup costs, test and validate first. Raising from institutions means you are "in the VC game", so align growth ambitions with investors. Primary does 12–15 deals a year out of 1,000+ seen. @10:16–12:28 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- **Why join a VC firm:** information asymmetry for career picking, boardroom and fundraising gaps, seeing market analysis across models, "try before I buy" with 50 CEOs. "I basically chose learning over those concerns about direct impact". Firm choice matters, because some firms rank portfolio support fourth or fifth. @17:37–22:48 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- **Advising:** find "the quickest, smallest win" to earn trust, design experiments rather than give advice, and template the repeat requests. @33:20–35:27
- **PMF signal:** "some form of sticky usage and then retention where it shows up in the dollars". The common failure is mismatched growth expectations (hiring too many salespeople, raising too much). @38:35–39:37 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- **Picking like an investor:** the best exec roles originate through VC firms and search firms. Ask for the investor deck. Use **"God of Beliefs"** (got-to-believes), the explicit list of risks and bets behind a thesis (e.g., cross-sell to products 2–4). Board management means no surprises and consistent monthly updates. @45:58–55:22 ([[fundraising-and-investability]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
- **GTM tech:** AI is retrofitting two decades of SaaS. Pricing leverage erodes (cheap call recorders). AI-native CRMs face an unwinding problem. Primary has seen 170 GTM-tech pitches in about 15 months. He "would not be surprised if one mega GTM tool emerges". @57:37–66:02 ([[ai-sales-stack]], [[build-vs-buy-software]])
- **Adopting AI:** map your GTM "supply chain" and attack the biggest time sink. Primary's BD team spends 15–20 hrs/week in Sales Navigator, with a target of under 5. Run a Compass-style "sales lab" of top sellers. Make AI a celebrated company theme. Predicts "$100 million revenue AI consulting firms". @69:13–75:26 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- **Great CROs** pair sales leadership with "deeply analytically fluent and P&L fluent" skills. Question the standard founder-led → AE → VP-sales playbook per business, since some founders should sell alone to $5–10M ARR. @75:26–77:35 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
