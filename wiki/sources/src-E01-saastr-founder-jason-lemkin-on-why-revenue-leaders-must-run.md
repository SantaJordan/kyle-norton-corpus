# E1: SaaStr Founder Jason Lemkin on Why Revenue Leaders Must Run Towards Bad News
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Jason Lemkin, founder @ SaaStr / SaaStr Fund (Owner.com investor and board member) · **Published:** 2024-09-25 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E01-saastr-founder-jason-lemkin-on-why-revenue-leaders-must-run/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- The board wants "honesty plus optimism". Lead with your commit, how the quarter went, and three good and three bad things. Unexpected bad news is the biggest confidence killer, so "run toward bad news".
- "Revenue leaders are on the chopping block" is mostly LinkedIn myth. First-time VPs more often get "topped" and quit. Great CEOs protect their revenue leader. Never blame the product in public.
- Diligence before you join: ask for the last board pack and get "as educated as you will be 30 days into the job". Arrive with two hires ready. Don't lower the bar to fill a gap.

## Guest's core ideas
- Board slide: what you committed to, how it went, and three good and three bad things (he uses GBU: good, bad, ugly). On slide 2, screenshot your own favorite dashboard. "Don't sell up. Don't schmooze." @17:44–20:48 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Unexpected bad news is "10 times more a confidence destroyer than anything else". Telegraph misses early "with a positive filter". A top-five mistake is committing to backloaded plans to make up a miss. @14:41–16:44 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Fear ends heads of sales. Their job gets harder every quarter, so they have to recruit better and better people. @21:48–22:49 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- The chopping block is a myth. Many first-time VPs "got topped" and quit. CEOs should tell them they have a senior role whatever happens. Firing the whole revenue team after a miss is "rarely an effective strategy". @23:53–31:05 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- Never blame the product in public. Productize where you win, then find counter-FUD for segments where you lose but sometimes win. @31:05–33:09 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- After product-market fit, "all that matters is the team". In closed sessions the board talks about the management team. A board member's highest value add is helping spot mishires earlier. @37:19–42:26 ([[talent-density]])
- Don't go over the CEO's head. If you don't trust the CEO, leave on good terms. @47:35–49:38
- Before signing, ask for the last board pack and "be as educated as you will be 30 days into the job". Start with two hires in your back pocket. Leave a gap open rather than hire a B+; "There is no Band-Aid in leadership." @55:49–64:11 ([[sales-hiring]], [[talent-density]])
