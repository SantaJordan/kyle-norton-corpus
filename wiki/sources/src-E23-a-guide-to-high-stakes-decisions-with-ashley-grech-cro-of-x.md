# E23: A Guide to High Stakes Decisions with Ashley Grech, CRO of Xero
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Ashley Grech, CRO @ Xero (ex-Square global head of sales, Recharge CRO, JPMorgan) · **Published:** 2025-03-05 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E23-a-guide-to-high-stakes-decisions-with-ashley-grech-cro-of-x/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Grech's problem-solving loop runs in three parts. Identify (the driver tree: revenue = quantity × quality × velocity, with level-1/2/3 metrics at different altitudes, plus gut, then data, then gut). Prioritize (resource constraints, linchpin blockers, impact vs effort scored in dollars, company alignment, and filtering noise). Solve (write down the principles a solution must satisfy, then score the options).
- Principles also work as a selling tool: people "might not like the answer, but they won't disagree with the principles." For expansion and channels she uses tiered investment "packets" (marketing-only → plus partner/outsourced call center → boots on the ground) and self-funding beachheads (UK → Ireland).
- Kyle says distilling Owner's operations into principles is "my primary function in 2025". He is rewriting Owner's operating methodology (values → customer → business → team → reps → managers, async by default, "treat people like adults") and building a Momentum → Salesforce → Snowflake single source of truth.

## Guest's core ideas
- **Why problem-solve:** you can't have "seen it all", running the same playbook without root cause is dangerous, and "if you can model it... then you can teach it". "I don't get paid to say no. I get paid to say how." @04:11–07:28
- **Driver tree** (built with finance): win rates by stage, deal volume per rep, deal size, inbound vs outbound source. Level-1 outputs are reviewed monthly or quarterly with the CEO, level-2 by channel, level-3 by rep in manager accountability meetings. Revisit whenever a "check engine light" is on. @08:28–19:08 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- **Gut, data, gut** (from Xero's CEO): a qualitative inkling (customer topics, analyst calls, competitors), then data, then judgment at 70–80% certainty. Square's CEO had her study manufacturing. @19:08–22:20
- **Call and ticket analytics:** measure whether mentioning product two raises or lowers win rates. At Recharge, fix first the issues that hurt both customers and teams. Insist vendors allow full data export. @24:26–28:46
- **Four prioritization conditions:** resource constraints, linchpin blockers, revenue impact vs effort (scoped to dollars), alignment with company priorities. Beware noise from loud customers or employees on sticky products. @33:05–39:32 ([[prioritization-5p]])
- **Principled solutioning:** define what a solution must solve for (replicable, above a revenue threshold, not disrupting resourcing), then score options. For Xero's comp redesign: "globally applicable", "The structure should be the same. The quota can be different", and multi-product scalable. With principles you get about 89% of what you wanted, vs 50% from white space. @43:53–51:19 ([[comp-plans]])
- **Teach by modeling:** do it together, co-write the principles, then hand it over. @55:30–56:37
- **Expansion:** tiered packets of investment. Self-funding beachheads (UK → Ireland, under-1-year payback). Push vs pull triggers (investors, regulation such as UK tax digitization). Keep one stable "anchor" while you experiment. @57:46–68:19 ([[capacity-planning]])
- **Channels:** follow the customer's jobs-to-be-done sequence (payments → bank account → employees). Treat a new channel "as an all-new business". Ask "what has to be true" to challenge limiting beliefs (e.g., reseller margins). @68:19–76:51
- **Quickfire:** a great CRO is "a truly enterprise leader" who can solve peers' problems. "whatever you are, be a good one". Her regret is holding the gray too long before making a people call. @76:51–81:00 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
