# E05: Mark Niemiec on Steering Business Transformation
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Mark Niemiec (transcribed "Nemec"), CRO @ SalesLoft (ex-Salesforce comms & media OU leader, ex-Cisco) · **Published:** 2024-10-23 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E05-mark-niemiec-on-steering-business-transformation/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- SalesLoft (Vista-owned) is moving from a category-creating product to an open platform, a hub for intent signals with partner extensibility ("more we than me"). That means longer, more complex deals, post-sales brought into pre-sales, and new point-of-view/business-value talent.
- Operational transformation: a uniform weekly/monthly/quarterly commit process, the same dashboard at every leader level, a pre-published 13-week quarterly calendar, and a hand-written "idealized" forecast-call script so everyone describes deals the same way.
- Kyle shares his own playbook: "point of view positioning" (choice, not comparison; Shopify as the example). He also gives an Owner detail: as a Series B company it builds on SalesLoft's APIs with Snowflake-based custom tooling.

## What Kyle said
- He is a SalesLoft customer who has "been frustrated by some of the walled garden approaches" of other vendors. Owner, a "high-velocity business with a lot of transactions and data", uses SalesLoft's API ecosystem "to be able to do our own data manipulation in Snowflake with custom-built tools", API-ing leads into specific cadences and pulling data back. He says this makes "our long-term attachment to Sales Loft much stronger". @16:50 ([[owner-com]], [[ai-sales-stack]], [[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Predicts "a new golden age of RevOps": the first golden age rode the sales-engagement explosion "around 2015 and onwards". This second phase needs "a very different set of skills", and "composability will matter a lot". He is "on my fifth sales loft implementation". @17:54 ([[revops]], [[ai-sales-stack]])
- On trusted repeat hires: "There is such a power in that, having a known commodity". He has "a few of those folks on my team today and it makes a world of difference." @25:22–26:25 ([[talent-density]])
- Operating cadence is one of his favourite topics ("I could do a separate two-hour podcast on just this topic"), and Owner is "rebuilding a bunch of our rhythms as well across the business." @29:27 ([[operating-rhythm]])
- He was surprised by the lack of grumbling at SalesLoft after the PE acquisition, contrasting it with the chaos "in 98% of private equity purchases". @39:55–40:56 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Point of view positioning: "I have long taught what I have named point of view positioning", rooted in the Art of War line "compete with no one and no one can compete with you". His Shopify example: the best-fit merchants see their business as "creating great products and telling great stories" and are "not trying to win on being a tech company"; the POV "makes it very clear who should opt in and opt out." From Mike Maples: "give people a choice, not comparison", because comparisons collapse into features and pricing. "really, I have to convince them of a point of view." @51:02–52:04 ([[point-of-view-positioning]], [[shopify]], [[icp-and-saying-no]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Coming from big companies, the work is adding missing functions and depth: design thinking, sales/value engineering, and executive-level business cases. Introduce them so they are "metabolized, accepted and welcomed". @04:23–06:32 ([[org-design]])
- He wrote an eight-page long-hand plan in VSEM format (Vision, Strategy, Execution, Measurement, from Cisco) rather than a V2MOM. @07:34–09:37 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Product to platform: SalesLoft as a "clearinghouse" for intent signals processed by "Conductor AI", open to partners and customer-built signals, rather than a walled garden. @12:42–16:50
- Talent: connect post-sales into pre-sales, and hire rare point-of-view people (storytellers, mathematicians, economists and engineers in one; "tell me what chocolate tastes like"). Look for hires who fit the culture but still drive change. @18:55–26:25 ([[talent-density]], [[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Rigor: a weekly/monthly/quarterly commit process with clear definitions of commit vs upside, champion and business case, and the same dashboard for first-, second- and third-line leaders. A pre-published 13-week calendar (e.g. week 7 reviews next quarter's commit) is run by sales ops. @26:25–31:33 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- The scripted forecast call: a long-hand "idealized call" everyone reads in the same format (customer, deal, problem, business case, next step), so that "sameness" exposes signal and peers cross-pollinate. @32:33–38:52 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Vision tied to problem size: small problems give small relevance, while board-level problems put you at the "CEO at the board level". Industry POVs (six industries) open the deal, followed by a "customer of a customer" business case. @45:00–50:02 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Quickfire: "Slow down and let people fail" (a "three-year decision"); leaders should "Get your hands on the wheel"; "you can't convince everybody all the time." @53:04–55:04 ([[career-and-leadership]])
