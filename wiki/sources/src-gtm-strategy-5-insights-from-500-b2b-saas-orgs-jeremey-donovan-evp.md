# E61: GTM Strategy: 5 Insights from 500 B2B SaaS Companies
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Jeremey Donovan, EVP Sales + CS @ Insight Partners · **Published:** 2026-01-28 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/gtm-strategy-5-insights-from-500-b2b-saas-orgs-jeremey-donovan-evp/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Donovan's survey of 150 CROs: top and average performers adopt much the same AI use cases. 2026 shifts from bottom-up tinkering to top-down, systems-access AI. CROs expect only a 5–15% productivity lift, and he sees "no kink in the curve yet."
- Outbound is getting harder: about six qualified opps per SDR per month, a ~40–50k ACV threshold, and ~70% of meetings booked by phone. Yet some top performers are growing outbound teams. Kyle counters with Owner's 8–10K ACV, two-call, three-day motion.
- Build vs buy currently skews to build (fast, but it rots without maintenance). Buy if you're regulated, big, or need predictive ML + GenAI together. Roles are being unbundled: SDRs absorbed into AEs, SEs split toward AEs and FDEs. "Disciplined execution" is what makes a great CRO.

## Guest's core ideas
- AI adoption is early. Top vs average CROs adopt similar use cases. Winners farm ideas bottom-up and syndicate them, and fix the data foundation first. 2026 will be "the year of top down sales AI innovation" because agents need systems access. @04:06–07:10 ([[ai-operating-model]])
- Governance: companies above ~$20–25M ARR, or in regulated industries, are locked down. Make security your partner. The fastest movers appoint an "AI czar" with organizational influence, sourced from RevOps, the PMO or finance. @08:11–11:14 ([[ai-operating-model]])
- Use cases move down-funnel (RFPs, deal risk, churn signals). RFP response is now about 50/50 packaged vs built. @11:14–12:14, @24:27–25:29 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- "Disciplined execution" beats tactics: rigorous weekly MEDDIC deal reviews, carved-out pipeline time, and the right ICP. When hiring a CRO he checks their operating rhythm through back-channel references. @12:14–15:19 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
- Outbound math: SDRs average about six qualified opps a month. At ~25% win rate the threshold is "around 40 or 50k ACV". About 70% of meetings are booked by phone. AI cold calling without opt-in is illegal. Effortful, human reciprocity still wins. @27:32–34:42 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- Build vs buy: building is faster and better but goes unmaintained within a year. Buyers fear the "instantly obsolete" point tool. Buy when regulated (SOC 2), large, or when predictive ML and GenAI must be combined, e.g. CS health → workflow → drafted message. @41:55–48:02 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- CROs commit to a conservative 5–15% lift. Net new ARR per ramped rep across the portfolio is rising slowly with no discontinuity. The unlock is a "system solve" downstream, not prompting, plus redesigning roles and processes. @52:06–58:10, @62:17–63:19 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- SDRs (especially inbound) get absorbed into AEs. SEs split: the low end goes to AEs, the high end to forward-deployed engineers. Centralizing in one GTM engineer (e.g., Clay owner) creates critical-path risk, so use an agency as backup. @59:13–61:15, @65:23–67:23 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]], [[gtm-engineering]], [[clay]])
- A CRO ripped out a tool that auto-extracted MEDDIC into Salesforce because AEs stopped knowing their deals. @72:30–74:32 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
