# E41: Jessica Chiew of Canva on Building AI-Native GTM Org
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Jessica Chiew (transcript: "Chu"), Head of GTM Operations & Strategy @ Canva; ex-Asana, McKinsey · **Published:** 2025-08-01 · **Kyle's role:** host (unusually opinionated co-discussant) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E41-jessica-chiew-of-canva-on-building-ai-native-gtm-org/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Canva's model: a central AI council (in IT, with security and privacy) gives functions the resources to bring their own AI programs. GTM ops has a dedicated "go to market AI lead" plus per-role "workflow experts" (AE, XDR, CS) who gatekeep what reaches the field, to keep the number of tabs down.
- Prioritize from jobs to be done and business bottlenecks, not vendor noise. Leaders must be hands-on (shadowing, reading activity feeds). Iterate fast on internal-only workflows and hold a high quality bar on anything customer-facing.
- Build vs buy: Canva spent nine months building an in-house CRM auto-fill and call-insight platform ("easy to prototype ... really hard" to productionize). Kyle bought Momentum early instead. Both treat early vendors like angel bets and avoid adding new rep surfaces.

## What Kyle said
- GTM AI transformation is "the topic by far that I get asked about the most". He's "trying not to make the podcast only GTMAI although it's quickly becoming that". @02:01 ([[revenue-leadership-podcast]], [[ai-in-gtm]])
- He calls it the most fun of his career, mixed with "constant existential dread that I'm not doing enough". After talking with Clay's CRO: "Oh my god, we need to go faster." He takes 2–4 demos a week. @04:04, @06:06 ([[clay]])
- Owner uses the same title, "GTM AI lead". Its job is workflow problem-solving: vibe-code an internal app, build "an extravagant clay table", teach people MCP, or ship "a prompt template library". The data layer (enrichment, waterfalls) sits instead with a strong VP of BizOps and data and their data scientists and engineers. @12:11–14:13 ([[gtm-engineering]], [[ai-operating-model]], [[revops]])
- Data should be centralized, with dedicated GTM and product data people under one function. The ideal business-aligned data scientist did a CS degree, then consulting (McKinsey), then a couple of years at a tech company. @14:13–17:18 ([[org-design]])
- His evolving opinion is to start with the data: solve third-party data first ("mobile phone numbers in particular"), then first-party (product data, CRM hygiene), because "AI is a pattern recognition and a replication engine." "this is becoming more of an opinion and so I keep stress testing that". @22:27–23:28 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- On CRM fields: "go just use momentum that's that's what we use for". He was "one of Momentum's first three or five customers". @24:29, @27:31 ([[momentum]], [[ai-sales-stack]])
- He picks early vendors the way he'd write an angel check: are we "philosophically aligned", and "do I think these founders can win?" Avara (sales simulator) was "pretty janky" at first, then got great. Partnering early gives a "compounding advantage" because the team has already adopted the tool by the time it's great. @27:31–29:31 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- "execution is the only moat" is "probably my most quoted thing from the podcast now" (from an early guest; Vanta context). @29:31–30:32
- Surfaces over solutions: "I'm okay if it's a point solution in the background". What he avoids is "new surfaces for the reps to live", since reps already juggle inbox, Slack, Salesforce and Salesloft. Owner runs an AI support bot, Guru, and is "piloting an AI SDR now". @33:35–34:36 ([[ai-sales-stack]], [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]])
- "I'm I'm airing on the side of prescriptive". He doesn't want reps improvising across tools unless they go deep from first principles. @40:42 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Managers must be hands-on with AI. During performance reviews: "if anybody's hand typing their whole performance reviews, I'm going to be really mad". Dictate with Wispr Flow or Superwhisper and have the model theme your 1:1 notes. His leaders build "recipe books". @45:46–46:46 ([[personal-ai-stack]])
- Owner built MBR workflows that repackage weekly written updates into a monthly. His podcast newsletter runs through a Claude project with a long prompt, multi-shot with hand-written examples. It took "three hours" by hand and now takes about 25 minutes. @48:49–49:49 ([[personal-ai-stack]])
- He agrees that middle-manager "translation layers" will shrink. @47:47
- Realism: LinkedIn makes it look like magic, but workflows take iteration ("you're three hours into trying to build that workflow"). "I love tinkering." @50:49
- Ride-alongs: will "sit down multiple days a week and make calls", and his HRBP and recruiter are doing BDR days too (inspired by Tess Whitaker at ZoomInfo). Leaders who change comp or workflow without time with reps "cannot have spent any time with a rep or a customer in a long time". @53:53–55:55 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Customer onboarding automation: he told a "skittish" team, "I want to automate all of the lowest value parts of your job". Freed time goes to teaching customers the tool and planning restaurants' marketing launches. The aim is to raise people's "level of abstraction". "BDR is doing lead research. Come on." @59:00–61:01 ([[churn-and-retention]], [[owner-com]])
- He replaced doom-scrolling with AI Twitter, going from about an hour a week to about an hour a day. He's building a list of the best AI follows, and on the Winning by Design AI award he says Clay "should just win all of these". @64:04, @69:09–70:10 ([[personal-ai-stack]])

## Guest's core ideas
- AI makes ops creative again, like being "product owners". Canva's founders added "how are you using air in your workflow?" to performance reviews. @03:03, @09:08 ([[ai-operating-model]])
- Make AI part of everyone's job description, but dedicated roles move much faster. The GTM AI lead blends PM, sales-workflow depth and technical project management. @10:09–12:11 ([[gtm-engineering]])
- Clay turns data engineers into business-context analysts (for example, re-checking industry tags against websites). @15:15–16:17
- Path: from IC GPT hacks, to the existing vendor stack, to jobs to be done and bottlenecks, then decide vendor, new vendor or build. Canva brought in a new vendor for hyper-personalization. @19:22–21:24
- Build what is a foundational "AI asset" (CRM auto-fill and call insights into Snowflake). Otherwise favor velocity and platforms (Relevance AI, Clay) over point solutions. @25:30–33:35 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Workflow experts per role gatekeep enablement ("maximum four tabs"). Test with 2–4 reps before rolling out. Watch for the top 10% of creative reps. @34:36–40:42 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Efficiency first, but the goal shifted toward "better higher quality experiences". They aren't yet transforming roles. @41:43–43:44
- Change management: moments of "delight" turn the feeling of threat into a feeling of leverage. Make the BDR leader the subject-matter expert. @56:58–59:00
- A great ops leader is a business leader who surrounds themselves with functional experts. In hypergrowth, every six months ask whether you can, and want to, do the next stage. @66:04–68:05 ([[career-and-leadership]])
