# Firmable (B2B Sales Blueprint), Ep. 10: Why "Best Practice" Is Killing Your Sales Team

**Show / host:** *The B2B Sales Blueprint* by Firmable, host Paul Perrett (co-founder & co-CEO) · **Published:** 2026-09-08 · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/firmable-best-practice-killing-sales/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=otTvdQ8d7cA

Note: Whisper renders "Firmable" as "Firmbull", "AI" sometimes as "I"/"killed" (e.g. "applied I lead", "I routing" = AI routing, "super AI killed" ≈ AI-pilled), "Brendan Short" as "Brendan Sherlock". The intro's "nearly $100 million" ARR and "triple-digit growth" are the **host's** words.

## TL;DR
- "high probability practice", not best practice: Owner runs BDRs and outbound at an $8–12K ACV because its own math (50%+ win rates, 2–3 rep-hours per deal, 200 dials/day) supports it — reason from first principles because AI is changing the math.
- Owner's AI edge is change literacy: culture (hire for adaptability), trust, pilot pods with top reps, and friction removal ("no new surfaces") — the tools themselves are "not that complicated".
- Updates the sophistication ladder with **"harness" language**: L3 = a GTM harness (tools, integrations, shared context, governance) that every agent builds on; Cursor's internal harness is the best he's seen; it needs architects with real engineering pedigree, not just Clay-table GTM engineers.

## What Kyle argued
- Best practice vs. "high probability practice": conventional wisdom says an 8–10K ACV is too small for BDRs/outbound, but that depends on "the math of your world"; practices from even nine months ago may no longer hold (@00:00, @08:23–@09:24). See [[smb-sales-motion]], [[outbound-at-scale]].
- Reason from the sales system's math: with >50% win rates and 2–3 capacity hours per deal, work back to ~two meetings booked per BDR per day and ~200 dials (@09:24–@10:24). See [[capacity-planning]].
- Ride-alongs/time study found a 2–3 minute lag between cold calls; the applied AI lead and a sales manager built AI pre-call research — "three fields" — so reps don't open tabs (@10:24–@12:26). Data first: fix bad contact data before anything else (@11:25–@12:26). See [[ai-sales-stack]].
- Owner is "not doing too many things that other people aren't actually trying to do"; the secret is "our ability to change behavior" — enablement, leaders, reps leaning in (@12:26). Friends copy his ideas and their teams don't adopt them "once every two weeks" (@13:29). See [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]].
- Change literacy is culture + leadership + systems: new hires get a "success at Owner session" and five book recommendations (incl. Brad Stulberg's *Master of Change* — "rugged flexibility"); "indoctrinating" into adaptability (@13:29–@14:30, @44:40). See [[culture-and-high-standards]].
- Pilot with the best reps others look up to; AI lead in the office; manager ride-alongs; feedback loop (@15:34). First AI projects decide trust — leading with an AI SDR on the homepage makes reps fear for their jobs; his team isn't fearful because AI has only made them more successful (@16:39–@17:41). See [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]].
- Reduce friction rather than push: "no new surfaces"; AI PCR injected on the SalesLoft dialer screen; cites Loran Nordgren's *The Human Element* and nudge theory; his head of RevOps has worked with him ~10 years (@18:44–@19:44). See [[revops]].
- Demand mix: a little more than half from new inbound (direct-response Google/Facebook); then BDRs calling old marketing leads, cold outbound, partnerships, cold email — all need an SDR calling back; restaurant owners book and no-show, so reps call, text, and walk them onto Google Meet on their phones (@19:44–@21:48). See [[smb-sales-motion]].
- Hiring: AEs who sold blue-collar/SMB do best; but ~80% of hiring is now new grads from top schools trained from scratch; Owner's data shows interview sales craft has "very little correlation" with performance — selects for DNA: hardworking, open-minded, mission-oriented, coachable, history of excellence (@21:48–@23:50). See [[sales-hiring]].
- Operating cadence: front-line managers' first three hours = "chop wood, carry water" — no meetings/Slack/spreadsheets, only operate/coach/craft on the front lines (from a seven-pillar revenue leadership framework); reps work from AI routing (lead selection by value, conversion, and pickup likelihood); spot sweeps; AEs prep in the morning then back-to-back demos (@24:52–@26:59). See [[org-design]], [[culture-and-high-standards]].
- Sophistication ladder recap, now with "harness" language: L3 = a GTM harness — tool connections (Snowflake, Salesforce, SalesLoft, call recorder), shared context (ICP, playbook, stage definitions), and governance — that every new agent points back to; Owner is "actively building" L3, "not masters" of it (@28:02–@30:06). Cursor's "chat GTM" is the best example (model-agnostic, agent library reuse) (@30:06–@31:08). See [[ai-in-gtm]], [[gtm-engineering]].
- Lock-in debate: Claude/Cowork are harnesses but lock you into one ecosystem; build outside and call in (Karp/Nadella debate) — "Almost nobody in GTM is talking about it" (@33:09). L4 = recursive GTM intelligence that evals and deprecates agents itself; "Nobody's here yet" (@34:10–@35:11). See [[build-vs-buy-software]].
- Data layer is L2 work; L2→L3 needs an architecture-first thinker. Typical GTM engineer is "mostly like a list builder person" ("the clay table guy") — valuable to reach L2, but L3 needs real engineering/systems-architecture pedigree (@36:14–@39:24). Benchmarks: Ramp, Perplexity, Rippling, Cursor, Vanta (@32:09). See [[gtm-engineering]].
- CRO role: systems thinking is "super powered" by AI but not mandatory if paired well — leadership team as a basketball team ("You can't have five centers"); if you lack it, "pay up a little bit more for your RevOps slash applied AI person" (@40:26–@42:37). See [[cro-role-and-board]].
- Culture: servant leadership and "the obligation of leadership"; values pervade book lists, language, emojis ("Bowser time" for when competitors take countermeasures because you're now big); lean into who you are — if you don't care about people, "Go be an enterprise rep" (@43:39–@49:46). See [[career-and-leadership]], [[culture-and-high-standards]].

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **High probability practice** (vs. best practice); first-principles sales-system math.
- **Change literacy** as the moat; **no new surfaces** / friction removal (*The Human Element*, nudge theory); **rugged flexibility** (*Master of Change*).
- **AI pre-call research (three fields)**, **AI routing**.
- **Chop wood, carry water** manager mornings; **seven-pillar revenue leadership framework** (operate / coach / craft named).
- **Sophistication ladder L0–L4 with GTM harness** (L3) and recursive GTM intelligence (L4); credited to Brendan Short; Cursor's harness as exemplar.
- **Leadership team as a basketball team**; **servant leadership / obligation of leadership**; **Bowser time**; **Success at Owner** onboarding session.

## New or changed vs. earlier positions
- **New vocabulary: "harness."** Kyle says he's newly adopting it (@28:02); L3 is reframed from centralized deployment platform / shared skills / context library ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]], May) to a governed GTM harness. Adds a vendor-lock-in view: build outside Claude/Cowork ecosystems and call in.
- **GTM engineering re-scoped:** the typical GTM engineer ("clay table guy") gets you to L2 only; L3 needs real engineering/architecture pedigree — sharper than his earlier GTM-engineering enthusiasm.
- **AI SDR stance softened:** "those are good and important tools" and Owner uses OneMind, but an AI SDR as the *first* project damages trust (vs. the Jan 2025 position that ~95% of AI-SDR is "pretty crap").
- **Hiring shift:** "80 percent of our hiring now is like fresh out of college" and interview sales craft barely predicts performance — a notable move toward new-grad, train-from-scratch hiring.
- **Sales headcount:** ~"close to 150" in sales. ARR "nearly $100 million" is host-stated; Kyle gives no new ARR number.
- **Consistent:** centralized applied AI lead, data first, deterministic AI PCR in existing surfaces, change management > tech (matches [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]]).
