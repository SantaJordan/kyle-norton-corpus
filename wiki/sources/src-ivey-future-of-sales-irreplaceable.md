# Ivey Business School (Sales ReFramed): The Future of Sales — How to Stay Irreplaceable in the Age of AI

**Show / host:** *Sales ReFramed* (Ivey Executive Education), host/narrator Eric Janssen · **Published:** 2026-04-23 · **Kyle's role:** guest (one of three interviewees in a narrated season finale; the others are Asad Zaman, CEO of Sales Talent Agency, and author Daniel Pink) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/ivey-future-of-sales-irreplaceable/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vjEE3ku_stE

**Attribution caution.** This is a produced, narrated episode, not a straight interview. Kyle's voice appears only in roughly @03:04–@12:25 (plus host narration about him through @13:25). Everything from ~@13:25 onward is Janssen, Zaman, or Pink — **not Kyle**. The homework vs. hard work frame and the car/"driving" metaphor are the host's, applied to Kyle's examples. The Owner.com description at @04:05 (approaching $100M ARR, $120M Series C, $1B valuation) is **host narration**, not Kyle's words.

## TL;DR
- Kyle tells educators/employers to assume constant AI use: he pushes everyone at Owner to use AI "all of the time" and wonders why new hires don't use it more.
- Reframes "where should I start with AI?" into "how do I transform my company to be AI-native?" — data foundations first, then a leader-owned capability/transformation plan, then use cases.
- Concrete Owner examples: Momentum structuring call transcripts into Salesforce fields; AI-filled personalization fields replacing hand-built ABM research; AI pre-call research, note-taking, CRM filling and follow-ups — but he does not want AI freely writing cold outreach.

## What Kyle argued
- Universities should steer "all the way into AI"; employers care about the work product, not whether an LLM co-wrote it (@03:04). See [[ai-in-gtm]], [[sales-hiring]].
- "where do I start implies" a linear path; AI transformation is "organic and recursive" — build anti-fragility into the organization (@04:05–@05:07).
- Step one is data foundations: "Nothing else will really work until you have good first-party and third-party data" (@05:07). See [[gtm-engineering]].
- The transformation plan falls on the leader; a consultant's AI strategy "is going to be out of date in three to six months anyways" — CEO/CRO and leadership must learn it themselves (@06:11). See [[cro-role-and-board]].
- He deliberately won't name a starting use case (AI BDR, AI forecasting): the answer is data foundations + capability transformation + "building a new type of team", then use cases in context (@06:11–@07:14).
- First-party data: Momentum runs prompts over call transcripts and writes structured answers (competitors evaluated, tech stack, POS, online-ordering vendor) into Salesforce (@07:14–@08:15). See [[ai-sales-stack]].
- Outreach: the old enterprise ABM practice of hand-researching custom Salesforce fields for templated emails is now AI-filled — "same methodology" at far greater scale (@08:15–@09:17). See [[outbound-at-scale]].
- He does not want an LLM free-writing cold email; give it the structure and have it fill specific fields — "hyper-personalization at scale" (@10:23). See [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]].
- "Every step of the way is basically AI enabled now": AI pre-call research (tech stack, Reddit complaints, 10-K, title challenges) sharpens the hypothesis of need; AI note-takers fill CRM fields and propose next steps (not followed blindly); AI drafts follow-ups (@11:23–@12:25).

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Transform to AI-native, not "where do I start"** — recursive, anti-fragile transformation.
- **Three-part order:** data foundations (1st + 3rd party) → leader-owned transformation/capability plan → context-specific use cases.
- **Structured, field-filled AI personalization** (deterministic template + generated fields) vs. free-form LLM cold email.
- (Host framing, not Kyle's: homework vs. hard work.)

## New or changed vs. earlier positions
- **Consistent** with deterministic flows before agents: AI fills fields inside a human-defined structure; no free-writing of outreach (Apr 2026). Contrast with Aug 2026 [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @17:29, where cold email is "blasted out through our cold AI generated email tooling" — likely still structured, but a clear move from the Nov 2024 "we don't do any AI outbound".
- **New emphasis:** the leader-must-learn-it-personally stance (no outsourcing AI strategy to consultants) and a hiring/education stance of *expecting* constant AI use.
- No numbers to compare; Owner ARR/valuation here are host-supplied.
