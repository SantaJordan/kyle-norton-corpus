# E52: Winning in Crowded Markets - The CRO Playbook
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Jonathan Leaf, CRO @ BambooHR ([[person-jonathan-leaf]]) · **Published:** 2025-11-05 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E52-winning-in-crowded-markets-the-cro-playbook-jonathan-leaf-cro-ba/transcript.md`

This episode was backfilled because the earlier collection missed it. The intro still says "by Topline", and the sponsor is UserEvidence. The transcript is Whisper-based with some mis-hearings: "Attentions" is probably the CI tool Attention, and "Garad Rencher" is the CEO's name as transcribed.

## TL;DR
- Kyle's frame: there have "never been more competitive" markets, because vibe-coded AI upstarts are joining incumbents in every category. In undifferentiated markets the theme is **execution**, plus a sales experience that serves as evidence of the post-sale experience.
- Leaf's playbook: the company "shows up" through mission and values. Be prescriptive about who you build for and sell to (Bamboo: SMB, ~20–2,000 employees). Hire to a defined profile and inspect it with conversation intelligence. Position on outcomes ("we sell peace of mind") backed by customer evidence, not features. And "you're not going to differentiate the ocean".
- Kyle brings Owner's **point-of-view positioning** (sell the trade-off, using the pratfall effect), values in scorecards and monthly report cards, Chaos Monkey meeting resets, and his career lessons: everything is "downstream of company selection", and turning down Salesforce in 2012 was his biggest mistake.

## What Kyle said
- Framing: "there are more competitors in all of these categories than ever before because it's so easy to vibe code some product". It's "insane proliferation in every category". @08:11 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- His first startup ran on BambooHR. @04:08
- In markets without obvious technical differentiation, buyers "default to the best sales experience" because it predicts implementation and support. He has implemented SalesLoft "five times now" over Outreach, mostly for the team experience, since the feature differences were "tit for tat". @15:27–16:32 ([[smb-sales-motion]])
- Niche specificity: in crowded markets, a crisp point of view for one segment "even though it means alienating potentially other parts of the market" wins more. @20:45–21:49 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Owner is "a momentum shop" for conversation intelligence. @30:05 ([[ai-sales-stack]])
- Operating rhythm at Owner: a monthly business review in the revenue org, a monthly churn deep dive, a product cadence, and bi-weekly "Tiger Teams" meetings that have "spider webbed out". They were consolidating so that big rocks get Tiger Team meetings every two weeks. @37:16–38:18 ([[org-design]], [[churn-and-retention]])
- **Chaos Monkey** (borrowed from Shopify): at the start of the year every leadership-team member deleted every meeting and rebuilt from scratch. "If you forget about that meeting, it wasn't that important." Meetings crept back and they were pruning again. He thinks it's needed "every 12 months". @38:18–39:19 ([[shopify]])
- Synthesis: "the theme is execution. Execution is what matters". Out-execute on rhythm, discipline, hiring profile and message specificity. @40:23
- **Point-of-view positioning:** Owner's competitors can "check the box" on online ordering and an app. Telling a buyer the rival's app is bad leads to a he-said-she-said standoff and paralysis. Instead, sell the trade-off ("if you care about this, you should go with this other vendor"). @44:30–46:36
- Owner has a "point of view positioning doc" that teaches the **pratfall effect** ("four star effect"): admitting flaws builds trust. It's a "radically transparent style of sales". Owner's pitch: "you're going to have less control, but that means that we will control it for you", backed by "literally the best restaurant marketers in the world" A/B testing. Owners say "I don't want custom fonts. I just want to make money." @46:36–47:41 ([[smb-sales-motion]])
- Values in measurement: company values are "in all of our interview scorecards" and in performance reviews. Frontline teams get **monthly report cards** covering performance, skills and values ("did you scale yourself?... Did you act like an owner? Did you bring good energy?"). @57:05–58:10 ([[hiring-scorecards]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- "If you can't hire against your values and you can't performance manage or review against your values, then your values probably don't mean anything." @58:10–59:13
- Cites J.D. Saint-Martin (president, Lightspeed Commerce): "you got to roll in the mud". @63:27
- His track record is scratch builds: League from zero to $25M ARR, Shopify team from 5 to 80 people, Owner from zero to a team "approaching 200". Almost everyone "other than like three people, I hired." @63:27–64:27 ([[owner-com]])
- "You can't blame the lion for eating a gazelle". Founder-CEOs' convictions are what made the company great, so "either get on board or find somewhere new". @68:36–69:39 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Joining Owner: he "emphatically did not want to leave Shopify". He had just had his second kid and the timing was terrible. Adam "put the tractor beam on me". Owner's values "almost mapped like one for one" with his. @69:39
- People griping about their bosses: "it's our fault most of the time... you picked bad." Everything is "downstream of company selection": product, category, founder. @70:41–71:44 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- He turned down his first CRO role "seven years ago" and wasn't CRO "on paper at owner until maybe 18 months ago". His biggest career mistake was turning down a smaller Salesforce role (BDR manager) "in like 2012". Instead of a CRO title back then he took "a senior lead title at Shopify", which was more valuable for learning. @71:44–72:47

## Guest's core ideas
- In a reseller business (Softchoice) with little IP of its own, the differentiation is how you show up: leadership, people development, culture. "that's what keeps them in seat" @06:10–07:10 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- The company has to "get out of its own way". Mission and values inform decisions and service. @09:12
- "You're not going to differentiate the ocean." Be prescriptive about what you build natively and where you partner. @10:15–11:19 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Bamboo's mission is to be #1 in SMB. With "the biggest in SAS" TAM, each point of US share is "exponential growth". They choose not to go upmarket so they can stay "opinionated". When GTM and product chase different segments, that "oftentimes leads to churn". @18:41–20:45 ([[smb-sales-motion]], [[churn-and-retention]])
- Inbound-heavy companies are "super good at demand capture" (the 5% in market) and weak on the 95%. As first CRO, Leaf diversified routes to market (outbound BDRs, EMEA/APAC/Canada, partner channels) and "went down to the studs" defining every role. @23:53–24:55 ([[outbound-at-scale]], [[org-design]])
- Hiring profiles come from internal lookalikes plus outside benchmarks, a regimented anti-bias process, and post-hire inspection through CI rubrics (call scoring, coaching, deal probability). @28:02–30:05 ([[sales-hiring]], [[ai-sales-stack]])
- New routes to market are judged against model assumptions (ramp speed, message resonance, qualified-opp definition, conversion, incentives) in weekly cross-functional reviews. Make "two-way door decisions". @32:09–34:13 ([[capacity-planning]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- The "critical six": six company priorities, each with an exec/GM owner who can draw on any resource. Leaf owns two and runs them weekly; the exec team reviews all six monthly. @35:16–37:16 ([[org-design]])
- Positioning: stop "chasing our tail on features", sell all-in-one plus a unified data model. "We don't sell a product. We sell peace of mind" (compliance, payroll accuracy, reliability), quantified with customer evidence. @41:24–44:30
- He pushes back on Kyle's idea that the best sales experience wins: "word travels" (G2, Trustpilot). It has to be a "virtuous cycle" end to end. @48:43–49:46
- Culture as revenue driver: without it you have "exclusively mercenaries". Mission plus a culture of winning is "magic". In practice that means interviewing on values, visible leadership (in Utah weekly, back in office 3 days/week), a Voice of Revenue council, Women in Revenue, and reviews weighted ~75% results / ~25% values ("bambooligan"). @50:50–57:05
- Change as first CRO: build credibility and trust, "influence people far more than I want to manage them". He held an auditorium all-hands on day two and communicates up, across and down. @59:13–62:21
- He chose the role through a six-month diligence process on the CEO, TAM and willingness to invest. "My job is to make the CEO successful." @64:27–67:34 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Great CRO = mechanisms for scale, clarity, and time to "roll in the mud". First-time VP advice: Amazon's "learn and be curious", then trust your intuition. @74:50–75:54
