# AI Can Do Everything a Salesperson Does. That’s Exactly Why Salespeople Matter More

**Type:** Substack essay (Kyle's written take on his guest appearance on *Sales Reframed*, hosted by Eric Janssen, alongside separately recorded segments with Daniel Pink and Asad Zaman; no guest in title) · **Published:** 2026-03-31 · **Paywalled:** no · **URL:** https://www.therevenueleadershippodcast.com/p/ai-can-do-everything-a-salesperson · **Raw:** `raw/substack/2026-03-31-ai-can-do-everything-a-salesperson.md`

**Attribution note.** Kyle was one of three interviewees; the post interleaves his own views with summaries of Pink's and Zaman's segments. Points from them are marked "(guest: Name)". Most statistics (McKinsey, Gartner, G2, Bessen) are third-party research Kyle cites, not Owner.com data.

## TL;DR
- AI is automating the "homework" of sales (research, notes, CRM, drafts) while the "hard work" (judgment, trust, reading a room) becomes more valuable.
- Wrong question: "where do I start with AI?" Right question: "how do I transform my company to be AI native?" It starts with data foundations and leader-owned AI fluency.
- AI multiplies expertise; weak reps get little from it. The SDR is not dead; the lazy SDR is.

## Kyle's argument
- AI adoption is "organic and recursive," not linear; reframe from a tool-purchase project to a permanent AI-native transformation. See [[ai-in-gtm]].
- Data foundations come first: "AI is a data model." At Owner.com, Momentum extracts structured fields (competitors mentioned, tech stack, POS platform) from call transcripts into Salesforce. See [[ai-sales-stack]], [[revops]].
- AI strategy "falls on the leader"; you can't outsource it to consultants for more than six months because the tech moves too fast. See [[cro-role-and-board]], [[career-and-leadership]].
- Homework vs. hard work framing (from host Eric Janssen): AI should eat the homework; judgment is the growing category. See [[ai-in-gtm]].
- Buyers now arrive pre-researched via AI chatbots, so the human value-add is perspective and trust; info parity / "seller beware" (guest: Daniel Pink). See [[buyer-ai-aeo-geo]].
- AI is a multiplier of expertise ("multiplying zero still gives you zero"); top researchers doubled productivity, bottom third got nothing (study cited by guest: Asad Zaman). Kyle maps this to AEs. See [[talent-density]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]].
- Universities should lean into AI; Kyle doesn't criticize new hires for using AI and wonders why they don't use it more. See [[sales-hiring]].
- AI SDRs become copilots, not replacements, partly because FCC/TCPA rules require AI voice disclosure (guest: Asad Zaman); Kyle's synthesis: zag toward depth and personalization when everyone zigs to volume. See [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]], [[outbound-at-scale]].
- "Upheaval, not apocalypse": ATM/teller history and Pink's ABCs (Attunement, Buoyancy, Clarity) (guest: Daniel Pink); Kyle adds the caveat that paradigm shifts (mobile banking) can eliminate jobs and "we're in the partial automation phase."
- Advice: fix data foundations, build leadership AI fluency, push reps to use AI as a research engine; early-career people should "become the person whose expertise makes AI useful." See [[career-and-leadership]].

## Key quotes
- "AI is changing the homework of sales, not the hard work."
- "Stop asking “where do I start?” and start asking “how do I transform my company to be AI native?”"
- "AI is a data model. Feed it garbage and you get garbage back, just faster."
- "You can’t outsource your AI strategy to McKinsey and expect it to hold up for more than six months."
- "AI is a multiplier, and multiplying zero still gives you zero."
- "Day one that somebody starts with me, I’m not criticizing their use of AI. If anything, I’m wondering why they’re not using it more."
- "When everyone zigs toward automation and volume, you zag toward depth and personalization."
- "Become the person whose expertise makes AI useful. Build the filter."

## Numbers & facts
- Owner.com described as "an AI-native sales org approaching $100M ARR" (as of 2026-03-31).
- Homework "used to eat 60% of a rep’s day" (Kyle/Janssen framing, not measured).
- Third-party, cited by Kyle: McKinsey 2025 State of AI: 78% of orgs use AI in ≥1 function (up from 55%); Gartner: by 2028 AI agents outnumber sellers 10x, <40% of sellers will say agents improved productivity; Gartner: sellers partnering with AI 3.7x more likely to hit quota; 2025 G2 survey: half of B2B software buyers start in an AI chatbot, up 71% in four months.
- (guest: Asad Zaman) Sales Talent Agency long-form InMails: 60–70% response rates vs. ~15% norm; FCC ruled Feb 2024 AI voice calls fall under TCPA.
- (guest: Daniel Pink, citing Bessen) ATMs cut tellers per branch 20→13 (1988–2004), banks opened 43% more branches; post-2010 teller employment dropped 50% in twelve years.

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Homework vs. hard work** (Eric Janssen's framing, adopted by Kyle).
- **"Where do I start" → "how do I become AI native"** reframe (Kyle).
- **AI as expertise multiplier / "build the filter"** (Kyle, from Zaman's study).
- **"The SDR isn’t dead. The lazy SDR is."** (section header).
- **New ABCs: Attunement, Buoyancy, Clarity** (Daniel Pink, *To Sell Is Human*).
- **Upheaval, not apocalypse; partial automation vs. paradigm shift** (Pink + Kyle's caveat).
