# E04: Mark Roberge on Redefining Sales Leadership in the AI Era
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Mark Roberge, co-founder @ Stage 2 Capital (ex-HubSpot first sales leader/CRO, HBS faculty) · **Published:** 2024-10-16 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E04-mark-roberge-on-redefining-sales-leadership-in-the-ai-era/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Topic is GTM technology strategy in the AI platform shift. Roberge's Stage 2 LP experiment (May 2023) produced 50 meetings, two pilots and no rollouts at big companies, mostly blocked by IT/legal lacking an AI policy. Portfolio startups without that red tape got AI beating BDR output.
- Adoption advice: "design big start small"; bring a vendor in through one curious rep, roll out only after proven ROI; agree success criteria for a pilot up front (gives and gets); reduce uncertainty as cheaply and quickly as possible; default to buy over build.
- Kyle's stance: he is a deliberate early adopter (2–4 hrs/week with vendors) and, for seed/Series A vendors, he bets on the founders over the current feature set (his Momentum example).

## What Kyle said

- He is a Stage 2 LP "for almost three years", and "most of my hiring formula is built on that hiring formula" from *The Sales Acceleration Formula*. @03:14 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Among Series B/C CROs he swaps notes with, "I don't see tons of In production use cases yet". @19:49 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- Early-adopter history: one of the earliest SalesLoft and Chorus customers ("Kyle Porter onboarded me himself"); early cadence use was "such an insane competitive advantage". He admits he "might even orient too much to chasing shiny tools" and dedicates "at least two to four hours a week with vendors". @22:55–24:00 ([[ai-sales-stack]], [[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Cites Matt Dixon's JOLT research (more than half of deals lost to no decision) to argue operators are frozen by fear and status-quo bias. @25:07 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- He requires the Teresa Torres opportunity-solution tree in his team's project template, because he has run pilots and realised "I can just sort of coach my around this problem", wasting the vendor's time and his. @40:56–41:57 ([[build-vs-buy-software]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Vendor selection for seed/Series A tools: "I'm basically making a founder bed"; "Just pick the founders that you think are gonna win that market Even if the product might might not be quite as good". @48:17 ([[build-vs-buy-software]], [[ai-sales-stack]])

Started as a small pilot with "controlled costs controlled blast radius", and it now "stretches across a bunch of different parts of our business". @50:27–51:28 ([[momentum]], [[owner-com]])

## Guest's core ideas
- A leader's job is to be best at their current role, not at every GTM role; if the CRO is the best seller, "You're not a good sales leader". @05:16–07:17 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- Cloud-transition analogy: incumbents hit the innovator's dilemma (Siebel), so the AI winners may be new players. He expects "a little bit of like an AI winter". @10:22–14:38 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- Stage 2 LP experiment: about 250 GTM-AI applicants in four weeks, about 75 promoted, 50 meetings, two pilots, no rollouts, because IT/legal shut them down. In the portfolio, AI set about 35 meetings a month vs a BDR team's 17. @16:38–18:43 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]])
- Why companies are slow: status-quo human nature, post-2022 scar tissue, and tools that are still "latch-ons" to existing stacks. @20:49–22:55 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- Rep input needs a "what's in it for the rep". His HubSpot example: $50k of Salesforce customisation took logging from 17 clicks to three. @27:14–30:28 ([[revops]])
- Bottoms-up adoption: pair a vendor with one high-IQ "tester" rep, scale to the team on ROI, then do a full RevOps deployment. A BDR's free Sales Navigator trial spread to 85 BDRs. @32:34–35:41 ([[ai-sales-stack]])
- Strong preference to buy and configure rather than build GTM tools, because engineering is busy on the core product. @36:44 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Pilots: agree up front on "the six specific success criteria" (ideally in the contract), who sets it up, and IT/legal involvement, framed as "gives and gets". @37:47–40:56 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Entrepreneurship is "about uncertainty management": a Google Sheet before Affinity at Stage 2. @43:03–44:06 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Coaching: don't make reps like you (the guard/forward/center analogy). To beat his own conflict avoidance he kept "a daily to-do to give negative feedback to someone". @51:28–55:37 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[career-and-leadership]])
