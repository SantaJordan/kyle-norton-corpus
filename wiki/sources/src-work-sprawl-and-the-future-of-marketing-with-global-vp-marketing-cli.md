# E48: Work Sprawl and the Future of Marketing
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Kyle Coleman, Global VP Marketing @ ClickUp (ex-CMO Clari, Copy.ai) · **Published:** 2025-10-08 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/work-sprawl-and-the-future-of-marketing-with-global-vp-marketing-cli/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- The marketing mandate hasn't changed, but the resources have: "a team of four people versus what used to take me a team of 40". CMOs must know how work actually gets done. Map every campaign deliverable, cut agency, contractor and tool bloat, codify processes so AI can run them (domain expertise first, then prompting), and free people for strategic thinking.
- Positioning should evangelize the *problem*, not the category name or the features. ClickUp reframed "convergence" as the answer to "work sprawl", after 100+ conversations. Automate everything transactional so humans do only what humans uniquely can.
- Kyle Norton adds Owner's 2026 line-by-line vendor review, its new head of applied AI for GTM, its traffic-light change-management model, his own podcast-to-newsletter AI workflow, and his "point of view positioning" (feature-dump first, then the POV).

*Note: both speakers are named Kyle. Only lines tied to Owner, Shopify or Kyle Norton's own content and talks are attributed to Kyle Norton.*

## What Kyle said
- **Owner 2026 vendor cuts:** Owner is "going through like line by line, vendor by vendor, agency by by agency, asking ourselves like is this absolutely necessary? Could we do with 50% less?" Why: "there's lots of mechanisms to add things to companies that there's no cadence or rhythm or ritual to remove things other than maybe annual planning if you have a CFO that has good stewardship." @09:09–10:10 ([[build-vs-buy-software]], [[operating-rhythm]])
- **Jobs:** "I'm not sold on all the jobs go away. But I I am sold that you're going to need to be a much different operator in in this world and be able to think strategically and in systems." @12:12 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- **Owner applied AI:** jamming context across Claude MCPs and OpenAI tools leaves you "in this wonky spot". "we hired a head of applied AI for um go to market to try to just like solve it in this part of the business." @23:24–24:24 (hire unnamed in the episode; Owner's applied-AI lead is [[person-yg]]) ([[ai-operating-model]])
- **AI literacy:** "you need to know enough of it to understand the primitives". He often asks "do you know the difference between pre-training and post-training?" Slop comes from chaining "multiple layers of generative outputs ... you're stacking lossiness, you're stacking hallucination". Companies also need in-house expertise to decide, for example, that "it's not Zapier that should be in the middle of this. this should be with like an MCP". @24:24–26:28 ([[ai-operating-model]], [[context-harness-and-memory]])
- **Heat-seeking missiles:** "we want heatseeking missiles for pain" (borrowed from "Tana Tanden", as transcribed). At Owner the balance is "a bunch of these super intense people with very high expectations that want to solve problems but still can treat each other really well". @29:34–30:37 ([[culture-and-high-standards]], [[owner-com]])
- **Owner's SMB motion:** "we want to be able to to serve the bottom of our market in like a much less hands-on way have the product do more and more of the work". Triggers send a rep in when a customer gets stuck, so "now you have product, demand genen, and sales all like doing something similar." @37:46 ([[smb-sales-motion]], [[org-design]])
- **RGAs:** "AI will allow sellers to do more selling". "We always say revenue generating activities over everything". @40:48 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- **His metric:** "my target is actually a company growth target ... the metric I I am optimizing for is like how fast is this company growing and how much enterprise value are we creating which means it's got to be efficient and low churn high retention". @40:48–41:49 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- **Change management at Owner:** at his GTM talk, change management "was like a footnote that I sort of expanded on". "the companies that can digest the most change" will win. For "the last I don't know 18 24 months" Owner has used a "traffic model": "green, yellow, or red light change", each with set communication and cascade expectations. It came about because reps said "you got to slow down. This everything is chaos", "the number one thing that came out in this engagement survey". Without that muscle, "either it creates chaos or you launch a bunch of change but nothing happens." @50:59–53:01 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[org-design]])
- **His writing workflow:** he hits "my whisper flow hotkey" and yaps a post brief. Without Wi-Fi he felt "so useless now without my little super super intelligence friend". For podcast newsletters, "the transcript goes into my Claude project which has the prompt and all the context". Then "I do like a 10-minute claw edit then I'll do a 20 minute hand edit". The first few newsletters were "literally three hours doing it by hand", done on purpose to learn "what good looks like". @55:03–57:03 ([[personal-ai-stack]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- **Personal CRM wish:** he wants a personal CRM that a call recorder updates, for the many intro and job requests he fields. @57:03–58:04
- **Positioning gripe:** buyers hear "we are an agentic platform to accelerate the transformation of value". "I actually tell them to feature dump right off the top": Owner is "website, online ordering, email, text message, marketing". This follows *Made to Stick*: "It's got to be concrete." @62:08–64:13 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- **Point-of-view positioning:** "the last like six or seven years, I've taught what I call point of view positioning". "if you believe this then uniquely owner or Shopify or league are the only companies who solve it in that way". Owner's POV: "restaurant owners don't want to be digital marketers", so opt into an opinionated, low-choice system that Owner upgrades weekly. A design-school owner who wants custom fonts is "not the fit". He cited Mike Maples' *Pattern Breakers* (choice vs comparison) and "compete with no one and no one can compete with you." @66:14–67:14 ([[point-of-view-positioning]], [[shopify]])

## Guest's core ideas
- The CMO mandate is "more similar than it ever was different". The resources changed: "a team of four people versus what used to take me a team of 40". SDR-leadership process discipline transfers directly to AI workflows. @03:02–05:05 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- Audit "the contractors that you have on staff the agencies you have on staff the tooling that you use". Not knowing how work gets done is "a dereliction of duty". Headcount is "not a brag anymore". @06:06–09:09 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Map small, medium and large campaign deliverables and owners, then decide line by line what AI does. "Start the maze at the end": domain expertise ("what good looks like") comes before prompting. Today you mostly need to describe your process in natural language ("10 bullet points"). @13:12–17:17 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- With grunt work automated, the leader's job is making everyone a strategic, POV-driven, problem-focused marketer. @10:10–11:12 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- "the best product wins" is false. Evangelize the problem with owned language (Category Pirates, *Play Bigger*, *The Existing Market Trap*: "name, frame, and claim"). From 100+ conversations, ClickUp landed on "work sprawl", with convergence → context → AI transformation. If sellers can't tell the story, "it is dead on arrival". @19:19–23:24, @60:06–62:08 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Leadership: get comfortable delegating and pushing. Everyone should "runs toward the fires, not away from them". Your "first team" is horizontal (cross-functional peers), and buy-in from SE, pricing and sales came before productizing ClickUp into SKUs. @27:31–34:42 ([[culture-and-high-standards]], [[org-design]])
- Lines are blurring: automate whatever is automatable (paywall signals, website chat). "many buyers want their buying process to be completely humanless". Build off-ramps so reps never transact small deals. @34:42–40:48 ([[smb-sales-motion]])
- Ownership: he owns pipeline (including sales-sourced), a head of growth owns self-serve revenue, the heads of sales own revenue, and the COO (Gaurav) connects them. "Friction is healthy". A center-of-excellence team builds internal agents and templates. @41:49–44:52
- Adoption: show people what's possible, require "one AI use case" per person per week, and lead it yourself. Start with a content waterfall from webinar transcripts and voice notes. A great CMO has internal influence. @47:56–55:03, @67:14
