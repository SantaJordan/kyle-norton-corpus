# Topline (Pavilion) E92: Kyle Norton of Owner.com on How Great CROs Drive Enterprise Value

**Show / host:** Topline (Pavilion) — this episode hosted by AJ Bruno (CEO, QuotaPath) and Asad Zaman (CEO, STA); Sam Jacobs absent · **Published:** 2025-01-19 (YouTube upload date; recorded at the start of 2025) · **Kyle's role:** guest (third co-host seat) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/topline-e92-great-cros-enterprise-value/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xW8nNE5AhAY

Attribution note: three speakers, no labels. Lines below are attributed to Kyle only where context is unambiguous (first-person Owner.com, his podcast, his Substack, his Canadian health story). The "less than 5% / 1% of CROs are world class" claim and the "RevOps leaders as CRO/COO" debate belong to the co-hosts, not Kyle.

## TL;DR
- From interviewing CROs on [[revenue-leadership-podcast]], Kyle's takeaway: great CROs are diverse; what unites them is honest self-awareness about their "superpower," leaning into it, and building a team around the gaps.
- Owner.com started ~$2.5M "two and a half years ago," is now "considerably more than 10x that," and he is confident of >2x in 2025 (past $60M); he plans in years and uses a 4-P bet framework (potentials, payoff, probability, perspiration).
- On AI, the o1/o3 reasoning models gave him "a more profound sense of unease"; he predicts fewer managers, elevated principal ICs, and people "managing virtual heads, like agents."

## What Kyle argued
- Great CROs vary widely; the common thread is leaning into an individual superpower (systems thinking vs. leader-manufacturing, e.g., Sam Blond at Brex) and building complementary skills around themselves @03:07–05:13. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Hiring a head of revenue is two bets: a "need to have" stage-appropriate leader for the next 24 months (checklist competencies) and a "nice to have" super-learner who could be your 10-year, through-IPO leader @08:22–10:26. [[sales-hiring]] [[cro-role-and-board]]
- Post-2015 ("post-sales acceleration era") revenue leaders under 40 skew toward structured, systems-oriented operators; non-systems CROs succeed with a long-time RevOps/BizOps partner @12:28–14:34. [[revops]] [[cro-role-and-board]]
- His Substack distinction: a "real revenue executive" vs. a senior leader — company-first decisions, understanding enterprise value, long-term revenue quality, reading a P&L, being a multiplier for peers @23:51–24:52. [[cro-role-and-board]]
- Real revenue executives plan on longer time horizons; building outbound only when inbound wanes is "nine months" too late @25:54–26:58. [[outbound-at-scale]] [[capacity-planning]]
- He uses a modified Annie Duke framework for bets — potentials, payoff, probability, perspiration — to fit revenue strategy with company strategy; "The commonality in all our jobs is the effective allocation of capital" @28:00–30:04. [[capacity-planning]] [[cro-role-and-board]]
- Micro-SMB must be "all systems all the time" with a small human-capital footprint; enterprise is more art/deal creativity, but still needs a different, surgical systems approach (e.g., scripted forecast calls) @31:05–35:19. [[smb-sales-motion]] [[metrics-and-forecasting]]
- o1/o3 reasoning models move AI beyond pattern-match "rinse and repeat" use (Momentum CRM field-fill, an AI roleplay simulator) toward ingesting a deal and giving actionable next steps @36:21–44:53. [[ai-in-gtm]] [[ai-sales-stack]]
- Fewer managers will be needed; Owner has created principal IC roles comped like team leads; people will increasingly manage agents @53:26–55:29. [[org-design]] [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]]
- Reasoning models could power a self-serve journey for the smallest deals @55:29. [[smb-sales-motion]] [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]]
- Most of his output (research, dockets, newsletter) runs on "little systems" that are mostly AI-powered @61:46–62:50. [[ai-in-gtm]] [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Changed his mind: he can no longer sit on the sidelines of Canadian politics; fears Canada will lose its innovation sector and best people to the US @66:58–69:06. [[career-and-leadership]]

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **Superpower + build-around** model of great CROs.
- **Two bets in a revenue-leader hire**: stage-appropriate (checklist) vs. super-learner (potential).
- **Senior leader vs. real revenue executive** (his Substack piece): company-first, enterprise value, revenue quality, P&L literacy, multiplier, systems thinker, followership.
- **4 Ps of bets** — potentials, payoff, probability, perspiration (modified from Annie Duke; opportunity-solution-tree inputs).
- **Planning horizon**: months → quarters → years as the business scales.
- **Systems vs. art by segment** (micro-SMB vs. enterprise; "jazz within boundaries" framing raised on the show).
- **Principal IC track** + "managing virtual heads."
