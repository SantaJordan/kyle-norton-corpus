# E34: Why Enablement Is the Most Overlooked Growth Lever with Luke Arno, CRO of Transcend
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Luke Arno, CRO @ Transcend (ex-Salesforce, Yammer, Box, Segment/Twilio Segment) · **Published:** 2025-05-28 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E34-why-enablement-is-the-most-overlooked-growth-lever-with-luke/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Enablement is the most leveraged dollar (citing John McMahon) because sales productivity is *the* revenue metric. Arno's first hire at Transcend was a head of enablement who had carried a bag.
- Three chapters: onboarding, ongoing (QBRs at least 50% enablement), and "field sales strategy", meaning in-quarter deal help through weekly office hours. Mutual commitment starts in the first interview.
- Kyle shares how he sets expectations in Owner interviews (everyone gets coached), his SMB "aggregation of marginal gains" view, his preference for building his own methodology, and his CRAMPS engagement framework.

## What Kyle said
- Working for world-class companies is "underappreciated". His advice: "just get into the best company you possibly can. Worry a little bit less about the role and the comp". @02:04–03:05 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- "great enablement looks way more like sports training than it looks like uh HR learning and development". Enablement "is criminally underinvested in", often "an afterthought". @05:07 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- He interviews every sales candidate "all the way to SDRs". He tells them what's *not* great about Owner: "We're not a big work life culture company. Like we work really hard... It's a performance culture." Even top performers get coached monthly on a "secret sauce" with their manager. A rep who wants to be left alone while crushing "won't like owner". @24:34–26:36 ([[sales-hiring]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- "it doesn't take too many people acting too cool for school". If top reps don't lean into enablement, disengagement "cascades pretty rapidly". @26:36 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Enablement is "underpaid" and "underresource[d]" as a function, so elite talent is hard to attract. @37:45
- He is "two years into doing the same thing here at Owner" (building enablement from scratch). @41:51
- SMB lens: "we're an SMB business and you're really just trying to pick up basis points across all of these little touch points". "Atomic Habits is one of our big like must-read books". @46:58 ([[smb-sales-motion]])
- Methodology: he's done a lot of Challenger and light Force Management work at Vision Critical, but has "always aired towards like assembling my own methodology. And maybe that's cuz I'm a control freak". @54:08 ([[sales-methodology]])
- "Flexibility within a framework": overly rigid scripts take away autonomy and agency, which has "very like negative motivational consequences". Shared deal language (e.g., Challenger Customer's skeptic/blocker) "accelerates things so much" and is "the oil that really like lubricates an organization". @57:13–59:15 ([[sales-methodology]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- "The basics is everything". LinkedIn sales content fixates on "the shiny stuff". Rock-solid discovery, crisp value language and cycle navigation are "95% of it". @60:16
- He is coaching managers away from carrots and sticks toward intrinsic motivation and an internal locus of control: "stoke the fire within somebody as opposed to like lighting a fire behind them." @64:21–65:24 ([[recognition-and-motivation]])
- Owner is rebuilding its competency matrix. He wrote an engagement-strategy doc that extends Pink's autonomy, mastery and purpose into "CRAMPS": community, recognition, autonomy, mastery, purpose, steps (career progression), plus promoting from within. @68:26–70:28 ([[recognition-and-motivation]], [[person-daniel-pink]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Business case: enablement is infrastructure whose gains are credited to reps. Measure against a pre-enablement baseline (time to pipeline and productivity), using leading indicators like pipeline coverage, pacing to ramp, and S1, then S2, then S3+ progression. @06:09–12:20 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Three chapters: onboarding, ongoing (methodology, PG, POV, cross-functional product launches), and field sales strategy (mid-quarter help moving stage 3 to stage 4, where close rates hit 85%, e.g. new TCO/ROI assets). @12:20–16:27
- What great looks like: enablers with field empathy, clear mutual expectations, and dynamic curriculum. Remote work killed osmosis, so get people together in person quarterly. @17:28–20:31
- Mutual commitment starts in the interview. His principle is "love and accountability". Hire high will, then drive to high skill fast. @21:31–24:34 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Hire an enablement leader who is a coach at heart, has carried a bag, and knows complex technical sales. Interview by having them present their enablement philosophy and how they implemented it. @31:39–42:54 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Build sequence: first a common language (Command of the Message and MEDDPICC), after diagnosing ICP sprawl. Then a competency framework (PG, complex deal management, territory-CEO mindset) tied to performance management and career ladders. @42:54–50:02, @67:25–69:27 ([[sales-methodology]])
- Pull from everywhere: Hoffman's "perfect meeting" for first calls, Challenger teach-tailor-take control for disruptive sells, Force Management for process and MEDDPICC. Reps can use any discovery style as long as value drivers and deal integrity hold. @55:08–58:14
- Leadership is "the intersection of data and psychology": put the right person in the right position. New second-line leaders must find their value in the chain of command. @61:18–63:20, @71:28–72:30 ([[career-and-leadership]])
