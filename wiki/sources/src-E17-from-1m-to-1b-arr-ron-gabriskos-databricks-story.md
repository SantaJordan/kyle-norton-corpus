# E17: From $1M to $1B ARR: Ron Gabrisko's Databricks Story
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Ron Gabrisko, CRO @ Databricks (transcript spells "Gabrisco") · **Published:** 2025-01-22 (RSS pubDate) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E17-from-1m-to-1b-arr-ron-gabriskos-databricks-story/transcript.md`

> Transcript quality is poor in places (Whisper mis-hearings, e.g., "re-invite" = reinvent). Quotes are kept as written.

## TL;DR
- Gabrisko's frame is four chapters ($0→10–20M product-market fit; →$100M; →$1B; multi-billion). The company, product and GTM team must be reinvented at each, which is how he has stayed CRO for 9+ years when most companies cycle through several.
- GTM moves: hire enterprise reps early to learn pricing; price at a slight premium to competitors; build regional and segment leadership; target about a 50/50 mix of internal promotions and external hires; run leadership training and a data-driven leadership index (vision, strategy, culture, hiring, execution).
- His personal growth comes from first-principles debate culture, hiring the best experts for each new blocker, and an exec coach (lesson: patience and listening). Kyle relates with his own coaching lesson and Owner's new values-based scorecards.

## Guest's core ideas
- "You have to re-invite [reinvent] yourself as a company and as a leader at every single stage" across product, go-to-market and team. @03:05–04:10 ([[org-design]])
- $10→100M: enterprise buyers bring different budgets, security needs and longer cycles. He hired an enterprise team early to meet every open-source enterprise user and shape pricing. @06:15–09:31 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Pricing: talk to customers, price "a slight premium to the competition" to signal quality and leave discount room. Pricing later moved to a sophisticated internal team "literally like PhDs." @09:31–13:40
- Leaders: gritty startup operators early; leaders "that have seen that movie before" for 10→100M; upgrades or layering from ~$100M–$1B; regions (Americas, EMEA, APJ) and verticals. @14:41–17:53 ([[org-design]], [[sales-hiring]])
- Aim for about 50/50 internal promotion vs external hire to be "a destination place" while bringing new blood. @18:54–20:02 ([[talent-density]])
- Leadership training (founders teach principles), high-potential programs, and a "leadership index" built from 360s and culture surveys covering vision, strategy, culture, hiring, execution. @23:07–25:09 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- First-principles culture: newcomers can't just bring "how I've always done it"; two-way vs one-way door decisions; interviews probe culture principles and "keep the bar up" in hiring. @39:00–44:17 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Beyond $1B you can't be in every decision, so focus on repeatability and trusting teammates. Execution, not product or network effects, is the durable advantage. A great CRO is defined by "Hiring culture." @45:18–51:43 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
