# Topline (Pavilion): E78: CRO Kyle Norton on Executive Fear, Boards, and the CRO Journey

**Show / host:** Topline (Pavilion) / Sam Jacobs, Asad Zaman (AJ Bruno named in the open but not audibly present) · **Published:** 2024-10-06 (hosts call it episode 79 on air) · **Kyle's role:** guest co-panelist, two to three weeks after launching *The Revenue Leadership Podcast* (episode 1: Jason Lemkin) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/topline-e78-executive-fear-boards/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nHa3Gxk0og

**Attribution caution:** a four-way conversation with no speaker labels (Kyle is mis-transcribed as "Karl"). Only lines clearly Kyle's (first person about Owner, his 2019 exit, his RevOps leader, his podcast) are attributed below. Sam Jacobs' and Asad Zaman's long segments (ZIRP-era CROs, Lencioni, HR outsourcing, Pavilion's 62→27 headcount, OpenAI valuation, optimism) are not Kyle's views. Later Topline appearance: [[src-topline-e92-great-cros-enterprise-value]].

## TL;DR
- Reacting to Lemkin's episode (boards and CEOs aren't out to get CROs): Kyle sides with Lemkin—fear in a revenue leader is fatal, and he lived it (afraid of his 2019 number, he left that company); going into 2025 at Owner he is "full of confidence".
- "Don't blame the product" means don't make it your excuse in a board meeting—not a ban on discussing product. To give product feedback well: be dispassionate, socialize one-on-one before groups, lead with data (avoid triggering reactance).
- RevOps is a "force multiplier", not a C-suite seat: he invests in it far earlier than most, and his RevOps leader of ~8 years is his "right-hand guy"; RevOps' real jobs are account/data supply, the sales-acceleration stack, business process, and reporting.

## What Kyle argued
- Reception of the Lemkin episode split roughly by quality: the CROs he respects found it heartening; the skeptics should consider "maybe it's not them, it's me" @04:07–05:07. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[person-jason-lemkin]]
- Fear "is sort of the end of you as a revenue leader": in 2019 he doubted his number had a plan, a CEO "for sure would have sensed that", and he left—contrast with his confidence about Owner's 2025 @05:07–06:13. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]] [[cro-role-and-board]]
- Lemkin's point is "don't blame the product" as an excuse; if the product truly doesn't work, why are you the CRO there? Product/GTM integration discussions belong in the exec team, not as a board excuse @19:48–20:52. [[cro-role-and-board]]
- Most revenue leaders deliver product feedback badly—"too direct, it's combative, it's done in a wrapper of excuse-making"—triggering reactance; he used to be "horrible at this" and fixed it with an exec coach @23:02–24:04. [[career-and-leadership]]
- Three rules for product feedback: dispassionate facts without us/them language; socialize privately (CPO/CEO one-on-ones) before any big group; lead with data, not the last big customer's request @24:04–25:05. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[org-design]]
- RevOps: probably no C-suite seat, but "one of the most important things" he builds teams around; he invests "much, much earlier than most people would" @36:31. [[revops]]
- His RevOps leader (Steve; ~8 years across three companies) is an extension of himself and a decision-making stand-in; an ex-sales leader who understands the rep's reality @36:31–37:31. [[person-steve-dinner]] [[revops]]
- What RevOps does: supply high-propensity accounts with high-quality data (territory carving matters less than people think), the sales-acceleration stack and automations (incl. AI sims), business process as "organizational lubricant", and reporting/scoreboards @40:38–42:43. [[revops]]
- RevOps works "hand-in-hand" with BizOps/data, which owns the single source of truth @43:43. [[revops]] [[org-design]]
- On OpenAI: "They're not playing a gross margin game"—it's a "winner-take-all race to AGI" @57:13 (Kyle per Sam's reply "what you just said, Kyle").
