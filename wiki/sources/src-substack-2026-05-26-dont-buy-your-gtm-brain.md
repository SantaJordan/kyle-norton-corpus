# Don’t Buy Your GTM Brain

**Type:** Substack essay (pure Kyle; no guest) · **Published:** 2026-05-26 · **Paywalled:** yes (preview only; ~2,605-word post, preview ends as the "build vs buy question is changing" section begins) · **URL:** https://www.therevenueleadershippodcast.com/p/dont-buy-your-gtm-brain · **Raw:** `raw/substack/2026-05-26-dont-buy-your-gtm-brain.md`

**Every claim on this page is (preview only).** The preview is long and substantive, but the post's prescriptive build-vs-buy section is cut off.

## TL;DR
- Core principle: "buy infrastructure, build (or at least own) your intelligence"; a vendor that owns the layer encoding how you sell means "you may be renting your GTM brain" (preview only).
- Kyle defines the AI stack: model, harness, agent, context & retrieval, memory (3 layers), evals; quality is "mostly a function of what it can see" (preview only).
- Proprietary GTM learning must live in your own systems to reach the "Recursively Self-Improving Revenue System" (Level 4 of his SaaStr Sophistication Ladder) (preview only).

## Kyle's argument
- Many GTM leaders will get AI build vs. buy wrong because the old software-buying model feels safe; AI systems learn and compound, so the stakes differ (preview only). See [[build-vs-buy-software]].
- Buy the dialer, CRM plumbing, anything where uptime/reliability/compliance/scale beat uniqueness; "no prize for vibe coding mediocre commodity infrastructure" (preview only). See [[build-vs-buy-software]], [[ai-sales-stack]].
- Be careful buying anything that encodes how your company "thinks, sells, scores, prioritizes, coaches, researches, forecasts, or wins" — that's operating intelligence (preview only).
- GTM leaders need precise definitions because "fuzzy language leads to fuzzy strategy" (preview only). See [[ai-in-gtm]].
- Agent is not the smart part; vendors selling "an AI agent" sell a bundle of model + harness + memory; evaluate the bundle (preview only). See [[ai-sdrs-and-agents]].
- Context/retrieval is "one of the most quietly proprietary layers": "Whoever decides what your AI sees, decides what your AI knows" (preview only).
- Memory has three layers (short-term state, structured knowledge, long-term learned patterns); learned patterns are most valuable and most often trapped in vendors; "your platform decision is also a memory decision" (citing Sarah Wooders, Harrison Chase, Eli Mernit) (preview only). See [[playbooks-and-documentation]].
- Evals are "arguably the most proprietary layer" because "your definition of “good” is your company" (preview only). See [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]].
- Examples where memory compounds: AI call coaching, pre-call research, lead scoring, forecasting, account planning, win-loss (preview only).
- Not dogmatic: "I am not religious about building everything internally (that’s usually a terrible strategy)"; opinions "shift rapidly with the market" (preview only).
- Context: delivered at SaaStr "last week"; promises a fuller AI-native GTM write-up the following week (preview only). See [[saastr]].
