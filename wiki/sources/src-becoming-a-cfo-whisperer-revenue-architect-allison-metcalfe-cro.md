# E56: Becoming a CFO Whisperer & Revenue Architect (Allison Metcalfe, CRO @ Zenoti)
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Allison Metcalfe, CRO @ Zenoti (ex-SVP @ LiveRamp; CRO @ Demandbase, Cloudera) · **Published:** 2025-12-03 (RSS; metadata has no date) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/becoming-a-cfo-whisperer-revenue-architect-allison-metcalfe-cro/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Executive relationship management during 2026 planning. Metcalfe's tools are *Multipliers* ("everyone's weighing in... whether or not you're inviting them to"), advocacy and inquiry ("show my work"), radical empathy for product and finance, and a personal-context deck.
- CFO: bring finance into everything so the CFO "could defend it and present it on my behalf". Plan with guardrails and "conditions for ignition". Present trade-offs. "Don't bring your feelings to a data fight."
- Kyle's own views: champion-based (not consensus) hiring, remote trust needs structured facilitation, his "blueprint" deck ("opinionated but open"), the financial literacy CROs need, and pushing back on 50/50 CRO comp in favor of enterprise-value alignment.

## Guest's core ideas
- A great CRO is a revenue architect, not the best salesperson. Her strength comes from GTM strategy/ops and GM roles: whole-journey and data-driven thinking. @03:03–04:04 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- *Multipliers* (Liz Wiseman workshop at Salesforce/Jigsaw): facilitate instead of presenting the vision. People buy into plans they help create. Diminishers "misunderstand their role as leaders". The 360 question: "how much of your intelligence do you use". @07:05–12:11, @22:22 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- Advocacy and inquiry: show your work and ask "can you help me understand how you got there?" Soliciting input is not consensus, and LiveRamp over-indexed on flatness. @14:12–16:15 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Signals of unhealthy exec relationships: feedback routed through the CEO, everyone talking about each other, head nods without behavior change. Rebrand "feedback" as "advice". @24:25–26:28 ([[org-design]])
- Radical empathy for product ("who's yelling at you?") and finance. Bring FP&A into leadership meetings. Optimize for the metric your CFO cares about (EBITDA vs S&M% vs ARR/employee). The aim is a CFO who could "defend it and present it on my behalf". @37:44–51:59 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- 2026 planning: guardrails plus "conditions for ignition" (green zone / red zone / double down). Present what you give up if you don't invest. @53:00–58:04 ([[capacity-planning]])
- Change via data: set a target (e.g. cut "contact and future" (as transcribed) by 15%) and let the team design and evidence the milestones. Push complainers to name 3–5 measurable deliverables. @58:04–63:13 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Grow horizontally, not just vertically (Shari Redstone). Use an alter ego ("Sasha Fierce") to take hard feedback less personally. @64:13–68:15
