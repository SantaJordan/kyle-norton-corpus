# E08: Sam Blond's Blueprint for Building Exceptional Leaders & Teams
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Sam Blond, co-founder & CEO of a new sales-system startup (ex-partner @ Founders Fund, ex-CRO @ Brex, ex-VP Sales @ Zenefits) · **Published:** 2024-11-13 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E08-sam-blonds-blueprint-for-building-exceptional-leaders-tea/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Blond's leader alumni (Matt Plank, Jameson Young, Brandon Boyle and others) were all #1 reps first. That required a never-satisfied mindset, a positive attitude and an intense work ethic, which a team will emulate. He builds leaders less by coaching and more by recruiting well and creating "a hypercompetitive system".
- Promote first-line managers from within, but "out of want and not out of need". At second line, only true rock stars get promoted; bring in experienced outside VPs. The test of a scaling leader is the ability to hire people more senior and better than you. Source leaders in-network, and never trade quality for urgency.
- Kyle: Owner just hired an external VP of Sales, while promoting first-line managers on a "Heck yeah or no" bar. His career advice is "quality of company over basically everything else".

## Guest's core ideas
- Every alumnus was "number one on the team" as an IC. Success is "not hitting my quota"; it is never being satisfied ("90 x percent of sales people Are satisfied with being above average"). @08:18–10:20 ([[talent-density]], [[sales-hiring]])
- Leaders need a positive attitude and an intense work ethic because the team emulates them. A top rep who golfs on Friday afternoons is fine, but "you're not going to be a leader". "Startups are really hard". @10:20–13:29 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- As an IC, where you come from is "largely irrelevant". For leaders, "I do solve for someone who has seen what great looks like". Hiring friends has worked when they'll "absolutely crush it". @14:34–17:40 ([[sales-hiring]])
- He promotes from within at first line (100% at Zenefits), but "Don't promote someone out of need". At director level and above, promote only rock stars and add experienced outside VPs. @18:40–21:45 ([[org-design]])
- Leader development: he is good at recruiting and at a "hypercompetitive system", and weaker at deliberate "Leadership coaching". People learn by doing in fast-growth environments. @23:47–29:04 ([[talent-density]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Scaling test: "one's ability to hire people that are both more senior and better than them", e.g. a senior RevOps leader around $10M ARR. Use in-network sourcing where "Their reputation precedes them". Leaders are "almost easier" to hire than SDRs because of their track records. @33:17–40:36 ([[sales-hiring]], [[revops]])
- Second-line readiness is when first-line managers bring strategic ideas "that I hadn't thought of and they would be right". In interviews, present a real current problem and see what you learn. @41:36–45:49 ([[hiring-scorecards]])
- Biggest mistake: "trading off quality for urgency", which ends up costing more time. @49:02–51:04 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Quickfire: his bad Adobe sales-ops experience made him wrongly resist RevOps at Zenefits ("I was obviously wrong"). New leaders: "this is just the beginning". @51:04–56:24 ([[revops]], [[career-and-leadership]])

## Numbers & facts
- Blond: SDR at EchoSign in 2008 (Jason Lemkin CEO), which Adobe acquired in 2011. VP Sales at Zenefits for 2.5 years, leaving at about $70M ARR and a $4.5B valuation; the sales org grew from 2 to 500 in about two and a half years. CRO at Brex, leaving at about $400M annualized interchange revenue and a $12.5B valuation (2021–early 2022). Almost two years as a Founders Fund partner. @02:02–03:02, @19:42, @51:04
- Zenefits' first-line management layer was 100% internal promotions. @18:40
- Blond is an investor in Owner (he says "as an investor in owner"). He calls Owner a company where "the writing is on the wall for this type of company and they're still pretty early". @57:27, @18:40
