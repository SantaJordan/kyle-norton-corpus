# Polaris Ops (LodeStar): LodeStar S1 EP 1: Kyle Norton

**Show / host:** LodeStar (Polaris Ops) / Cliff Simon · **Published:** 2025-10-10 · **Kyle's role:** guest (recorded at a conference; he mentions giving a talk "here at GTM25") · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/polaris-lodestar-s1e1/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G5oEN3vVy1U

## TL;DR
- Owner chooses the long term over the short term again and again. Everyone's comp is paid on launched customers, not closed-won. A 90-minute churn review runs every month. Kyle offered sales headcount back to product during 2026 planning. He says Owner could have grown 3x instead of ~2.5x by pushing harder.
- His 2-to-10 playbook: carve out time to build systems (onboarding, positioning, a bare-bones methodology). AI can now generate enablement content in about a quarter of the time. Revenue leaders need a first-principles understanding of AI (start with Karpathy's videos), and that understanding can't be outsourced.
- Revenue Leadership Academy and operating methodology: meetings are not for updates, recurring meetings expire after six weeks, MAPLE weekly written updates, zero-based meetings, *chop wood, carry water* manager mornings. He ends on purpose-driven, servant leadership as what keeps him going.

## What Kyle argued
- Owner spends more time on product and customer value than on "how to grow". It has repeatedly shrunk its ICP or "added friction to the buying journey" to improve first-90-day retention @02:06. [[icp-and-saying-no]] [[churn-and-retention]]
- A monthly 90-minute churn review covers macro trends and then goes churn by churn. Cases that look good on paper but churned get a diagnostic run by the data science team @02:06–03:12. [[churn-and-retention]] [[operating-rhythm]]
- Comp is shifted right. The BDR is paid only on closed-won (not meetings or opps). The AE is paid only on customer launch. The launch team is paid on launch and steered by a "growth potential score". Kyle's own comp is on overall company growth (churn, GPV growth), not the sales number @03:12, @06:26–07:28. [[comp-plans]]
- Restaurant churn is a moat, not an obstacle. Competitors in SMB vertical SaaS "come hot out of the gates" and then hit a wall at the Series C, when investors start scrutinizing unit economics and retention @04:18–05:22. [[smb-sales-motion]] [[fundraising-and-investability]]
- Long-term bias: month-to-month contracts, the comp plan, and product investment. In 2026 planning he (and the CMO) said they could do with less GTM headcount so it could go to engineering. "Product is what matters" @08:28–09:29. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[org-design]]
- CROs who hyper-optimize for the sales number to protect their job create a "self-fulfilling prophecy" and leave Lencioni's "first team". Owner's high-trust exec team can misallocate, miss and recalibrate without finger-pointing @10:32–12:37. [[cro-role-and-board]]
- In the 2-to-10 stage, resist pure firefighting and carve out time to build systems. A solo VP can hire and ramp "maybe like six reps" before systems are required @12:37–13:43. [[playbooks-and-documentation]] [[sales-methodology]]
- AI-generated enablement: dump a training transcript into a model with his "enablement prompt" (mixture-of-experts structure, prescribed output; the prompt itself was AI-built). This takes "literally a quarter of the time" @13:43–14:44. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]] [[ai-in-gtm]]
- His GTM25 talk is about the order of operations for layering AI into the org. "There's no course. There's no consultant". Revenue leaders must understand pre- vs post-training and how to give gen AI structure to reduce hallucination, starting with Andrej Karpathy's videos @15:46–17:53. [[ai-in-gtm]] [[career-and-leadership]]
- You can't outsource AI fluency to "an AI guy" or RevOps. Don't swap AI into human processes; redesign from first principles. That "doesn't mean your whole go-to-market org will be AI" @18:54–19:58. [[ai-in-gtm]] [[gtm-engineering]]
- Revenue Leadership Academy (started mid-2024): train-the-trainer modules for lieutenants, with AI coaching sims through Avara, including rep–manager versions. The operating methodology was the first module @19:58–21:04. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Meeting rules: a meeting is not for updates (if you speak 80% of the time, send a Loom and a doc). Owner is hybrid but operates async and document-first. Recurring meetings get a six-week expiry. All meetings were zero-based (deleted, then re-added) @21:04–24:16. [[operating-rhythm]] [[office-vs-remote]]
- Managers' rituals: *chop wood, carry water* (transcribed "chop would carry water"; first three hours coaching and dialing, nothing internal), "ghost busting" stuck launches, and an all-in L1 push in the last week of the month. Modules (6–8 weeks each) cycle every 24 months @24:16–28:27. [[operating-rhythm]] [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]] [[culture-and-high-standards]]
- Personal systems: TextExpander and Superhuman snippets. A podcast-intake flow (Calendly → Tally webhook → Clay table research). A Claude project with a "monster prompt" turns a Riverside transcript into a newsletter in his voice @28:27–31:38. [[personal-ai-stack]] [[clay]] [[revenue-leadership-podcast]]
- Sustainability comes from purpose-driven, intrinsic motivation (citing Dr. Michael Gervais): serving his leaders and reps and the small-business mission. Extrinsic goals like CRO title and money give "fleeting" fulfillment @31:38–34:51. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]] [[career-and-leadership]]
