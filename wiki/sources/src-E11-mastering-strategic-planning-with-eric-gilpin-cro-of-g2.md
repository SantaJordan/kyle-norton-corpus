# E11: Mastering Strategic Planning with Eric Gilpin, CRO of G2
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Eric Gilpin, CRO @ G2 (ex-chief sales officer @ Upwork, ~7.5 yrs) · **Published:** 2024-12-04 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E11-mastering-strategic-planning-with-eric-gilpin-cro-of-g2/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- A revenue executive differs from a sales leader by owning "who does what" and the why, rather than the how. "Team one" is your executive peers. Before tactics, align on mission and vision, then on "who are we chasing" (G2 narrowed 160,000 listed products to a 3,000-account target list), then on buyer-journey jobs to be done, input metrics, and clear decision rights (DACI/RACI, DRIs).
- Planning: a V2MOM with functional children; an honest review of last year's plan; growth levers; collaboration rather than negotiation over containers; a stop-doing list; good/bad/sideways contingencies; monthly method deep-dives with single-threaded owners; monthly in-person "sync weeks".
- Kyle reveals a lot of Owner detail: the mission ("arming restaurant owners to take on their Goliaths"); a new comp plan paying reps only when the customer goes live; a beatable base plan plus a gated upside plan; 100% remote with a new SF office; "a hundred percent no slides"; ChatGPT RAG over book PDFs; a GTM-engine doc from mission to playbooks.

## Guest's core ideas
- Leadership altitude: first-line managers execute, VPs set what and strategy, SVPs and CROs set "who does what" and why. "Team one" (Lencioni) is your executive peers, not your direct reports. Hire for team "inventory" and range. @04:13–08:21 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[org-design]])
- Align on the why and the what, then let teams own the how, which makes it "happening through them versus to them". @09:31–13:47 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- "You don't chase your competition, you chase your customers". G2 went from 160,000 listed products, through propensity cuts, to 19,000 and then 3,000 target accounts for account-based orchestration. "The hardest part was getting to the 3000." @14:52–19:21 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Map the buyer journey cross-functionally (don't ship the org chart). "The only one that should get credit should be the customer". Then set input metrics (a deal cycle under 90 days means seeing 90 days ahead). @20:49–23:59 ([[marketing-alignment]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- V2MOM with linked functional sub-V2MOMs in Confluence. Next unlock: DACI/RACI decision rights. Leaders push accountability down: "accountability creates pressure… pressure creates friction", and friction "is where the magic is". "Short toes" plus assuming positive intent. @23:59–29:06, @38:28–40:32 ([[org-design]], [[operating-rhythm]])
- He was converted to six-page memos (*Working Backwards*; Bill Carr worked with his company). He uses NotebookLM podcasts of case studies for rep onboarding. @30:06–33:12 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[ai-in-gtm]])
- His leadership principles: hire the best people possible ("you promote what you permit"); absolute clarity on where and why; give people all the tools and resources; "stay out of their way". @40:32–43:45 ([[talent-density]])
- Planning: review last year's plan honestly, start from the year-end jumping-off point and "who are we chasing", run it as "more of a collaboration than a negotiation", keep a stop-doing list, and contingency-plan for good, bad and sideways (e.g. Upwork after the Ukraine invasion). Single-threaded owners per method, monthly risk-focused deep-dives, and monthly three-day in-person sync weeks. @50:03–64:39 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[capacity-planning]])
- Quickfire: great CROs keep stretching and learning outside their walls. Know your own input metrics ("I can actually go back 67 days ago and tell you like when you got off track"). "the only thing difficult About difficult conversations is not having them." @70:49–76:05 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[career-and-leadership]])
