# E68: Are You UNINVESTABLE? The New GTM Reality

**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Cassie Young, General Partner @ Primary Venture Partners (ex-CRO Sailthru, transcribed "Sailthrough"; Marigold) · **Published:** 2026-05-07 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality/transcript.md`

**Attribution note.** No speaker labels. Below, a line counts as Kyle's only when it's his intro or question, it's first-person about Owner (LATAM/Canada team, BDR numbers, PRs per engineer, YG, Kai/OpenClaw, Shopify comp), or the guest's reply makes the switch clear (e.g. "It is such an important framing, Kyle"; "the world just needs more Kyles"). PRIME, Core Four, "taking your CFO to lunch", the elite-score prompt and the Claude Code bar are Cassie's. The cold open bleeps several words ("****").

## TL;DR
- AI inference costs erase the 80%-gross-margin cushion that hid "GTM bloat". ARR/employee is rising while CAC payback and magic number stagnate. Boards are "done rewarding effort" and want direct P&L impact, scored with Cassie's **PRIME** framework.
- Kyle's counterpoint is **Jevons paradox**. Owner ships 4x the PRs per engineer and is hiring more. AI-driven BDR productivity (about $10K closed-won MRR per outbound BDR last month) is pulling headcount forward, and quotas "only go up".
- Both say centralize AI to avoid initiative sprawl and develop **P&L fluency** (Core Four). Cassie's bar is that every exec can do basic things in Claude Code. Kyle shares his Kai/OpenClaw chief of staff, his XDR hiring analysis, and why he dislikes 50/50 CRO comp.

## Guest's core ideas
- **GTM bloat era is over:** 80% gross margins once absorbed OpEx "nonsense". Inference costs end that. Iconic benchmarks show ARR/employee up while CAC payback and net magic number are stagnant. "you're out of excuses" @03:09–05:12. [[fundraising-and-investability]] [[cro-role-and-board]]
- Scare tactic: she had Claude rank 600+ event attendees by an "elite score" with reasoning. "you're gonna be exposed" if you're not leading @05:12–06:21. [[ai-in-gtm]]
- Foundation-model risk: she doesn't worry about vibe-coded CRMs in enterprise ("Everybody wants a throat to choke") but sees extreme risk for point solutions. Defensible categories are domain-specific and deterministic (Mayburn fund admin, Lyric supply chain). She passed on a lovable team because buyers said "we could ultimately do with Quad [Claude]" @18:17–23:26. [[fundraising-and-investability]] [[build-vs-buy-software]]
- **Pricing is strategy now:** "every new agent has insane inference costs". ARR-based comp breaks at 30% gross margins. Partner with the CFO on "the math" @25:32–27:38. [[comp-plans]] [[metrics-and-forecasting]]
- "boards are done rewarding effort and want to see impact". **PRIME** = Productivity (revenue/employee), Retention (NRR/GRR), Investment efficiency (magic number, CAC payback), Momentum (top-line growth), Expense reduction. She won't invest in GTM tools that don't hit one, and buyers should use it too @27:38–31:47. [[metrics-and-forecasting]] [[cro-role-and-board]]
- Why AI fizzles: initiative sprawl (the Yahoo "peanut butter manifesto"), immature org design (Becca at Clay's maturity curve: individual, centralized, GTM alpha), and under-resourced implementation ("phase two never happens"). Primary built a central "Primary OS" team. Token leaderboards are "you've got to be kidding me" @34:48–41:04. [[gtm-engineering]] [[org-design]]
- "A lot of them don't understand the P&L". Her number-one hack: "you better be taking your CFO to lunch". Executives need to be in the tools (Elena Verna/Lovable) @42:06–44:09. [[cro-role-and-board]]
- Boards judge outcomes first ("nothing really matters if you aren't hitting a plan") and want rich transformation stories, not incremental A/B anecdotes. "take credit for your work". CEOs decide whether an exec is "the executive that's going to take you to the promised land", with AI now part of that calculus. Primary's GTM engineering practice (Jason Gellman) arms board members @49:28–53:29. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[gtm-engineering]]
- **Bar:** "every executive needs to be able to do basic things in cloud code", including understanding MCP. She credits Kyle's "no new surfaces" bit (shared in SF in January) @54:34–55:34. [[cro-role-and-board]] [[ai-in-gtm]]
- **Core Four:** P&L fluency and command (revenue vs. cash, full financial health), first-team thinking (Lencioni), pulse on macro (read the news and form a perspective), strategic network. Example: a VP of sales cutting quotas on August 30th instead of helping generate pipeline. "play for the front of the jersey, not the back" @59:49–68:09. [[career-and-leadership]] [[culture-and-high-standards]]
- New metrics are unsettled. Iconic's EGI/Enterprise Five, Kyle Poyar's (transcribed "Poyer") AI-native metrics, John Gleason's "Outcome Metrics 1.0" (outcome cost efficiency). Reads: Mostly Metrics (CJ Gustafson), Clouded Judgment (Jamin Ball, Altimeter) @62:54–65:00. [[metrics-and-forecasting]]
