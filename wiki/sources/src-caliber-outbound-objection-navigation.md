# Caliber: Introducing the Outbound Objection Navigation System

**Show / host:** Caliber (formerly pclub.io). This is a ~5-minute **course intro monologue** by Kyle, not an interview · **Published:** 2026-06-04 · **Kyle's role:** guest / course instructor · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/caliber-outbound-objection-navigation/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9ppK03I-Hy0

## TL;DR
- On a cold call, early brush-offs ("we already have a vendor," "call me later," "we're under contract") are **not real objections**. They are System 1 "protective reflexes" (Kahneman), because the prospect doesn't yet know enough to object.
- So don't handle them, **break the pattern**. Owner uses the **NASCAR** framework: Notice, Acknowledge, Sidestep, Continue, Ask a question, Real talk. Back it with gravitas, tonality, and a research-based hypothesis of need.
- The goal is not to sell the product but to sell "listening to me for the next 10 or 20 seconds." On email brush-offs, he would often just call.

## What Kyle argued
- Credentials framing: Owner is "a unicorn" that was "built... on the backs of the phone." Kyle is a partner in GTM Fund and Stage 2 Capital, an advisor and angel investor, and host of the Revenue Leadership Podcast @00:00. [[owner-com]] [[outbound-at-scale]]
- He tells his own early-career failure story: he folded at the first "we already have a vendor" and pitched five reasons while the prospect hung up @00:00–01:02. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- Cold-call brush-offs differ from post-discovery or post-demo objections. Prospects are "just protecting their time" @01:02. [[outbound-at-scale]]
- System 1 versus System 2 (*Thinking, Fast and Slow*): move the prospect from System 1 into System 2 rather than arguing, e.g., don't argue budget when "they didn't even know what this thing cost" @02:06. [[smb-sales-motion]]
- NASCAR in practice: "yeah, not a problem, John, the reason for the call is," then a question, then "real talk" that names the cold call itself @02:06–03:08. [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- Rep anecdotes: one rep got three extra callbacks a week by asking what's on their plate. Another asked "What's working best about your current setup?" in response to "under contract." A real-talk opener "consistently shifted everything" @03:08. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]]
- The patterns are universal across industries because they come from "the same principles of psychology" @04:11.
