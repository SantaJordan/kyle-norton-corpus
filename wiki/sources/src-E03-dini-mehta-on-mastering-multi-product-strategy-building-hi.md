# E3: Dini Mehta on Mastering Multi-Product Strategy & Building High-performance Teams
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Dini Mehta, EIR @ Peak XV Partners (ex-CRO @ Lattice, VP Global Sales @ Drawbridge) · **Published:** 2024-10-09 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E03-dini-mehta-on-mastering-multi-product-strategy-building-hi/transcript.md` (transcript ends around 49:50, mid-answer)

## TL;DR
- Lattice went from one product to four in about 4.5 years. The second product (engagement surveys) was aimed at mid-market new logos, but it actually lifted win rates and ACVs across the existing base and made "product velocity and product breadth" part of the strategy.
- Default to one rep selling every product to the same persona. Make the case with certification, SPIFs, contests and recognition, not comp-plan changes. Add specialist SEs earlier than Lattice did.
- An engaged, "ship shipmate self" team made it possible to launch without "comp plan magic". Lattice reported over 100% attainment and under 1% voluntary attrition.

## Guest's core ideas
- First launch: she and the COO cleared two days to certify every client-facing person against a rubric. "not everyone passed on the first go". Certification signals that the new product matters and keeps reps from under-selling it. @11:26–13:34 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Launch assumptions are usually wrong ("learned to stay open"). Multi-product lifted win rates and ACVs and differentiated Lattice in a noisy, "competing with free" market. @13:34–17:50 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Specialist vs generalist reps depends on persona, shipping velocity and per-product accountability. Specialists give technical depth and a simpler GM-style rollout. Generalists give buyer experience and bundling. @18:54–23:01 ([[org-design]])
- Incentives: SPIFs, "first 10 deals" contests and all-hands shoutouts, not comp-plan changes. Paying more for outbound than inbound was "a mistake". Quota calculators show reps they "can give yourself a raise". @28:10–35:23 ([[comp-plans]], [[recognition-and-motivation]])
- Enablement reported to her as "the glue". PMM quarterbacks before launch and enablement after. A small workshop (PMM, enablement, 1–2 AEs) writes the narrative a couple of months out. @35:23–39:31 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[marketing-alignment]])
- No selling ahead of launch. Existing customers got it about a month early, and reps practiced and certified for two weeks before the "big bang". @39:31–42:37
- Engagement pillars are community, growth and purpose. "you give trust and you get trust". @45:46–49:50 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
