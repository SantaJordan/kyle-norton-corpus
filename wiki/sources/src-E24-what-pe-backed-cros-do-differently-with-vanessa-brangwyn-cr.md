# E24: What PE-Backed CROs Do Differently with Vanessa Brangwyn, CRO of Motus
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Vanessa Brangwyn, CRO @ Motus (ex-Achievers CSM → chief customer officer → CRO over 12 years) · **Published:** 2025-03-12 (RSS) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E24-what-pe-backed-cros-do-differently-with-vanessa-brangwyn-cr/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Brangwyn went through every ownership model at Achievers (VC/Sequoia → owned by public Blackhawk → Silver Lake PE spin-out). PE differs from VC because returns are a multiple of profitability, hold periods run 3–5 years (so ideas are judged by what "a future buyer would want to inherit"), portfolio playbooks are shared (Thoma Bravo's "QAM"), and span-of-control and support-resource cases are scrutinized harder.
- PE boards are engaged, data-first and helpful. Board members "love closing deals". In Motus' Everlance acquisition, both sales teams are paid if either wins an overlapping deal.
- Kyle shares Owner details. Owner's outbound "efficiency" is muddied because more than half of its booked opps were former inbound leads. Its VP and director of sales, and its AEs, are paid on *launched* MRR rather than closed-won. He thinks CROs who can truly own the customer journey are rare.

## What Kyle said
- **Pavilion Toronto:** Brangwyn is "part of our chapter of leadership here for Pavilion in Toronto." @02:02 ([[pavilion]])
- **VC portfolio support:** "I don't see it a lot in the venture backed world... I don't really know of any VC that's saying, this is our growth modeling process. This is how we think about quota setting." @17:38–18:45 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- **First-time founders need more structure:** "You could definitely make the argument that, uh, first time founders in venture backed companies need more of the structure, not less". "I'm fascinated by venture. I think at some point in my career, that's a... place I could see myself." His differentiator there would be "playbooks, like come in with opinions and structure and like really teach people". He recommends Pete Kazanjy's *Founding Sales* "all the time". Messages from first-time VPs of sales and founders arrive "like every day". @19:45–20:48 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- **"High probability practice":** "I'm on a mission to get people to stop saying best practice and replace it with high probability practice". He likewise tells reps "don't set a next step, give... the customer an offer of value". "I think language has such... power." What worked "in another vertical SaaS SMP context" doesn't transfer automatically: "I sold to restaurants, you sold to mechanics." @21:48–22:53 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[sales-methodology]])
- **VC vs PE time horizons:** VC "is playing the power log [law] game" and needs an IPO-scale outcome, while PE runs on a "three to five year time horizon". @27:02–28:05 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
- **Span-of-control trade-off (his question):** "more reps per manager is good on a spreadsheet", but managers should improve total output. @34:15
- **Owner channel attribution:** "We're trying to break things down in a more granular fashion by channel because inbound affects outbound". "our outbound team looks like it's got incredible efficiency, but more than half of the ops that they book were former inbound leads. So how do you attribute cost appropriately and spend intelligently?" He tracks cost per opp, cost per closed-won, and whether LTV differs by channel ("Is a partner deal bigger or higher retained than an inbound or an outbound deal?"). @37:19–38:22 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[outbound-at-scale]], [[owner-com]])
- **Boards, VC vs PE:** "In venture, your biggest investor might have 15%", so incentives diverge (a seed investor sitting on a 20x "could be excited for liquidity" vs the C-round lead who needs 5–10x more). PE is "a more seamless alignment". @43:34–44:34 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- **Two altitudes:** "I talk about this a lot. The best revenue leaders can live at two simultaneous different levels of abstraction". Get in the weeds on call, connect and conversion data, "and then also think about where we go in 24 months." "The world is skewing towards the operator more and more" (P&L and system design). @45:37–46:43 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- **Why roll-ups:** cross-sell, "Take oxygen out of the competitive landscape. Basically, stop a race to the bottom", accelerate the roadmap. Acquiring Everlance also improves its retention "because people won't graduate out of that product." @50:50, @54:02, @56:12
- **Double comp on overlapping deals:** "the additional revenue you'll win by nobody discounting is probably worth the double comp". On a $40,000 deal a 10% discount is $4K, but "The commission paying the double comp is not going to be four grand. It'll be two." @58:18 ([[comp-plans]])
- **CS + sales under one leader:** "I am very much of the mindset that these two functions need to live with one another. But the challenge is there's not enough leaders out there who can actually do it", meaning leaders who will "actually give up revenue, if it means setting better expectations, selling into a tighter ICP, being better about handoff mechanisms and spending money post-sales." @63:33 ([[churn-and-retention]], [[org-design]], [[icp-and-saying-no]])
- **Owner pays on launch:** "my VP of sales and director of sales, like the AE director, are both paid not on close one business, but on launched MRR." "I think paying them on retention is probably a step too far". "Our launch cycle's only two to three weeks, so it's workable, but the AEs are also paid not on close one, only on launch." On clawbacks: "The AE has pre-spent the money half the time." @64:40–66:49 ([[comp-plans]], [[churn-and-retention]], [[owner-com]])
- **Leading functions you never ran:** relying on your team risks "abdicating your responsibility". The leader is "the person who's supposed to try to pull things back to first principles, even if you've never run a BDR team or never run a CS team or never run onboarding. These were all new functions for us at some point." @69:51 ([[org-design]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- **Range:** "I'm a big believer in range, like from David Epstein's book". Learning from product management and from outside SaaS "has been a big source of my learning". @75:02 ([[career-and-leadership]])

## Guest's core ideas
- **Ownership eras at Achievers:** under VC (Sequoia Series C, US push) there was freedom, but whale prospects questioned financial stability (customers pre-fund reward budgets). Under public Blackhawk (2015–2018) stability came with earnings-driven hiring freezes. Under Silver Lake it was spun out as an independent PE-backed company (2020). @06:12–11:23
- **PE culture:** "not all PE firms are created equal". Motus is co-owned 50/50 by two firms, "like cousins". The expectation is a positive outcome "9.9 times out of 10, not one out of 10". Thoma Bravo CROs meet twice a year and share templates such as the QAM (quota allocation model). Templates are recommendations, not prescriptions. @13:27–17:38, @20:48–21:48 ([[capacity-planning]])
- **Profitability and horizon:** PE sells on a multiple of EBITDA, so "profitable growth matters most". Long-payback bets (new verticals, regions) get weighed against the hold period. @24:56–31:09
- **Org efficiency:** comp is similar to VC (pay for performance), but support headcount (enablement, RevOps, leadership) needs a harder business case. Span-of-control benchmarks of about 6–8 need nuance (tenure, SC coverage). Efficiency metrics are benchmarked against both PE portfolios. Enterprise vs commercial payback trade-offs (30–60 days vs 9–12 months). @32:10–40:28 ([[org-design]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- **PE boards:** the CRO speaks first and longest. Dinners make board members "real people". Start from data, then stories. Ask board members for deal help because they "love closing deals". The CEO brings high-potential leaders to present each quarter. @40:28–48:44 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- **Everlance integration:** start with brand and marketing identity, work overlapping pipeline jointly with "if one of us wins, we all win" comp, extend TAM (B2C with 4M+ end users, sole proprietors, freemium), then systems (HubSpot/G Suite vs Salesforce/Microsoft). @50:50–61:27
- **CS lens for CROs:** put the customer at the center of decisions and comp. Her CEO's case for making her CRO: "you know the customer experience better than anybody". Own both bookings and retention targets. @61:27–64:40 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- **Leadership:** great CROs align to long-term outcomes, work at different altitudes and build teams that shine. "give people safe spaces to fail". Authenticity as a female leader. @66:49–75:02 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
