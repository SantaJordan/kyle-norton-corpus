# Sales Leadership Podcast: #15: Kyle Norton of League — Slow is Smooth...Smooth is Fast

**Show / host:** The Sales Leadership Podcast (sponsored by Xvoyant) / Rob Jeppsen · **Published:** 2018-09-25 · **Kyle's role:** guest (then VP of Sales, League, ~2 years in) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/sales-leadership-podcast-15-league/transcript.md` · **URL:** https://salesleaderpodcast.fireside.fm/15

Note: the host's post-interview "so what" recap runs @35:34–end and is not Kyle's view. Not a duplicate of [[src-gtmnow-5-slow-is-smooth]] (2022) — that episode is Kyle looking back on League from Owner; this is Kyle *at* League. Read together they show the hired-too-fast lesson already forming in 2018.

## TL;DR
- Pre-Owner snapshot: Kyle built League's GTM from a sales team of zero (joined July 2016; ~20 employees → ~150–160) with a C$33M Series A; selling an unwanted change (benefits/HR) required "evangelists" with a challenger mentality.
- Learned by staying close to customers: ~30 late-stage loss interviews in 2017 by enablement + marketing drove strategy changes (multi-carrier, open architecture) and a move upmarket — average deal size ~20x vs a year earlier.
- Admitted mistake: hired too many reps too fast before funnel generation and CS could keep up ("slow is smooth, and smooth is fast"); would also start with "a little less process" and more room to test.

## What Kyle argued (2018)
- League's GTM system was a scratch build (AEs, BDRs, sales ops, enablement) with unusual resources — but success came from "testing, iteration, making a whole ton of mistakes" @03:08–05:14. [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- HR buyers are risk-averse; selling meant getting people "excited about something that they probably don't want to talk about" — a challenger mentality and aspirational vision @06:15–08:19. [[point-of-view-positioning]] [[sales-methodology]]
- Early team came through his network (former colleagues, references); with little traction "you basically just have to sell what you do have" — the team and the learning @09:19–10:20. [[sales-hiring]]
- Messaging pivot: aspirational employee-health pitch wasn't enough; had to pitch HR champions on what's in it for them, and product invested in HR-admin features @11:25–12:27. [[point-of-view-positioning]]
- 2017 "really sputtered" until cross-functional strategy changes (one carrier → many; defined plan → open architecture) — "It wasn't really that sales figured it out" @12:27–13:29. [[marketing-alignment]]
- Loss interviews by non-salespeople (enablement, marketing, later VP product marketing) remove sales bias and speak from the voice of the customer; being repeated after moving upmarket @13:29–15:34. [[churn-and-retention]] [[icp-and-saying-no]]
- "slow is smooth, and smooth is fast" (jiu-jitsu, now Navy SEALs): they hired too many reps too quickly and couldn't ramp funnel generation or CS @16:39–17:46. [[capacity-planning]]
- Adding steps to the sales process lengthened it but raised win rate and "actually helping us go faster" @17:46. [[sales-methodology]]
- Specialization: AEs generate only ~20–25% of their pipeline; BDRs + inbound do the rest because "seasoned account executives don't want to pick up the phone"; BDR leaders aligned to revenue (stop booking tier 3–4 accounts, get senior first meetings) @18:49–20:58. [[org-design]] [[outbound-at-scale]]
- Prospecting: SalesLoft-powered process with research, personalization and novelty; LinkedIn/Twitter; Vidyard video for higher-tier accounts; trigger-based and hypothesis-based outreach (the HR-leader-in-a-hoodie buyer) @20:58–24:02. [[outbound-at-scale]] [[ai-sales-stack]]
- Video works early (novelty, clicks) and late (mini demos, proposal recaps to share internally) — "do it now before everybody else gets there" @24:02–27:07. [[outbound-at-scale]]
- Process, technology and work ethic reinforce each other; "If you're designing process for the sake of process, for the sake of oversight, you're doing it wrong"; reject "work hard, play hard"; servant leadership — fly across the country for one meeting @28:10–30:19. [[culture-and-high-standards]] [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
- Biggest challenges: status quo, and too many things to test — League assumed an SMB high-velocity motion, then had to go "way upmarket"; would build "a little less process up front" with more room to fail @30:19–32:29. [[icp-and-saying-no]] [[playbooks-and-documentation]]
