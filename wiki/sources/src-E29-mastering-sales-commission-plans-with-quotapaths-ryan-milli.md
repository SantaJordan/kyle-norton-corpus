# E29: Mastering Sales Commission Plans with QuotaPath's Ryan Milligan
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Ryan Milligan, VP of Sales, Marketing & RevOps @ QuotaPath · **Published:** 2025-04-16 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E29-mastering-sales-commission-plans-with-quotapaths-ryan-milli/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- A great comp plan passes the "grandma test", ties to the one metric the business must move this year, feels achievable, and (Kyle adds) is fair to the customer, the rep and the business.
- Use spiffs and accelerators to buy *incremental* behavior change. Run them hot for a quarter, then taper. QuotaPath moved two-year deals from 15% to 85% of revenue in one quarter.
- Set quotas with a bottoms-up activity/throughput model so that 80–90% rep attainment hits plan. Watch for warring-party plans, accelerators that don't change behavior, and reps who can't explain their plan.

## What Kyle said
- Owner moved comp from closed-won to *launched* revenue in July. In a high-velocity SMB business the pay point "only gets moved out by between four and 14 days". Deal quality and early churn "Everything got better". @13:33–14:38 ([[comp-plans]], [[churn-and-retention]])
- Why he did it: Owner had a clawback model, and "clawbacks suck". They breed blame because "humans have negativity bias". Pay-on-launch puts everyone on one team. Reps now pressure the launch org, which he calls "positive friction" (citing his G2 interview with Eric Gilpin). @63:47–64:47 ([[comp-plans]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- His comp-philosophy addition is that a plan "has to be fair for the customer, the rep, and the business". He couldn't remember whether he got it from *Sales Acceleration Formula*. @15:39 ([[comp-plans]])
- Spiffs must "drive some warp" (= WAR, wins over replacement), meaning truly incremental behavior, not something reps already do. This is also his challenge to CSM variable pay. @21:47–22:50 ([[comp-plans]], [[recognition-and-motivation]])
- Bottoms-up quota math is "one of the most important activities" for a revenue leader, especially in mid-market. It often shows that win rates or pricing aren't good enough. Founders go through a "willingful suspension of disbelief" that "leads to disappointment in 12 months". @54:32–55:33 ([[capacity-planning]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- "Off-ramping," a concept he created at League: find the point in the cycle to offer prospects a way out, where good customers stay and bad ones opt out. Track time-to-lose; in many orgs it is "almost the same as time to win". @56:36–57:38 ([[sales-methodology]])
- Owner ran an end-of-month spiff that reps "incinerated", "at least double what we thought the cost was going to be". The channel turned it into a game ("make Dopp pay") instead of grumbling. @69:52–70:52 ([[recognition-and-motivation]])
- He likes consistency bonuses ("I sort of love this. I've never thought about this.") and asked about second-order gaming. @66:49–67:50
- Kyle also said: comp psychology is counterintuitive, and "anybody who's run sales teams for a long time has been surprised by the second-order effects of their comp plan." @10:28

## Guest's core ideas
- Comp design belongs to a "triangle of influence": finance, sales and RevOps, all with positive intent. Siloed commissions-analyst orgs lack context. @05:08–07:17 ([[revops]], [[comp-plans]])
- Plan objectives: the grandma test (2–3 sentences), alignment to the core metric (e.g., GRR), and achievability with up-funnel math shown to the rep. @11:30–13:33
- Everyone at QuotaPath, including product and engineering, has variable comp. The mix scales with control (e.g., 90-10 vs 50-50). CS should carry GRR/upsell numbers in their own book. @18:43–23:52 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Design flow: name the metric, define role jobs-to-be-done, set team totals, build quotas with coverage, then add 1–2 deal-parameter accelerators that tell reps "what great looks like". A logistic regression on the traits of renewing customers chooses the parameters. @23:52–28:56
- "Pizza topping rule": no more than 2–3 criteria. Pay the full rate on one thing instead of a point or two on many. @29:57
- Incrementality: run a "comically high" rate for a quarter as a budgeted test, paired with enablement (stop showing 1- and 2-year options side by side). Size the spend between the AM renewal rate and double the new-business rate. @33:02–38:09 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Cadence: the base OTE mechanics are annual. Accelerators and spiffs are quarterly (or monthly) and bring reps above OTE. @44:20–48:22
- Warning signs: accelerators that don't shift mix (a 2% bump can't offset a 30% discount), reps who can't explain the plan, and low blended attainment (one team was at 50%). @48:22–51:29
- Quota setting: a throughput/calendar model (meetings per deal, hours, demand) plus day-in-the-life math and historicals. Reps should be "bought into the number, not handed the number." @52:31–61:41 ([[capacity-planning]])
- Failure modes: warring-party plans (marketing at 105% OTE, sales at 64%) and undefined "great revenue". Trends: usage- and outcome-based comp, and consistency bonuses (watch for deal-sliding). @61:41–68:51

## Numbers & facts
- QuotaPath two-year spiff: two-year deals went from 15% to 85% of revenue in one quarter, at "almost double the commission rate". Before that, 75–80% of revenue was on one-year contracts. @31:01–32:01
- Rule of thumb: 80–90% summed rep attainment = revenue plan. Manager/director layers are buffered toward 100%. The observed range is 70%–110%. @25:53, @41:13–42:15
- QuotaPath team was "100% plus" blended in all four quarters last year. It raised OTEs, bases and quotas together on 50-50 splits. @15:39–17:43
- Guest named 2024 Startup CRO of the Year (Pavilion). @02:00
- Owner (Kyle): pay-on-launch moved payment by 4–14 days; a late-month spiff cost about 2x the estimate. @13:33, @69:52
