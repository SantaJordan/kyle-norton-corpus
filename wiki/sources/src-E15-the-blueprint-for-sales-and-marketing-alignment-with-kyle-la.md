# E15: The Blueprint for Sales and Marketing Alignment with Kyle Lacy, CMO of Jellyfish
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Kyle Lacy, CMO @ Jellyfish (transcript spells "Lacey") · **Published:** 2025-01-08 (RSS pubDate) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E15-the-blueprint-for-sales-and-marketing-alignment-with-kyle-la/transcript.md`

> Both speakers are named Kyle. Lines below are attributed to Kyle Norton only when he is asking, framing, or speaking about Owner, Shopify, or his own past.

## TL;DR
- CROs should back marketing's illogical, long-time-horizon brand bets, build pipeline and capacity models jointly with marketing and finance, and give sales and marketing "one number." Attribution is an input, not a source of truth.
- Lacy runs an 80/20 (or 70/30) split: most spend must create pipeline, and the rest funds unmeasured brand plays (Cards Against Engineering, a Lego set). Signed revenue handbooks with SLAs settle sales–marketing disputes.
- In an AI world of infinite generic content and AI SDRs, Kyle and Lacy both argue that brand, uniqueness and founder brand matter more. AI SDRs are a short-term edge that will "torching the inbox."

## What Kyle said
- Framing: sales–marketing alignment "is just generally better in the market today than it has been." He likes the message that CMOs must own the number and not celebrate MQLs when bookings miss. @08:15 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- As a first-time VP of sales building a 2019 plan, he told his heads of BDR/RevOps and demand gen to "just build me one plan." They turned off Google and Facebook and funded BDR-run ABM, which was "the most innovative plan that I've seen in a very long time." His advice now: "just give those two teams one number." @14:25–16:28 ([[marketing-alignment]], [[capacity-planning]])
- Attribution is "a rat's nest". Optimizing for it can lead "to the wrong place." @16:28 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Owner's brand bet: the CEO is "very brand centric" and decided "we are going to be the most trusted source of restaurant marketing content." Owner spends on YouTube and comprehensive guides; ROI was hard to show, but the market's tone is now "night and day." @20:37–21:38 ([[owner-com]], [[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Owner org: "Growth reports into me, but then brand and content reports directly into the CEO". It's "a funny setup, but it actually works great for us." He told his head of growth that challenging conversion rates is "exactly what I want you to be doing." @30:55 ([[org-design]], [[marketing-alignment]])
- His test for a good marketing counterpart: do they "say stuff that makes you go, huh?" The benchmark came from Matt Nelson at Shopify. @31:56–32:56 ([[shopify]])
- AI SDRs: an early-mover advantage exists. He cites [[person-jordan-crawford]]'s "micro-targeted campaigns with multiple agents in a waterfall" that drove so much pipeline clients had to pause. But "I generically am not a big believer in AI SDR writ large" because it is "a race to the bottom," and he expects a 3–6 month payoff then diminishing returns. "Don't let this be like your 80% bet." @41:09–46:13 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]], [[outbound-at-scale]])
- His LLM writing workflow: generic LLM LinkedIn content was bad, so he loaded "all of my previous writing and... all the podcast episodes I've done" into memory. He writes bullets in about five minutes and edits for 10. @48:17–49:18 ([[personal-ai-stack]])
- Brand in AI: citing Brian Halligan, "uniqueness and quality" will matter most, so "brand may be re-emergent in an AI world." @54:23 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Cites Lemkin's brand-by-ARR framework ($1M little sense → $1B all of marketing). Early on, "it's just founder brand"; founders who can't do it can use specialists like Finn Thormeier (met via a GTM Fund event). "I'm not a really big believer if a founder can't build himself a brand." @59:31, @63:36–65:36 ([[person-jason-lemkin]], [[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Agrees a great CRO cares about "the entire funnel, not just close one" ("one of my biggest beefs"). @68:46 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Feedback style: he is skeptical of Radical Candor and Praise-Correct-Praise because of "reactance". He prefers "real empathy and seeking to understand." @70:49–71:49 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Quoting Udi (Gong): marketing makes sales easier, and sales should take risks with marketing. Sales leaders' risk aversion comes from short-term KPIs vs brand's long payoff. @08:15–11:21 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Finance sees sales and marketing as one CAC line. Trade dollars across them, and include the CMO in capacity-model and envelope conversations. @13:25–14:25 ([[capacity-planning]])
- Attribution is an input. Use direct-source plus multi-touch, and stress-test the model every six months because long cycles break first-touch. @17:29–18:30 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- 80/20 budget: if 80% delivers the pipeline, the brand 20–30% isn't measured. "You kill creativity when you try to measure it." @18:30–20:37 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Build trust with jointly built models and upfront agreements (e.g., 40–45% inbound contribution). Lessonly's signed "revenue handbook" of SLAs was reviewed every 6–12 months. @24:47–27:53 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- A good marketer asks about capacity, coverage and conversion, and is willing to disagree. CMOs' underappreciated burden is context switching. Brand also drives candidate quality. @28:54–38:03 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Over-measurement (Marketo, Bizible) pushed marketing into analytics over brand. Quoting Rory Sutherland's *Alchemy*: "logic always gets you to exactly the same place as your competitors." @39:06–56:24 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Early brand comes through happy customers and the founder (Lessonly's *Do Better Work*). Between $10M and $100M is the hard transition, which explains CMO turnover. @59:31–64:36 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])

## Numbers & facts
- Lacy career (Kyle's intro): ExactTarget (through the Salesforce acquisition), OpenView, five years as CMO at Lessonly (acquired by Seismic), and now two years as CMO at Jellyfish. @02:05
- Lacy's budget split: 80% of headcount and program spend for pipeline and bookings, 20–30% for brand. @18:30
- Example inbound contribution agreement: 40–45% of pipeline. @26:50
- At Seismic his team went "from 12 people to 90"; he left after nine months, burnt out. @73:52
- Lemkin framework (as Kyle quoted it): $1M ARR "makes little sense," $10M "let's do some," $100M "most of marketing," $1B "all of marketing." @59:31
