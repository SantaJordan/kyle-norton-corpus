# E28: How Cresta Built a Category Leader with CRO Alex Cramer
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Alex Cramer (transcript: "Kramer"), CRO @ Cresta · **Published:** 2025-04-09 (RSS title: "Interns to Enterprise: How Cresta's CRO Built a Category Leader") · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E28-how-cresta-built-a-category-leader-with-cro-alex-cramer/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Cresta cold-started straight into the enterprise: the founders interned inside Intuit's contact center, turned Intuit into customer #1, then used it as social proof for Cox. The hard "dip" becomes the moat.
- Early deals were won on a sharp ROI case proven with free 30-day A/B tests, against a hand-picked list of 80 target companies. Later, that narrow ROI framing became a liability.
- Cramer argues large enterprise can't be fully playbooked. Run a loose sales process, hire A players, and scale by shrinking books as reps land whales.

## What Kyle said
- Enterprise suitability test he gives mid-market reps: "There's three people that want to do it. There's two people that absolutely don't want to do it." If the Game-of-Thrones puzzle sounds fun, go enterprise. If it feels like an uncontrollable external force, don't. @06:08 ([[sales-hiring]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- Enterprise today is "way more sales craft than it was 10, 15 years ago". He compares it to a 90s athlete struggling in today's NBA. @09:15 ([[sales-methodology]])
- He did a course with Chris Orlob (pclub) to get SMB sales content in front of sellers as a talent-pipeline experiment. He signed without looking at the commercials. @11:17–12:21 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Enterprise startups "basically have to do free consulting for the first year, maybe even two years". @22:54
- League: "I was personally involved in all of the biggest deals." It "really didn't feel like we were doing a ton to build a lot of repeatability." @39:35 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Owner's SMB data science: 400–500 demos a week is enough sample to model. For some channels, calling a form-fill right away lowered win rates, so Owner delays the SDR call 5–10 minutes. @46:51–47:54 ([[smb-sales-motion]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- "My style of revenue building is very operationally centric". He tunes time-to-first-touch with Slack triggers, and the optimal opps per AE per month (a ramped AE could take 60–80, because these are often one-call closes). @47:54–48:56 ([[smb-sales-motion]], [[capacity-planning]])
- Advice to his ex-League reps now in strat roles: hit your baseline, but "your main job is looking for your mega whale". You're hunting the 4X attainment year. @53:01
- Owner hires in "pockets": a director and reps from Bench, a pocket from TouchBistro, and one from Levelset after its acquisition. They have "all done great because they're so low risk." @65:36–66:39 ([[sales-hiring]])
- "My Shopify equity was very real compared to my previous startup equity". @75:08 ([[shopify]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Today's enterprise deals need consensus at the user, mid-exec and C level at the same time. A CEO relationship alone no longer closes. @08:14
- Cold start: find a trusting champion and do the readiness work (security reviews, policies). Start with an innovative upper-mid-market logo such as DoorDash or Chime, not Wells Fargo. Without pedigree, pair a founder with an experienced enterprise rep. @19:45–26:01 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- A sharp ROI value prop: "make everybody as good as your best", +10% revenue, proven free in a 30-day A/B test. Over-indexing on ROI later turns you into an affiliate network judged monthly, so they broadened it to CX and EX value. @32:14–36:24 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Know the customer's business cold. Observe their operation under NDA, then come back with a point of view. @36:24–38:32 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Process, not playbook: qualified = a problem we can solve + a champion + a known exec approver. Then discovery, custom demos, and split technical and business tracks. Deviation is allowed with a reason. Spend energy on hiring A players instead. @40:39–43:49 ([[sales-methodology]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Best practices spread through Gong, weekly "Alex's office hours", leaders surfacing field learnings, and enablement-run win/loss reviews. @44:49–45:50 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Strat book design: 15–20 ICP accounts, tiered to a top 5–10. After a first win the book is trimmed to 8–10 so the rep can expand. Year three is typically "the million-dollar W-2 year." @48:56–51:59 ([[capacity-planning]])
- Foundations: a strong CMO partnership and pipeline-source math (AE self-source is 25%); sales engineering (SEs, strategy consultants, solution architects, forward-deployed engineers); and customer stories, selling "pretending that we all have the same product". @55:06–59:21 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Hiring: intelligence and writing, why Cresta, five years of attainment back-channeled, a preference for application sellers, referral "pockets" (Medallia, Qualtrics, Yext, Sprinklr), and grit. @61:23–64:29 ([[sales-hiring]])
