# The GTMshift: Kyle Norton — Your GTM Worked Live

**Show / host:** The GTMshift (live event session; sponsors read at top: FullEnrich, Gradient, Captivate Talent, Clarify, Clay, Outworks) · **Published:** 2026-08-05 · **Kyle's role:** guest (live office-hours-style session: short intro, then audience members ask him their real GTM problems; a moderator closes with a final question) · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tX5lb-vx_Bw

**Format.** Not an interview. It's a live, in-person session at a GTMshift event, following a speaker named "Elaine" (who showed a scoring/enrichment/Slack workflow). Questions come from: an unnamed attendee (how to get reps to adopt AI / revenue control room), a Great Place to Work leader (FY27 capacity planning), and a solutions engineer at an AI role-play vendor (how to differentiate), then the moderator. Audience questions are not Kyle's views.

## TL;DR
- Owner's AI edge is mostly **change management**, not tech: change one thing at a time, "finish the swing", and run in-person "experiment pods" with the applied AI lead (YG) iterating live with top reps ("pair prospecting" / "lock-in protocol") — AI PCR produced 86% more decision-maker connects by day two.
- Capacity planning is always-on, not annual; you almost always have too much AE capacity — push ops per AE until reps "break" (60→80), then pull back.
- Differentiate with **point-of-view positioning**: sell a worldview (Owner's pitch: sacrifice choice and flexibility, make more money), compete in a market of one, and let misfits disqualify themselves.

## What Kyle argued
- AI investments fail when "the number doesn't really move"; Owner is "selling boring old SaaS" yet extracts efficiency from the GTM system (@00:00–@02:05). See [[ai-in-gtm]].
- Start with "semi-decent data": third-party market map (Clay, FullEnrich, scoring, contacts) then first-party (one call recorder, Momentum-style CRM field filling) (@02:05–@03:06). Owner doesn't "really do the signals thing" — not relevant for its market (@04:06). See [[gtm-engineering]].
- Owner's tech is "not that much more sophisticated" than others'; the difference is change management and enablement (@03:06–@04:06). Rep apathy/non-use is "the most important signal" that a tool isn't good enough (@04:06). See [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]].
- **One change at a time** and **finish the swing**: don't "hurl it into a Slack channel"; concentrate training, coaching, inspection on one behavior per month (@05:07–@06:09).
- **Experiment pods:** YG (applied AI for sales, NYC) builds a prototype, sits with 2–4 respected top reps in Toronto for ~four days; 3–5 vibe-coded iterations a day from live rep narration — inspired by Shopify pair programming → "pair prospecting"; ~40-minute "lock-in protocol" blocks (@06:09–@11:23). See [[ai-sales-stack]].
- **AI PCR design:** only "the three things" that matter on a cold call, injected via the SalesLoft API on top of the dialer screen; reps forbidden from opening other tabs; ride-alongs revealed 120–180 seconds wasted between calls (@07:10–@09:15).
- RevOps must sit beside reps and hear "why what you've built sucks" without taking offense (@12:24). See [[revops]].
- **Context layer is unsolved:** model outputs get "locked in Slack"; Owner is investing in a knowledge graph/context layer shared by sales and marketing (cites Netflix's experiment knowledge base) (@14:27–@16:29). See [[marketing-alignment]].
- **Cold email:** reps no longer send emails; it's AI-generated and blasted out; replies were ignored until a manager became single-threaded DRI with a daily standup — cold email went from ~0.5% to ~5% of qualified lead funnel after ~6 weeks of drift (@17:29–@18:30). See [[outbound-at-scale]], [[org-design]].
- "all the value is in the last 20%" — leaders must keep people from splitting across too many prototypes (@19:31).
- **Capacity planning:** always-on; trades conversion rates vs. cost; "fire your worst reps and give those ops to your best ones"; great CROs live at two altitudes (spreadsheet and rep's day) — "go see the work" (Dan Heath, *Reset*); he used to "creep" ~20 calendars; built a per-rep capacity heuristic; overloaded one team from 60 to 80 ops/month to find the break point; "gut data judgment" (@21:33–@26:46). Math is "pipeline times conversion rates", not reps × quota (@25:45). See [[capacity-planning]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]].
- **Point-of-view positioning:** "basically nobody believes anything we say"; sell a worldview before the product — Owner's two paths (assemble Toast/BentoBox/"Chowli" [sic]/Mailchimp for flexibility vs. Owner's opinionated box that changes your site without asking but makes you more money); Toast can't copy it without incoherence; Shopify's analog (@28:50–@35:02). Fit-less buyers DQ themselves — "that was fake" pipeline (@35:02). See [[icp-and-saying-no]], [[marketing-alignment]].
- Demo/evidence: methodology "plus profit" — O = "offer evidence"; for Owner, show a worse-reviewed restaurant outranking the prospect on Google (@36:03–@38:07). Shared messaging must be hammered in, with triggers when reps go off script (@38:07–@39:07). See [[playbooks-and-documentation]].
- Closing advice: "pick one thing and run it to ground"; sales leaders are distractible; AI makes prototyping easy, so ROI dies in the un-run last mile of evals and context engineering (@39:07–@41:13).

## Numbers & facts he cited
- AEs: ~$150K/yr average earnings → $3M ARR/year each, "like a 20X multiple" (@01:01).
- BDRs: $75K USD take-home (Canada) → on average $1.2M closed-won ARR sourced per year (@01:01).
- AI PCR pilot: 120–180 seconds wasted between calls pre-tool (@09:15); by day two, "86% more decision maker connects than their previous baseline"; reps talking to ~25 decision makers/day; full rollout did not keep the full 86% opp lift due to handoff diminishing returns (@11:23–@13:25).
- Experiment pods: 2–4 reps, ~4 days, 3–5 iterations/day, ~40-minute lock-in blocks; iteration "takes less than an hour" (@07:10–@11:23).
- Cold email: from "half a percent" to "almost 5%" of total qualified lead funnel; ~6 weeks stalled before ownership fix (@18:30).
- Capacity test: one transactional team pushed from 60 to 80 ops/month; suggests testing +20% to +40% ops per AE (@24:39–@27:50); ~2% of companies are demand-rich exceptions (@27:50).
- Sales team mostly in Toronto; YG in New York (@06:09).

## Frameworks & named ideas
- **One thing at a time / finish the swing / run it to ground.**
- **Experiment pods, pair prospecting, lock-in protocol** (live-narrated iteration with top reps).
- **AI PCR "three things"**, no new surfaces (injected into SalesLoft).
- **Revenue control room** / shared **context layer / knowledge graph** (aspirational).
- **Single-threaded DRI pod** for a new channel (cold email).
- **Capacity planning as always-on**; "trying to break them"; **gut → data → judgment**; two levels of abstraction for CROs.
- **Point-of-view positioning**, "compete in a market of one", *The JOLT Effect*; **plus profit** methodology ("offer evidence").

## New or changed vs. earlier positions
- **AI outbound — clear change.** Nov 2024: "we don't do any AI outbound". Aug 2026: "sales reps don't send emails for us" — cold email is AI-generated and blasted out, now ~5% of qualified lead funnel (@17:29–@18:30). Also contrasts with Apr 2026 [[src-ivey-future-of-sales-irreplaceable]] @10:23 ("I don't personally want to just let AI write my cold outreach").
- **ARR per rep jumps to $3M** (vs. "over 2 million" in May 2026 [[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @02:01, both at $150K and "20X"). The 20X matches $3M, not $2M — either the metric grew or one figure is misstated.
- **BDR productivity consistent:** $1.2M/yr ≈ the May "over $100,000 in ARR per month".
- **AI PCR lift described differently:** May = "85% more ops" for 50 BDRs; Aug = "86% more decision maker connects" by day two of a pod, and explicitly "we didn't get a full like 86% op op growth". Prefer the August, more specific framing.
- **New:** explicit no-signals stance for Owner's market; the context layer named as the unsolved problem; pair prospecting / lock-in protocol vocabulary; point-of-view positioning detail and "plus profit".
