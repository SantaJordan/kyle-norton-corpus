# E40: Partnership Lessons from COELEVATE with Author Richard Ezekiel
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Richard (Rich) Ezekiel, author of *Co-Elevate*; ex-partnerships leader at Yahoo, Netflix, Amazon, Seagate, Andreessen Horowitz · **Published:** 2025-07-23 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E40-partnership-lessons-from-coelevate-with-author-richard-ezeki/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Start partnerships from your own strategy, not a partner wishlist. Write a strategic business plan (KPIs, BHAGs), run a SWAT on yourself and a "P SWAT" on prospects, decide core vs context (Geoffrey Moore), and only then build a partnership strategy and plan.
- Crawl, walk, run: incubate one to three bespoke partnerships against an ideal partner profile, then scale to a program (Netflix went from one-off Xbox, PlayStation and Roku deals to self-serve certification and the remote-control button).
- Partnerships fail (the "70% ... after two years" figure) because nobody owns them after the press release. Fix it with a "virtual company": shared KPIs, an accountable owner with incentives, multi-phase plans, and scenario planning before signing.

- Partnerships need "a level of business acumen that's unique", and they become relevant for CROs at about "20 to 50 million AR". @72:33 ([[cro-role-and-board]])

## Guest's core ideas
- There are 7–8 flavors of partnership (product, platform, marketing, co-brand, white-label, reseller...). Some "deals" are only masquerading as partnerships. @03:03–04:04, @35:46
- Assessment as a "three- layer cake": strategic business plan → partnership strategy → partnership plan. Self-reflect on what limits growth (talent, capital, culture, product-market fit), then split core vs context. @11:11, @16:17–17:18 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Example: in Japan, Evernote worked through local incumbents as reseller of record and co-brand instead of building a local team. @12:14–13:14
- The "partnership T": a balanced value exchange between the two companies, held up by joint customer value. Pitch "what you can do with that company for the customer". The "X factor": building something neither could alone (the pen-and-ink example). @27:33–31:40
- Ideal partner profile: objective criteria (brand, reach, regions) plus subjective ones (partnering DNA, past partnerships, where their partner people came from). Read their job board to see their gaps. @53:06–57:13
- Failure modes: deal-makers are rewarded for the press release, not for operating the partnership. Short-term point solutions with no multi-phase path. No success metrics ("the date launch date doesn't matter"). Fix it with a "virtual company" that has its own KPIs and incentives, plus scenario planning and game theory before signing. @43:52–51:02, @67:27–70:31 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Audit partner ROI: at one company, "about three of the partners drove 80% of the audience". @71:32
- Great partner leaders think and act long term. The hardest lesson is saying no to partners and staying true to your own strategy. @76:38–78:40
