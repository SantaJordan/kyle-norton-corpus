# E72: I Collapsed My GTM Org Chart. This Is Why
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Ian Tickle, CRO @ Freshworks · **Published:** 2026-08-06 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E72-i-collapsed-my-gtm-org-chart-this-is-why/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Freshworks merged its sub-500-employee (India-centralized) and 500+ (field) sales orgs, then split by product line (CX vs EX/ITSM). CS now rolls into the revenue leaders, and tech support is a central standalone function. The goals: remove duplication and "shadow orgs," end parent/child account fights, and own net new ARR end to end, because "customers don't care about my org structure."
- Unifying CS under the CRO is not a default. It depends on stage, how much expansion and retention matter, and how much ongoing product change customers face.
- Simplicity is an operating discipline. Manage span of control and reporting layers (the "Russian dolls" dilution of the CEO's message), keep clear single accountability (the "gray" in overlapping Venn circles), run a monthly major-projects review, use a single source of truth, and move to AI-driven exception-based reporting.

## What Kyle said
- Framing: "how do you build simplicity into an enterprise organization as it grows? Because complexity isn't necessarily maturity." @01:04 ([[org-design]])
- Shopify: "We talked about this a lot of Shopify, like not shipping the org chart." He re-orged the revenue org "a few different times" there, and you can tell you're shipping the org chart when reps say "I can't talk to you about that product. I'll bring somebody in." @09:17–10:17 ([[org-design]], [[shopify]])
- Pushback: the usual counter-argument to CRO-owned CS is that revenue leaders "won't pay enough attention to CS" because new business is "easier to move" and familiar. He asked whether this should be the default. @27:07 ([[churn-and-retention]], [[cro-role-and-board]])
- Observation: GM-oriented leaders are "a lot more common in Europe" because a US company's first hire there is often a GM. In North America you can spend "a 10, 15, 20-year career as a sales leader and then get planted into a senior revenue role where you now have CS and you're figuring it out from scratch." @37:41–38:48 ([[sales-hiring]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- Root causes of bloat, his hypotheses: (1) when another team underdelivers, leaders build shadow versions ("I've done this myself"), and the real cause is mismatched performance standards and unclear accountability conversations; (2) the Elon point that organizations have mechanisms to add headcount, budget and tools but "no business process to say, all right, every 90 days, we're going to sit down and delete some stuff." @54:25–56:31 ([[org-design]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- He calls these "locally maximizing decisions," "not ill intent." @56:31
- Asked where the line is between rigor and bureaucracy: extra meetings and docs feel like rigor ("It feels rigorous to have another meeting") but become "a massive drag and then you spend more time talking about the business than actually like building the business." @67:01 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- On not being involved in everything: "You and me both." @74:24 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Context from the guest: Tickle says Kyle is "getting up towards a hundred reps" @64:56, and references Kyle's conversation "with Jason" about building and governing AI agents @45:02 ([[person-jason-lemkin]], [[ai-in-gtm]]).

## Guest's core ideas
- Rationale for collapsing: duplication in supporting teams and systems, too many layers for messages to travel through, and parent/child account conflicts across regions. "It wasn't slowing us down, but it wasn't optimal." @06:13–08:13 ([[org-design]])
- He owns every customer touchpoint outside product and engineering. Good deals must not burden CS and support later. @08:13–09:17 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Signals that you're shipping the org chart: customer and CAB feedback, internal TAM/ICP clashes, partner friction, and sales-cycle and deal-review data. @10:17–12:23 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Process: an 8–12-week design inside FY26 planning, constrained by the CFO's cost envelope, then sanity checks with finance on what could break. The whole project took about five to six months, but deployment was quick. RevOps carries the "heavy lift" (territories, quotas, comp, TAM, ICP). @20:47–27:07 ([[revops]], [[capacity-planning]])
- Disconnected systems mean "AI is actually doing like a shadow RevOps org." @22:53–23:57 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[revops]])
- Whether CS should report to the CRO depends on stage, the product's need for ongoing CS, and how much NRR and expansion matter. At a prior single-product, new-business-heavy company he kept CS separate. @28:11–32:23 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Hiring revenue leaders: test whether they're "passionate about revenue as opposed to sales" (billing, ARR, ACV, what happens when a customer is unhappy). Prefer a GM mindset. Forecast net new ARR by month across new business, expansion and retention. FP&A is a key partner. "Inspect what I expect." @33:29–37:41 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[sales-hiring]])
- From Oracle: influence matters more than reporting lines. "Just care about it" (tech support trends, CS) whether or not it reports to you. @39:53–41:59 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- Simplicity levers: span of control and layers between an IC1 and the CEO; no "reporting on reporting"; synced systems (NetSuite, Adaptive, Workday, recruiting); clear job role vs job spec; a monthly leadership review of about 8 big projects with a DACI owner and 24–48h pre-reads; monthly all-hands alternating whole org and managers only; reading every employee-survey comment. @42:00–67:01 ([[org-design]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Exception-based reporting: a daily Claude Cowork job tells him whether pipeline is within tolerance. Catch dips after 48–72 hours, not a month later. Redeploy report-builders to higher-value analysis. Ask what you are doing with the efficiency. @68:04–72:18 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]], [[ai-in-gtm]])
- A great CRO shows "sustainable consistency." Advice: "Prioritize and learn to say no." @72:18–74:24 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
