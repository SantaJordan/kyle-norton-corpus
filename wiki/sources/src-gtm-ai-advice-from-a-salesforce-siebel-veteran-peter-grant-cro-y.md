# E55: GTM AI Advice from a Salesforce & Siebel Veteran (Peter Grant, CRO @ You.com)
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Peter Grant, CRO @ You.com · **Published:** 2025-11-26 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/gtm-ai-advice-from-a-salesforce-siebel-veteran-peter-grant-cro-y/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Grant: the AI era differs on "speed and scale". Sales cycles are compressed (about $200K ASP, about 87 days), the market is already made, and AI solutions reach every department, so yield per customer exceeds CRM or ERP.
- Adoption is the whole game. Use top-down sponsorship, training, leaderboards that tie AI usage to revenue, usage-based pricing priced for the median user, and use-case prioritization by economic value. Reps who can't adapt are "not the right profile".
- Kyle's own practice: no new surfaces for reps, centralized AI at Owner (pushed into the rep's existing surface), and heavy up-front investment in reusable prompts (Clay podcast-prep table, a "style" snippet).

## What Kyle said
- "One of my biggest pieces of advice is no new surfaces". Bring the agent and workflow into the surfaces reps already live in. Reps are "beyond the saturation point" (CRM, sales engagement, email, Slack, texting). @22:19–23:20 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[ai-sales-stack]])
- At Owner, AI on the front line is "actually centralized". Reps aren't required to do their own pre-call research in ChatGPT, which he doesn't think is super valuable "in an SMB context". Enrichment, prompting and context engineering happen centrally and get pushed into the rep's surface. @57:01–58:01 ([[ai-operating-model]], [[owner-com]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- He wrote "my own version of the Toby memo" to his managers. If you're not deep in the tools and building, "it's going to be really hard to be successful". @57:01–58:01 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Podcast prep: guest intake form → webhook → [[clay]] table that does the research and drafts questions, which he curates. That would have been "a 4hour manual process" and now takes "oh maybe 20 minutes". [[person-jordan-crawford]] built a lot of it with him. It took hours, he still tinkers, and it "has given me back 10x those hours". @31:31, @59:02–60:03 ([[personal-ai-stack]], [[revenue-leadership-podcast]])
- Generic AI emails have "bot breath". Pick a task you'll repeat and tune the prompt up front ("sharpen your axe"). @59:02 ([[personal-ai-stack]])
- Style snippet: he fed 150 pages of his writing into ChatGPT to describe his tone "in excruciating detail". The output was about four pages and took about 60–90 minutes to refine. He fires it anywhere via TextExpander ("Z style"). @60:03–61:03 ([[context-harness-and-memory]])
- Metaprompting: he holds the function key in Whisper Flow, talks through what he wants, and has the LLM write the prompt. @62:03 ([[personal-ai-stack]])
- Revenue leaders should be "deep in the tools themselves", leading adoption from the front. If you're not already deep, "it might even be too late." @52:57–53:57 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- On enterprise AI, the rep "is going to get pulled much deeper into the customer journey" because pricing is usage- or outcome-based. @41:44 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- He quips that "anything that's AI is an agent" now, and says he has "lost that argument". @24:22–25:22 ([[ai-sdrs-and-agents]])
- Dashboards: "a dashboard is an abstraction". Teams obsess over the metric instead of customer value, pipeline quality, craft and skills. @66:06–67:07 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Framing: AI touching every employee is like AWS becoming the biggest line item, so "the scale of outcomes really like breaks your brain". On Palantir's service-plus-software model: "Peter Teal was right". @17:13–20:16

## Guest's core ideas
- Background: Siebel grew from $39M to $2B in four years. He was employee nine in Europe at Salesforce and built the UK business ("every single month was a quarter end"). At You.com he wants to go "from a million to a billion". @03:02–06:04, @10:08
- You.com pivoted from consumer to enterprise about 18 months ago because of 40–70% consumer attrition. Its products: web-grounded search with citations, vertical "inch wide and a mile deep" indices, and an "AI factory". @10:08–13:10 ([[buyer-ai-aeo-geo]])
- The MIT stat that 95% of deployments show no ROI comes from point solutions, failing to embed in workflows, and going it alone instead of using forward-deployed engineers. @18:15–19:16 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[build-vs-buy-software]])
- Spend benchmark: about 2% of revenue on gen AI, growing 30% compound to 2030. @26:23–27:25
- Drive adoption with rolebased training, usage tracking and leaderboards, and human vs SDR-agent stack ranks linked to pipeline. He runs daily seller standups. Price for the median user to avoid attrition. @33:34–38:39 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]], [[churn-and-retention]])
- The rep profile is shifting technical (API, Python). Interviews put the tools in candidates' hands ("show me what you built"). Pre-sales and SDR ratios shrink. The future team includes an FDE, a domain expert, an AI strategist, the rep and a PM. @38:39–41:44, @52:57 ([[sales-hiring]], [[org-design]])
- Use-case prioritization spreadsheet: vision, use case and metrics baseline, data needs, UX, a "golden set" of 50 queries and answers, T-shirt sizing, economic value vs cost, then a roadmap. @45:47–47:50
- The biggest failure mode is inaccuracy: "Not speed, but accuracy." @50:54
- Leaders are "context setters not goal setters" (Kyle's framing of the guest's view). Focus on the customer problem and the numbers follow. Qualify on the compelling event ("why now why us"), not "ASAP". Work backward from the deadline like Grove's boiled egg. "Tactics dictate strategy" (the 435-meeting Reuters deal). @63:05–72:11 ([[sales-methodology]], [[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Quickfire answers: carry the bag before leading; focus on top 30 accounts and "carpet bomb them"; hire better than you. His hardest lesson was overselling and refunding: "If you can't make him successful, don't take the business." @72:11–75:13 ([[icp-and-saying-no]], [[career-and-leadership]])

## Numbers & facts
- Siebel: $39M → $2B in four years, $68B market cap, 8,000 hires. @03:02
- Salesforce at his joining: about $25M revenue, about 300 people. Early UK ASP about $28,000 (commercial $4,000, with a three-to-four-day cycle), 18–22 deals a month. @04:02–06:04
- You.com: revenue was "about $5,000" (as transcribed) when he joined 18 months ago; now "many tens of millions" run rate going into 2026. ASP about $200K, sales cycle about 87 days. 40 LLMs on the back end. A waitlist meant for 500 people drew 22,000 sign-ups. @11:09, @16:13, @28:26, @29:28
- Reps spend 23–27% of time customer-facing. The ARI research agent reads "a thousand websites in five minutes". @14:11
- Engagement on bring-your-own UIs ranged from 4% to 52%. Average human creates 15–20 agents (guest claim). @33:34, @36:36
- Old CRM math: improving leads, deal size, win rate and cycle = "22% more money" (a $1M target → +$220K). @58:01
