# E36: Why Product Thinking Beats Process Obsession in GTM with Sangeeta Chakraborty
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Sangeeta Chakraborty, former CRO @ Miro (transcript spells her "Sangita Chakraorti") · **Published:** 2025-06-11 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E36-why-product-thinking-beats-process-obsession-in-gtm-with-san/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Build GTM like product: a continuous learning loop of hypotheses and experiments, run in isolated "pods" with psychological safety and comp protection, and a RevOps person acting as the product manager. Don't import a prior company's playbook.
- Kill the term "post sales". Everyone in GTM is in sales. The CS mission is to make the business case come true, and CS leaders who want to be CRO have to carry commercial skills.
- On the CRO job search: market first, then team and product. Diligence customers and the board deck, and walk away from hubris or a company that won't share its financials.

## What Kyle said
- He "went to school on product stuff" at Shopify to be taken seriously, and says time at product-first orgs is healthy for revenue leaders: at Salesforce "sales rules the roost", but "at Shopify you definitely did not feel like the most important thing going on." @04:04, @08:08–09:10 ([[shopify]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- He emailed Lenny to say he "started this to be Lenny's podcast for revenue." He uses Teresa Torres' opportunity solution tree to get to root cause. Shopify eventually had an "innovation pod" for tests. @08:08, @09:10, @13:17 ([[revenue-leadership-podcast]])
- His self-identified fault: he gets "really excited about good ideas", attention can fray and he ends up doing too many things. @23:31 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- He frames GTM "as a systems engineering challenge" and calls product thinking one of his "most important evolutions over the last six years." @24:33, @78:27 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- First-time VPs at roughly 20–30M ARR need a completely different skill set "but then no one teaches these skills". He credits Winning by Design. @28:38 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- He talks about "P&L fluency": to be CRO you must generate enterprise value, not just hit your number. Otherwise you make "local maxima" decisions instead of driving down churn or protecting NRR. @31:41–32:41 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[churn-and-retention]])
- His own mistake as a first-time VP of sales: "bull in a china shop hit the number", "too disagreeable". @32:41 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- Before promoting reps to manager, he gives them exposure (a project, meetings, coaching a rep). Wannabe managers often reveal themselves with "I can't come to that meeting. I got to demo." @36:47–37:48 ([[sales-hiring]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- A CRO candidate who isn't given the full financial model should walk: "then something's up." @68:16 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Job-search advice: with a good profile, "go to venture" because incentives align; recruiters "just want to fill roles". @73:22 ([[career-and-leadership]])
- He had someone on his team read *How We Learn* and says many enablement people don't understand it. His kids are in Montessori, and he is weighing homeschool or micro-school. @76:24–77:27 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Traditional GTM is "a straight line" with a process owner. Modern GTM is a "circular loop" of learning, so "the best organizations are the best learning organizations." @06:06 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Experimentation framework: a hypothesis backlog that gets groomed. Pods (reps, BDR, SE, renewal manager) run tests in the US and Europe. Head of sales Adam Carr made it "a no judgment call", and reps were protected on comp. @08:08, @13:17–14:19 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- A RevOps person with a management-consulting background acts as "product manager of this process". Each test has a kickoff, an action plan and an inspection cadence (weekly, or every other week with the CFO for pricing). Rep interviews matter more than data before scale. @15:19–18:24, @22:29 ([[revops]])
- Tests reset Miro's competitive positioning away from feature comparisons ("we solve a different problem") and avoided "a very incredibly wrong pricing approach". @20:25 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Leadership is inspire, inspect, coach. Real coaching isn't telling. First-line leaders need "no uncertainty". VPs must master the financial plan, and a CRO becomes a company leader. @25:35–31:41 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Ban "post sales", a term from the 1980s perpetual-license era. Call it customer growth or "post land". "There's nobody in the go to market team who is not in sales." @04:04, @39:51–43:56 ([[churn-and-retention]], [[org-design]])
- Testing for talent: deep adversity examples, curiosity, knowing what a closed customer actually did with the product, and the questions the candidate asks. @52:02–53:03 ([[sales-hiring]])
- CRO search: market > team ≈ product. Never compromise on culture, and avoid hubris. Diligence customers at a kickoff, the last couple of quarters' board decks, churn understanding and AI-era moats (regulatory or vertical needs, culture). @60:07–68:16 ([[fundraising-and-investability]])
