# E18: Confessions from a First-Time CRO with Catie Ivey of Walnut
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Catie Ivey, CRO @ Walnut (interactive demos); ex-Pendo, Demandbase, Marketo, Pardot, Meltwater (transcript spells "Katie") · **Published:** 2025-01-29 (RSS pubDate) · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/E18-confessions-from-a-first-time-cro-with-catie-ivey-of-walnut/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Ivey's first-CRO lessons at a small Israel/US startup: expect no process, but the surprise is how much had been built on missing foundations (ICP, personas, operating rhythm). Her regret is too much consensus-building; she should have been more decisive and admitted sooner when she didn't know.
- Her foundations work: codify the 14 use cases, separate bottoms-up from top-down motions, make the team "customer zero" of its own product, run a structured product feedback loop, and hire enablement early.
- In "knife fight" markets the differentiator is the GTM motion and the rep. Kyle lays out his radically transparent, point-of-view sales methodology at Owner (staggered discovery, referring buyers to BentoBox) and the shorter time-to-lose it produced at Shopify.

## What Kyle said
- Communication: "we usually say three points of contact. It's a rock climbing term... three different ways, three different times." @11:10 ([[operating-rhythm]])
- Operating rhythm "is a bit of an obsession of mine" and has to change by stage. Owner now has "second and third line leaders in my revenue org," and he is rewriting "this document called my operating methodology" (communication principles, process building, 1:1s, team meetings). @11:10–12:15 ([[operating-rhythm]], [[playbooks-and-documentation]], [[owner-com]])
- "Some of the most successful companies are the worst managed because they don't need it." With extreme product-market fit you can reach "$100, $200, $300 million in ARR" while messy, and fixing it may mean a leadership change. @12:15–14:17 ([[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Focus advice to founders: "You don't have to say no all the time. You just have to be clear about what you are and what you're not." Don't "persuade and cajole" a $150K deal past product gaps, because "it never works." @19:28–20:28 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- He has owned onboarding, CS and support on and off for about six years: "As a VP of sales, you never really appreciate the downstream impact until you're the one cleaning it up." @20:28–21:29 ([[churn-and-retention]])
- Career "learning FOMO": at Shopify he learned "so much about in particular product management," and he'd "love to get bought by Vista at some point... just to see the experience." @28:45–29:46 ([[shopify]], [[career-and-leadership]])
- Some of his most valuable lessons came from his worst boss, a top-down authoritarian. With an exec coach he "had to quickly build a bunch of new tools in my toolkit." (Stoicism, Ryan Holiday.) @30:47–31:51 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Enablement: "Everybody should be investing in enablement way earlier than they do. I say this all the time." @31:51 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Owner's founder Adam gives customers his cell ("Christmas Eve, New Year's Eve, my birthday"), and both co-founders (Adam, Dean) stay close to customers. Kyle says "I still get on demos myself... I was on a demo this week, a churn call last week," and he expects managers on calls too. @42:04–43:07 ([[owner-com]], [[culture-and-high-standards]])
- Decision speed: Bezos's 70–80% information rule. He paraphrases Elon: if you're not reversing course "10 to 20% of the time... you're probably going too slow." Most decisions are two-way doors. @45:12–46:12 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Early-stage enterprise hiring: enterprise rep and startup are "almost mutually exclusive." His success has come from promoting "a former mid-market rep" with more sophistication and wrapping them in training. @49:16 ([[sales-hiring]])
- AI: "the craft of sales will become more important as AI becomes more prominent," because easier company creation means more companies need more salespeople. In crowded spaces (~40 call-transcription vendors) brand, community and sales experience decide winners. "The pendulum is swinging away from the technical person to the go-to-market executive." @54:19–55:20 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- His "radically transparent sales methodology" (taught "seven or eight years"): as a buyer he wants "this is what we're good at... what our competitors are good at." @58:22 ([[sales-methodology]], [[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Owner pitch: "staggered discovery" means giving value first and alternating, not 15 minutes of interrogation. The opener lays out the landscape: a competitor gives "a prettier website... more control," but "we believe that the control is not good for you"; Owner's identical templates across thousands of sites let it test and deploy best practice. Reps refer design-driven fine dining to BentoBox ("They're awesome"). "It's called point of view positioning." @59:22–62:24 ([[point-of-view-positioning]], [[sales-methodology]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Result at Shopify: "time to lose went way down. So win rates stayed about the same, but... we were spending way less time on deals we ultimately lost." @62:24 ([[shopify]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Coaching: his Substack doc "Coaching Mastery" holds that "Great coaching is questions based." He's rereading *Trillion Dollar Coach* and takes notes on episodes because he writes an article after each. @64:26–65:27 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
- Owner's market: restaurant owners "will not visit a website or go through an interactive product. We're like text messaging and sending them PDFs." He would have wanted Walnut at Shopify and League. @68:29 ([[smb-sales-motion]])

## Guest's core ideas
- Expect process gaps at a small company. What surprised her was misalignment from tech, teams and decisions built without ICP and persona foundations, made worse by the Israel–US split and October 7 (she signed her offer the day before). @05:20–08:47 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- First 3–6 months: build a company operating rhythm, decision ownership, and repeated communication across channels. @08:47–11:10 ([[operating-rhythm]])
- Map demand sources, positioning and messaging (14 use cases → where to win), bottoms-up vs top-down motions, and high-touch vs scale CS. @14:17–19:28 ([[icp-and-saying-no]])
- Year-one regret: "I did too much crowdsourcing or consensus building"; her first revenue kickoff covered too much. For year two: two priorities, 15 deprioritized, plus Command of the Message / golden pitch. @22:33–26:37 ([[executive-performance-and-mindset]])
- Hired a head of GTM enablement early. Documented the bottoms-up vs top-down playbook in Notion. Made reps power users of Walnut ("customer zero") with a Slack feedback channel and biweekly/monthly product sessions. @31:51–41:03 ([[playbooks-and-documentation]])
- Startup enterprise hires need enterprise chops plus comfort with ambiguity. She has one external hire and one promoted mid-market rep building it together. @46:12–50:16 ([[sales-hiring]])
- Knife-fight markets: know where you sit (Marketo = powerful but harder, sold on the customer's own roadmap, plus the "Marketing Nation" community). "The biggest competitive differentiator you have is your go-to-market motion and your rep." @50:16–58:22 ([[point-of-view-positioning]])
- Great CROs find superpowers people don't see in themselves. Her managers are focused on "asking better questions" and doing less managing, more coaching. @63:25–64:26 ([[sales-coaching-and-enablement]])
