# Revenue Diaries: Episode 4: The Raw DNA of a Competitive Psycho & Loving Father

**Show / host:** Revenue Diaries / host not named in transcript (a friend of Kyle's; mentions his own leadership role at Seismic after the Lessonly acquisition @27:38) · **Published:** 2025-01-26 · **Kyle's role:** guest · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/guest-appearances/revenue-diaries-ep4/transcript.md` · **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IxJwD9_6vGs

Note: a ~29-minute personal (non-GTM) conversation. Recording date unknown; Kyle says he is "a 40-year-old man" @22:30 and that his workout streak will hit two years "seven days from today" @05:05, which suggests it was recorded before the publish date.

## TL;DR
- Treat yourself as a "corporate athlete": daily exercise is part of mental health and executive stamina; he rebuilt from his worst shape (2021) to a two-year unbroken daily workout streak by going slowly.
- Martial arts taught him to lose productively and learn in "chunks and sequences" — the same way he got good at sales; his "competitive psychopath" wiring is why he ended up in sales.
- Stoicism/mindfulness (physiological sigh, *Search Inside Yourself*) keep him even-keeled; "if you don't have control over yourself, I can't entrust you with people."

## What Kyle argued
- High workplace performance requires treating yourself as a corporate athlete; physical activity is central to his mental health @02:03. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- After COVID (kid born two weeks after the first lockdown, a re-org, a house move) he woke up in early 2021 "in the worst physical condition of my life" and has rebuilt since @03:03. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Habit formation is identity formation (*Atomic Habits*): he declared "I am a person that works out every day" via "every day October" @04:05–05:05. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Slow is smooth: it took ~14 months to build from running twice a week to daily training; rushing gets you "dinged up" and kills motivation @07:07–08:08. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- "Competitive psychopath" litmus test (the Monopoly player who makes and breaks alliances) is how a recruiter steered him toward sales; "not losing means arguably more" than winning @08:08–09:10. [[career-and-leadership]]
- Martial arts taught him to lose productively and to learn in "chunks and sequences" then practice — exactly how he got good at sales calls @12:10. [[sales-coaching-and-enablement]] [[career-and-leadership]]
- His MMA academy was "foundationally not a great business" (no scale, low margins, high churn from injuries); he'd not recommend a brick-and-mortar passion project out of college; use the Venn of what you want, what you're good at, what the world values (credits Scott Galloway) @14:15–16:20. [[career-and-leadership]]
- Tried to stay un-materialistic: chose to let the 150-year family cottage be sold rather than follow "sentiment and nostalgia" into a bad financial decision @18:24–20:28. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Parenting is non-stop practice in equanimity; each trigger is "a productive rep," a bicep curl for the mind (from *Search Inside Yourself*) @21:29–22:30. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- In-the-moment reset: notice where emotion shows up in the body, then do a physiological sigh; meditates intermittently, often 2–3 minutes before meetings @22:30–24:33. [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
- Leaders must manage themselves first; he has taken LinkedIn flak for saying it, but without stamina and mental discipline "I can't entrust you with a team" @25:36–26:36. [[culture-and-high-standards]] [[executive-performance-and-mindset]]
