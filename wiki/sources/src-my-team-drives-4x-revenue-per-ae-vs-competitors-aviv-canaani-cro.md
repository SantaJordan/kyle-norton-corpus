# E64: My Team Drives 4x Revenue Per AE vs Competitors
**Show:** The Revenue Leadership Podcast · **Guest:** Aviv Canaani, CRO @ Datarails · **Published:** 2026-03-04 · **Kyle's role:** host · **Raw:** `raw/podcasts/own-show/my-team-drives-4x-revenue-per-ae-vs-competitors-aviv-canaani-cro/transcript.md`

## TL;DR
- Canaani joined Datarails as VP Marketing and later took over sales and then the whole revenue org without ever carrying a quota. He moved the company from 90% outbound to 90% inbound through paid media plus organic brand (memes, a niche FP&A podcast).
- His thesis, which Kyle mostly shares: in SMB and mid-market, AEs should not prospect. Fill their calendars from marketing and BDRs, and hire AEs only when pipeline exists. Revenue is pipeline times conversion rate, not AEs times quota.
- Predictability comes from a "waterfall"/revenue-architecture model (cost per meeting, then meetings, opps, closed-won, then AE and SC headcount). RevOps sits under growth, and AI (Momentum, Clay) fills the sales data gap.

## What Kyle said
- On "AEs should never prospect": "I happen to agree for the most part." @02:04 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- Brand: at [[owner-com]] the founder's conviction drove heavy brand/organic spend from about Series A. It "didn't really help us that much until you know maybe like 12 or 18 months later and now it is like a monster superpower" (140,000 Instagram and 40,000 YouTube followers). @10:14 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Sales machismo ("macheismo" in transcript) around AE self-prospecting. Owner reps ask customers for referrals and work closed-lost, but generate no "more than 10 or 15% of their own deals." The rest is inbound or SDR/BDR. A BDR costs a third to half of an AE, so AE cold-calling economics "fall apart." @17:23–18:24 ([[outbound-at-scale]], [[smb-sales-motion]])
- Revenue math (crediting Jenny Dingis, sales at Cleo): revenue is not "account executives times quota." It is "pipeline times conversion rate," plus a separate calculation of the ideal meetings per AE per day before win rates diminish. @19:25–20:26 ([[capacity-planning]], [[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- AEs will "never" prospect as well as BDRs (fewer reps). Most BDRs want out of the role fast. @21:27 ([[outbound-at-scale]])
- Owner per-rep productivity is 3–4x most competitors', which allows fair quotas: OTE attainment about 138%, about 80% of reps hit target, and the business can "pay top of market" and fund RevOps, data, enablement and BDRs. @22:27–23:28 ([[comp-plans]], [[talent-density]])
- Capacity: AE target is four booked demos a day, three held, "That's about where things start to crack." Recently every AE was on a demo at once, so managers took hot-handoff demos and the fix is BDR promotions. @24:29–25:29 ([[capacity-planning]])
- [[momentum]] (outside the sponsor read): Owner was an early customer. It compared rep-entered fields with Momentum's against transcripts, found AI more complete and more accurate, and flipped to Momentum writing all fields without rep approval. Now any new question becomes "a momentum prompt and a field." @33:36–34:38 ([[ai-sales-stack]], [[revops]])
- Salesforce: he went from a "big Salesforce skeptic" in the Einstein years to optimistic. Despite custom-coding at Owner, he expects enterprises to use Salesforce as the governed "home for agents." "Nobody's ripping out Salesforce for an AI CRM when they're like 100 million plus ARR." @35:38–36:40 ([[build-vs-buy-software]])
- AI efficiency does not mean fewer reps: "I'm pulling forward headcount," moving June headcount into March. Outbound unit economics have improved "significantly," and it is the first time Owner hasn't had enough AE capacity. @38:41–39:43 ([[capacity-planning]], [[ai-in-gtm]])
- Being systems-oriented now matters far more than sales craft, because AI multiplies leverage. @41:44–42:45 ([[cro-role-and-board]])
- Learning AI: he uses only the X "for you" feed (currently all openclaw), pings product colleagues, and says "There is no course out there. You just have to do it." Leaders must be AI-native themselves. @48:51–49:52 ([[ai-in-gtm]])
- He mentioned being on a tech-stock buying spree and said Shopify's drop "makes no sense to me." @37:41 ([[shopify]])
- Close: AE prospecting is "potentially overrated depending on your market, your segment", a case for first-principles thinking. @55:56

## Guest's core ideas
- Start demand gen with agencies per channel (LinkedIn, Facebook, Google) that "speak to you in dollars," then bring it in-house. Inbound converted far better than outbound after the 2022 tech meltdown. @05:08–07:13 ([[marketing-alignment]])
- Brand pitch to sales-minded execs: show results this quarter while making long-term bets, like hiring AEs now for Q3. The VP of Brand is the only person without a number. @09:13, @30:34
- A marketer can run sales if they build a predictable model. He promoted from within: an AE became team lead and later VP Sales over 30 AEs. @12:15–16:22 ([[sales-hiring]])
- AEs shouldn't prospect, by analogy to sports roles: "you don't count like assists if the shooting guard didn't make the shot." Hire AEs only when pipeline supports them. AEs can get 100+ meetings a quarter. @16:22–26:30 ([[outbound-at-scale]], [[capacity-planning]])
- Waterfall math: target, then deals, then opps at a conversion rate, then meetings, then cost per meeting (e.g. $3,000), then budget and AE/SC headcount. Model on real productivity, not quota. @24:29, @27:31–28:31 ([[metrics-and-forecasting]])
- Org: RevOps and SDRs under growth, then sales, then solution consultants. A new data team is being built. AI-run win/loss interviews (paying churned prospects $50–100) replace AE-picked loss reasons. @29:32–35:38 ([[revops]])
- AI use: AI video (Sora, Gemini) and 100+ campaign pieces a month, plus a Clay plus OpenAI spam/lead-validation workflow built by RevOps. Hire AI-open people, run internal AI competitions, and share examples in Slack. @43:46–49:52 ([[ai-in-gtm]], [[clay]])
- A great CRO is a "revenue architecture" thinker. Make people decisions fast. Set deadlines for when to leave a company. @52:55–54:56 ([[cro-role-and-board]], [[career-and-leadership]])
