# Context, Harness & Memory (the "Company Brain")

> As of 2026-09-08, Kyle's central AI-architecture thesis is that GTM leaders must build and own a company "meta harness": shared context (ICP, playbook, stage definitions), tool connections, governance, memory and evals that every agent points back to. Owner is still building it ("not masters"). The context layer is the unsolved part ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @28:02–@33:09; [[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @05:19; [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @14:27–@16:29).

**Corrected by the 2026-10-05 backfill.** This page used to say that before Mar 2026 Kyle had only talked about deterministic constraints and evals (Sept 2025). That was wrong. The vocabulary (Gardener, harness, memory layers, dark factory) is new since March 2026, but the ideas underneath it are older:
- **Warehouse-as-truth plus an LLM on top** (written Dec 2023; spoken Mar 2025).
- **Narrow scope to limit hallucination** (Dec 2024).
- **"Context engineering"** of positioning and tone into prompts, and **lossiness** (Sep 2025).
- **The "mega model" vs. connected-tools question** (Dec 2025).
- **A principles markdown file** and **"the context graph thing"** (Jan 2026).

## Current position (as of 2026-09-08)
Unchanged by the backfill.
- **Own the brain.** "Buy infrastructure, build (or at least own) your intelligence." If a vendor owns the context, memory and eval layers, "you may be renting your GTM brain" ([[src-substack-2026-05-26-dont-buy-your-gtm-brain]], preview only). He calls the brain "that compounding asset that is the brain of your company" ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @21:10–@23:14).
- **Stack vocabulary:** model → harness → agent → context & retrieval → memory (three layers: short-term state, structured knowledge, long-term learned patterns) → evals. "Whoever decides what your AI sees, decides what your AI knows" ([[src-substack-2026-05-26-dont-buy-your-gtm-brain]], preview only).
- **L3 = GTM harness:** tool connections (Snowflake, Salesforce, SalesLoft, call recorder), shared context, and governance that "every new agent points back to." Cursor's internal setup is the exemplar. Build outside the Claude/Cowork ecosystem and call in to avoid lock-in ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @28:02–@33:09).
- **Data substrate: Snowflake, not the CRM.** "We our system of record is Snowflake not Salesforce." ([[src-E45-the-inside-scoop-on-gtm-engineering-with-clays-everett-berr]] @44:54). "At Owner, we made Snowflake our system of record (not CRM) and put tons of resourcing against data quality." ([[src-linkedin-2026]], post 2026-01-15). Salesforce remains the workflow surface (see Tensions).
- **The Gardener.** Owner's VP of RevOps built a living business-context file maintained from Slack and Notion. GTM context is consolidating in GitHub ([[src-E67-your-buyer-s-ai-is-pitching-your-product-badly]] @49:12–@50:15). The companion post calls it "a master business-context file" that every agent treats as ground truth ([[src-substack-2026-04-30-your-buyers-ai-is-pitching-your-product]]).
- **Context is the unsolved layer.** Outputs get "locked in Slack." Owner is investing in a knowledge graph/context layer shared by sales and marketing ([[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @14:27–@16:29). The org-level "context graph and a baseline set of skills" needs safety so agents won't "nuke a bunch of Salesforce records" ([[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @48:25–@49:28).
- **Evals are proprietary and human.** "Without evals, you are vibing" ([[src-substack-2026-05-26-dont-buy-your-gtm-brain]], preview only). "we are the eval as well." The biggest failure is giving up after the MVP ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @33:33–@35:35). ROI dies in the un-run last mile of evals and context engineering ([[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @39:07–@41:13).
- **Ungoverned context lies.** An MCP-wired Claude "lies to you... it fills in the gaps" ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @22:10). Dumping everything into Claude gives "super confidently wrong" answers, which is why a "data harness" is an emerging category ([[src-E73-the-ai-enabling-600-customer-facing-reps]] @58:01–@65:09).
- **Sequencing in big orgs:** win a couple of use cases first and build momentum until you can say "I can't do the next thing without the infrastructure" ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @05:19–@06:19).

## How his view evolved
- **2023-12-17: warehouse as source of truth (written).** Salesforce is his can't-lose app, but "Maybe the shift to the data warehouse as the source of truth (Snowflake for us) and AI change my opinion here though." ([[src-web-2023-12-17-saastr-my-app-stack-my-app-stack-kyle-norton-cro]])
- **2024-10-23: composability over walled gardens.** Owner pulls SalesLoft data into Snowflake for its own data manipulation with custom-built tools. "composability will matter a lot" in a new golden age of RevOps ([[src-E05-mark-niemiec-on-steering-business-transformation]] @16:50–@17:54).
- **2024-12-03: narrow scope and clean data.** AI outcomes depend on CRM data quality. "the wider the surface area that you give an AI solution, the more hallucinations, the worse the outcome is gonna be." ([[src-momentum-planning-2025-ai-strategy]] @04:07–08:11). Momentum turns unstructured calls into discrete fields: "I can't ask a rep to fill in that field every time, but I can definitely ask AI to do it." (@18:20)
- **2025-02-05: Snowflake models feed the surfaces.** Two Snowflake models (eGMV, eWin) power the nightly iRoute into SalesLoft ([[src-E19-architecting-systems-for-hypergrowth-with-nate-follen-of-ram]] @22:04–23:08).
- **2025-03-05: an LLM on a single source of truth.** Momentum writes custom-prompted fields into Salesforce. The next step is Snowflake, toward a "single source of truth where I will be able to just slap a language model on top of it" ([[src-E23-a-guide-to-high-stakes-decisions-with-ashley-grech-cro-of-x]] @29:51–30:55). This is the earliest spoken form of the company-brain idea.
- **2025-03-26 → 2025-05-28: documentation as context.** Apps are "just UX on top of a CRUD database", and AI may make Snowflake "way more important than it's ever been" ([[src-E26-signals-that-separate-great-companies-from-risky-bets-with-n]] @26:54–27:54). His personal 150-page writing corpus becomes a reusable style profile ([[src-E30-building-ai-powered-sales-teams-with-finallys-cro-kevin-kd]] @68:31–71:35). Documentation culture is the AI advantage: Avara sims mimicked his methodology because it was already written, and Crescendo improved only once the knowledge base was fed weekly ([[src-saastr-ai-sales-gtm-2026-lemkin]] @31:56–@37:07).
- **2025-08-01:** Start with the data, because "AI is a pattern recognition and a replication engine." ([[src-E41-jessica-chiew-of-canva-on-building-ai-native-gtm-org]] @22:27–23:28)
- **2025-09-03 → 2025-09-16: lossiness and context engineering.** From a "half-written Substack post": broad problems plus chained prompts without a human or deterministic check compound into slop. "That's where the slop comes down in my mind". He would put Owner's positioning, banned words and tone into the prompt: "this is the context engineering thing" ([[src-E45-the-inside-scoop-on-gtm-engineering-with-clays-everett-berr]] @16:17–19:21). "You just can't let AI layer seven AI steps on top of itself." His answer is deterministic constraints, human-in-the-loop and evals. He chose Momentum because he could write the prompts per field ([[src-gtmnow-163-2m-to-50m-arr]] @36:00–@43:22).
- **2025-10-08 → 2025-10-30: fragmentation as the problem.** Jamming context across Claude MCPs and OpenAI tools leaves you "in this wonky spot", which is why Owner hired a head of applied AI. Chaining outputs means "you're stacking lossiness". In-house expertise decides that "it's not Zapier that should be in the middle of this. this should be with like an MCP" ([[src-work-sprawl-and-the-future-of-marketing-with-global-vp-marketing-cli]] @23:24–26:28). "AI is a pattern recognition machine. Feed it bad patterns and you get bad outputs." ([[src-linkedin-2025-h2]], post 2025-10-21). The winners have "structured businesses and good data and process and documentation" ([[src-how-lightspeed-commerce-built-a-1b-sales-engine-jd-saint-martin-p]] @50:56).
- **2025-11 → 2025-12: central context, mega-model question.** Enrichment, prompting and "context engineering" happen centrally and get pushed into the rep's surface. His style snippet distills 150 pages of writing into about four pages ([[src-gtm-ai-advice-from-a-salesforce-siebel-veteran-peter-grant-cro-y]] @57:01–61:03). "AI loves writing and docs" ([[src-signal-learn-build-scale]] @11:14–12:17). On E58 he asked whether to "build one mega model of our own to power all of the customer journey" or connect many tools that share insight, and he leaned toward Salesforce for "security and the data and and the governance" ([[src-replacing-your-sales-team-with-ai-agents-jason-lemkin-founder-ceo]] @14:17–15:17, @68:04). His writing style became a Claude skill that "marries that with a bunch of context about owner" ([[src-usage-based-comp-ai-and-the-end-of-the-sdr-tyler-will-vp-of-revops]] @75:13–76:14).
- **2026-01-22 → 2026-03-04: principles files and the context graph.** He writes what Claude learns about him back to "a principles markdown file" holding his decision principles plus Owner values and operating methodology ("I think mine is actually a bit of a mess"). In project-scoped work, context "can compound" ([[src-the-claude-code-era-mastering-autonomous-gtm-agents-jordan-crawford]] @12:19, @58:15–@61:17). Without Salesforce, Snowflake and research tied together ("the context graph thing"), outputs are "thin". Claude Enterprise's "centralized skill management" makes one fix propagate to everyone ([[src-gtm-strategy-5-insights-from-500-b2b-saas-orgs-jeremey-donovan-evp]] @07:10–@16:20). Momentum writes all CRM fields without rep approval, and Salesforce is the governed "home for agents" ([[src-my-team-drives-4x-revenue-per-ae-vs-competitors-aviv-canaani-cro]] @33:36–36:40).
- **2026-03-25 (new):** Context handling for his weekly-update workflow is "the thing that I'm still figuring out" ([[src-E65-inside-our-ai-sales-stack-kyle-norton]] @23:14).
- **2026-04-05 (new):** Everything market-facing shares central "skills, context files, and prompts," kept separate from product AI ([[src-topline-world-class-ai-in-gtm]] @31:36).
- **2026-04-29 / 04-30 (new):** The Gardener and the GitHub consolidation are revealed. He adds the "mini retrospective" habit: on bad output, update the skill or context ([[src-E67-your-buyer-s-ai-is-pitching-your-product-badly]] @44:02–@50:15; [[src-substack-2026-04-30-your-buyers-ai-is-pitching-your-product]]).
- **2026-05-07 / 05-13 (new):** The org-level context graph is "the big unsolved architecture question" ([[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @48:25). He admits he lacks clean principles for context engineering ([[src-E69-stop-paying-for-saas-the-ai-playbook-driving-a-1b-trajectory]] @07:13–@08:16).
- **2026-05-26 (new):** The full stack and memory taxonomy appear (Substack, preview only). At SaaStr, L3 includes a "context library," and evals are named as the grind ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @04:05–@07:07, @33:33–@35:35).

## Tensions, contradictions & open questions
- **"New" harness language that isn't new.** On Sep 8 he says he's "starting to really use like the harness language more" ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @28:02). Six days earlier he said "I've talked a lot about building this company meta harness" ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @05:19). The May 26 Substack already defined "harness" as a stack layer (preview only). The backfill adds that the *question* is older still: "one mega model" vs. connected tools (Dec 2025) and "the context graph thing" (Jan 2026).
- **Where does the brain live: Snowflake, Salesforce, or a company harness?** Snowflake is "system of record" (Sep 2025, Jan 2026). Salesforce is where he's "leaning into Salesforce more than ever" (Dec 2025, E58 @63:00) and the governed "home for agents" (Mar 2026, E64). The 2026 doctrine says to build and own a harness outside any single vendor and avoid Claude/Cowork lock-in (Firmable). These fit if Snowflake holds the data, Salesforce is the workflow and governance surface, and the harness is the connective layer. He has never stated that three-layer split himself.
- **System-first vs. momentum-first.** He champions the meta harness but pushes back on starting with the system at big companies ("50 cooks in the kitchen"), and says to fix data first, not all of orchestration ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @05:19–@06:19). At SaaStr, "start with the data" and then give the team wins ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @20:20–@25:25). The backfill shows Owner itself went data → narrow use cases → harness over roughly 2022–2026. He does not say when to switch to infrastructure.
- **Build your intelligence vs. buy a data harness.** "building your own intelligence" ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @21:10) sits next to his heavy use of a vendor data harness (Vasco) and Momentum for first-party capture. In 2024–25 he also chose Momentum because he could write the prompts himself ([[src-E19-architecting-systems-for-hypergrowth-with-nate-follen-of-ram]] @34:35–36:41), which is an early version of owning the intelligence inside a bought tool. His reconciliation, "still take it out" (portability), is a criterion he hasn't spelled out in detail.
- **Gardener sources.** E67 says the Gardener runs over Slack channels and Notion docs, and his *personal* version adds call recordings (@49:12–@50:15). The Substack describes the Gardener itself as scraping Slack, Notion and call recordings. These are minor discrepancies, and the companion post may conflate the two.
- **Still unsolved.** Across Jan–Sep 2026 he repeatedly calls the context layer or graph unsolved or messy ("a bit of a mess", E60; E68; GTMshift; Firmable "not masters"). No source describes it as working at Owner.
