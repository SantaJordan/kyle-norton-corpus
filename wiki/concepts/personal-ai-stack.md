# Kyle's Personal AI Stack

> As of 2026-09-02, Kyle runs a self-built "compounding personal AI system." It includes an AI chief of staff, "Kai," with its own email, logins (via a 1Password agent vault) and a Robinhood virtual card. It runs on OpenClaw, which he is migrating to "Grok bot" because OpenClaw crons "just stop firing." Skill files live in a GitHub repo that he builds against in Cursor. He tells other leaders to "lead from the front" by building their own ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @63:08–@79:37; [[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @36:37–@42:42).

**Corrected by the 2026-10-05 backfill.** This page used to say the agentic stack was entirely new since March 2026, with only ChatGPT-era hacks and "little systems" before that. Only **OpenClaw, the skills repo, the LLM wiki and the agent card** are new since March 2026. The earlier history is longer:
- **Claude Code** from Dec 2025, and **Kai** ("Kai$") as a Claude-based chief of staff in Jan 2026.
- A **Clay podcast-research table** built with [[person-jordan-crawford]] by early 2025.
- **Vibe-coded apps** from Mar 2025, and **Claude ↔ Notion MCP** by Apr 2025.
- **Meta-prompting** from Feb–Mar 2025, and a **writing-style corpus** reused across ChatGPT, Claude projects, TextExpander and a Claude skill.

## Current position (as of 2026-09-02)
Unchanged by the backfill.
- **Leaders should build a personal compounding system** of skills, repos and context. Inspired by Garry Tan, his advice is to start with the Karpathy videos, pick one harness (OpenClaw, Hermes, Claude, Codex), then pick one project and grind it to quality ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @36:37–@40:40). "Lead from the front" ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @42:42). He has said versions of this since May 2025: leadership starts with being "in these tools constantly" ([[src-saastr-ai-sales-gtm-2026-lemkin]] @02:05–03:06).
- **Kai, AI chief of staff:** Kai keeps dossiers on every leader, drafts leaders' weekly updates, and runs over Telegram ([[src-crew-podcast-how-best-cros-hire]] @60:48–@66:05). It updates his Notion task list and has its own email and Slack bot ([[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @46:21–@48:25). It has logins through a 1Password agent vault so he doesn't hand over his own passwords ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @68:20–@70:26). Its purpose: "My my goal is actually not to outsource my judgment, but it's to not go to those meetings" ([[src-the-claude-code-era-mastering-autonomous-gtm-agents-jordan-crawford]] @62:19).
- **Infra:** OpenClaw runs on a DigitalOcean VM, "because I just don't want it on my, like, work computer yet" ([[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @46:21). As of Sep 2026 he is migrating to "Grok bot" (as transcribed), keeps separate channels per job, builds in Cursor, and writes everything back to a GitHub repo of skill files shared across surfaces ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @63:08–@67:19).
- **Personal context file:** a weekly job runs over his internal call recordings, Notion workspaces and to-do list so "any of my agents can be most effective" ([[src-E67-your-buyer-s-ai-is-pitching-your-product-badly]] @49:12–@50:15). The file lives "local and in GitHub" and covers business objectives and "how I decision make and problem solve" ([[src-E69-stop-paying-for-saas-the-ai-playbook-driving-a-1b-trajectory]] @13:22–@14:25). It descends from the Jan 2026 principles markdown file and decision principles mined from his Notion meeting transcripts ([[src-the-claude-code-era-mastering-autonomous-gtm-agents-jordan-crawford]] @58:15–@61:17).
- **Content machine:** writing skills built from a 150-page doc of his posts. A podcast-prep pipeline drafts the guest docket. A transcript → newsletter → LinkedIn pipeline has human choice points (5 hooks, 10 takeaways) ([[src-E65-inside-our-ai-sales-stack-kyle-norton]] @18:02–@22:12). Every episode is ingested into an LLM wiki of "atomic ideas" per Karpathy ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @40:40–@42:42). Guests get pre-written LinkedIn posts in their own voice ([[src-E67-your-buyer-s-ai-is-pitching-your-product-badly]] @37:42–@39:48).
- **Agent payments:** a Robinhood virtual card for agents with a $500 monthly limit and approvals off. Its first successful purchase was reordering a particular sock ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @74:31–@79:37).
- **Operating habit:** "always being in the loop." On bad output, update the skill or context, and ask the model for a "mini retrospective" (likely Kyle) ([[src-E67-your-buyer-s-ai-is-pitching-your-product-badly]] @44:02–@46:08). For any repeated task, invest up front: "pick a thing you're going to do over and over and over and know that upfront." ([[src-gtm-ai-advice-from-a-salesforce-siebel-veteran-peter-grant-cro-y]] @59:02)

## Tensions, contradictions & open questions
- **Dockets vs. "no docket."** His prep pipeline exists to produce a guest docket ([[src-E65-inside-our-ai-sales-stack-kyle-norton]] @19:03–@21:10; [[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @40:40). In Sep 2026, crons check 48h before recording that the docket "is created and shared" ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @63:08–@67:19). But on E71 (Jul 2026) he says he no longer sends a docket or outline to guests because guests pre-wrote answers "and those episodes were the worst," and adds "I don't need to do a bunch of research to know what interesting questions to ask" ([[src-E71-the-most-efficient-marketing-org-in-the-world-35-people-350m]] @71:14–@72:18). A possible reconciliation is that the docket is for his own use, but "shared" doesn't fit.
- **Clay table → OpenClaw.** The podcast-research workflow was a Clay table by early 2025 (coffee, E30, E45, Polaris, E55) and still in Mar 2026 (E65). By May 2026 (SaaStr) it is an OpenClaw skill chain. He never explains the migration, but it falls in the window when he adopted OpenClaw (Mar–Apr 2026).
- **Overbuilt and then rebuilt again.** He described himself as possibly orienting "too much to chasing shiny tools" back in Oct 2024 ([[src-E04-mark-roberge-on-redefining-sales-leadership-in-the-ai-era]] @22:55). His tool sequence runs ChatGPT custom GPTs → projects (Mar 2025) → Claude projects → Claude Code (Dec 2025) → OpenClaw (Mar 2026) → "Grok bot" (Sep 2026), and his vibe-coding platforms run Replit → Windsurf → Bolt/Lovable/Codex → Cursor. In May 2026 he says OpenClaw is "probably overbuilt" and that he "could get most of" it in Replit or Claude (E69 @15:28). In April he was "pot committed" (E67). This churn sits awkwardly next to his advice to "pick one harness... grind it to quality" (SaaStr @36:37–@40:40). He acknowledges it: "some of what worked isn't working anymore" (2026-07-16 post).
- **Personal stack vs. org doctrine.** He builds heavily himself but argues reps and managers shouldn't, and that leaders "probably" don't need a terminal and a GitHub repo ([[src-E65-inside-our-ai-sales-stack-kyle-norton]] @25:25–@26:26). His own setup is terminal plus GitHub. In Mar 2025 he regretted being "the most deeply entrenched in AI right now of anybody in my go-to-market team" ([[src-E26-signals-that-separate-great-companies-from-risky-bets-with-n]] @31:59). See [[ai-operating-model]].
- **Critical thinking.** He says "I feel like I'm I'm using more critical thinking than ever" designing the system, but warns "it's very easy to go the other way" ([[src-gtm-strategy-5-insights-from-500-b2b-saas-orgs-jeremey-donovan-evp]] @75:33). His decision-principles doc also "over relies on the principles in this document" (E60 @60:17–61:17). He has not said how he guards against outsourcing judgment, beyond saying that isn't the goal.
- **Security posture.** He keeps OpenClaw off his work computer (May) and gives Kai a 1Password vault and a spend-limited card with approvals off (Sep). He has not discussed governance of an agent holding real credentials and money.
