# Point-of-View Positioning

> As of Aug 2026, Kyle's position is that buyers don't believe vendor claims ("basically nobody believes anything we say"), so you should sell a worldview and an explicit trade-off, compete in a "market of one", and let misfits disqualify themselves ([[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @28:50–35:02). The idea is much older than the Owner era: it was born at League (2016–2019), refined on Shopify POS, and he has called it a teaching of "six or seven years" ([[src-elite-selling-pov-selling]] @03:09–11:33, [[src-work-sprawl-and-the-future-of-marketing-with-global-vp-marketing-cli]] @66:14).

## Current position (as of 2026-08-05)
- Sell a worldview before the product. Owner's version gives the buyer two paths: assemble Toast/BentoBox/Mailchimp-style pieces for flexibility, or take Owner's opinionated box, which changes your site without asking but makes you more money [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @28:50–35:02.
- State the trade-off out loud: "you get less choice and flexibility on owner" in exchange for centralized optimization by "an army of... the best software engineers in restaurant tech" [[src-andys-pod-saying-no-record-growth]] @48:15–53:29.
- Force a choice, not a comparison (Mike Maples, *Pattern Breakers*). Comparisons collapse into features and price [[src-elite-selling-pov-selling]] @29:21–36:38, [[src-coffee-scaling-smarter]] @25:40–29:54.
- Be concrete first. He tells sellers to "feature dump right off the top" (website, online ordering, email, text marketing), then state the point of view [[src-work-sprawl-and-the-future-of-marketing-with-global-vp-marketing-cli]] @62:08–67:14, [[src-20vc-20sales-2m-to-40m-arr]] @35:15–38:19.
- Admit weaknesses to build trust (the pratfall or "four star" effect). Owner has a "point of view positioning doc" that teaches this [[src-E52-winning-in-crowded-markets-the-cro-playbook-jonathan-leaf-cro-ba]] @46:36–47:41.
- Competitors can't copy a coherent point of view without becoming incoherent themselves. Toast can't adopt Owner's [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @28:50–35:02. His test: "if your competitors could reasonably assume the same positioning as you, your positioning is no good." [[src-coffee-scaling-smarter]] @28:50.
- Back the worldview with specific evidence. In +PROFIT, the O is "offer evidence", e.g. showing a worse-reviewed restaurant that outranks the prospect on Google [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @36:03–38:07.
- The fit question settles itself: buyers who don't share the view disqualify themselves, and keeping them in the funnel creates "fake" pipeline [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @35:02. The payoff he measures is shorter "time to lose", not a higher win rate [[src-E18-confessions-from-a-first-time-cro-with-catie-ivey-of-walnut]] @62:24.

## How his view evolved
- **2015-09 (Vision Critical):** The seed, not yet the doctrine. His "checkpoint selling" cold call builds intrigue rather than dumping features, and he recommends challenger-style messaging [[src-hunter-craft-radio-vision-critical]] @05:04–09:11, @40:00–43:05.
- **2018-09 (League):** Selling an unwanted change (benefits) meant getting HR buyers "excited about something that they probably don't want to talk about", with a challenger mentality. About 30 late-stage loss interviews drove positioning changes [[src-sales-leadership-podcast-15-league]] @06:15–08:19, @13:29–15:34. In 2025 he names League as where the point of view was born: deep-funnel losses "were never truly on board with this vision" [[src-elite-selling-pov-selling]] @03:09–06:19.
- **2019–2020 (Shopify POS):** He refined it selling Shopify POS for "the first 18 months": if POS is an operational/reporting tool, Shopify isn't the answer; if it should drive revenue tied to e-commerce, it is [[src-elite-selling-pov-selling]] @10:29–11:33. In 2020 he leaned on Challenger fundamentals (teach, tailor, take control) for the downturn [[src-shopify-head-of-revenue-retail-2020]] @30:33–34:45. At Shopify, "time to lose went way down" while win rates stayed about the same [[src-E18-confessions-from-a-first-time-cro-with-catie-ivey-of-walnut]] @62:24 (told in 2025).
- **2024-02:** First stated on a podcast as a signature idea, drawn from League, Shopify and Owner. Concede what you're not (e.g., Lightspeed's reporting is better) and sell the belief. "Choose bento box" if design matters most [[src-grow-and-tell-selling-to-smbs]] @15:25–17:28, @35:58–39:03. The clip [[src-grow-and-tell-selling-skeptical-icp]] @02:04 is the same content, not separate evidence.
- **2024-06:** "I lead with the trade off": less customization in exchange for tests across thousands of customers [[src-the-simmer-owner-cro]] @17:37.
- **2024-10:** The formula is "if you believe X, then we're the only solution". He cites the Shopify global narrative rewrite and the April Dunford / Andy Raskin messaging books [[src-venture-podcast-cro-owner]] @45:59–52:20. On his own show he says "I have long taught what I have named point of view positioning", rooted in "compete with no one" and Maples' "give people a choice, not comparison" [[src-E05-mark-niemiec-on-steering-business-transformation]] @51:02–52:04.
- **2025-01:** The fullest early treatment: a customer-voice Mad Libs, a "lead balloon" test (iterate five to ten times until prospects react strongly), and choice over comparison [[src-elite-selling-pov-selling]] @16:46–17:48, @29:21. On his show he describes "staggered discovery" and referring design-led fine dining to BentoBox, and says he has taught radically transparent selling for "seven or eight years" [[src-E18-confessions-from-a-first-time-cro-with-catie-ivey-of-walnut]] @58:22–62:24.
- **2025-05:** Point of view is one of three keys to early customers: "go to market with a point of view". He adds off-ramping, and calls Gong's "better to be different than better" "different language for the same thing that I say" [[src-andys-pod-saying-no-record-growth]] @41:01–45:10, @52:26. Positioning is one of his four pillars, with the competitor-copy test [[src-coffee-scaling-smarter]] @10:38–12:49, @28:50. On 20VC: the drag on sales cycles is "time to lose" [[src-20vc-20sales-2m-to-40m-arr]] @37:18.
- **2025-06/07:** The same stance appears as radical transparency in a market burned by bad vendors [[src-collin-cadmus-growing-owner-1b]] @13:42–16:47. Lead with the point of view and one 10x feature, not superiority ("Nobody believes that"), and act like a "buying Sherpa" to avoid reactance [[src-pocus-scaling-3m-to-21m]] @17:59–27:24.
- **2025-10:** "feature dump right off the top", then the POV ("restaurant owners don't want to be digital marketers") [[src-work-sprawl-and-the-future-of-marketing-with-global-vp-marketing-cli]] @62:08–67:14.
- **2025-11:** He names it ("We call it point of view positioning") and explains the mechanics. Telling a buyer a rival's app is bad creates a he-said-she-said standoff, so sell the trade-off instead [[src-E52-winning-in-crowded-markets-the-cro-playbook-jonathan-leaf-cro-ba]] @44:30–47:41.
- **2025-12:** He frames the wedge as solving "the money problem" and draws an analogy: Shopify:Lightspeed = Owner:Toast [[src-verticals-revenue-machine]] @49:17–54:30.
- **2026-02:** In AI markets, "the level of trust in AI is very low because there's so much vaporware", so sellers must make the product tangible [[src-cro-life-from-a-20b-exit-to-a-hyper-growth-ai-org-michelle-donnell]] @38:42.
- **2026-04 (new):** The idea extends to AI buyers. If the CRO curates positioning for reps' AI, it becomes "10 times more powerful" [[src-E67-your-buyer-s-ai-is-pitching-your-product-badly]] @51:16. "If your strategy for AI buyer discovery is “we’ll just keep doing good marketing,” your LLM is improvising" [[src-substack-2026-04-30-your-buyers-ai-is-pitching-your-product]].
- **2026-08 (new):** The fullest treatment yet: "nobody believes anything we say", market of one, competitor incoherence, offer evidence, and *The JOLT Effect* [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @28:50–38:07.
- No reversals. The core idea is stable from League (c. 2017–2018) to 2026. What has grown is the operational detail (a doc, a methodology letter, AI context) and one tactical addition: concrete feature-listing up front.

## Numbers he's cited
- None that measure positioning directly, except time to lose: at Shopify, win rates "stayed about the same" while time spent on lost deals fell [[src-E18-confessions-from-a-first-time-cro-with-catie-ivey-of-walnut]] @62:24.
- POV test: iterate "five to ten times" until prospects react strongly [[src-elite-selling-pov-selling]] @17:48.
- Wrong-fit buyers opt out within about 15 minutes [[src-pocus-scaling-3m-to-21m]] @17:59–21:03 (page summary).
- League: average deal size ~20x a year earlier after loss-interview-driven repositioning and moving upmarket [[src-sales-leadership-podcast-15-league]] @13:29–15:34.
- Context figures: reps close "probably 3x the revenue on a per rep basis" vs. competitors [[src-grow-and-tell-selling-to-smbs]] @41:07. Customers often double direct delivery business "in like 90 days" @34:57.

## Tensions, contradictions & open questions
- **Best sales experience vs. point of view.** In Nov 2025 he also argued that in undifferentiated markets buyers "default to the best sales experience" [[src-E52-winning-in-crowded-markets-the-cro-playbook-jonathan-leaf-cro-ba]] @15:27–16:32. That is a different theory of winning (execution) from worldview positioning. He offered both on the same episode without ranking them, and the guest pushed back on the sales-experience claim (@48:43–49:46).
- **Early-stage tactic or permanent doctrine? (largely resolved).** In 2025 he justified it because "Early products are at their worst" [[src-andys-pod-saying-no-record-growth]] @41:01–45:10. The older sources show it was never only an early-stage tactic: he ran it on Shopify POS inside a large public company [[src-elite-selling-pov-selling]] @10:29–11:33 and has taught it for "six or seven years" [[src-work-sprawl-and-the-future-of-marketing-with-global-vp-marketing-cli]] @66:14. The 2025 framing is one justification among several, not the origin.
- **Feature dump vs. intrigue without a feature dump.** In 2015 his cold-call method built intrigue and avoided feature dumping [[src-hunter-craft-radio-vision-critical]] @05:04–09:11. By 2025 he tells sellers to "feature dump right off the top" [[src-work-sprawl-and-the-future-of-marketing-with-global-vp-marketing-cli]] @62:08. The contexts differ (cold-call opener vs. demo opener), and the 2025 "dump" is a concrete product list in service of *Made to Stick* concreteness, not a feature-superiority claim. He hasn't drawn the line himself.
- **Undifferentiated features.** He doesn't want reps showing undifferentiated features, but if asked, reps should say "Oh yeah, we can do that thing" [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @36:03. That sits a little awkwardly next to radically transparent pitching.
- **How long has he taught it?** He gives "seven or eight years" in Jan 2025 [[src-E18-confessions-from-a-first-time-cro-with-catie-ivey-of-walnut]] @58:22 and "six or seven years" in Oct 2025 [[src-work-sprawl-and-the-future-of-marketing-with-global-vp-marketing-cli]] @66:14. Both point to the League years (he joined League in 2016 [[src-sales-leadership-podcast-15-league]]). The small mismatch is loose recall, not a change of view.
