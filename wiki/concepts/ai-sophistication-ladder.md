# The AI Sophistication Ladder (Levels 0–4)

> As of 2026-09-08, Kyle frames GTM AI maturity as a ladder whose decisive rung is Level 3, a governed company "GTM harness" (tool connections, shared context, governance) that every agent builds on. He says Owner is "actively building" Level 3 but is "not masters" of it, and that nobody has reached Level 4, recursive self-improvement ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @28:02–@35:11).

**The numbered ladder is still new since March 2026.** The 93-source backfill (2015 → Mar 2026) contains no numbered maturity ladder from Kyle; the first appearance remains 2026-04-14. The backfill does show **precursors**, listed below: an AI-literacy curriculum for CROs (2025), a distinction between agents and AI workflows (2025), an "AI as infrastructure" metaphor in his 2024–25 Revenue Operating System, and a 2026-01 shift from "chat" to systems-connected AI.

## Current position (as of 2026-09-08)
Unchanged by the backfill.
- The ladder: L0 is chat used as search; L1 is individuals building custom skills or GPTs; L2 is applied-AI or GTM-engineering teams automating end-to-end workflows; L3 is centralized infrastructure; L4 is a recursively self-improving system. He credits Brendan Short (*The Signal*) ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @04:05–@07:07; [[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @28:02).
- L3 is now described in "harness" language: tool connections (Snowflake, Salesforce, SalesLoft, call recorder), shared context (ICP, playbook, stage definitions) and governance that "every new agent points back to." Cursor's internal "chat GTM" is the best example he has seen ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @28:02–@31:08).
- Owner places itself honestly. It is "actively building" L3 and "not masters" of it ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @28:02–@30:06). He says "Owner is not there" on L4 ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @04:05–@07:07).
- L4 means agents that eval and deprecate agents, a "recursive GTM intelligence." "Nobody's here yet" ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @34:10–@35:11). In the Substack this becomes the "Recursively Self-Improving Revenue System," and reaching it requires owning your proprietary GTM learning ([[src-substack-2026-05-26-dont-buy-your-gtm-brain]], preview only).
- Talent gates the rungs. The data layer is L2 work. Going from L2 to L3 needs an architecture-first thinker with real engineering pedigree, not the typical "clay table guy" GTM engineer ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @36:14–@39:24).
- The gap is "competitively existential." The difference between L3 companies and their peers "is widening fast" ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @07:07).
- Operating model is tied to the rungs. A decentralized approach "often has companies stalling out at level one or level two" ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @08:07). See [[ai-operating-model]].

## Precursors (before the numbered ladder)
- **2024 → 2025-01-27: "Iron Man suit" infrastructure.** His Revenue Operating System is a set of tools and rituals he calls "your Iron Man suit" ([[src-linkedin-2025-h1]], post 2025-01-27). His advice is to build it slowly and automate only once the rhythms work ([[src-saastr-ai-revops-playbook]] @30:42–@36:53). It is an operating-system, infrastructure-first idea that predates the "AI as infrastructure" (L3) language ([[src-linkedin-2022-2024]], notes). See [[operating-rhythm]].
- **2024-12-03: narrow beats broad.** "the wider the surface area that you give an AI solution, the more hallucinations, the worse the outcome is gonna be." ([[src-momentum-planning-2025-ai-strategy]] @07:11–08:11). AI is "the world's best intern" (@12:16). This is the floor below the later rungs.
- **2025-03-26: two levels of abstraction.** A CRO must think years ahead about agents while learning the inputs ("How is an agent different than just an AI workflow?") ([[src-E26-signals-that-separate-great-companies-from-risky-bets-with-n]] @33:02–34:02).
- **2025-05-21: a CRO learning curriculum.** He lists four baskets: tools and foundation models, prompt engineering, architecture (how the pieces plug together, without learning Python) and the art of the possible. He defines an agent as something that can "reason, take action and then, and choose from different paths and access other tools", cites Sam Altman's five levels, and calls input-then-action "an AI workflow" ([[src-E33-what-it-takes-to-be-a-revenue-leader-in-the-ai-era-with-mike]] @39:16, @42:29).
- **2025-10 → 2025-12: primitives as the literacy bar.** Leaders "need to know enough of it to understand the primitives". He asks whether candidates know pre-training from post-training and says chaining generative steps means "you're stacking lossiness" ([[src-work-sprawl-and-the-future-of-marketing-with-global-vp-marketing-cli]] @24:24–26:28). "There's no course. There's no consultant", so start with Karpathy ([[src-polaris-lodestar-s1e1]] @15:46–17:53). His bar repeats on E59 ([[src-usage-based-comp-ai-and-the-end-of-the-sdr-tyler-will-vp-of-revops]] @06:05–08:08). "AI native" is not the same as asking ChatGPT for answers. It means daily use, first-principles understanding and, following Lemkin, implementing one tool end to end ([[src-signal-learn-build-scale]] @05:05–07:07). He gave that talk at The Signal's summit, whose host is "Brendan", likely Brendan Short, the later source of the ladder (inference).
- **2025-11-26: one-off prompt to reusable asset.** "pick a thing you're going to do over and over and over and know that upfront." ([[src-gtm-ai-advice-from-a-salesforce-siebel-veteran-peter-grant-cro-y]] @59:02). This is an implicit L0 → L1 step.
- **2026-01-22 → 01-28: past chat, toward systems.** On E60 he says "cloud code is basically an agent" that you treat as a master agent. Tasks should be broken down to "the prime number" and given one agent each ([[src-the-claude-code-era-mastering-autonomous-gtm-agents-jordan-crawford]] @29:42–@40:54). The guest frames GTM in three eras: ChatGPT → Clay → Claude Code. Owner's approach is "almost purely top down" because "you can only get so far with chat". Real value needs Salesforce, Snowflake and research tied together ([[src-gtm-strategy-5-insights-from-500-b2b-saas-orgs-jeremey-donovan-evp]] @07:10–08:11). This is the L0/L1 → L2/L3 argument without the numbers.

## How his view evolved
- **2026-04-14 (new):** The first ladder is an individual tool-fluency ladder: chat → custom GPTs/gems → Clay/Make/n8n workflows → Claude Code/Cowork → vibe-coded internal apps. "if you are not doing even custom GPTs, gems, or cloud projects, you're way behind" ([[src-captivate-talent-stop-vibe-hiring]] @02:02–@04:03).
- **2026-05-07 (new):** He cites Brendan Short's maturity levels L0–L3, from chat use up to "systems builder." His open question is how to be "both central and decentral" ([[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @37:52–@38:54).
- **2026-05-26 (new), SaaStr keynote:** The ladder becomes organizational, L0–L4. L3 is "a deployment platform," shared skills and a "context library." L4 is added as an unreached end state ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @04:05–@07:07). That same day the Substack ties L4 to owning your memory and evals ([[src-substack-2026-05-26-dont-buy-your-gtm-brain]], preview only).
- **2026-07-01 (new):** He frames individual copilots as past tense: "why individual co-pilots are table stakes and why we're really past them now" ([[src-E71-the-most-efficient-marketing-org-in-the-world-35-people-350m]] @02:04).
- **2026-09-02 (new):** He restates the ladder on his own show with different numbering: L1 chat as a Google replacement, L2 working agents and workflows, L3 "AI as infrastructure" (a governed platform instead of personal MCPs and "a monster skill file"), L4 a "recursive self-improving loop." His "Grok bot" inspection bot watches his other bots. "The best companies are getting here like sort of now" ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @42:00–@44:14).
- **2026-09-08 (new):** L3 is reframed as a "GTM harness." "And now I'm starting to really use like the harness language more." He adds a lock-in caution: Claude/Cowork are harnesses that lock you into one ecosystem, so build outside and call in ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @28:02–@33:09).

## Numbers he's cited
- At L3, "the doubling of output on a per person basis" rather than "a 10 or 15% lift" ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @07:07, 2026-05-26; raw transcript). For comparison, the CROs in the Jan 2026 Insight survey expected only a 5–15% lift (guest's data, [[src-gtm-strategy-5-insights-from-500-b2b-saas-orgs-jeremey-donovan-evp]] @52:06). That is roughly the "10 or 15%" he contrasts L3 against.
- About 70% of a leader's AI literacy should come from you and your team ([[src-signal-learn-build-scale]] @13:17), a capability-building number that sits beside the ladder.
- No other quantified measures of the ladder.

## Tensions, contradictions & open questions
- **Numbering drift.** At SaaStr (May), chat-as-search is L0 and individual skills are L1. On E74 (Sep 2), chat-as-Google is L1 and agents/workflows are L2. Captivate (Apr) uses a five-step tool ladder with no level numbers. E68 (May 7) cites only L0–L3. The rungs are stable in substance, but the labels shift by one depending on venue.
- **Is anyone at L4?** SaaStr (May) says "No one's really at level four." Firmable (Sep 8) says "Nobody's here yet." But E74 (Sep 2), six days earlier, says "The best companies are getting here like sort of now," and he describes his own inspection bot monitoring his bots. The difference may be personal stack versus company, but the statements don't line up cleanly.
- **What L3 is.** L3 moved from "centralized deployment platform / shared skills / context library" (May) to a "governed GTM harness" (Sep). He calls the harness vocabulary new on Sep 8 (@28:02), yet on Sep 2 he said "I've talked a lot about building this company meta harness" (E74 @05:19), and the May 26 Substack already defines "harness" as a stack layer (preview only). In Dec 2025 he already asked whether to "build one mega model of our own" or connect many tools ([[src-replacing-your-sales-team-with-ai-agents-jason-lemkin-founder-ceo]] @15:17). See [[context-harness-and-memory]].
- **Individual ladder vs. org ladder.** The April ladder measures personal tool fluency. The May–Sep ladder measures organizational infrastructure, and individual building (L1) is where decentralized companies "stall." These are two different ladders that share one name. The 2025 precursors (learning baskets, primitives) belong to the individual one.
- **Credit.** He attributes the ladder to Brendan Short (SaaStr, E68, Firmable). The Substack calls it "his SaaStr Sophistication Ladder" (preview only). That Kyle spoke at The Signal's summit in late 2025 fits the attribution, but no backfill source shows the ladder being discussed there.
- **Agent vs. workflow.** In May 2025 he held an "old school" definition: an agent reasons, acts, chooses paths and uses tools (E33 @42:29). By Nov 2025 he said "anything that's AI is an agent" now and he has "lost that argument" ([[src-gtm-ai-advice-from-a-salesforce-siebel-veteran-peter-grant-cro-y]] @24:22–25:22). In Jan 2026 he preferred "workflows" to "AI employee" (E61 @63:19–65:23). The ladder's L2 "agents and workflows" blurs the distinction he once drew.
