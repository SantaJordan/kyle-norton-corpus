# Build vs. Buy Software

> As of 2026-09-08, Kyle's rule is "buy infrastructure, build (or at least own) your intelligence". He buys anything central, integration-heavy, governance-critical or uptime-critical (Salesforce, the dialer). He builds or owns what encodes how Owner sells (scoring, pre-call research, context, evals). He is newly wary of harness lock-in to a single AI ecosystem ([[src-substack-2026-05-26-dont-buy-your-gtm-brain]] (preview only); [[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @33:09). The underlying instincts are old: back early vendors as "a founder bet", demand pilots, and avoid walled gardens (2024–2025).

## Current position (as of 2026-09-08)
- "My opinion is that you should be buying your infrastructure and building your intelligence." Five questions decide it: stability/uptime, customization, ROI vs. build cost, whether it is your intelligence, and competitive advantage [[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @14:12–16:14.
- Worked examples: the dialer is a buy (Twilio-class infrastructure) and AI pre-call research is a build. He buys Momentum, OneMind and an AI sim platform [[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @16:14–20:20.
- Never vibe-code the system of record. "we're not going to go vibe code Salesforce". Core systems that manage governance, ecosystem and security are "best bought" [[src-E69-stop-paying-for-saas-the-ai-playbook-driving-a-1b-trajectory]] @31:58–33:00. Same view in Mar 2026: "Nobody's ripping out Salesforce for an AI CRM when they're like 100 million plus ARR." [[src-my-team-drives-4x-revenue-per-ae-vs-competitors-aviv-canaani-cro]] @35:38–36:40.
- Build peripheral tools. Owner built its own LMS and a contest/recognition system because they're "not that central" and an outage isn't fatal. Always ask what "incremental benefit above the quality of the tool today" building would unlock [[src-E69-stop-paying-for-saas-the-ai-playbook-driving-a-1b-trajectory]] @38:16–39:17.
- Own the intelligence and avoid black-box or walled-garden vendors. A platform is acceptable if you can see the intelligence and "still take it out" [[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @21:10–23:14; [[src-substack-2026-04-30-your-buyers-ai-is-pitching-your-product]].
- New lock-in concern: Claude/Cowork are harnesses that tie you to one ecosystem. Build outside and call in. "Almost nobody in GTM is talking about it" [[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @33:09.
- When picking early-stage vendors, he looks for philosophical alignment and the chance to shape the roadmap [[src-E73-the-ai-enabling-600-customer-facing-reps]] @10:11. This is the same founder-bet test he has used since 2024 ([[src-E04-mark-roberge-on-redefining-sales-leadership-in-the-ai-era]] @48:17; [[src-E41-jessica-chiew-of-canva-on-building-ai-native-gtm-org]] @27:31–29:31). Always push for a pilot ([[src-E19-architecting-systems-for-hypergrowth-with-nate-follen-of-ram]] @60:39–61:40).

## How his view evolved

- **2023-12-17.** He picks Momentum because the vendor ships fast, and keeps Salesforce as the one app he can't lose, though "the shift to the data warehouse as the source of truth (Snowflake for us) and AI" might change that [[src-web-2023-12-17-saastr-my-app-stack-my-app-stack-kyle-norton-cro]].
- **2024-10-16.** Vendor selection as venture investing: for seed and Series A tools, "Just pick the founders that you think are gonna win that market Even if the product might might not be quite as good". He bet on Momentum's founders with a "controlled blast radius" pilot, and requires an opportunity solution tree before any pilot [[src-E04-mark-roberge-on-redefining-sales-leadership-in-the-ai-era]] @40:56–51:28.
- **2024-10-23.** Composability over walled gardens: Owner builds custom Snowflake tooling on Salesloft's APIs, which makes "our long-term attachment to Sales Loft much stronger" [[src-E05-mark-niemiec-on-steering-business-transformation]] @16:50–17:54.

## Tensions, contradictions & open questions
- **The dialer.** In Apr 2025 he wanted "a hyper-bespoke scripting tool" but did not want to maintain custom software. In May 2025 he wrote a dialer PRD and said "we're thinking about just like actually building it ourselves internally"; in Oct 2025 he "earmarked" a custom build. In May 2026: "I don't think we would vibe code a calling tool", and the dialer is his canonical "buy" example. Treat this as a reversal, or at least a hardened view.
- **Salesloft rip-out vs. don't vibe code what reps use daily.** The idea predates Mar 2026 (Oct 2025). Ripping out Salesloft to "building natively on top of Salesforce" would mean building a rep's daily surface, which his April 2026 rule forbids. No follow-up has been reported.
- **Built vs. bought enrichment.** He says he'd now buy Clay instead of the internal waterfall, and was already talking about "converging on Clay" in Sep 2025. Yet under his own rule, data that encodes Owner's market knowledge is arguably intelligence. Read this as a shift toward treating enrichment as infrastructure.
- **"Point solutions can be built internally"** (Mar 2026) vs. "you can't win as a point solution" for vendors (Jan 2026) vs. "I am not religious about building everything internally (that's usually a terrible strategy)" (May 2026, preview only). Same direction, different emphasis.
- **Salesforce bear phase: now documented.** The earlier gap is closed. Bear-side statements: the 2023 caveat about a warehouse plus AI, "somebody could definitely get Salesforce now" (Feb 2025), the Einstein anecdote (told Dec 2025). The bull turn is on record by Dec 2025, so his Apr 2026 "24 months ago" framing compresses a roughly 10-month shift (Feb → Dec 2025).
- **Founder bets vs. incumbents.** He has backed early-stage founders since 2024, yet an incumbent (Guru) beat AI-native startups in his own bake-off (Dec 2025), and he is now leaning into Salesforce. He has not reconciled the two heuristics.
- **Paywall gap.** The prescriptive section of "Don't Buy Your GTM Brain" is cut off. The full build/buy framework in that post is unverified.
