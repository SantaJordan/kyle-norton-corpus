# Executive Performance & Mindset

> Kyle's current position (2026-05-29): executive performance starts in physiology, not willpower. "You can't think your way out of a bad physiological state". He admits he lived in flight response "closer to 110% of the time", and now runs a stack of daily cold plunges with heart-rate feedback, sleep hygiene, gratitude, meditation and near-zero alcohol. He leads it from the front and keeps it opt-in for the team ([[src-substack-2026-04-03-how-to-optimize-exec-performance]]; [[src-E70-manufacture-motivation-sell-more]] @04:02–05:04, @57:37–66:54).

## Current position (as of 2026-05-29)
- **Physiology first.** He accepts guest Kevin Bailey's performance chain (physiology → emotions → feelings → cognition → behaviors → results). Kyle: "You can't think your way out of a bad physiological state" [[src-substack-2026-04-03-how-to-optimize-exec-performance]]; [[src-E66-win-more-by-losing-less]] @08:10–11:15.
- **Self-diagnosis: chronic flight response.** "Mine is closer to 110% of the time" [[src-substack-2026-04-03-how-to-optimize-exec-performance]]. The matching E66 line ("110% of the time") has no speaker label [[src-E66-win-more-by-losing-less]] @17:27.
- **Cold plunge as a "daily laboratory".** He has a plunge at home and uses it in the morning. His heart rate went from about 100 on his first plunges to "the low 60s" by slowing his exhale. "it's literally a choice to be overwhelmed" [[src-E66-win-more-by-losing-less]] @27:38–38:50. It's "the most underrated awareness tool available to executives" [[src-substack-2026-04-03-how-to-optimize-exec-performance]].
- **Sleep and recovery stack.** Oura ring hooked into his AI agent; glycine and theanine for deep sleep; mouth tape, blue-light glasses, eye mask and an Eight Sleep, each added one at a time. Drinking at "basically zero a week". HRV was 51 after three or four drinks vs. 88 "today" [[src-E70-manufacture-motivation-sell-more]] @16:27–17:29, @57:37–66:54.
- **Stoic reframes, honestly self-assessed.** He practices amor fati (Daily Stoic coins) and calls himself "a suppressor for sure", possibly adopting anger journaling [[src-E66-win-more-by-losing-less]] @60:17–61:18, @69:27. His starter list: morning gratitude, meditation, something physically hard, and drop one dopamine waster [[src-E66-win-more-by-losing-less]] @86:50–89:54.
- **Rest is hard for him.** He gets "stir crazy if I'm not making progress on something" [[src-E70-manufacture-motivation-sell-more]] @52:32–55:35. The serotonin day "sounds like a nightmare to me", though recovery is "part of the operating model" [[src-substack-2026-05-29-manufacture-motivation-sell-more]].
- **Self-regulation is a leadership qualification.** "If someone can't regulate their own state, I have a hard time trusting them with the states of the people who report to them" [[src-substack-2026-04-03-how-to-optimize-exec-performance]].
- **Lead yourself first (his oldest stance on this topic).** "you can't lead people until you can lead yourself" (2020) [[src-shopify-head-of-revenue-retail-2020]] @37:54, restated as "So you can't manage others unless you can manage yourself." (2025) [[src-revenue-diaries-ep4]] @25:36. In 2026 he frames it at team scale: "My big takeaway: mental performance is sales infrastructure." [[src-linkedin-2026]] (post 2026-05-29).
- **Fear is the failure mode, planning is the cure.** "fear is sort of the end of you as a revenue leader" [[src-topline-e78-executive-fear-boards]] @05:07; "Fear is a planning problem" [[src-web-2025-12-04-sta-topline-asad-zaman-q-a-qa-kyle-norton-on-scaling-yourself]].
- **Lead from the front with AI too.** He builds a personal compounding AI stack (OpenClaw "Kai" chief of staff, skills, an LLM-wiki knowledge graph) and urges leaders to "lead from the front" [[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @36:37–42:42; [[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @46:21–48:25. See [[personal-ai-stack]].

He calls himself not naturally organized and compensates by calendaring everything [[src-hunter-craft-radio-vision-critical]] @10:12–12:14, @57:25–59:28.
