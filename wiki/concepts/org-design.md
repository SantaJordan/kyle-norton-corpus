# Org Design

> As of 2026-09-08, Kyle's org-design view is "more about the people in the seats than it is the org chart". Build leadership teams like a basketball team ("You can't have five centers"), centralize AI, specialize the front line, and periodically delete roles, tools and process because "complexity isn't necessarily maturity" ([[src-captivate-talent-stop-vibe-hiring]] @35:36; [[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @40:26–@42:37; [[src-E72-i-collapsed-my-gtm-org-chart-this-is-why]] @01:04, @56:31).

## Current position (as of 2026-09-08)
- **People over boxes.** There's no one answer: SMB leaders must be systems-oriented so RevOps reports to them; enterprise "deal jockeys" may not suit that ([[src-captivate-talent-stop-vibe-hiring]] @32:33–@35:36).
- **Complementary leadership teams.** "You can't have five centers"; if the CRO isn't a systems thinker, "pay up a little bit more for your RevOps slash applied AI person" ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @40:26–@42:37). Same basketball framing two weeks earlier ([[src-E74-your-agent-isn-t-broken-its-foundation-is]] @45:16).
- **Kyle's scope today:** sales, customer onboarding, partnerships, RevOps and enablement; not marketing (CMO reports to the CEO) ([[src-captivate-talent-stop-vibe-hiring]] @30:32–@35:36).
- **Centralize AI.** Applied AI sits in data/BizOps, not under Kyle; reps shouldn't run agents ([[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @12:11–@14:12, @26:26–@27:27). "You don't necessarily need an AI czar" ([[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @36:52).
- **More specialization, not less.** Unbundle the JD into tasks, move them to agents, workflows or central teams; "There is no way that any of my AEs could cold call the way my BDRs call now." Convergence happens at leadership, not the front line ([[src-E65-inside-our-ai-sales-stack-kyle-norton]] @13:45–@14:50).
- **Redesign the work, don't automate the org chart** ([[src-substack-2026-05-17-stop-paying-for-saas-the-ai-playbook]]). AI is "the death of the administrator, and the rise of the orchestrator" ([[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @06:21–@07:45).
- **Delete on a cadence.** Organizations can add headcount, budget and tools but have "no business process to say, all right, every 90 days, we're going to sit down and delete some stuff" ([[src-E72-i-collapsed-my-gtm-org-chart-this-is-why]] @54:25–@56:31).
- **Single-threaded DRIs for new channels.** Cold email stalled ~6 weeks until a manager became DRI with a daily standup ([[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @17:29–@18:30).
- **(new) The CRO seat itself "integrates or specializes."** Scope expands and contracts as functions split out or consolidate: "So what changes is not just size of scope, but the structure around the role and the degree to which the CRO is being asked to integrate versus specialize." Whatever the scope, keep "distance to frontline" short ([[src-web-2026-07-29-n2growth-ownercoms-kyle-norton-on-succeeding-in]], 2026-07-29).
- **(new) AI is already removing roles.** "Roles I would have hired for 18 months ago don't exist on my org chart anymore." "The tech is the easy part." ([[src-linkedin-2026]] post 2026-04-20).

*These planks are older than the March 2026 corpus suggested.* Centralized AI dates to May 2025, front-line specialization to 2018 (League), and the "100 points to allocate" team-composition idea behind "five centers" to Sep 2025. See the evolution below.

## How his view evolved
- **2018-09-25 (League, earliest):** Front-line specialization was already his model: AEs generated only ~20–25% of their own pipeline, BDRs and inbound the rest, because "seasoned account executives don't want to pick up the phone because they're out of practice and they don't want to get embarrassed." ([[src-sales-leadership-podcast-15-league]] @18:49–@20:58).
- **2020-08-17 (Shopify):** As Head of Revenue, Retail, his scope was sales, enablement, RevOps, solutions engineering and implementations, a team that was "like seven people when I started" ([[src-shopify-head-of-revenue-retail-2020]] @05:09).
- **2022-11-01:** New markets: a senior revenue leader must own the push; seed them with a proven rep plus a local hire, or a manager-led pod once there's PMF ([[src-gtmnow-5-slow-is-smooth]] @38:02–@44:09).
- **2024-02-05:** Shopify's late-2020 "every region sells all products" reorg was "basically a train wreck"; he wrote an eight-page doc against it ([[src-grow-and-tell-selling-to-smbs]] @18:28–@20:33).
- **2024-02-13:** Hiring Owner's first second-line sales leader to consolidate "the 2 AE and 3 XDR teams"; the role had to be "third line ready" ([[src-linkedin-2022-2024]] post 2024-02-13).
- **2024-04-18 (event: Oct 2023):** **Single-threaded ownership**: the sales leader owns post-sales (adopted October 2023), which kills finger-pointing, but AEs shouldn't do onboarding ([[src-saastr-gtm-efficiency-smb]] @24:30–@26:33, @48:12–@49:13).
- **2024-08-01:** **Full-stack revenue org**: bringing onboarding and then CS under him improved outcomes, and he had RevOps spend 60% of its time on CS systems: "So I believe that having a full stack model makes a ton of sense." At this point Owner was remote-first with no employee offices and ~60% of his team in LatAm ([[src-startwell-cro-tells-founders]] @57:22–@61:28, @08:10–@09:10).
- **2024-10-06:** RevOps is a "force multiplier," probably not a C-suite seat; it works "hand-in-hand" with BizOps/data, which owns the single source of truth ([[src-topline-e78-executive-fear-boards]] @35:29, @43:43).
- **2024-10-09:** Multi-product: Shopify POS ran as a separate team, and folding it back slowed it for a year. His rule: "the default answer should be give it to the existing reps unless it is" too complex ([[src-E03-dini-mehta-on-mastering-multi-product-strategy-building-hi]] @23:01–@27:08).
- **2024-10-30:** Host intro lists Kyle's scope as sales, partnerships, onboarding, success, support, RevOps, enablement ([[src-bridge-the-gap-scaling-startups]] @00:00). At a startup you stage and gate leadership investments ([[src-bridge-the-gap-scaling-startups]] @05:06–@07:07).
- **2024-11-13:** In year one at Owner he was "executive revenue owner second line manager And sort of first line manager because I was in the weeds writing scripts"; he then hired an external VP of Sales, which "Significantly levels up our leadership capacity" ([[src-E08-sam-blonds-blueprint-for-building-exceptional-leaders-tea]] @21:45, @43:44).
- **2024-12-03:** Revenue leaders should own onboarding; SEs stay through implementation. His scope includes demand gen/growth and hands off to CS/support ([[src-testbox-lessons-in-scaling]] @06:11, @37:46–@38:50).
- **2024-12-04:** Leadership altitude: first-line managers execute, VPs set strategy, SVPs and CROs set "who does what." Owner was "100 remote uh the entire company's history," and he was speaking from a new San Francisco office ([[src-E11-mastering-strategic-planning-with-eric-gilpin-cro-of-g2]] @04:13–@08:21, @64:39).
- **2025-01-01:** "I hate the team lead role." With two layers now between him and managers, "consistency of system is, is critical." Owner is "a fully distributed team and it's working great." ([[src-E14-a-systematic-approach-to-gtm-infrastructure-and-talent-with]] @58:57, @63:06, @36:10).
- **2025-01-08:** Marketing was split even then: "Growth reports into me, but then brand and content reports directly into the CEO" ([[src-E15-the-blueprint-for-sales-and-marketing-alignment-with-kyle-la]] @30:55).
- **2025-01-19:** Fewer managers; principal IC roles comped like team leads; people "managing virtual heads, like agents" ([[src-topline-e92-great-cros-enterprise-value]] @53:26–@55:29).
- **2025-02-05 (rec. Jan 2025):** At $1–3M ARR, "don't hire a true cro at two million hire a vp of sales", a ~24-month bridge. His scope: onboarding, RevOps, enablement, partnerships and BD. The Toronto hub opened with all new SDRs/BDRs five days in office ([[src-bravado-scaling-smb-30m]] @35:53, @15:52, @58:13–@62:22).
- **2025-02-13:** First-time VP mistake: over-hired against an unchallenged model and laid off "like 20% of the team" ([[src-getaccept-change-management]] @07:22).
- **2025-02-26:** Decision hierarchy for org choices: "It's customer, company, team, rep, manager, last." Also: "it's not until you own it that I think you viscerally feel the pain of sales reps being sloppy with their handoff" ([[src-E22-designing-a-gtm-strategy-for-multi-product-companies-with-je]] @55:59, @35:50).
- **2025-05-28 (re-dated: centralized AI starts here, not 2026):** "most of the automation and AI is centralized. I'm, it's in my biz ops and rev ops team." ICs needn't be AI power users, and he's hiring a GTM AI lead. Also: "I'm not into the full cycle rep because I believe much more in specialization." ([[src-saastr-ai-sales-gtm-2026-lemkin]] @07:18, @22:42).
- **2025-05-30:** RevOps, enablement, data and BizOps "way overweight" by design; ~70 people in LatAm ([[src-20vc-20sales-2m-to-40m-arr]] @46:30–@51:44).
- **2025-06-11:** Strategic congruence: all-digital (Owner) or in-market (Toast) both work; "no man's land" fails ([[src-collin-cadmus-growing-owner-1b]] @18:53–@23:05).
- **2025-08-01:** Owner's "GTM AI lead" handles workflows; the data layer sits with "a strong VP of BizOps and data". So the applied-AI-in-BizOps structure dates to Aug 2025, not Mar 2026. He agrees middle-manager "translation layers" will shrink ([[src-E41-jessica-chiew-of-canva-on-building-ai-native-gtm-org]] @12:11–@14:13, @47:47).
- **2025-08-07:** He hedges on specialization: "every time you get more specialized, your sales engine gets more complex and sometimes brittle." ([[src-E42-cutting-the-fat-and-building-sales-machines-that-scale-with]] @24:31).

## Tensions, contradictions & open questions
- **Post-sales ownership drifted away (now better dated).** In Aug 2024 he said he had brought onboarding *and then CS* under him ([[src-startwell-cro-tells-founders]] @57:22–@61:28). By Jan 2025 his listed scope was onboarding, RevOps, enablement, partnerships and BD, with no CS ([[src-bravado-scaling-smb-30m]] @15:52). So CS moved off his plate between Aug 2024 and Jan 2025. Muddying this, an outlet-written July 2026 bio lists "customer success, support" in his scope again ([[src-web-2026-07-29-n2growth-ownercoms-kyle-norton-on-succeeding-in]]); that may be stale boilerplate. In 2023–2024 Kyle championed sales owning post-sales (single-threaded ownership, Oct 2023) and revenue leaders owning onboarding. The Oct 2024 host intro lists success and support in his scope. By Dec 2024 he "hands off to CS/support," and by Dec 2025 CS is "formerly" his. He still owns *onboarding*, but not CS. On his own show (Aug 2026) he raised the usual objection, that revenue leaders "won't pay enough attention to CS", and asked whether CRO-owned CS should be the default ([[src-E72-i-collapsed-my-gtm-org-chart-this-is-why]] @27:07). He has never explained publicly why CS moved.
- **Marketing moved off his plate.** Not a 2026 event. Marketing was already split in Jan 2025: growth reported to him, while brand and content reported to the CEO ([[src-E15-the-blueprint-for-sales-and-marketing-alignment-with-kyle-la]] @30:55). The CMO was in place for 2026 planning by Oct 2025 ([[src-polaris-lodestar-s1e1]] @08:28). The first evidence that growth moved too is Dec 2025 (new CMO "not even 60 days in", [[src-verticals-revenue-machine]] @55:36–@56:40). Apr 2026 confirms the CMO reports to the CEO ([[src-captivate-talent-stop-vibe-hiring]] @33:35). He frames it as neutral ("people in the seats") and praises the CMO as revenue-focused ([[src-captivate-talent-stop-vibe-hiring]] @34:36).
- **Specialize vs. collapse.** He argues for front-line specialization ([[src-E65-inside-our-ai-sales-stack-kyle-norton]] @13:45) and for simplicity and fewer silos ([[src-E72-i-collapsed-my-gtm-org-chart-this-is-why]]). These fit only if specialization is at the IC level and collapse at the leadership level, which he says explicitly ("convergence at the leadership level").
- **Centralized AI vs. his guests.** He contrasts his central team with Backbase's harness-plus-business-builders model ([[src-E71-the-most-efficient-marketing-org-in-the-world-35-people-350m]] @35:01–@36:09) and with Webflow's merged GTM engineering org. He also asks how to be "both central and decentral" ([[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @37:52–@38:54). The "who owns it" question is open ([[src-E69-stop-paying-for-saas-the-ai-playbook-driving-a-1b-trajectory]] @55:47).
- **"Fewer managers" (Jan 2025)** vs. a sales team approaching ~150 run ~50/50 by internal promotes ([[src-captivate-talent-stop-vibe-hiring]] @22:23). He has since reframed this as fewer *pure people* managers rather than a lower count ([[src-the-new-rules-of-executive-recruiting-in-the-ai-era-asad-zaman-ceo]] @08:11; [[src-E41-jessica-chiew-of-canva-on-building-ai-native-gtm-org]] @47:47), but has never given a manager-to-rep ratio target.
- **Specialization: champion or skeptic?** He has been pro-specialization since League (2018) and in May 2025 was "not into the full cycle rep". In Aug 2025 he warned that specialization makes the engine "brittle" and destroys capacity spreadsheets once churn is modeled ([[src-E42-cutting-the-fat-and-building-sales-machines-that-scale-with]] @24:31–@27:38). The reconciliation seems to be: specialize, but staff deep enough that one promotion doesn't break a segment.
- **Remote-first org (2024) vs. in-person hub (2025+).** Hiring posts said "fully remote" with no office plans through Nov 2024 ([[src-linkedin-2022-2024]]), and he called Owner "fully distributed… working great" in Jan 2025 ([[src-E14-a-systematic-approach-to-gtm-infrastructure-and-talent-with]] @36:10). Yet he later says the in-person decision was made "midway through 2024" ([[src-web-2026-06-15-morning-brew-revenue-brew-ownercoms-hiring-and-coaching-strategies-for]]), and the Toronto hub launched Jan 2025. The public messaging lagged the decision by about six months. See [[office-vs-remote]].
