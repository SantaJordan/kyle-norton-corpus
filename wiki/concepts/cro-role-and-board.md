# The CRO Role & the Board

> As of 2026-09-08, Kyle defines the real CRO as a company-first "multiplier" who owns enterprise value and P&L, not just the closed-won number. Since spring 2026 he has added that the CRO must personally lead AI ("lead from the front"), understand "the financial architecture of the business", and work at two altitudes, the spreadsheet and the rep's day ([[src-substack-2026-05-13-are-you-uninvestable-cassie-young]]; [[src-saastr-2m-arr-per-rep-with-ai]] @42:42; [[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @21:33–@26:46).

## Current position (as of 2026-09-08)
- **Enterprise value over the number.** A legit revenue leader, "not just like a dressed up sales manager with a VP of sales title," owns enterprise value ([[src-pavilion-decoding-churn-retention]] @14:40); this is "one of the three biggest changes" from VP Sales to CRO ([[src-andys-pod-saying-no-record-growth]] @10:19).
- **GM-minded and financially fluent.** "Most CROs were trained for a world that no longer exists"; next-gen CROs win "by understanding the financial architecture of the business and using AI to change it"; "The bloat era is over. The GM-minded CRO era is starting." ([[src-substack-2026-05-13-are-you-uninvestable-cassie-young]]).
- **AI strategy is the CRO's job.** It "falls on the leader"; a consultant's AI strategy is "out of date in three to six months" ([[src-ivey-future-of-sales-irreplaceable]] @06:11; [[src-substack-2026-03-31-ai-can-do-everything-a-salesperson]]). Winning CROs are "modeling the way themselves" ([[src-substack-2026-04-30-your-buyers-ai-is-pitching-your-product]]). He asked how boards should treat a CRO who hits plan but hasn't learned AI ([[src-E68-are-you-uninvestable-the-new-gtm-reality]] @51:29).
- **The CRO curates the intelligence.** A rep asking a generic chatbot how to beat a competitor gets "slop"; if the CRO curates positioning it becomes "10 times more powerful" ([[src-E67-your-buyer-s-ai-is-pitching-your-product-badly]] @51:16).
- **Two altitudes.** Great CROs live in the spreadsheet and in the rep's day; "go see the work" ([[src-gtmshift-your-gtm-worked-live]] @21:33–@26:46). This is one of his oldest lines, not a 2026 idea: "The strength of a great revenue leader is be able to operate at two levels of abstraction simultaneously." ([[src-startwell-cro-tells-founders]] @17:20, 2024-08-01). Executives must "scale down": "The old model was delegation through layers. The new model is compression." ([[src-substack-2026-05-17-stop-paying-for-saas-the-ai-playbook]]).
- **Systems thinking isn't mandatory if you staff for it.** "You can't have five centers"; a non-systems CRO should pay up for RevOps/applied AI ([[src-firmable-best-practice-killing-sales]] @40:26–@42:37).
- **(new) Time horizon and "distance to frontline."** "So the transition from head of sales to CRO is partly a transition from managing execution to staging the future." Distance to frontline is "one of the most important ideas in the job" ([[src-web-2026-07-29-n2growth-ownercoms-kyle-norton-on-succeeding-in]], 2026-07-29). This restates his Dec 2025 line: "your time horizon and level of abstraction go up as you as you get more and more senior" ([[src-web-2025-12-04-sta-topline-asad-zaman-q-a-qa-kyle-norton-on-scaling-yourself]]).
- **Board relationship:** run toward bad news; losing board trust means "long-term you're, you're absolutely cooked" ([[src-andys-pod-saying-no-record-growth]] @26:42); stop complaining about VCs and founders ("fair is not a thing here") ([[src-finn-thormeier-sales-life-career]] @29:50–@31:53).

## How his view evolved
- **2022-11-01 (earliest, SVP Sales ~5 months in):** Against the 50/50 head-of-sales plan, citing Shopify (senior revenue leaders got base plus stock, no variable). Founders should tie revenue leaders to company growth numbers. The leader under CEO, board and comp pressure has to be "the sober second thought" ([[src-gtmnow-5-slow-is-smooth]] @19:32–@26:44).
- **2024-02-07:** Teams miss targets from bad target setting; executives and finance avoid "the reality of the ground floor" ([[src-accord-documentation-dry-runs]] @36:28).
- **2024-04-18:** Revenue leaders need P&L fluency to go "being like mediocre to great"; first board meeting: Jason Lemkin said none of it was showing up in revenue ([[src-saastr-gtm-efficiency-smb]] @13:16, @37:54).
- **2024-08-01:** "two levels of abstraction simultaneously": fine-grained mechanics plus bets quarters or years out ([[src-startwell-cro-tells-founders]] @17:20–@18:20).
- **2024-09-25 (own show E1):** Kyle's first-board story: his first full month at Owner was worse than the month he started, and Lemkin answered his leading indicators with "but it's not revenue". He "sulked away from that meeting feeling terrified" ([[src-E01-saastr-founder-jason-lemkin-on-why-revenue-leaders-must-run]] @43:27–@44:29).
- **2024-10-04 (LinkedIn):** "To have true C-suite influence, you must transition from solely managing a sales team to operating as an 'executive'." ([[src-linkedin-2022-2024]] post 2024-10-04).
- **2024-10-06:** "fear is sort of the end of you as a revenue leader"; "I was personally afraid of the number that I was given in 2019", and he left that company. Don't blame the product in a board meeting; socialize product feedback one-on-one first ([[src-topline-e78-executive-fear-boards]] @05:07, @19:48–@25:05).
- **2024-10-30:** Time-horizon gap: landing a January plan with an eight-week hiring cycle, three-month ramp and six-month deal cycle means "you're already cooked"; "you can torture a financial model and make it tell you anything you want" ([[src-E06-untold-strategies-of-high-impact-sales-recruitment-with-asad]] @10:17).
- **2024-10-30:** Senior leader is *additive*, executive is a *multiplier* (from Lemkin/SaaStr and an executive coach); three C-suite metric competencies ([[src-bridge-the-gap-scaling-startups]] @11:14–@21:34).
- **2024-10-31:** Real revenue leaders make hard short-term trade-offs; "amateurs" churn through VP Sales roles; he slowed growth intentionally and explained it to the board with confidence ([[src-venture-podcast-cro-owner]] @09:21–@14:39).
- **2024-11-27:** Discloses his own Owner comp, "80 base 20 variable": "you don't want me on a 50 50 plan. I cannot resist my own urges to make money." ([[src-E10-mark-bergen-on-aligning-sales-product-partners-and-mission]] @21:40).
- **2024-12-03:** 50/50 plans for revenue executives are "pretty silly"; revenue leaders should own onboarding ([[src-testbox-lessons-in-scaling]] @34:40–@38:50).
- **2024-12-18:** "Why are you putting a VP of sales on a 50-50 plan? They need to think six to 18 months in the future." ([[src-E12-bravados-ceo-sahil-mansuri-on-the-future-of-sales-reps-and]] @50:49).

## Tensions, contradictions & open questions
- **Owning CS vs. handing it off.** He says everybody owns retention and a CRO's job is enterprise value, yet CS moved off his plate between late 2024 and Dec 2025 (see [[org-design]]). On his own show he questioned whether CRO-owned CS should be the default ([[src-E72-i-collapsed-my-gtm-org-chart-this-is-why]] @27:07).
- **How much variable does Kyle carry?** He has opposed 50/50 leader plans since 2022 ([[src-gtmnow-5-slow-is-smooth]] @24:42). In Nov 2024 he disclosed an 80/20 base/variable Owner plan ([[src-E10-mark-bergen-on-aligning-sales-product-partners-and-mission]] @21:40). By Oct 2025 he said his comp is tied to overall company growth ([[src-polaris-lodestar-s1e1]] @03:12), and by May 2026 he had "very little variable". Either the plan was restructured or 20% counts as "very little"; he hasn't said which.
- **How hands-on with AI must a CRO be?** The AI-era CRO framing predates 2026.
